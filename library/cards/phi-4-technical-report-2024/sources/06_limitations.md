- No training corpus is released. The model repository contains weights and metadata, not pretraining documents, synthetic records, SFT conversations, Pivotal Token Search pairs, judge-guided pairs, safety records, candidates, verifier outputs, or rejection decisions.
- The 400B figure counts unweighted synthetic source tokens, while 9.8T is the reported final training-token budget. Final source weights, repetitions, unique records, stage allocations, and a crosswalk between these quantities are unknown.
- The report names source classes and examples but not a complete source inventory, revisions, record IDs, proportions, attribution, or license matrix. The later data summary confirms commercial and other third-party data while leaving some underlying detail unavailable.
- Most synthetic generator and SFT evaluator identities, prompts, decoding settings, seeds, candidate counts, thresholds, acceptance rates, and compute are unknown. “LLM-based evaluation” is not a replayable selection contract.
- Pivotal Token Search depends on noisy success-probability estimates from sampled continuations. The binary search may miss pivotal tokens when success is not near-monotone, and the rollout count, thresholds, variance, memoization state, and failure audit are not released.
- Task oracles are partial. Code tests and mathematical final-answer comparison can establish an endpoint under one checker, but do not validate each reasoning step, factual explanation, safety response, or domain without an oracle.
- GPT-4o creates labels for the second DPO round using accuracy, style, and detail. Judge ordering, raw scores, disagreements, calibration, style/verbosity effects, false accepts, and rejected pairs are unavailable.
- Decontamination uses 13-gram and 7-gram methods, but the report concedes that rephrased overlap may survive. Exact thresholds are not reliably available in the public HTML, and no benchmark-revision, match, or removal ledger is released.
- November 2024 AMC is fresh relative to the June 2024 collection cutoff, but it tests model behavior on four contests; it cannot prove that other evaluations or the training mixture are uncontaminated.
- Model weights use MIT and the manuscript uses CC BY 4.0. Those licenses do not grant rights to unreleased public, acquired, licensed, or synthetic training records; record-level rights and generated-output terms remain unknown.
- The official model repository evolves after release. Reusers should pin a revision because tokenizer or generation metadata can change independently of the original training run.
- Benchmark gains, hardware scale, and data volume are model-development evidence. They do not prove corpus quality, provenance, diversity, verifier accuracy, safety completeness, or legal compatibility.
