The strongest artifact evidence is the official model repository. It is labeled MIT, totals about 29.3 GB, and exposes six BF16 safetensor shards plus configuration, tokenizer, generation, license, model-card, and data-summary files. This verifies that the phi-4 checkpoint is downloadable. It does not verify a training-data release, because no corpus files, SFT rows, DPO pairs, candidate responses, or verifier logs are present.

The official model card reports a 14B dense decoder-only model, 16K context, 9.8T training tokens, 1,920 H100-80G GPUs, 21 training days, a June 2024 cutoff for public data, and about 8% multilingual data. The later official data summary confirms public sources, commercial licensing agreements, acquired data, synthetic data, cleaning, and a 30 June 2024 latest collection date. It does not enumerate sources or records and says some other third-party information cannot be provided because underlying data are unavailable.

The report provides unusually concrete construction counts. About 50 synthetic families total roughly 400B unweighted source tokens; SFT consumes about 8B tokens; first-round Pivotal Token Search DPO has 250,297 examples; second-round judge-guided DPO has 841,842 pairs and is described as approximately 850K. These figures establish report-level scale, not unique record count, released-row count, or a complete lineage into the 9.8T-token run.

Post-training ablations separate model behavior after SFT, Pivotal Token Search DPO, second-round-only DPO, and both DPO rounds. For example, the report lists GPQA as 47.3 after SFT, 53.6 after first-round DPO, 52.4 after second-round-only DPO, and 56.1 after both; MATH is 77.1, 80.5, 77.6, and 80.4. These comparisons support the reported optimizer sequence under its evaluation setup. They do not certify the correctness, rights, diversity, or contamination status of training records.

Fresh November 2024 AMC evaluation occurs after the June 2024 collection cutoff and is useful model evidence against direct exposure to those particular contests. The report nevertheless acknowledges that n-gram decontamination can miss rephrased overlap. A fresh benchmark cannot replace a released corpus-level match and removal ledger.

The report itself identifies weaknesses: SimpleQA, DROP, and IFEval are weak relative to several comparisons, and strict instruction following was not a focus of the synthetic generation. These results are evidence about model behavior and recipe tradeoffs, not evidence that more synthetic data or higher scores automatically means higher data quality.
