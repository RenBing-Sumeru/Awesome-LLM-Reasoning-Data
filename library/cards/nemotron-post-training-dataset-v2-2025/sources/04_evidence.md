The release-level evidence is exact and directly relevant to the data object. The official card reports 239,467 math, 175,000 code, 355,000 STEM, 627,720 chat, 975,202 Japanese, 1,015,314 German, 1,016,503 Italian, 935,704 Spanish, and 1,001,504 French rows. These nine values sum to 6,341,414. The same card identifies version 2.0, release date 20 August 2025, Parquet format, an SFT configuration, contact-information gating, and a repository-level CC-BY-4.0 label.

The generator ledger is not a row ledger: DeepSeek-R1-0528 is listed for 5,713,694 samples; Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct for 3,928,913; Qwen3-30B-A3B for 627,720; Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct-AWQ for 1,015,314; and Qwen3-235B-A22B for 627,720. The card explicitly warns that multiple models may produce one record. The sum therefore must not be interpreted as dataset size or one-teacher attribution.

The linked report gives a different accounting surface: approximately 80 billion SFT prompt-response tokens and Table 7 domain counts of 1.5M math, 1.1M coding, 2.0M science, 400K tool-calling, 1.5M conversational, 2K safety, and 5.0M multilingual samples (Section 3.1, PDF pp.13-14). Those figures describe the model's SFT stages, not a row-for-row manifest of this 6.34M release; no official reconciliation is supplied.

For model behavior, Table 8 reports the aligned 12B checkpoint at 85.42 AIME-2024, 76.25 AIME-2025, 97.75 MATH-500, 64.48 GPQA-Diamond, 70.79 LiveCodeBench, 89.81 IFEval, 66.98 BFCL v3, 83.36 RULER@128k, and 74 ArenaHard. The evaluation averages 16 runs for AIME, four for MATH-500/GPQA/LiveCodeBench/IFEval, and one for the remaining listed benchmarks (Section 3.3, PDF p.16). These scores concern a checkpoint trained through multiple data and optimization stages. They do **not** isolate this release or prove row-level correctness, provenance, decontamination, licensing, or verifier quality.

No independent reproduction, per-row verifier audit, rejection-rate table, or immutable file checksum ledger was found in the confirmed official artifacts. Artifact existence and model performance are therefore stronger claims than artifact completeness or data quality.
