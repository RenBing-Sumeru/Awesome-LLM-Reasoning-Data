No training scale or mixture is disclosed. Named upstream counts such as RoboRefIt's original scenes do not reveal how many records MiMo selected, duplicated, filtered, replayed, or weighted. Without per-stage media, tokens, CoT, trajectories, and RL rollouts, exposure and domain balance cannot be audited.

Same-source train/evaluation risk is material. Many training families reappear as evaluation families or close derivatives, and inherited MiMo-VL exposure is unknown. No row, media, perceptual, temporal, scene, route, subject, geography, or synthetic-derivative manifest establishes clean separation.

Rule rewards are not environment safety. Exact answers, IoU, point-in-mask, and format checks do not verify manipulation stability, navigation completion, collision avoidance, comfort, traffic compliance, uncertainty calibration, or fail-safe behavior. The NAVSIM branch lacks released rewards, simulator revision, rollouts, planner code, and checkpoints.

Open-loop evidence cannot establish closed-loop or sim-to-real robustness. NAVSIM is offline/non-reactive, proprietary trajectories use open-loop L2, and “real-world” robot evidence is qualitative target-point visualization rather than controlled execution. Reactive actors, sensor failures, actuator dynamics, distribution shift, and compounding errors remain untested.

Proprietary driving and egocentric media create unresolved privacy and governance risks involving faces, plates, bystanders, drivers, vulnerable road users, precise time/location, geography, retention, access, consent, annotation labor, redaction, and deletion. MIT/Apache code and weight labels do not grant rights to those records.

Stage-3 CoT may be plausible but unfaithful because its teacher, prompts, validation, and rejects are closed. LingoQA additionally depends on an unspecified classifier checkpoint. Finally, 7B branding conflicts with the 8B Hugging Face display, and the release does not reconcile parameter-count conventions or provide a clean immutable lineage.
