Formal planning is sensitive to specification mistakes: a strong solver can return a valid solution to the wrong formal problem. Long-horizon tasks also remain difficult for language models, as the authors emphasize. Since the surveyed systems may use different planning languages, planners, and task settings, their outcomes should not be treated as directly interchangeable.

This means final plan validity is necessary but may not be sufficient for judging a system against the intended natural-language task. A formally valid result can preserve an earlier interpretation error. The survey does not impose one common representation or measurement protocol, so comparisons should retain the specification language, planner, and definition of successful goal achievement used by each study.
