The central idea is to treat a language model as a planning formalizer rather than as the final planner. It surveys ways to turn or refine planning specifications into forms usable by off-the-shelf automated planning systems, then discusses methods and challenges. This distinction matters because fluent plan text is not the same thing as a formal model a solver can execute reliably.

This framing separates responsibilities: the language model handles natural-language interpretation or refinement, while the planner operates on a formal representation. It gives readers a way to locate errors at the handoff rather than attributing every failure to planning ability. The survey is useful for comparing systems that use similar planners but differ in how the planning model is constructed.
