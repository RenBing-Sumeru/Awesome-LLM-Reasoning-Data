Phi-4-Mini-Reasoning asks whether a 3.8B model can acquire long-form mathematical reasoning through a coordinated post-training pipeline rather than a single small distillation set. The paper motivates this question with two observations: DeepSeek-R1-style distillation can transfer reasoning to small models, but detailed recipes are often missing; and naively fine-tuning Phi-4-Mini on LIMO or s1K lowers the paper's reported AIME 2024, MATH-500, and GPQA Diamond results relative to the base model.

For this atlas, the decision boundary is disclosure rather than leaderboard rank. The relevant data objects are math questions, long-CoT responses, verification outcomes, difficulty and domain attributes, correct-over-incorrect preference pairs, and online RL rollouts. The relevant feedback interfaces are a math-verification tool, GPT-4o-mini rechecking and difficulty labels, DPO preferences, and binary final-answer rewards. The report is useful because it exposes these layers and their approximate scale, while a complete training corpus, source manifest, verifier implementation, and contamination audit remain unavailable.
