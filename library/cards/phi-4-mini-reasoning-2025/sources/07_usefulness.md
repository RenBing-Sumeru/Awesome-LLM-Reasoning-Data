Within the Frontier Reports and Data Disclosure Ledger track, this Card supports a stage-by-stage comparison of small-model reasoning releases. Curators can ask the same questions across reports: Which questions and traces enter mid-training? Which records survive verification? How is the SFT subset selected? Are rejected generations retained as preference evidence? Is RL feedback executable, judge-mediated, or mixed? Which counts and artifacts are actually released?

Phi-4-Mini-Reasoning supports precise labels. It describes trace distillation, answer-outcome rejection sampling, correct-over-incorrect preference learning, and answer-level RLVR. It also discloses several sequence lengths, learning rates, evaluation settings, and sampling interventions. It does not justify labels such as open training data, process-supervised reasoning, fully programmatic verification, released reward stack, contamination-audited corpus, or reproducible end-to-end recipe.

The paper is especially useful for separating three evidence layers: reported training construction, released model artifacts, and reported benchmark behavior. The 10M rollouts and stage subsets belong to the first layer but are unavailable; the Hugging Face weights belong to the second; benchmark and ablation numbers belong to the third. Keeping these layers distinct prevents an open-weight model or a strong score from being misread as an open or validated data pipeline.
