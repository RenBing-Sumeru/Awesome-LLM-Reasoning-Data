Scale evidence must preserve two denominators. Paper §4.3 and Appendix Table 2 report **9,533 faulty trajectories** containing **24,843 injected agent/error instances**, not 24,843 trajectories. By task, the trajectory counts are MATH 2,048, SciBench 1,871, GSM8K 1,741, HumanEval 1,497, MMLU 1,446, and GAIA 954. By framework they are LLM Debate 2,404, MacNet 2,359, AgentVerse 1,995, DyLAN 1,845, SmolAgents 481, and Magentic-One 449. The arithmetic in both partitions sums to 9,533.

Table 1 reports attribution results under the paper's training setup. Aegis-SFT with Qwen2.5-14B averages 26.51 across the Aegis-Bench and Who&When metrics shown, compared with 13.99 for the corresponding base model; Aegis-GRPO reports 18.41. The DCL method reports 12.61 versus a 4.08 random baseline. These are author-reported aggregate scores under disclosed model/training combinations and were not independently reproduced for this Card.

The human check tests label fidelity on only a sample. Three experts independently label 100 Aegis-Bench trajectories; Appendix B.3 reports human-human Fleiss' κ=0.85 and program-human κ=0.81. This is evidence that planned attribution often agrees with expert reading on that sample. Recruitment, raw judgments, per-mode errors, disagreement adjudication, and whether the sample represents all frameworks and rare modes are not released, so the result does not validate every row or establish causal necessity.

The paper also reports negative evidence. In the SFT epoch study, out-of-distribution Who&When performance peaks after two epochs and then declines while in-domain Aegis-Bench continues to improve; the authors attribute this divergence to overfitting synthetic style (Paper §7, Figure 3c). Appendix A.2 shows a geography case in which every tested model, including Aegis, misses a subtle numeric root cause. Macro-F1 also trails Micro-F1 for rarer error modes. These results limit claims of transfer and taxonomy coverage.

Artifact evidence is stronger for availability than for replay. The official HF repository exposes train, validation, and test JSONL files with audited SHA-256 hashes `add3aae4…6466a8`, `fe0b3d0e…f1ffe49`, and `a2f2793f…9f65bd1`; the GitHub repository exposes construction/evaluation code at audited commit `ced13443…`. However, no common version binds paper, code, data, model APIs, and evaluators. Benchmark gains therefore demonstrate performance in the reported setup; they do not prove data quality, split isolation, verifier correctness, legal reuse, release completeness, or deterministic replay.
