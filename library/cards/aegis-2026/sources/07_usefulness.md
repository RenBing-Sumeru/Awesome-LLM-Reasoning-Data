For the assigned `environment_agent_trajectory_data` track, Aegis is most useful as an offline full-episode corpus for studying multi-agent failure attribution. A researcher can preserve the query, ordered interaction history, framework and agent metadata, planned intervention, evaluator outcome, agent/error labels, and correct-answer provenance, then separate errors in generation, communication, verification, and attribution. The card also supplies an audit template for asking whether an “agent trajectory” is paired with its baseline, terminal predicate, discarded attempts, and replay metadata.

The released supervision supports a bounded SFT recipe: serialize the complete episode plus the 14-mode taxonomy and predict structured JSON identifying faulty agents and error modes. It also supports the paper's structured GRPO/RLVR reward, including exact-pair credit, one-time partial credit, and format/duplicate/false-positive/excess penalties. These uses should retain the source split and version metadata and should report Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 separately, because rare modes are obscured by aggregate frequency.

The construction pipeline is reusable as an experimental recipe: obtain evaluator-confirmed successful baselines, design explicit agent/error interventions, apply prompt or response corruption, rerun the MAS, retain only outcome reversals, and preserve both the plan and full failed trace. A stronger follow-up should additionally release all attempts, including non-failing and self-corrected runs, and group splits by upstream task and baseline. This would permit resilience analysis and cleaner counterfactual comparisons rather than learning only from interventions that defeat the evaluator.

The mixed verifier is a concrete research surface. Teams can compare normalized answer checks, executable tests, tolerance rules, extractors, and the GAIA LLM judge; measure false positives and false negatives; vary evaluator versions; and test whether GRPO learns formatting or attribution shortcuts. The 100-trajectory expert study can be extended with released raw labels, stratification by mode/framework, blinded adjudication, and causal-ablation checks that remove one injected error at a time.

Reuse class: **conditional research training and evaluation**, not a replayable agent environment. Pin the audited code/HF revisions and hashes, verify local license and upstream-rights requirements, keep Aegis-Bench isolated, and disclose that only induced failures were released. Do not broaden the evidence to generic agent training, dense process supervision, preference learning, or model-checkpoint reuse; exact model artifacts, complete baseline lineage, grouped splits, evaluator snapshots, and immutable replay remain unresolved.
