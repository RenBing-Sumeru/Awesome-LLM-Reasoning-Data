The model card reports hallucination, occasional slowness or timeouts, and over-refusal of benign requests. A knowledge cutoff of January 2025 limits freshness. Parallel thinking may increase coverage by exploring multiple hypotheses, but no branch count, diversity control, stopping rule, aggregation rule, or compute-normalized comparison is disclosed. A 192K output ceiling is not the hidden thinking budget, and best-of-32 evaluation is not a serving or training budget.

The feedback contract is the largest construction gap. Human and critic feedback are named, while the family report describes a Data Reward Model and a rubric-driven prompted Critic. Deep Think-specific reward equations, rubric content, critic checkpoint, human-comparison protocol, weighting, calibration, and false-positive/false-negative audit are unknown. As a curator inference, a critic may reward fluent rubric compliance or converge across correlated branches without detecting a subtly false proof; a model may also optimize stylistic or length signals rather than correctness. No evidence shows how reward hacking or critic gaming was tested during reasoning RL.

Training data and release limitations are equally material. Source manifests, counts, mixture weights, generators, filters, rejected traces, rights, splits, and Deep Think-specific decontamination are absent. Family-level decontamination cannot establish that newly added theorem-proving and mathematics-solution corpora do not overlap benchmarks. No data, weights, code, reward implementation, parallel trace, or evaluation transcript is released, preventing independent reconstruction.

Version mapping creates a separate evaluation risk. FSF tests used a preliminary implementation with a different thinking prompt and serving configuration; the app release is a faster variation of the hours-long IMO model. The report gives no checkpoint equivalence study. Cyber evaluations also vary attempt counts and tool affordances by task and model. Consequently, capability and safety aggregates should remain attached to their specific configuration, tool set, attempt budget, and date.

Safety evidence is broad but incomplete. CBRN threat-model details are withheld; external deceptive-alignment testing and the final CBRN critical-capability determination were unfinished at publication. Manual checks reported no invalid reward-hacked RE-Bench experiments, but raw trajectories, sampling rates, adjudication and false-negative analysis are unavailable. Deployment monitoring and rate limits reduce product risk but do not demonstrate that the training reward itself prevents misuse.
