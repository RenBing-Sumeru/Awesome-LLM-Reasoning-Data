This Card is most useful as a disclosure and audit reference. A model builder can convert it into a minimum reporting checklist: name each reasoning record class; publish prompt and trace schemas; distinguish human preferences from critic scores; specify reward aggregation and calibration; report branch count, selection, stopping, and compute; and map every evaluation to a checkpoint, thinking prompt, tools, and sampling budget.

For feedback-system design, the family-level Data Reward Model plus rubric-prompted Critic is a comparison point, not a Deep Think recipe. A controlled study could compare human preference, critic grading, programmatic mathematical checks, and hybrid rewards under matched rollouts, then measure critic false positives, correlated branch errors, reward hacking, and proof validity. Deep Think's missing disclosure identifies exactly which artifacts such a study must release.

For test-time-compute evaluation, researchers should report pass@1 separately from best-of-N, equalize total sampled tokens and tool calls, and avoid translating a 192K output limit into an internal reasoning budget. The app, preliminary FSF, and IMO variants should be evaluated as distinct configurations unless checkpoint and serving equivalence is demonstrated.

For safety audit, the Model Card offers a useful template spanning automated development tests, human and automated red teaming, held-out assurance, expert CBRN review, cyber autonomy, ML R&D, deceptive alignment, correctness checks, and deployment mitigations. Reuse should add raw or privacy-preserving prompts, transcripts, grader versions, adjudication, failure retention, and false-negative analysis.

Reuse class: safe for reading, audit design, and evaluation-protocol comparison. Capability aggregates may be reused only with their stated budgets and version conditions. Training reuse is blocked because no trainable records, reward implementation, weights, licenses, or reproducible construction artifacts are released.
