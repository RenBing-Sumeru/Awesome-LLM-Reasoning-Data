The prior Gemini 2.5 family report already describes sparse-MoE multimodality, SFT, reward modeling, RL*F, decontamination, and agentic evaluation. Deep Think does not make those components new. Its distinct disclosure is the combination of additional multi-step reasoning, problem-solving and theorem-proving material, curated mathematics solutions, novel RL for extended paths, and inference-time parallel exploration that can revise or combine hypotheses.

For reasoning-data research, the new object is not a released dataset but a partially specified frontier pipeline. The report links named data classes to an extended-reasoning training stage and then to a multi-hypothesis inference behavior. It also places capability results beside unusually broad frontier-safety evaluation. This makes it possible to audit four separate interfaces: source records, human/critic feedback, parallel inference budget, and safety evaluation.

The report does not establish that parallel branches become training traces, that the critic verifies mathematical truth, or that family-level Data Reward Model plus Critic fully defines the Deep Think reasoning reward. Nor does it introduce a reproducible selection method, verifier, dataset, or open environment. Much of the contribution is scale, proprietary integration, and disclosure rather than an open construction recipe.

Before reuse, a researcher would need the reasoning-corpus manifests, generators, schemas, selected and rejected traces, reward and critic versions, branch/aggregation policy, Deep Think-specific contamination results, and checkpoint mapping. Without those artifacts, the Card is a direction and audit reference, not evidence for safe training-data reuse.
