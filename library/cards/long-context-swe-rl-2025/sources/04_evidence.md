The main evidence is an author-reported training progression on SWE-bench Verified. Pass@1 is 11.4% for the base Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct agent, 20.5% after RFT, 35.7% after Stage 1 RL, and 39.0% after Stage 2 RL. The final checkpoint reaches 35.0% on the held-out May SWE-rebench split and 31.7% on June. These numbers are tied to the paper's scaffold, validation harness, long-context budgets, and checkpoint-selection process; no released model or independent reproduction was verified (Table 1).

The staged comparison supports the narrower claim that terminally verified self-generated RFT followed by on-policy RL correlates with higher repair pass rates under the reported setup. It does not isolate every change: Stage 2 simultaneously changes task selection, context length, turn limit, problems per iteration, and batch size. The score increase therefore does not prove that longer context alone, the 2,028-task curriculum alone, or the underlying trajectory corpus is higher quality.

A concrete negative result exposes sampling sensitivity. During training, a vLLM change began honoring model-config `top_k` and `min_p`, truncating the intended rollout distribution; performance degraded after 5–10 iterations. Restoring unfiltered sampling recovered performance. This is evidence that seemingly minor inference-version or configuration drift can alter the on-policy data distribution and training outcome, not merely an implementation footnote.

The paper also reports that deleting all over-context repetitive episodes removes useful negative examples. Its final design instead applies a soft length penalty, allowing both successful and failed RL trajectories to contribute when their ten-rollout group has reward variation. This contrasts with RFT, where failed attempts are rejected. The paper does not publish counts by RL outcome, trajectory lengths, zero-advantage drop rates, verifier false positives/negatives, or raw logs needed to audit those explanations.

All results remain author claims. Repeated use of Verified-50 across more than 100 RL iterations may influence checkpoint and design selection, while May/June exclusion is only a temporal training boundary. Neither benchmark scores nor passing tests establish dataset legality, lack of contamination, semantic patch quality beyond the tests, safe agent behavior, or replayability.
