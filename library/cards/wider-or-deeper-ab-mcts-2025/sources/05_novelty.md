AB-MCTS changes the branching decision from a fixed search hyperparameter into an online allocation problem. Independent best-of-\(N\) sampling spends every call on width. Iterative refinement spends calls on depth. Standard fixed-branching MCTS predetermines how many children expansion creates. AB-MCTS instead keeps a `GEN` option in competition with existing answer paths and lets Bayesian Thompson sampling decide whether the next call should open a new response or revise a prior one.

The response-tree representation is equally important for reasoning-data analysis. A non-root edge is not merely an abstract search transition: it links a complete answer to a new complete answer generated from the task, the prior response, and evaluator feedback. The tree can therefore encode alternative attempts and explicit revision lineage in one object. The separation between tree width and revision depth makes allocation policy, generation budget, evaluator result, and final selection inspectable at the node level when local artifacts are retained.

The two variants make different modeling choices rather than introducing new sources of supervision. AB-MCTS-M models adaptive branching through a mixture; AB-MCTS-A aggregates observations through Gaussian or Beta conjugate models. Neither adds human step labels, a learned judge, or a process reward model. Its feedback remains the task evaluator: public test fraction, ARC demonstration accuracy, or MLE validation score.

For data curation, the important novelty is the distinction between **trace-generating capability** and **trace release**. The official code supports full `MCTSResult` tree serialization and per-call request/response logging, so it defines a strong capture schema for future releases. Yet the published artifacts stop before distributing the realized experimental instances. This makes AB-MCTS both a search recipe and a useful audit case: open algorithms, aggregate tree figures, and selected answers are not equivalent to releasing every generated node and its feedback lineage.

Novelty should not be extended beyond the evidence. The method does not establish a new training objective, a new benchmark, a public trace dataset, explicit failure pruning, or a general theorem that deeper/wider adaptation is optimal. Its empirical contribution is limited to the tested benchmark surfaces, model APIs, evaluators, and nominal generation-call budgets.
