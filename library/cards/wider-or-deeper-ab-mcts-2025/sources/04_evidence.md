**What the paper demonstrates.** Across CodeContest, LiveCodeBench, ARC-AGI, and MLE-Bench, the paper compares adaptive branching against single-response refinement, repeated independent sampling, and standard MCTS under generation budgets that grow mainly to 128 calls. Its reported benchmark curves support the narrower claim that dynamically reallocating calls between new attempts and revisions can improve task success under the tested models, evaluators, and budgets. The width/depth analyses and complete illustrated trees up to node 128 show that AB-MCTS changes search geometry rather than merely returning more independent answers.

**What the extended study demonstrates.** ARC-AGI analyses explore budgets up to 512 nodes, and the ARC-AGI-2 extension uses 250 calls with three precisely named model snapshots. It reports discovery-oriented Pass@k as well as final-selection measures. This distinguishes the ability of a tree to contain a correct branch from the ability of the selection rule to submit the correct answer. The 250-call result must not be reported as part of the main 128-call protocol.

**What the implementation verifies.** The official supplemental code supplies stronger evidence about the trace object than aggregate benchmark tables alone. `MCTSResult.save()` serializes the root and recursively reachable nodes; `Node` records expose parent/child topology, prompt, completion, evaluation result, model name, visits, and values. The solver’s revision prompt contains the prior generation and evaluator-derived feedback. OpenAI-compatible calls can log request/response payloads and token/price usage. Thus the published recipe is technically capable of retaining the complete answer tree, all locally generated alternatives, revision lineage, and scoring context.

**What is not demonstrated or released.** No public experiment corpus contains those serialized trees or per-call logs. The supplemental logging directories are empty, TreeQuest publishes tooling rather than paper-run outputs, and the ARC-AGI-2 repository says newly generated logs appear under `outputs/` without committing the realized outputs. Figures and aggregate tables do not substitute for machine-readable nodes, all responses, unselected branches, evaluator histories, or generation order. No explicit pruning path was found, so locally unselected nodes can remain recoverable; however, their absence from the public release prevents direct verification of this behavior on the reported runs.

Benchmark improvements are not evidence that the generated trace data are unbiased, licensed for reuse, decontaminated, or independently reproduced. The evidence supports an inference-time search method and an implementable trace contract. It does not support claims of a released dataset, step-level correctness, semantic faithfulness, training effectiveness, or universal superiority across evaluators and cost models.
