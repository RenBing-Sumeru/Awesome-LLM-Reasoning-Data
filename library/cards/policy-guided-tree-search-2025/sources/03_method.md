PGTS is trained and evaluated across GSM8K, MATH/MATH500, AQUA, StrategyQA, PrOntoQA, GPQA, and Blocksworld, covering mathematics, commonsense reasoning, logical deduction, and planning. Policy training uses at most 1,000 examples from a benchmark’s training partition. GSM8K and AQUA use their original splits; MATH training data trains the policy and MATH500 is used for evaluation; StrategyQA randomly reserves 1,000 examples for test and uses the remainder for training; PrOntoQA follows RAP splits; and Blocksworld uses split-v1 for training and split-v2 for testing. Exact sampled IDs, the StrategyQA random seed, a unified split manifest, and GPQA’s detailed policy-training split are not released.

LLaMA 3.1 8B and 70B act as the target generators. Main generation uses temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.9. A reasoning step is one sentence for most tasks; MATH permits a sentence or line, and Blocksworld uses one action sentence. When a leaf is expanded, the audited implementation requests up to `max_breadth` candidate actions, sorts them by action log-probability, executes them, and attaches all returned children. The learned policy therefore controls tree navigation, but candidate production and within-sibling ordering still depend on target-model likelihood.

The policy state encodes the revealed tree rather than only the active chain. Public code nodes contain task state, scalar reward, auxiliary `info`, parent, all children, visit count, depth, node ID, trace, node features, and the graph edge index. Policy learning records the selected action, selected-action log-probability, value prediction, reward, next state, and constraints. It does not retain the full probability distribution over all legal and illegal navigation actions. Consequently, the public runtime supports on-policy PPO updates but cannot by itself answer counterfactual questions about how much probability every unchosen action received.

Search budgets are task-specific. GSM8K, AQUA, StrategyQA, and PrOntoQA use breadth 4, depth 16, and at most 64 search/reasoning steps. MATH500 uses breadth 4, depth 64, and at most 256 steps. The 4- and 8-step Blocksworld settings use corresponding depths and at most 32 steps. GPQA’s depth and maximum step cap are not explicitly disclosed. Evaluation also includes reward-weighted self-consistency over four or eight independently generated complete chains; these aggregation runs should not be confused with the branches inside a single PGTS tree.

The controller is a two-layer GPS graph policy/value network trained with PPO. The paper discloses clip 0.2, discount 0.99, GAE lambda 0.95, the four action costs, and training from random initialization. It does not disclose learning rate, batch size, PPO update count, rollout count per update, random seeds, retry policy, or complete per-dataset configuration files. No trained policy checkpoint is released.

At runtime, the root tree can represent more than the selected answer. Expansion attaches all generated siblings, including children that may retain `visits == 0`; visited paths abandoned after a branch or backtrack also remain linked. `visualize(prune=True)` can hide zero-visit nodes at rendering time, but the implementation does not assign semantic labels such as accepted, rejected, or pruned. Evaluation can write `outputs.pth`, `auxiliary.pth`, and `metrics.pth`, and visualization loads the serialized auxiliary object. This shows that full and abandoned branches are representable by the code; it does not show that the paper’s experiment trees or branch decisions were publicly released.
