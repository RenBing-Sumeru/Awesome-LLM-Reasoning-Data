For **Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data**, PGTS is a concrete example of treating search control itself as data. The target LLM’s partial reasoning states are organized into a graph, and the controller contributes an aligned sequence of navigation actions, masks, costs, scalar rewards, selected-action probabilities, value targets, and stop conditions. This lets researchers compare not only final answers or complete rollouts, but also how a fixed inference budget was allocated among continuing, branching, and abandoning work.

Evidence-backed uses are limited to `agent_training`, `evaluation`, and `test_time_compute`. The published recipe can guide training of an external search controller with PPO, evaluation of learned navigation against fixed tree-search heuristics, and analysis of how breadth, depth, action costs, maximum steps, and self-consistency counts affect accuracy and generated tokens. It does not support SFT, preference learning, process supervision, or RLVR training of the target LLM because the paper does not use or release those data contracts.

A research reproduction should instrument the runtime before generating new data. Each record should pin the problem source and split ID; target-model and controller revisions; prompt and sampling settings; complete tree with stable node/edge IDs; current node at every decision; legal-action mask; full action distribution; selected action; action cost; immediate and terminal reward components; value prediction; generated, visited, abandoned, and never-visited branch status; task-terminal, policy-terminate, or budget stop reason; and per-example calls, tokens, latency, and cost. This would turn a code-representable object into an auditable trace release.

The paper is also useful for controlled studies. One can hold the generator, prompts, and maximum budget fixed while varying the learned controller, likelihood ordering, action costs, or backtracking depth; compare paper-defined termination with code-level task/budget termination; and ask whether full policy distributions predict unnecessary exploration. Abandoned branches are especially valuable negative evidence because they reveal what the controller considered and later left, but they need explicit semantic labels rather than being inferred from `visits == 0` or visualization pruning.

Practical reuse is conditional. The official code and selected inputs can support inspection and a new research implementation, but no trained checkpoint, paper-exact configs, raw run trees, split manifest, or full policy logs are available. License and upstream data rights are `unknown`, so public visibility is not sufficient permission for redistribution or production training. Until those gaps are resolved, the appropriate use is a strong recipe and audit reference, not a reusable PGTS trace dataset.

When comparing results, report accuracy alongside generated tokens, model calls, wall-clock latency, unique nodes, generated/visited/abandoned branches, and the distribution of stop reasons. Benchmark accuracy alone cannot demonstrate that the search traces are well calibrated, complete, uncontaminated, or legally reusable.
