The paper reports experiments with LLaMA 3.1 8B and 70B on seven benchmark families: GSM8K, MATH500, AQUA, StrategyQA, PrOntoQA, GPQA, and Blocksworld. The comparisons cover Chain-of-Thought prompting, tree-search baselines, PGTS, and reward-weighted self-consistency variants. Across the reported tables, the authors attribute higher task accuracy and lower generation cost to learned tree navigation. These are author-reported benchmark results; the public release does not include the paper-run trees, controller checkpoint, result files, or per-item decisions needed for independent reconstruction.

The most directly auditable compute evidence is aggregate. On MATH, the paper reports MCTS using 16.25 times the Chain-of-Thought token count, versus 5.28 times for PGTS. On GSM8K, it reports 13.33 times for MCTS and 1.29 times for PGTS. These normalized totals support the narrower statement that the reported PGTS runs consumed fewer generated tokens than the compared MCTS setup. They do not establish equal wall-clock time, equal model-call structure, or equal per-example budgets, and they do not reveal whether savings concentrate on easy items while difficult items consume the cap.

The AQUA breadth ablation makes budget sensitivity explicit. At breadth 2, 4, and 8, PGTS is reported to generate 252.16, 283.24, and 370.54 tokens, respectively; MCTS is reported at 399.13, 1241.37, and 3282.41. This is evidence that search width changes cost and that the compared controllers scale differently in this setup. It is not evidence that a released data object has complete token accounting, nor that every baseline was matched on generated tokens, latency, or monetary cost.

The policy-training curve reports convergence around 1,000 training examples, and the experimental protocol caps policy training at 1,000 examples from each dataset’s training split. This supports a sample-efficiency observation about the controller in the reported setup. Exact sample IDs, random seeds, rollout counts per update, optimizer batch settings, and the trained checkpoints are absent, so the result cannot be converted into a fully reproducible controller-training dataset.

Implementation inspection adds evidence about representability, not about released experimental records. `pgts.py` maintains parent–child trees with node rewards, visits, traces, features, and IDs; stores selected action log-probability, value, reward, and trajectory return; and can pass the root and trajectory into serialized evaluation outputs. Generated-but-unvisited children and abandoned visited paths can remain attached. No committed paper-result `.pth` files, replay buffers, full action distributions, or semantic branch manifests were found at the audited revision.

Benchmark performance must not be read as proof of search-data quality. Terminal correctness can coexist with invalid intermediate reasoning; likelihood-shaped rewards can prefer fluent errors; and aggregate accuracy cannot establish provenance, branch coverage, reward calibration, contamination control, or licensing. The evidence supports the PGTS recipe and its reported compute/accuracy behavior, while the item-level data claims remain bounded by the missing artifacts.
