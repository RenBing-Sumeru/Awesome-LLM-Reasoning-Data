The public data does not expose the complete selection history. For SFT, nine rejected candidates per prompt and their scores are absent. For DPO, non-selected pairs and supporting evidence are absent. The original 4,000-prompt online split, GRPO rollouts, claim-level verifier decisions, retrieved documents, and reward-component logs are also absent. The released preparation script can derive a GRPO prompt table from the DPO file, but that is not equivalent to releasing the original online interactions.

The feedback contract is judgment-heavy and non-stationary. Claim boundaries, search results, evidence ranking, verifier model outputs, and the relevance comparison can all change or be wrong. Reproducing the reported service also required substantial infrastructure: the paper reports 32 H100 GPUs for eight Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct verifier workers, in addition to training and policy-inference workers. A mock reward server in the code repository is only for smoke testing and does not produce meaningful factuality rewards.

There are further audit gaps. Only the LongFact demonstrations are explicitly identified as non-test; broader benchmark and retrieval-corpus overlap is unknown. The paper reports one-epoch training, whereas current repository configs contain larger epoch/step limits, so an exact run needs commit- and command-level provenance. Evaluation relies on automated claim verification and an LLM judge over six benchmarks rather than a full human data audit. Finally, CC-BY-NC-4.0, Llama terms, third-party content obligations, and the dataset card's model-naming condition constrain downstream use.
