Long-chain-of-thought post-training has largely been optimized for tasks with inexpensive deterministic checks, such as mathematics and code. Long-form factual answers do not offer that contract: a response may contain dozens of claims, evidence has to be retrieved, and both claim decomposition and support judgments can be wrong. The paper further shows why factual precision alone is an unsafe reward. A model can improve precision by saying very little, while a reward for the number of supported claims can be inflated with correct but tangential material.

The target data problem is therefore not simply “collect factual answers.” It is to construct fact-seeking prompts, long reasoning responses, preference pairs, and online feedback that jointly expose factual precision, supported detail, and relevance. The card keeps two evidence planes separate: the released offline SFT/DPO records, and the online GRPO interactions that the paper describes but does not release. This distinction matters because the latter depends on live retrieval, claim verification, an LLM judge, and current-policy sampling rather than a static label.
