The authors state that paying about $4,000 for 100K GPT-4 judgments constrains further data scaling. Consequently, a practitioner cannot assume that larger or domain-specific judge data can be obtained at the same quality or price; compare synthetic-teacher and human-audited subsets before scaling.

The main evaluation target is GPT-4, and the authors themselves note GPT-4 may carry cultural, societal, linguistic, position, and knowledge biases. A high agreement score can therefore reproduce teacher behavior rather than establish normative correctness. Audit samples against independent humans and test both answer orders before deployment.

The appendix restricts the GPT-4-derived dataset to academic research and prohibits commercial use. Do not redistribute it or use it for commercial training without verifying the current repository terms.
