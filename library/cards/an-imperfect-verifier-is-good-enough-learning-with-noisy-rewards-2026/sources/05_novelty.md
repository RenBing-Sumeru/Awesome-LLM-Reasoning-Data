Nearby RLVR practice typically treats a programmatic verifier as ground truth or reports a single model judge without locating its error tolerance. This paper changes the comparison object: it perturbs the verifier's individual decisions at several granularities while preserving an oracle reward for online diagnosis, then compares that controlled contract with actual model verifiers. The contribution is therefore a causal robustness study of reward errors, not a new policy-optimisation algorithm or a claim that an LLM judge is universally sufficient. Its practical distinction is that it separates precision from recall and finds false positives, reflected in low precision, more damaging than missed correct answers in the tested setting.
