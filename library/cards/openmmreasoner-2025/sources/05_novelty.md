Prior multimodal reasoning work often opens only a model, one training stage, or a narrow RL dataset. OpenMMReasoner's specific contribution is to connect a teacher-distilled cold start, answer-diversity scaling, cross-domain mixing, RL prompt curation, online group sampling, mixed terminal rewards, GSPO optimization, and two released checkpoints in one inspectable stack.

Two distinctions matter for data curation. First, the SFT experiments treat answer diversity as a scaling axis while showing that the tested length and difficulty filters can remove useful variety. This is a construction claim about candidate coverage, not evidence that unfiltered traces are universally high quality. Second, the RL study compares optimization and rollout designs while keeping the released prompt pool conceptually separate from responses sampled by the changing policy.

The open release is useful because it exposes both final training objects and substantial recipe code. At the same time, the strongest audit claim must remain narrower than the paper's language of full transparency: the release omits the SFT selection ledger, rejected teacher traces, the online rollout corpus, per-response reward/judge evidence, and a reconciled mapping between the paper's eight RL training sources and the live Hub configurations.

What is not new should stay explicit. The work does not introduce SFT, teacher distillation, RLVR, GSPO, rule-based answer checking, learned judging, or embedding deduplication individually. It also does not provide process-validity labels or prove that benchmark improvements identify a generally superior dataset. The direction signal is the integrated recipe and the unusually visible boundary between open artifacts and missing decision lineage.
