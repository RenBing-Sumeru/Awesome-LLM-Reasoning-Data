- **Release composition is ambiguous.** The live RL collection totals about 74.7K only after adding a 1,724-row validation configuration to roughly 73K training rows. The paper and launch script refer to eight training files and include PuzzleVQA, but the checked collection exposes no PuzzleVQA train configuration. Exact paper-run-to-Hub mapping is unknown.
- **Rollouts are not released.** GSPO samples 16 responses online, and the supplement says rollout logs were recorded for analysis. RL-74K contains prompts and metadata rather than those responses. Response text, rollout IDs, extracted answers, rule results, learned-judge calls, group statistics, rejected responses, and update-step joins are unavailable.
- **`tokens` and `avg_reward` are under-documented.** Train schemas expose both fields, but the official dataset page does not identify the exact model revision, rollout count, decoding settings, seed, or run that produced them. Validation has `tokens` but no `avg_reward`. Their paper-run meaning is therefore unknown.
- **SFT validation is not fully operationalized.** The paper describes rigorous step-by-step validation, while its method section explains format validation and final-answer checks using rules and a learned judge. Public SFT rows contain no step labels, validator traces, confidence, teacher revision, sampling index, or rejected alternatives.
- **Verifier errors propagate.** Relaxed matching and parsers can mishandle equivalent forms; `math_verify` covers only supported expressions; the fallback judge can be sensitive to model version and evaluation instruction. The public material reports no calibration set, repeated-decision agreement, false-positive/false-negative analysis, or per-source error rate.
- **Terminal reward is not reasoning truth.** The selected reward is 90% answer correctness and 10% format compliance. A correct final answer can follow flawed or visually ungrounded reasoning, and a valid path can be penalized by answer extraction or formatting. No process-validity supervision resolves this gap.
- **Selection changes the observed distribution.** SFT keeps candidates passing verification and later chooses not to apply tested length/difficulty filters; RL verifies answers and removes similar questions. Without accepted/rejected counts and row IDs, source-specific survival rates, answer diversity, deduplication asymmetry, and failure coverage cannot be audited.
- **Contamination and rights remain partial.** No released exact, near-duplicate, or semantic train-evaluation overlap ledger covers the combined SFT and RL sources. Code is Apache-2.0 and RL-74K is CC-BY-NC-4.0, but SFT-874K has no top-level license and record-level upstream text/image/teacher-output rights are unknown.
- **Reproduction is mutable.** The repository had no formal release when checked. Launch files contain local paths and service endpoints, while model, data, code, and judge repositories can update independently. Hardware hours, random seeds, and checksums are unknown.
- **External validity is narrow.** The experiments use one Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct family and primarily image tasks. Video, audio, richer temporal inputs, other architecture families, and the upper limit of further SFT/RL scaling are not established.

Benchmark gains are downstream model evidence. They do not certify rationale faithfulness, source cleanliness, reward calibration, license safety, or complete release quality.
