The one-sentence contribution is: U-MATH pairs unpublished university-course math problems with an explicit LLM-judge evaluation protocol, then uses mu-MATH to measure how reliable that judging protocol is.

The core mechanism has two layers. First, the benchmark raises the problem distribution above school-level arithmetic and algebra by using university curriculum topics, open-ended answers, and a visual subset. Second, because open-ended math answers are hard to score automatically, the paper treats judging itself as an object of evaluation: mu-MATH samples U-MATH problems, attaches multiple model-generated solutions, and provides ground-truth correctness labels so judge models can be evaluated for F1, bias, conservatism, leniency, reasoning quality, and coherence.

The feedback contract is answer-level correctness under a disclosed judging prompt. U-MATH is best categorized as a benchmark/evaluation surface with a mixed verification contract: reference answers constrain correctness, but free-form scoring depends on LLM judges whose reliability must be audited. The closest comparisons are MATH and GSM8K for textual math, MathVista and MATH-V for visual math, and judge/meta-evaluation work that tests whether model graders are dependable rather than assuming their labels are ground truth.
