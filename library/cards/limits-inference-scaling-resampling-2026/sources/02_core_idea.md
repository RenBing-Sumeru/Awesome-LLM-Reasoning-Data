The central quantity is conditional correctness after the weak verifier accepts a candidate. Unlimited resampling lets the weak model approach the probability that an accepted sample is truly correct; it does not erase verifier false positives. Consequently, if a strong model's single-sample correctness exceeds the weak model's correctness conditional on passing the verifier, the weak model cannot catch up through resampling alone, even with an infinite budget.

Finite-budget behavior also depends on utility. The analysis gives a true positive benefit of 1, a rejected-all-candidates outcome value of 0, and negative values to false positives. A larger K raises the chance of finding a true positive, but also raises exposure to falsely accepted programs. Because easy tasks tend to be solved earlier, repeated rejections concentrate the remaining population on harder tasks, where false positives are relatively more likely. The resulting optimum may be small, and can be K=0 when accepting an incorrect answer is sufficiently costly.
