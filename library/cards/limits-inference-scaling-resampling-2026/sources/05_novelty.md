The contribution is not another pass@k curve. It separates the acceptance verifier from a stronger audit oracle and asks whether selection actually closes a generalization gap. That distinction exposes a ceiling determined by conditional correctness, whereas evaluation with the same tests used for selection would report only that a candidate passed the gate.

The paper also joins three pieces that are often treated separately: an infinite-budget limit, a finite-budget utility analysis, and a task-mixture explanation for rising false-positive risk after repeated rejection. This turns “more test-time compute” from a monotone assumption into an auditable stopping problem. Relative to broad reward-overoptimization arguments, the unit-test setup gives a programmatic, record-level contract that can be reconstructed from candidate order and paired weak/strong test outcomes.
