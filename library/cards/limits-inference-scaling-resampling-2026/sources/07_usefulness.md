For the data-construction and open-release-recipes track, this paper supplies a concrete audit schema for verifier-filtered reasoning data. A useful release should preserve every candidate's task, generator and version, attempt order, weak-verifier result, stronger-audit result, acceptance budget K, stopping rule, and any false-positive cost used for selection. Publishing only accepted answers destroys the evidence needed to estimate conditional correctness or detect selection-induced shifts toward harder tasks.

The study can guide pre-release stress tests: stratify verifier precision by task and generator, plot true- and false-positive rates against K, compare against a stronger held-out verifier, and report utility sensitivity rather than a single success metric. Its results warn against treating benchmark gains under the same acceptance tests as evidence of data quality. They also motivate retaining rejected and near-miss samples, since those records reveal how the selected distribution changes over the course of resampling.

This card should not be used as evidence that all rejection sampling fails or that K=5 is universal. The numerical optimum depends on the generator, verifier, task mixture, and cost model. The transferable contribution is the audit procedure and the requirement to document the feedback contract.
