The reported scaling curves show an inverse relationship between a model's single-sample accuracy and the correctness of its samples conditional on passing the limited tests. In both HumanEval+ and MBPP+, models below the paper's cutoff cannot match GPT-4o's single-sample accuracy through unlimited weak-verifier resampling. This is evidence about the verifier-generator pair, not proof that any released sample set is intrinsically high quality.

The finite-budget experiments make the practical risk concrete. At a false-positive cost-benefit ratio of 4, Figure 4 reports an optimal K no larger than 5 for all four displayed models; at larger costs, the optimum can be 0. False-positive rates rise with K because later unresolved tasks are systematically harder. On task subsets whose verifier precision is below 90%, the paper counts 70 of 150 HumanEval+ tasks and 128 of 321 MBPP+ tasks, and reports an even stronger relationship.

The code-quality branch reports that false positives perform worse across all tested compliance measures; this supports a link between functional false positives and the measured style failures, but not broad claims about simplicity or maintainability. A manual inspection of ten false positives—five per benchmark—found logical errors, missed edge cases, and inefficient implementations. The paper also excludes suspected oracle-mismatch tasks rather than silently treating the extended tests as infallible: 14 from HumanEval+ and 57 from the post-EvalPlus MBPP+ pool.
