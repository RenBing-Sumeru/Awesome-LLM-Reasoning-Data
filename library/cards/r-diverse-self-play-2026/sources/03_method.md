Both Challenger and Solver start from Qwen3-4B-Base or Qwen3-8B-Base and are optimized with GRPO in EasyR1. The disclosed Challenger prompt requests one brand-new competition-math problem, aimed below a 30% solve rate for advanced high-school students, plus a boxed final answer; no seed corpus is disclosed. The reported run has five evolution iterations, each with 5 Challenger and 15 Solver optimization steps. Global batch size is 128, learning rate is 1e-6, and weight decay and KL coefficient are 1e-2. (Paper §4.1; Appendix A.1–A.2.)

For each question the Solver produces five rollouts. Extracted final answers are partitioned into equivalence groups; consistency is the largest group's fraction, and uncertainty is `min(s, 1-s)`. The majority answer becomes the pseudo-label, and a Solver rollout receives binary reward for matching it. The paper illustrates a valid uncertainty interval of 0.3 to 0.8 for memory insertion. It discloses a GPT-4o Yes/No answer-check prompt, but not its exact placement, sampling coverage, fallback behavior, or interaction with equivalence grouping.

SAM asks Qwen2.5-Coder-7B at temperature 0 to convert a question into a comment-free `solver` function, move numerical values into default arguments, and anonymize semantic variables. Its prompt explicitly says to infer the intended valid interpretation when the question is ambiguous, flawed, or poorly phrased. Jina-Code-Embeddings-1.5B encodes the result. The code is used as a similarity representation; the paper does not report executing it as a verifier.

MAP combines nearest-memory and mean-memory cosine similarity, with mixing coefficient 0.5, max and mean activation thresholds 0.5 and 0.25, and penalty weights 1.0. Accepted memory tuples retain the question, embedding, and pseudo-label; historical pairs provide 30% of Solver training examples. Challenger training uses four rollouts and Solver training five, both at temperature 1.0 and top-p 0.99.

Experiments use eight NVIDIA H20 GPUs with BF16 and FlashAttention 2. The paper reports about six hours for one Qwen3-4B evolution iteration after time-multiplexing optimization, versus 7.5 hours for its R-Zero comparison. Total run cost, token counts, 8B timing, random seeds, exact answer-equivalence code, memory capacity and eviction, correction policy, and record counts are not disclosed. The official repository does not supply the implementation or records needed to rerun the recipe.
