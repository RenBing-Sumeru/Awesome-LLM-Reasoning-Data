Relative to R-Zero, the concrete change is to make diversity control both history-aware and procedure-aware. MAP uses persistent memory as a repulsive regularizer across iterations and reuses the same history for Solver replay. SAM maps natural-language questions through canonical solver code before computing similarity. Together they distinguish lexical novelty from novelty in the procedures exercised by the Solver. (Paper §3.2–§3.3.)

The Challenger-Solver loop, GRPO, uncertainty curriculum, majority pseudo-labeling, experience replay, code generation, embeddings, and LLM judges are not new individually. The paper's contribution is the diagnosis of Local and Surface Diversity Illusions and the placement of MAP and SAM inside the data-production loop. Its ablation and diversity measurements are aligned with that diagnosis.

The boundary matters: SAM is a data-selection and reward proxy, not program execution or a correctness verifier. MAP preserves history and discourages revisiting it, but does not make stored labels reliable. The novelty is therefore a construction control for an evolving curriculum, not a new source of ground-truth supervision or a completed open-data release.
