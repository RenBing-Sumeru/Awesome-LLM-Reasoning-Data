R-Diverse adds two controls to the R-Zero-style loop. Memory-Augmented Penalty (MAP) keeps a persistent bank of prior valid questions and penalizes a new question's maximum similarity to any history item and its mean similarity to the bank. The same memory supplies historical question-answer pairs for Solver replay. Skill-Aware Measurement (SAM) changes the comparison space: Qwen2.5-Coder-7B maps each question to a canonical Python `solver` function, and Jina-Code-Embeddings-1.5B embeds that code for cosine comparison. (Paper §3.2–§3.3.)

The feedback contract remains partly self-referential. For each question, five Solver answers are grouped by equivalence. If the largest group's share is `s`, Challenger uncertainty is `min(s, 1-s)`, which is largest near a split vote; the largest group also supplies the pseudo-label. Challenger reward subtracts within-iteration and memory-aware similarity penalties from uncertainty. A Solver response receives binary reward for matching the pseudo-label. MAP and SAM therefore reshape curriculum difficulty and novelty, but they do not add an independent truth verifier.

There are three distinct feedback roles that should not be conflated: uncertainty targets questions near current Solver competence, SAM/MAP discourage procedural recycling, and the majority pseudo-label supervises Solver answers. GPT-4o appears in answer rechecking and paper-side diversity/curriculum analyses; the paper does not specify enough training-time coverage to reinterpret the whole loop as externally verified.
