Primary source: Wang et al., "Math-Shepherd," ACL 2024 long paper. The paper asks how to build process reward supervision for mathematical reasoning without paying for human step-by-step annotations. The decision boundary is automatic process-supervision and PRM use, not a new math problem benchmark and not formal proof checking. The data object is a math solution prefix or step with an automatically induced reward signal derived from whether sampled continuations can reach the correct final answer. Its atlas value is the feedback-contract design: step labels are backed by final-answer verification, not human proof judgment.
