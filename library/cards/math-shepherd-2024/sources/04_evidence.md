The ACL paper's evidence is comparative: automatically induced process supervision improves mathematical reasoning when used to verify or reinforce generated solutions, and it is compared against outcome-supervision style baselines. Instance-level evidence is not a human proof label; it is a sampled continuation set, final answers for those continuations, answer-checker verdicts, and the aggregated reward assigned to a prefix. The boundary is therefore probabilistic and budget-bound: labels change with generator quality, rollout count, decoding parameters, and answer-checking reliability.
