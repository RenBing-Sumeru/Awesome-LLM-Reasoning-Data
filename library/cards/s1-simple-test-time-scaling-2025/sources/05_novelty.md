The prior baseline is scale-first reasoning distillation: collect many answer-keyed questions, generate teacher traces, and train on most successful outputs. s1 changes the emphasis to a compact selected set. It combines three proxies—quality, difficulty relative to Qwen2.5-7B/32B, and Claude-labeled domain diversity—and validates them against random, diversity-only, longest-trace, and full-pool training.

The concrete data contribution is not just “1,000 examples.” It is a staged selection interface: preserve successful/format-valid Gemini traces, remove questions solved by either downstream-family probe, fix a high-quality seed, then sample across domains while favoring longer traces. Because only 53.6% of final generations are later judged correct, the object is best understood as selected reasoning imitation rather than verified solution supervision.

Budget forcing is a separate methodological contribution. It uses the model's learned end-of-thinking delimiter as a decoding control: emit the delimiter to stop at an upper budget, or suppress it and append `Wait` to extend reasoning. It does not add training data, label steps, optimize a reward, or revise s1K. Its novelty is controllable sequential test-time compute with minimal machinery, not a new data-construction signal.

Several components are established techniques: teacher distillation, SFT, model-based difficulty, LLM judging, domain-balanced sampling, and stop-token manipulation. The paper's value lies in their compact integration, transparent ablations, and open artifact surface. The current artifact surface, however, no longer equals a single paper snapshot.

For reasoning-data research, the important distinction is among four releases: the 59,029-question raw candidate pool, the 58,986-row generated full pool, the original 1,000-row Gemini s1K, and later 1,000-question DeepSeek-R1 s1K-1.1. Treating them as one “s1 dataset” would erase the teacher, filtering, count, model, and license boundaries.
