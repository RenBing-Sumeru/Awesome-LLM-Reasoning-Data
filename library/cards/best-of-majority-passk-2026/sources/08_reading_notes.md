Read the paper as a budget-allocation and selection result, not as evidence that sampling alone is a universal scaling law. The useful audit table has four columns: N generated responses, k submitted responses, the frequency threshold, and the reward-model error or calibration. The key empirical question is whether a gain remains after those quantities and all scorer calls are counted. The theory is especially valuable because it supplies counterexamples to the attractive but unsafe assumption that simply increasing best-of-N must help.
