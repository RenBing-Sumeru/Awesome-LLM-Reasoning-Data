The strongest evidence is conditional on the released training recipe and the three evaluated Qwen3 scales. Paper Table 4 reports that SFT improves average scores by +8.67 on BFCL-v3 multi-turn, +4.29 on Tau-Bench, and +11.57 on ACEBench-Agent. For Qwen3-8B, subsequent RL adds +4.88 on BFCL and +3.46 on Tau-Bench. These are author-reported benchmark deltas under the paper’s configurations; the Card did not independently rerun training or evaluation.

The negative result matters: RL does not improve every model and benchmark combination. Qwen3-1.7B slightly declines on Tau-Bench after RL, which the authors attribute to a smaller model’s sensitivity to noisy reward. This bounds any claim that more generated environments or programmatic outcome checks automatically produce monotonic gains.

The construction evidence includes both scale and rejection. Of 266 synthesized environment candidates, 191 survive the 100-round not-fail selection and 75 are rejected, a 28.2% rejection rate. The paper identifies type/attribute errors and state-management inconsistency as prominent failure classes. Table 2 reports 4.58 constraints, 21.38 state categories, and 18.58 tools per retained environment on average. However, because warning counts with pass in the at-least-0.85 selector, the retained count is not evidence that 85% of strict checks passed.

The release evidence was checked at the object level. The pinned official datasets expose 191 environment rows, 4,684 SFT scenarios, 2,550 RL scenarios, and a claimed 9,022 SFT trajectories. The SFT scenarios omit check functions; RL scenarios include executable checklist functions. The trajectory revision’s incompatible raw and masked schemas break the default Hugging Face loader/viewer, but the individual source files remain downloadable. There is no RL rollout dataset in the official collection.

The paper also asks Claude-4.5-Sonnet to score tool-program alignment, functional correctness, and robustness and reports averages above 8.5 with agreement to manual judgment. This is an author-run LLM assessment, not an independent semantic verifier audit. It does not establish check-function coverage, absence of false rewards, resistance to reward hacking, safe code execution, or replay equivalence across versions.

Taken together, the evidence supports the narrower claim that programmatically synthesized environments can supply useful SFT and outcome-reward training surfaces under the stated setup. It does not prove that every released SFT trajectory is terminally correct, that the generated verifier is unbiased, that RL rollouts are publicly reproducible, or that benchmark gains establish data quality or legal/safe reuse.
