Frontier models that approach perfect scores on conventional multi-turn benchmarks all score below 50% on MultiChallenge; the strongest reported system, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, reaches only 41.4%. This indicates that the benchmark exposes capability gaps hidden by older evaluations. Experienced human annotators independently apply the instance-level rubrics, and the paper reports high agreement between automatic judges and human decisions, supporting scalable scoring. The evidence is persuasive, but low scores jointly reflect model capability, rubric strictness, and judge behavior. The benchmark is therefore more suitable for relative comparison and error analysis than for treating one score as a complete measure of conversational ability.
