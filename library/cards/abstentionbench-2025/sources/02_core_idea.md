AbstentionBench treats abstention as a calibrated decision under insufficient evidence, not a fixed refusal phrase or a safety-policy refusal. It combines 17 curated datasets with constructed underspecified versions of GSM8K, GPQA, and MMLU-Math, yielding 20 datasets and more than 35K questions across six cases: unknown answers, false premises, stale facts, subjective questions, missing context, and unclear intent. Every item states whether abstention is appropriate; most answerable items also retain a reference answer. A validated Llama 3.1 8B judge identifies whether a response genuinely abstains, while a separate judge scores correctness when answering was appropriate. Recall measures missed abstentions, precision exposes blanket refusal, and F1 balances both. This separation matters because a model can be accurate on answerable tasks yet confidently invent missing facts. The benchmark’s central finding is that scale alone does little for abstention, and reasoning fine-tuning lowers it by 24% on average even as reasoning-task accuracy rises, showing that longer reasoning does not necessarily mean better uncertainty handling.
