Compared with direct distillation of one long teacher trace, Marco-o1 v2 constructs multiple paths from scratch under a typed, configurable reasoning graph and uses rule outcomes to backtrack. Compared with MCTS recipes that only keep a best trajectory or train a process reward model, it explicitly reuses path length and branch overlap to form different SFT targets and fine-grained DPO pairs. The strongest direction signal is this linkage from search structure to the training record: node role, terminal rule result, relative length, common-prefix boundary, and chosen/rejected status have distinct meanings. MCTS, reflection prompting, DPO, cDPO, and joint SFT-preference objectives are prior components; the paper's contribution is their CoT-aware assembly and analysis. It does not establish that predefined node roles mirror human reasoning, or that shorter correct paths are universally better supervision.
