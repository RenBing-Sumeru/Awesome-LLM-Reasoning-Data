Directly distilling very long chain-of-thought from large reasoning models can exceed the learning capacity of smaller models and can transfer repetitive reflection, stalled reasoning, or failure to emit a final answer. Marco-o1 v2, published at ACL 2025, reframes this as both a data-construction and post-training problem: build tree-structured traces with ordinary instruction models, then choose path lengths and preference pairs that do not reward formalistic long-time thinking. The atlas data objects are MCTS trees with typed thought nodes, rule-checked terminal answers, extracted correct paths of different relative lengths, and positive/negative pairs for SFT and DPO. A card-worthy claim therefore depends on the tree and pair lineage, not only on downstream benchmark scores or the availability of a model checkpoint.
