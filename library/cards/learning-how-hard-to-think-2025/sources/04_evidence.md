Across code, math, and chat, the learned allocation improves the stated efficiency frontier. On math, both online and offline adaptive best-of-k consistently beat uniform best-of-k across reported budgets. On code, the offline binned version consistently wins, while direct online allocation can fail at high budgets because tiny prediction errors make zero-success problems look worth searching. The paper reports that math and code can match non-adaptive quality with up to 50% less compute.

Routing provides a second test of the same decision rule. For model routing and value-augmented sampling, the system matches the expensive decoder while calling it only 50–75% as often; value augmentation reports a 25–40% reduction in expensive calls at comparable reward. Predictor analyses show 72–84% above/below-median marginal-reward classification accuracy, but the error analysis makes clear that calibration, not just ranking, matters when allocation becomes aggressive.
