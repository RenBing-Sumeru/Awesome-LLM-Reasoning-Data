Most multimodal benchmarks use multiple-choice questions, short answers, or a single correct label, mainly testing recognition, knowledge question answering, and deterministic reasoning. They do not adequately measure whether a model can generate diverse yet plausible associations from images and concepts. Human associative intelligence combines divergent generation of candidate relations with convergent explanation of hidden connections, while open-ended outputs lack unique answers and are difficult to score reliably. The paper therefore studies how to construct psychometrically motivated open-ended multimodal association data and how to design an LLM-as-a-judge that evaluates final associations, reasoning processes, plausibility, and diversity.
