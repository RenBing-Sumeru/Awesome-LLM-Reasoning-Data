The benchmark scale and label protocol are the first evidence layer: 3,400 test cases, four math subsets, human expert labels, and a three-agreement rule with up to five annotators. The paper reports an overall discard rate of about 30% when expert agreement cannot be reached, which is a quality-control signal but also a selection boundary.

The construction evidence shows why final-answer reward is insufficient. Among sampled solutions with correct final answers, the reported process-error rates rise with problem difficulty: 3.5% for GSM8K, 18.8% for MATH, 32.2% for OlympiadBench, and 51.8% for Omni-MATH. This is row-level evidence only relative to their expert process labels and sampling protocol; it does not prove a universal rate for all math models or all prompts.

Evaluation evidence: existing PRMs degrade on harder subsets, while prompted critic models and the authors' PRM800K-finetuned PRM do better under the paper's settings. The reported average F1 is 56.5 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B-PRM800K, 61.2 for Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct as a critic, 71.5 for QwQ-32B-Preview, 61.9 for GPT-4o-0806, and 87.9 for o1-mini. These numbers depend on the official prompt, thresholding, majority voting, model versions, and exact split composition.
