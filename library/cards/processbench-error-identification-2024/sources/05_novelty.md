The prior baseline is not "no process supervision"; PRM800K already provides human process annotations, MathCheck provides step-error checks over GSM8K-style synthetic cases, and CriticBench evaluates critique behavior. ProcessBench is new in its target and evaluation surface: a larger expert-labeled earliest-error-identification benchmark over harder math problems and diverse natural model solutions.

What changes is the feedback granularity. The label is neither final-answer correctness nor general helpfulness; it is the earliest step where the reasoning path first becomes invalid, with `-1` reserved for fully correct traces. This avoids judging downstream steps after a first error, where local derivations may be algebraically valid but globally attached to a wrong premise.

The direction signal is strong for scalable oversight: it exposes cases where a correct final answer hides faulty reasoning and lets PRMs and critic models be compared on the same step-localization contract. What is not new: the math source datasets, LLM-generated solution traces, PRM scoring, and prompt-based critique are existing ingredients. Before reuse, inspect license, split/revision, data overlap with PRM800K and public math benchmarks, annotation ambiguity, discarded-sample bias, and whether the dataset has already entered target model pretraining.
