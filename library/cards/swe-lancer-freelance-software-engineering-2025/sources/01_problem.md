Existing software-engineering benchmarks mostly extract short-term fixes from open-source issues and omit real client constraints, commercial value, and project-management decisions. Their resolution rates therefore have no direct economic interpretation. Real freelance projects also include multi-week feature development, ambiguous requirements, existing codebases, and proposal selection. Unit tests or synthetic exercises alone cannot show whether a model can complete work for which customers actually paid.

SWE-Lancer collects more than 1,400 completed Upwork tasks with roughly USD 1 million in real payouts, covering both independent engineering work and managerial selection among technical proposals. For the public SWE-Lancer Diamond split, the paper provides a unified Docker environment and engineer-reviewed evaluation criteria, allowing model performance to be mapped to completed tasks and their associated economic value.
