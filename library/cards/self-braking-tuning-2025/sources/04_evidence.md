The paper reports accuracy and average output tokens for four model families across four mathematical benchmark groups. SBT variants often reduce tokens substantially, but the accuracy tradeoff varies: some settings retain or improve accuracy, while others lose several points. Ablations compare step-level versus token-level detection, natural-language cues versus a special token or no guidance, and score components. The reported step-level and natural-language variants offer the best tested tradeoffs under their configurations.

These results evaluate models trained with the recipe. They do not verify that each braking boundary is semantically correct, that every masked segment is redundant, or that the two derived datasets are uncontaminated and properly licensed. Token reductions are not record-level data-quality evidence.
