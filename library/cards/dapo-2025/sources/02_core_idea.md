DAPO's central contribution is to co-design which online RL records enter an update and how their tokens are weighted. Starting from GRPO's group-normalized outcome advantage, it combines four changes: Clip-Higher decouples the lower and upper importance-ratio bounds; Dynamic Sampling keeps sampling until the update batch contains groups with mixed correctness; Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss aggregates across valid tokens rather than first giving each response equal weight; and Soft Overlong Punishment adds a linear negative adjustment over the final 4,096 tokens before the 20,480-token cap (Paper §3, Equations 8-13).

One intended released item is a processed prompt plus an integer ground truth. The current Hub schema materializes `data_source`, one-message `prompt`, `ability`, `reward_model.ground_truth`, `reward_model.style`, and `extra_info.index`. During training, that item expands into a group of 16 rollouts. The outcome rule sees the extracted final answer; the length shaper sees response length; Dynamic Sampling sees only the group's reward variation. None of these signals checks whether individual reasoning steps are valid.

Relative to naive GRPO, the data-side change is explicit: all-correct and all-wrong groups are excluded from the update buffer even though they were generated. Relative to DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, DAPO publishes a fuller algorithm and release surface, but its evidence is limited to the reported AIME 2024 setting. The direction signal is therefore an auditable coupling of data flow, reward, and optimization, not a claim that the public rows are clean or that benchmark accuracy proves data quality.
