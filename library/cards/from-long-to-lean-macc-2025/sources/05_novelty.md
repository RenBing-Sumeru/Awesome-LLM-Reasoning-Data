MACC makes the **stopping point of rationale compression** a per-example construction decision. A single-pass compressor yields one shortened rationale; a fixed-round method applies the same number of rewrites to every item. MACC instead follows one dependent revision chain and uses the first token-length rebound as a local indication that further rewriting is no longer shortening the trace. The selected full rationale then becomes answer-level/full-episode SFT supervision.

The recipe separates two levels of selection. At the record level, Algorithm 1 uses only token counts to choose \(r^*\). At the configuration level, the Performance Estimation Hypothesis uses compression rate, PPL, original-length, and target/compressor accuracy features to predict downstream accuracy and output length, helping choose a compressor and round setting. These levels must not be conflated: the regression model is not a semantic verifier for individual revisions.

Answer-conditioned sequential rewriting is also distinct from ordinary self-compression. The paper formally starts from a target-model-generated \(r_0\), but an external API model authors each revision and sees the final answer. This can create concise explanations aligned with a known label without demonstrating that the shortened rationale preserves the causal path of the original. The work explicitly distinguishes itself from knowledge distillation, yet the external compressor still materially shapes the training text.

For reasoning-data research, the novelty is an explicit long2short lineage \(x\rightarrow r_0\rightarrow r_1\ldots\rightarrow r^*\) combined with a cheap programmatic selector and downstream SFT. It makes round budget, tokenization, API model, answer exposure, and selection rule first-class data variables. The Table 6/Algorithm 1 mismatch further shows why a published conceptual selector needs record-level stop logs and token counts.

The novelty does not include step labels, a learned reward model, semantic equivalence checking, multiple candidates per round, an open generated corpus, or a demonstrated non-SFT objective. Reported accuracy and efficiency gains do not establish that the chosen revision is the most faithful trace or that token-length rebound is generally optimal.
