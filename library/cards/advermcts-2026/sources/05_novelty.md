The paper's main novelty is the **closed-loop coupling** of candidate generation and verifier-data construction at test time. Solver MCTS changes the candidate population; Attacker MCTS searches for inputs that expose disagreement in that population; an LLM Arbiter converts selected inputs into labeled tests; and those tests alter future Solver rewards, pool admission, and final ranking.

The individual ingredients are not new in isolation: Monte Carlo tree search, public-test execution, test generation, LLM judging, and candidate reranking all predate this work. The claimed contribution is their persistent adversarial co-evolution, including a global filtering memory that carries accepted counterexamples through the remainder of one problem. The paper's comparisons and ablations support the utility of that integration under its evaluation settings.

From a data-curation perspective, the distinctive object is an online stream of test proposals, divergent execution tuples, judge decisions, retained counterexamples, and downstream penalties. That stream could support verifier training or audit if released with lineage. It is currently transient: the repository does not publish it as a stable dataset, and the paper trains no new model on it. The contribution should therefore be described as a data-construction **recipe and inference-time environment**, not as an open dataset or a demonstrated post-training corpus.
