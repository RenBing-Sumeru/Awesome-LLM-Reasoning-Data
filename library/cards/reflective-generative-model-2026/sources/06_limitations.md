Final-answer correctness is a noisy proxy for intermediate reasoning: a flawed path can end correctly and a sound path can make a formatting error. Sharing a backbone may reduce memory but can couple policy and verifier biases, and the dynamic pseudo-label filter can reject informative difficult cases. The cost of producing multiple long trajectories remains substantial even with a small scoring head. Reuse should compare against a separate verifier at matched total memory, tokens, and latency, and inspect whether step scores transfer beyond the mathematical-style data used in training.
