Across three reasoning language models and four datasets, the paper reports that thought calibration can preserve model performance while reducing thinking tokens by up to 60% on in-distribution data. On distribution-shift evaluation with AIME24, GPQA Diamond, and MATH500, it reports token reductions of up to 20%. The comparison is important because the baseline is not simply a shorter answer: naïve token limits are described as producing marked accuracy loss, whereas calibrated stopping aims to selectively remove low-value continuation.

The evidence also bounds the claim. The out-of-distribution reduction is smaller than the in-distribution result, and the guarantee concerns the calibrated decision setup rather than universal accuracy preservation for any model or dataset. The paper states that in its worst reported case the policy is no less efficient than naïve budget constraints. These results support an adaptive early-stop policy, but its practical value depends on accessible hidden states, valid calibration data, probe overhead, and whether the deployment distribution resembles the calibration conditions.
