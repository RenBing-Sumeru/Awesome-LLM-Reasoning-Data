Thought calibration interprets an extended chain of thought as a reasoning tree whose growth represents novel computation. If later tokens only walk within or repeatedly revise an established structure, further continuation may have little value. Lightweight probes over hidden states estimate reasoning structure and response consistency without requiring a separate external language-model judge. The system therefore bases a serving decision on information present during generation, not on a post-hoc inspection of a completed trace.

The method then calibrates the stopping rule rather than trusting raw probe confidence. Using Learn-then-Test, it frames possible thresholds as multiple hypothesis tests and selects a rule that controls the risk associated with early termination under its assumptions. This connects the probe, a user-chosen risk level, and the final token allocation. The contribution is consequently an online compute policy: spend another thought segment only while the calibrated evidence indicates meaningful reasoning growth.
