Earlier approaches often use a fixed length, train a model to be shorter, or score a completed trace with an external judge. This paper changes the operational question to an online risk-controlled stopping decision. Its reasoning-tree view turns “more thinking” into a structural process: extra tokens are useful only if they grow or substantially revise the current tree. Hidden-state probes supply a lightweight signal before the model has finished generating.

The distinctive contribution is calibration of the action to stop, not merely calibration of a score. By applying Learn-then-Test to thresholds, the method expresses a token-saving decision in terms of an explicit risk budget. It does not prove that hidden reasoning trees faithfully represent semantic reasoning, nor eliminate distribution shift. Its reusable insight is that early stopping should be compared as a budget policy with quantified failure risk, rather than as a generic length-reduction heuristic.
