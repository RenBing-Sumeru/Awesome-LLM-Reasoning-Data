The main evaluation uses Llama 3.1 70B on a shuffled sample of 1,000 MMLU-Pro questions, with BBH and Llama 3.1 8B used as robustness checks. All methods receive zero-shot reasoning prompts at temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.95. Self-consistency varies sequences, self-refinement varies revision iterations, debate varies agents and rounds, and mixture-of-agents varies proposer models and aggregation layers. The final multiple-choice answer is selected by the highest log-likelihood after a fixed answer prompt.

Across 34 configurations and more than 100 evaluations, the authors estimate runtime from both operations and model-weight memory transfer, then compute the Pareto front. They examine budgets from a single chain of thought up to roughly twenty times that compute. A difficulty analysis groups MMLU-Pro items by a 20-run chain-of-thought solve rate, allowing the study to test whether expensive scaling is most useful on tasks that the base pipeline rarely solves.
