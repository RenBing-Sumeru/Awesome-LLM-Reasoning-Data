The paper frames meta-judging as a response to weaknesses in LLM-as-a-Judge. Its six organizing perspectives are conceptual foundations, mechanisms of meta-judging, alignment training methods, evaluation, limitations and failure modes, and future directions. Rather than saying a meta-judge is intrinsically correct, it asks how the evaluator should be tested, trained, and constrained.

The relevant feedback contract is judgment-required: an LLM assessment is a model-mediated signal, not a programmatic correctness proof. In an atlas, the key object is therefore not simply an answer score but the full judge record: candidate, task context, rubric or prompt, judge identity/version, sampling policy, output format, and reference or human comparison. Closest comparisons are LLM-as-a-Judge surveys, reward-model surveys, and judge-reliability papers; this Card emphasizes the extra layer that evaluates the judge.
