Automated LLM judges increasingly turn a candidate response and an evaluation prompt into a score, ranking, critique, or rationale. That feedback can be convenient for evaluation, filtering, and training, but the judge is itself a fallible model. The primary source is the 2026 arXiv preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17312, which asks how the emerging LLM-as-a-Meta-Judge paradigm can assess or improve the trustworthiness of automated evaluation.

The decision boundary is evaluation methodology, not a release of a new judge dataset. One evaluation instance contains a candidate output, a prompting/rubric context, and an automated assessment; the assessment's stability and bias are central properties. The authors name prompt sensitivity, systematic bias, verbosity effects, and unreliable or hallucinated rationales as vulnerabilities of ordinary LLM-as-a-Judge. This official source and local bilingual Card support its inclusion as an evaluation primer.
