MiniMax-M1 asks how a large reasoning model can extend output-time computation to tens of thousands of tokens while training on a mixture of easy-to-check reasoning tasks, executable software work, and open-ended general prompts. The report combines an efficient hybrid-attention architecture with continual pretraining, a Long-CoT supervised warm start, CISPO reinforcement learning, and a curriculum that grows the output limit from 40K to 80K.

For a frontier disclosure ledger, four claims must remain separate. MiniMax releases model weights; MiniMax publishes model-definition and deployment code; the report describes aggregate data and reward recipes; and benchmark tables measure resulting model behavior. Only the first three are evidence about openness, and none by itself establishes that the underlying training records, reward traces, or environments can be inspected and replayed.

The central audit problem is therefore lineage across stages. The report gives concrete domain counts and feedback contracts for much of RL, but much less for the 7.5T-token continual-pretraining corpus and Long-CoT SFT mixture. It does not release item-level prompt origins, SFT responses, policy rollout groups, reward outputs, software sandboxes, or a run manifest connecting records to MiniMax-M1-40k and MiniMax-M1-80k.
