The official arXiv report is concrete about aggregate stages. It gives the 7.5T continual-pretraining budget and 70 percent priority mixture, the SFT domain mix and 60 percent math/code share, RL domain counts, pass-rate filters, software execution contract, GenRM scoring schemes, CISPO design, long-output curriculum, selected stability fixes, and the three-week 512-H800 RL run. This supports a report-level recipe, not record-level replay.

The release artifacts are real but narrower than the recipe. The official Hugging Face collection contains MiniMax-M1-40k, MiniMax-M1-80k, and corresponding Transformers-formatted weights. The official GitHub repository contains the report, model configuration and implementation, inference entry point, tokenizer assets, and deployment/function-calling guides under Apache-2.0. No official M1 training dataset, policy rollouts, reward traces, GenRM checkpoint, sandbox bundle, or end-to-end training code was confirmed.

Benchmark tables support model-behavior claims under stated settings. For example, the report lists 55.6 and 56.0 on its 486-task SWE-bench Verified setup for the 40K and 80K models, respectively. The subset excludes 14 tasks incompatible with MiniMax infrastructure, so direct comparisons require that caveat. Multi-sample math/code scores, TAU-bench with a user simulator, and model-judged MultiChallenge likewise depend on evaluation protocol.

Ablations and training curves provide evidence about CISPO and longer-output scaling in the studied runs. They do not prove that every training trace is correct, every generated test captures the issue specification, the reward model is calibrated outside its benchmark, the data rights are complete, or contamination is absent. Compute cost and benchmark performance are model/process evidence, not data-quality evidence.
