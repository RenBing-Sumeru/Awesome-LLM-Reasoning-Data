Table 2 evaluates GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, o1, Claude Sonnet 3.5 v2, Claude Sonnet 3.7 Extended Thinking, and Llama-3.3-70B through VisualWebArena, Claude CURI, or browser tool-calling scaffolds. Across the reported model/scaffold/defense settings, `ASR-intermediate` ranges from 0.167 to 0.857, `ASR-end-to-end` from 0 to 0.167, and utility from 0.027 to 0.622 (Paper Table 2, §4.1–§4.2). These are author-reported rates over the fixed suites, not independently reproduced estimates.

The large intermediate-versus-end-to-end gap is the central empirical result. Some agents begin executing the injected objective frequently, yet complete it rarely. The paper attributes part of that gap to agent capability: a hijacked agent may still be unable to carry out the malicious workflow. Consequently, low end-to-end ASR is not evidence of refusal or robust security; it can be “security by incompetence.” Utility must be read alongside both ASRs because a scaffold that cannot complete benign tasks also has fewer opportunities to finish attacks.

Tables 3–4 report that URL-anchor injections generally produce higher intermediate ASR than plain text, while task-agnostic prompts reduce ASR but do not drive it to zero. Defensive system-level instructions reduce some GPT results, yet the tested instruction-hierarchy placement does not eliminate hijacking (Paper §4.2, Tables 3–4). These ablations support sensitivity to prompt format and message privilege, but they do not establish robustness to adaptive attacks, broader websites, or unseen attacker goals.

The released implementation provides direct evidence about measurement behavior. GPT-4o judgment is performed per action and aggregated by any-positive, whereas the paper's prose can be read as judging concatenated reasoning/actions. Parser failures default to `not_compromised`, and no human-labeled calibration set or agreement statistic is reported. For exfiltration, the pinned evaluator returns success from expected URL text in agent actions rather than independently checking attacker-server receipt. These code-level facts narrow what the reported ASRs mean.

The paper reports no error bars or statistical-significance analysis (NeurIPS checklist item 7). Exact model snapshots, temperatures, seeds, retries, and total retained runs are unknown. Benchmark performance therefore demonstrates that the two failure stages can diverge under the reported settings; it does not prove task quality, judge validity, release completeness, or suitability as training data.
