Video-MMMU asks whether large multimodal models can acquire usable knowledge from professional educational videos rather than merely answer short clip-recognition questions. The final venue source is ACL Anthology entry 2026.acl-long.1281, titled "Video-MMMU: Evaluating Knowledge Acquisition from Multidisciplinary Professional Videos"; the arXiv/project title uses "Multi-Discipline Professional Videos."

The decision boundary is a benchmark and evaluation surface for video-based knowledge acquisition. The benchmark contains 300 expert-level videos and 900 human-annotated questions across six disciplines. Questions are organized into three cognitive tracks: Perception, Comprehension, and Adaptation. The Adaptation track is central because it asks whether a model can apply knowledge from a video to a novel problem, not just identify content visible or spoken in the clip.

The concrete data object is a video-question record: video content, discipline/subject metadata, question, answer options or answer target, stage label, ground-truth answer, and evaluation setting such as whether transcript or audio is available. Its screening relevance comes from providing a structured multimodal evaluation surface for learning-from-video, with a performance-gain metric that compares model behavior before and after video access.
