The prior-work baseline is split between video QA benchmarks and college-level multimodal exams. Many video benchmarks emphasize temporal perception, event understanding, or content recall; MMMU and MMMU-Pro emphasize multidisciplinary college-level reasoning but are not centered on learning from video over time.

Video-MMMU changes the target by making the video a learning substrate. Its staged taxonomy asks whether the model can extract information, understand it, and then use it in a new scenario. The performance-gain framing is a direction signal because it evaluates improvement from video access, which is closer to knowledge acquisition than static visual question answering.

What is not new: the benchmark does not introduce multiple-choice evaluation, video QA, college-level multimodal questions, or expert annotation from scratch. Its novelty is the combination of professional educational videos, six-discipline coverage, three-stage cognitive taxonomy, adaptation questions, and before/after-video gain measurement. Reuse checks should focus on source-video licensing, overlap with MMMU/MMMU-Pro, transcript policy, and whether public videos or questions have entered model training data.
