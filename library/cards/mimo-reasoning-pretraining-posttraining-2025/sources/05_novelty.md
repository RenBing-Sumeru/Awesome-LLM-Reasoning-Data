MiMo's useful contribution is the connection between reasoning-oriented pretraining and verifiable posttraining. The pretraining recipe treats equation/code preservation, reasoning-density tagging, staged mixture changes, synthetic responses, and Multi-Token Prediction as preparation for later exploration. The posttraining recipe then contrasts direct Base-to-RL with distilled-SFT-to-RL rather than presenting RL as independent of the base-policy data history.

At the feedback layer, test-difficulty reward is more specific than all-tests-pass code reward. Pass rates from generated solutions define difficulty groups, and strict or soft partial-credit schemes provide signal on problems that would otherwise be reward-sparse. Dynamic sampling, a retained easy pool, and a rollout engine that overlaps generation and verification make the effective data distribution and infrastructure part of the recipe.

None of the individual ingredients—deduplication, model quality taggers, distillation, GRPO, executable tests, dynamic sampling, or asynchronous evaluation—is new by itself. The distinctive disclosure is their lifecycle composition and the report's discussion of failure cases. It remains a mechanism-level disclosure, not a complete open recipe, because the source rows, teachers, tests, verifier configurations, trajectories, and training implementation are absent.
