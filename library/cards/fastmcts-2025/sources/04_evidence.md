Under the paper's training-synthesis accounting, FastMCTS uses slightly fewer average generated tokens and obtains more accepted paths per problem. On EN Math Hard it reports 26.2K tokens and 5.88 correct paths versus rejection sampling's 27.8K and 3.46; on CN High School Math Hard, 17.4K and 13.70 versus 18.2K and 8.15. The abstract's more general headline is over 30% more correct paths as token generation scales. This is not a claim of 30% lower token cost.

For the English mixture with at most 16 training trajectories per problem, Qwen2.5-7B trained on rejection-sampling data averages 52.7 across nine benchmarks; FastMCTS SFT averages 55.6, a 2.9-point gain. Adding 152K Branch-DPO pairs reaches 56.6, which is the 3.9-point headline relative to rejection sampling. The Chinese table reports smaller, benchmark-dependent changes. Additional 3B/7B comparisons improve the reported average but include individual regressions. These results support the complete synthesis-and-training setup under the paper's budgets; they do not validate each backed node or preference label.

The official repository materially supports the recipe: it includes tree classes, asynchronous generation and evaluation, SFT serialization, Branch-DPO pair construction, training scripts, and examples. Reproduction remains partial because paper-matched configs, manifests, outputs, checkpoints, seeds, pinned dependencies, and a license are absent.
