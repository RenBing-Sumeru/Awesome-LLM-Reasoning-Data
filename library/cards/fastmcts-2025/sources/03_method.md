For training synthesis, 46,000 English problems are drawn from NuminaMath, MetaMath, and InternLM-Math train, and 50,000 Chinese high-school problems are collected from the internet. Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct first produces five solutions per problem; 100%-pass questions are removed. Keyword rules exclude proof, show-that, find-all, URL, and image cases; broken or incomplete formatting is removed; hashes are deduplicated and reported evaluation overlaps excluded. The resulting mixtures are called EN Math Hard and CN High School Math Hard, but their manifests are not public.

Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct generates step-formatted solutions with temperature 1 through SGLang and randomly composed few-shot examples. Selection uses UCT plus two changes. Adaptive Stay may expand an internal node when its value is very low or high rather than always descending to a leaf. Dynamic Exploration multiplies the UCT exploration constant by the current node score after repeated visits. Expansion and simulation are merged: root expansion uses degree 3, later expansion degree 2, and every completed simulation is retained. The main synthesis uses 16 iterations; efficiency sweeps use 4-20 iterations and expansion degree 1-2.

The same Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct model judges each complete solution against the standard answer. The prompt asks whether the final answer matches and is not guessed or vague; it does not independently verify every intermediate step. Three samples must agree, and disagreements trigger another round until unanimity. Output length is capped at 32 tokens, but the retry count has no published or implemented cap. Binary terminal outcomes are backed up to node values.

Qwen2.5-7B is trained for three SFT epochs with maximum length 4096, global batch 32, learning rate 1e-5, and 0.1 warmup ratio; the best validation checkpoint is selected. Branch-DPO then trains three epochs with batch 16, learning rate 1e-6, beta 0.4, AdamW, cosine scheduling, and 0.1 warmup. The public processors serialize accepted paths and pair children using the backed-score heuristic, but the checked example configuration uses 32B, one judge sample, and eight iterations rather than the paper's main setup.
