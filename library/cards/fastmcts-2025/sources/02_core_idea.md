The root stores a math problem and reference answer. A partial solution is a state; the next numbered natural-language step is an action and node. From a selected node, the generator completes one or more trajectories. FastMCTS splits every generated completion at `Step n` markers and retains all segments as a branch, so simulation tokens become reusable tree data rather than disposable rollouts. Terminal trajectories receive a binary LLM judgment, which is backed up to visits, wins, and `win_count / visit_count` at each ancestor.

The same tree yields two training objects. Verifier-accepted complete paths become SFT records, selected across different branches to increase diversity. For preference data, a child with any successful descendant is treated as chosen-capable; a zero-score child is treated as rejected. Repeatedly visited zero-score children form step-level pairs, while once-visited children form branch-level pairs because their local step is considered uncertain. The paper reports up to five pairs per tree, producing 152K and 215K preference records on its two hard-math mixtures, but those experimental records and trees are not released.
