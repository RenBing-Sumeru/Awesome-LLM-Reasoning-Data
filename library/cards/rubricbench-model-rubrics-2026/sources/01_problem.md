Rubric-guided reward models aim to reduce length and style biases, but automatically generated criteria may omit real requirements, add irrelevant standards, or fail to distinguish fluent but incorrect responses. Existing preference benchmarks usually provide only winner labels and no human gold rubrics, so they cannot tell whether failure comes from rubric generation or later scoring.

RubricBench constructs difficult response pairs with expert atomic criteria to evaluate automatic rubric generation and final judge reliability separately.
