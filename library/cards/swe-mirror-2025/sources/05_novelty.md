The closest construction baselines expose two different scaling levers. SWE-rebench collects fresh historical tasks and invests in configuring their environments; SWE-Smith reuses prepared environments but generates synthetic defects from the target repository itself. SWE-Mirror combines the two ideas by taking the semantic pattern from an authentic external issue while placing the executable task inside a reusable target Gym.

The new data interface is the paired transfer object: source issue/PR abstraction, target `problem_statement`, generated hidden `test.patch`, fault-inducing `mirror.patch`, inverse `fix.patch`, and three-state test logs. The feedback interface also contributes a stricter transition contract than a single final test run: tests must behave coherently before the added validation, fail after the issue is introduced, and recover without listed regressions after the inverse fix.

Many components are inherited rather than novel: GitHub issue mining, Agentless-style localization and patch generation, SWE-style task schemas, executable tests, OpenHands/MOpenHands interaction, rejection of unsuccessful trajectories, and Qwen2.5-Coder SFT all have prior precedents. The scale of 60,671 tasks and reuse of 40 Gyms is an engineering expansion, while cross-repository semantic re-instantiation is the conceptual change. Before treating that change as a reusable recipe, curators must inspect source-to-target fidelity, duplicate semantics, hidden-test adequacy, environment pinning, release completeness, and benchmark overlap.
