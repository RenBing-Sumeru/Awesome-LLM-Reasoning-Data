Under the full-resolution arXiv v2 setting, MobileRL with Qwen2.5-VL-7B reaches 72.0% success on AndroidWorld and 42.5% on AndroidLab. MobileRL with GLM-4.1V-9B-Base reaches 80.2% and 53.6%, respectively. Table 1 compares these with V-Droid at 59.5%/38.3%, UI-Tars-1.5 at 64.2%/38.3%, and UI-Genie-Agent at 41.2% on AndroidLab. These are author-reported success rates in the specified emulator/judge setup, not independent reproduction or data-quality certification.

The staged ablation separates initialization from online RL. Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct starts at 27.6%/10.1%, rises to 50.2%/36.9% after reasoning-free SFT, 56.8%/38.7% after reasoning SFT, and 72.0%/42.5% after AdaGRPO. GLM-4.1V-9B-Base starts at 7.7%/10.1%, then reaches 48.1%/42.7%, 66.2%/45.0%, and 80.2%/53.6%. The large SFT gains mean the final score cannot be attributed to online RL alone.

The AdaGRPO ablation uses the Qwen reasoning-SFT initialization and AndroidWorld's rule-based verifier to avoid AndroidLab reward-model bias. Averaged over three runs, full MobileRL scores 71.1%; removing AdaPR gives 63.6%, removing SPA 69.1%, removing both 58.5%, removing FCF 64.8%, and omitting all AdaGRPO 56.8%. These results support the combined selection recipe under a 100-step compute budget, but do not reveal how many episodes each filter retained or discarded.

Figure 4 reports eight temperature-1.0 trials by AndroidWorld task complexity. Relative pass@k improvements grow on the highest-complexity group, reaching +11.3, +15.7, +19.9, and +24.3 percentage points at pass@1/2/4/8. SPA also wins more often on step count across complexity groups. This is evidence about paper-defined efficiency, not proof that short paths are safer or semantically preferable.

Two negative results constrain interpretation. The learned AndroidLab reward model reaches 86% accuracy on its curated 1,000-trace set, and the authors report less smooth AndroidLab training curves; MobileRL-9B's best result is obtained with RL only on AndroidWorld. Also, Table 6 shows that image resolution changes GLM results: compressed-image MobileRL-9B gives 75.8%/46.8%, while full-resolution images give 80.2%/53.6%. The official evaluation README warns that inference engine, deployment, and compression can cause 1–2 point variation and reports three-run averages.
