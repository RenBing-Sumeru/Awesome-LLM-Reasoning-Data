The central contribution is Difficulty-Adaptive GRPO (AdaGRPO), which makes the online trajectory distribution an adaptive training signal rather than a passive stream. A warmed-up policy generates groups of Android episodes; binary terminal outcomes are adjusted for path length; high-advantage successes can enter a replay buffer; low-advantage trajectories are pruned; and tasks with repeated all-zero groups are cooled down and eventually removed.

The state-action object is concrete. At turn `t`, the policy observes the current device screenshot and compressed XML accessibility hierarchy together with the original instruction, then emits reasoning and one of Tap, Type, Swipe, Long Press, Launch, Back, Home, Wait, or Finish. Android OS/application dynamics produce the next screen and XML state. An episode ends at Finish in a successful state or the 50-turn horizon; choosing Finish alone is not the success verifier.

The feedback contract is mixed. AndroidWorld training instances expose rule-based success checks. AndroidLab's 1,103 generated training tasks do not have those tests, so strong proprietary VLMs label full screenshot/action traces, a GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking reward model is fine-tuned on several thousand such labels, and the selected model reaches 86% accuracy on 1,000 verified traces. The learned verifier observes the task, screenshot sequence, XML/page states, and actions, but its binary label cannot guarantee absence of collateral or unsafe actions and inherits teacher/judge bias.

SPA rescales only successful trajectories using their length relative to the shortest success for the same task group; early failed termination remains zero. AdaPR inserts the top advantage fraction into a 256-entry replay buffer and mixes selected replay with fresh on-policy data. Negative pruning caps the positive-to-negative ratio at 1:2 by discarding low-advantage negatives. FCF sends a task with two consecutive all-zero epochs into a three-epoch cooldown with sampling weight `exp(-f)` and removes it if failure persists.

Compared with offline mobile imitation such as DigiRL or offline DPO/action datasets, MobileRL trains on closed-loop consequences of the current policy. Compared with single-step GUI RL, it optimizes full multi-turn episodes. The novelty is not GRPO, replay, or curriculum separately; it is their task-difficulty-aware integration with expensive parallel Android sampling. Its audit boundary is equally important: the official release contains evaluation code and a checkpoint, not the adaptive training records that determine the claimed data efficiency.
