Math-Cosmo raises MATH accuracy by 1.72 points in its reported run, but the same row also reports GPQA-Diamond -5.05, HellaSwag -1.26, and Sanitized-MBPP -0.77 relative to baseline (Appendix Table 9). NaturalReasoning-QwQ raises MATH by 1.92 and MathBench-a by 1.47, while MMLU, HellaSwag, GSM8K, HumanEval, and Sanitized-MBPP decline (Table 3). These mixed task deltas support the narrower claim that candidate format and teacher choice alter a multi-task mixture; they do not show that either corpus is uniformly higher quality.

The weakness-guided results are similarly non-uniform. Plain retrieval creates more than 75,000 examples and 82M tokens yet reports MATH -1.70. Retrieval plus augmentation reports MATH +3.72 but GPQA-Diamond -3.03 and HumanEval -1.83 (Table 4). Textbook datasets built by the same procedure also diverge: Intermediate Algebra-QA reports MATH -0.24, while Calculus-QA reports MATH +4.66 but MathBench-a -2.93 and MathBench-t -5.87 (Appendix Table 10). This is evidence of mixture and domain interaction, not a stable record-quality ordering.

The open-dataset tables provide useful negative controls. OpenR1-Math reports MATH +8.96 in one setup while MathBench-t and both code benchmarks fall (Appendix Table 8). Small curated LIMO and s1K produce modest, mixed changes (Appendix Table 7). The paper also reports little benefit from dependency-graph problems, adding Long-CoT traces, or stratifying difficulty by reasoning length (§5). Those failures argue against token count and trace length as sufficient selection criteria.

All results are from the paper's unified but incompletely specified pipeline. The authors explicitly note that confidence intervals and p-values are absent. No repeated-run variance, seeds, or generated corpora are available, and candidate recipes change multiple variables at once. Accordingly, benchmark performance is evidence about these interventions under one scaffold, not proof of data correctness, diversity, legality, decontamination, or causal superiority.
