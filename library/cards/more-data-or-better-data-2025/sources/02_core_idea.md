The unifying device is an 80/20 annealing-style comparison. A large, validated but undisclosed code-and-math corpus provides 80% of samples; one candidate dataset supplies 20%. DeepSeek-V2-Lite or Qwen2.5-3B is trained through pretraining or SFT stages, and the resulting checkpoint is evaluated on knowledge, reasoning, math, and code benchmarks. The paper operationally calls a candidate "effective" when adding it improves the baseline, while also watching for regressions outside mathematics.

Within that scaffold, the paper compares several hypotheses: broad web aggregation versus curated datasets; raw OCR book text versus Qwen2.5-72B educational reconstruction (Math-Cosmo); original NaturalReasoning answers versus QwQ-32B regeneration with consistency filtering; retrieval around MATH failures versus LLM augmentation; and textbook question-answer extraction with LLM filtering and MinHash deduplication. It also records unsuccessful dependency-graph generation, Long-CoT mixing, and reasoning-length difficulty bins.

This is an aggregate model-selection feedback contract, not a record-level verifier. Answer agreement filters some synthetic traces, LLM judgments filter textbook QA, and MinHash removes near-duplicates, but exact prompts, thresholds, adjudication, and error rates are mostly undisclosed. The 80/20 outcome says whether a candidate mixture helped under the reported run; it does not certify every retained example.

The closest atlas comparison is `advancing-math-data-synthesis-2025`, which contrasts four problem-solution transformations and CPT/SFT allocation. This paper instead holds a nominal 80/20 baseline/candidate ratio across a broader set of external and internally constructed candidates and foregrounds cross-task regressions. Both are recipe studies with unreleased training records; neither supplies a formal mathematical verifier or supports direct corpus reuse.
