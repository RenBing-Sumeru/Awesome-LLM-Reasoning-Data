The official EMNLP 2025 Industry Track paper, published by ACL in November 2025, studies how mathematical-reasoning data recipes behave inside a common training mixture. Data work often labels a corpus "high quality" after a model trained with it improves on one benchmark, but that shortcut conflates the source material, teacher model, record format, filtering, mixture weight, token budget, optimizer, and evaluation overlap. This paper asks a narrower operational question: when different candidate datasets are inserted into one industrial-style mixture, which selection and synthesis recipes improve the resulting model, and which do not?

The study spans both pretraining and SFT data. Its candidate objects include scraped mathematical text, OCR-derived book passages, educational rewrites, distilled question-answer reasoning traces, retrieval-selected examples around model failures, LLM-augmented questions, and textbook-derived question-solution pairs. It belongs in Data Construction and Open Release Recipes because the central object is the source-to-mixture construction policy and its negative controls; it is not an open data release, a record-level verifier paper, or a general scaling law. The Card remains at L3 evidence depth because the paper and appendix support recipe and outcome analysis, while the baseline corpus, constructed records, code, and run manifests are unavailable. A benchmark delta is evidence about one trained mixture rather than proof that its records are intrinsically correct, diverse, licensed, or uncontaminated.
