- **Scope and task model.** The benchmark covers benign personas in banking, airline, and retail. Goal shifts are predeclared, ordered, and often explicit. It does not test implicit, ambiguous, interleaved, adversarial, browser, OS, code, or robotic changes. Results therefore do not establish robustness to unrestricted goal drift or real customers.
- **Recovery definition.** GSRT's binary recovery predicate requires acknowledgment and no human transfer; relevant tool use and shifted-goal outcome are timed but do not determine recovery. This can produce a false positive when an agent politely recognizes the change but does not act or succeed. A detector miss can produce a false negative. The shift detector model, prompt, calibration, repetition policy, and error rate are undisclosed.
- **Judge dependence.** `gpt-4o-mini` judges communication from the transcript, but the exact snapshot, prompt, temperature, calibration, and repeated-judgment protocol are unknown. Scores may move with style, verbosity, phrasing, or service updates. Natural-language assertions and task-specific predicates can also omit semantic requirements or reward benchmark-specific behavior.
- **Metric blind spots.** TCRR catches exact duplicate calls in a three-turn window or excess same-function calls, but can miss semantically redundant calls whose arguments differ and can penalize justified retries after transient failures. API/schema validity is not the same as selecting the correct action sequence, and a weighted TSR can conceal which component failed.
- **Construction uncertainty.** The task generator, full prompt, decoding settings, batch count, acceptance yield, rejected candidates, item-level lineage, and review decisions are not disclosed. The claimed 10% manual audit lacks annotator count, sampling rule, agreement statistic, adjudication, and revision history. Hidden generator/simulator traits may therefore shape the benchmark.
- **Count and result inconsistencies.** The stated source components add to 314 rather than the reported 315 tasks. The 2,835-sequence claim lacks a task/run manifest. Table 7 conflicts with the following persona-recovery prose. These issues prevent record-level scale and persona-result auditing until the underlying artifact is reconciled.
- **Release and replay.** The supplementary ZIP is officially listed but was not retrievable. No inspected manifest establishes task files, harness code, database snapshots, dependencies, checksums, per-run logs, split, success/failure retention, or replay instructions. Closed-model endpoints, unknown simulator/judge seeds, and an unpinned tau-bench-derived environment further prevent exact historical replay.
- **Rights, splits, and contamination.** The paper licenses differ across arXiv (CC BY 4.0) and MTI-LLM OpenReview (CC BY-ND 4.0); neither establishes a code/dataset/trajectory license. The rights chain for inherited tau-bench templates and model-generated records is unknown. No train/dev/test manifest or decontamination protocol is disclosed, and public inherited tasks create benchmark-exposure risk.
- **Version drift.** arXiv v1 lists eight authors, while the official MTI presentation surface lists five; a parallel LAW @ NeurIPS 2025 record uses another note ID and workshop footer. Citations and supplements must name the chosen surface, note ID, version, and access date rather than combining them into an imaginary immutable release.
- **Use boundary.** The paper evaluates agents and does not report SFT, preference optimization, reward-model training, process supervision, RLVR, or agent training. Benchmark performance is not proof of trajectory quality or training utility. Until release contents, license, split, lineage, failures, and replay are verified, the resource is safe for evaluation analysis only.
