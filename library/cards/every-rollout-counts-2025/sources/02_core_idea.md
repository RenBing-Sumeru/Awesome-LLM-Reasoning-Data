DORA combines candidate quality with semantic uniqueness. Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B scores each active partial trajectory; softmax with T_b=0.1 converts scores into quality weights. BGE-M3 embeds the trajectories, cosine similarities form a matrix, and row-wise softmax with T_s=0.01 forms a soft affinity matrix. The diagonal affinity P_ii is interpreted as candidate i's uniqueness, roughly inverse to the size of its underlying direction. Quality times uniqueness is renormalized and multiplied by total budget N to obtain allocations.

The theoretical analysis models unknown candidate success probabilities with Beta priors centered on normalized PRM scores. It shows how optimal allocation changes with confidence and why candidate multiplicity biases solution-level allocation. Theorem 1 recovers direction-level optimal allocation only when candidates can be grouped into directions and all candidates within a direction share the same PRM score. DORA's embeddings and PRM are practical proxies for those latent quantities, not proofs that the inferred directions or scores are correct.
