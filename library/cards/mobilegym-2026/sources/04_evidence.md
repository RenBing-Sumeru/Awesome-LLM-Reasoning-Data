On the 256-template test split, nine agents span 9.4%–58.8% Success Rate. Gemini 3.1 Pro reports 58.8±1.4 SR and 72.1 PR; Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct reports 9.4±0.6 SR and 20.1 PR. Open-source models use four trials with resampled parameters, while most proprietary rows are single-run; the table marks only Gemini's repeated estimate among proprietary systems. Unexpected Side Effects range from 4.7% to 14.5% and do not decrease monotonically with SR, showing that success and unintended mutation are distinct measurements (Paper Table 2 and §5.1).

The GRPO case study raises Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct from 9.4% to 22.2% SR on Test256, a +12.8-point gain. Difficulty SR changes from 71.2 to 92.5 on L1, 12.3 to 37.7 on L2, 0.6 to 11.7 on L3, and 0.3 to 1.2 on L4. The near-flat L4 result is an important negative boundary: ten training steps did not solve the hardest stratum (Paper §5.2).

For sim-to-real, tasks are first bucketed using four simulator rollouts per base/trained model. Of 67 tasks in the Uplift, Stable-pass, and Mid buckets, eight are excluded because they cannot be equivalently or safely reproduced; 59 are run on a Redmi Note 12 Turbo. On this selected signal subset, simulation rises 33.9%→76.7% (+42.8 points) and real-device execution rises 32.2%→72.9% (+40.7 points), retaining 95.1% of the simulation-side gain. Fifteen sampled Stable-fail tasks score 0/15 for both models on device. This is an author-described existence proof, not comprehensive transfer across the full 256 tasks (Paper Figure 5 and §5.2; Appendix H.5).

Manual audit of 118 signal-subset real-device trajectories finds 12 Qwen3.6-Plus judge errors (10.2%); GPT-5.4 also yields 12 errors on a partially different subset. This supports the paper's motivation for state-based verification inside the simulator, but it does not establish that every authored state checker is correct (Paper §5.2 and Appendix J).

Efficiency measurements report about 400 MB RAM and 50 MB disk per browser instance, about 3 seconds cold start, and a full 256-task evaluation in roughly 6 minutes with 256 parallel instances using about 100 GB RAM and under 10% CPU on the authors' server (Paper Table 1 and §5.3). These system measurements and all performance results are author-reported; this Card did not independently rerun them.
