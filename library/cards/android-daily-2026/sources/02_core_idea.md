AndroidDaily's central contribution is a judgment contract for closed-source mobile episodes: task-specific external guidelines plus a two-layer VLM judge replace privileged application-state assertions with process-aware evaluation over the visible action trajectory.

The guideline supplies three distinct tiers. Operational obligations specify actions or conditions that must be satisfied; output-quality criteria assess the produced result; negative constraints bound unsafe, irreversible, or otherwise prohibited behavior. GRADE's Evidence Layer walks the screenshot/accessibility/action sequence, removes uninformative steps, maintains working memory, and assembles action history, confirmed facts, target bindings, comparison states, and blocker signals. Its Verdict Layer checks each tier, arbitrates the findings, emits a final pass/fail value, and retains diagnostics such as obligation coverage, boundary violations, and failure tags (Paper Sections 3.2-3.3; Algorithm 1).

The feedback can observe pixels, available accessibility metadata, actions, and temporal changes captured in the trajectory. It cannot directly inspect an application's backend state, hidden business logic, unrendered consequences, or facts absent from the recorded episode. GRADE is therefore a learned, judgment-required verifier, not a deterministic state oracle; its final Boolean is an evaluation label, not a training reward in this paper.

Relative to AndroidWorld and OSWorld, the change is to support real closed-source apps without privileged internal-state assertions. Relative to a one-shot terminal VLM judgment, the change is the evidence/verdict decomposition and explicit task guideline. The 2025 235-task AndroidDaily release predates this paper's 350-task, 94-app benchmark and is not evidence that the current GRADE tasks or code are released.
