The closest privileged-state baseline is represented by benchmarks such as AndroidWorld and OSWorld, where success can be checked through access to internal application or environment state. AndroidDaily changes the feedback interface for closed-source commercial apps: task authors externalize obligations, quality criteria, and negative constraints, and a VLM judges the full observable trajectory when hidden state is unavailable (Paper Sections 1-2.2; Table 1).

The method also differs from coarse terminal VLM scoring. GRADE first constructs persistent, step-linked evidence and only then applies tier-specific verdict checks and arbitration. The resulting object carries both an episode label and diagnostic process signals, making visual misses, uncovered obligations, boundary violations, and blockers inspectable rather than collapsing all feedback into a single unstructured score (Paper Section 3.3; Algorithm 1).

What is not new remains important. The work does not introduce Android, ADB control, general-purpose VLM judging, physical-device rollout, pass@1 evaluation, or the AndroidDaily name. The official 2025 predecessor contains 235 tasks, whereas this paper reports 350 tasks over 94 apps with GRADE; the paper does not disclose an item-level predecessor mapping or overlap audit. Expanding app/task count and evaluating 12 models are scale and integration contributions, not evidence of a new verification primitive by themselves.

For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is the explicit coupling of episode schema and feedback contract under inaccessible state. Reuse still requires checking the current task/guideline release, evaluator implementation and prompt, backbone/version pin, app/account snapshot, intervention ledger, raw evidence retention, predecessor overlap, and license; none can be inferred from benchmark performance.
