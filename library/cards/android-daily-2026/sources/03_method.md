Inputs begin with high-frequency closed-source Android applications selected from app-store download rankings and public active-user statistics. Task allocation is frequency-weighted: more-used apps receive more tasks, while lower-frequency apps receive essential-use tasks. The resulting 350 tasks span 94 apps; 173 are Information & Decision, 99 Creation & Communication, and 78 Execution & Operations. There are 124 tasks with at most two constraints and 226 with three or more. Exact ranking sources, dates, thresholds, task authorship, allocation formula, deduplication, and quality-control rules are unknown (Paper Section 3.1; Figure 3).

The benchmark pairs each task with a natural-language instruction and three-tier guideline; record-level authorship is unknown. Before rollout, logged-in accounts, pre-loaded content, and required app states are prepared on physical Android devices. 12 models, comprising 5 general-purpose VLMs and 7 GUI-specialized agents, act through ADB. Each model-task run uses pass@1, a maximum of 60 interaction steps, and a 40-minute wall-clock limit; released inference protocols are used when available, otherwise configurations are tuned for best performance. A monitor may handle unexpected device/system states or abort a boundary-approaching run, but intervention logs and exact reset recipes are not released (Paper Sections 3.2 and 4.1).

Each full chain-of-action trajectory receives a unique session ID and can be written as task `x`, guideline `G`, and trajectory `tau = {(o_t, a_t)}`, where each observation includes screenshots and available accessibility metadata. GRADE's Evidence Layer compresses the trajectory, filters uninformative steps, updates working memory, and builds an evidence bundle. The Verdict Layer evaluates obligations, quality criteria, negative constraints, and blockers; arbitration returns a Boolean completion verdict plus process diagnostics (Paper Section 3.3; Algorithm 1).

The output is an evaluation record with step-level diagnostic evidence and a full-episode pass/fail label. The paper uses it only for benchmark evaluation and evaluator analysis; it reports no SFT, preference optimization, reward-model training, RLVR, or other agent-training objective. No benchmark train/dev/test split or contamination control is disclosed. Raw trajectories are not released, and current 350-task definitions, guidelines, evaluator prompt/configuration, app/APK manifest, session manifest, code URL, data URL, license, and immutable release revision remain unverified (Paper Sections 4-5).
