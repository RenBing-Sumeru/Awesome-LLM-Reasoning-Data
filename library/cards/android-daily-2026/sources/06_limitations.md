- GRADE is a fallible learned judge rather than a privileged-state verifier. On the 879-session reliability set, its best reported configuration still produces 86 false positives and 25 false negatives, and accuracy changes from 65.64% to 87.37% across evaluator backbones (Paper Table 5).
- Screenshots and available accessibility metadata cannot reveal every backend commit, hidden constraint, delayed side effect, or off-screen state. A visually plausible trajectory may therefore satisfy GRADE while the real task remains incomplete; this is a feedback-observability limit supported by the reported false positives.
- Closed-source apps drift through releases, A/B tests, personalization, pop-ups, network conditions, and account state. The paper says controlled APK versions and standardized accounts mitigate drift, but it does not disclose an app/APK/device/account/reset manifest, so reported scores are time-specific snapshots (Paper Section 5).
- Evaluation is pass@1 with no multi-seed variance, each session may run for 40 minutes, and non-official model configurations are tuned for best performance without complete prompt, temperature, decoding, or seed disclosure. Model ranking uncertainty and exact replication cost cannot be recovered from the paper alone.
- The paper claims release of task definitions, guidelines, and evaluation code but supplies no verifiable current-version URL, license, hash, or tag; raw trajectories are explicitly not released. Successful/failed sample retention, evaluator inputs, intervention logs, and immutable session lineage are therefore unavailable for external audit (Paper Section 5).
- Benchmark split, task-source citations, record-level authorship, deduplication, decontamination, and overlap with the 235-task predecessor or other GUI benchmarks are unknown. The 879-session judge set and 100-session challenging subset are evaluator-validation samples, not disclosed train/dev/test partitions.
- The paper reports dedicated author-owned accounts, no end-user accounts or third-party data, screenshot review and PII redaction, negative constraints for irreversible actions, and human abort monitoring. Even so, downstream access must respect app terms, and intervention decisions are not reproducible without logs (Paper Section 5).
- Professional, enterprise, and accessibility-specific workflows are outside the reported scope. Curator inference: if future agents are optimized directly against GRADE, visually persuasive partial completion or diagnostic blind spots could become verifier-gaming targets; this paper evaluates models but does not test that training scenario.
