On the 350-task benchmark, Gemini 3 Flash reports the highest overall success rate, 62.0%, at 14.7 seconds per step. Its success falls from 69.4% on tasks with at most two constraints to 58.0% on tasks with three or more. Gemini 3 Pro falls from 63.0% on tasks involving at most one app to 41.9% on tasks involving two or more apps. These pass@1 slices show sensitivity to constraint and cross-app complexity under the paper's real-device protocol; there is no multi-seed variance or independent reproduction (Paper Table 2; Section 4.2).

GRADE reliability is measured against 879 baseline sessions manually reviewed and calibrated through three annotation rounds. With Gemini 3 Pro, full GRADE reaches 87.37% accuracy with 188 true positives, 580 true negatives, 86 false positives, and 25 false negatives. Evidence-only evaluation reaches 84.76% and 106 false positives; adding the Verdict Layer raises accuracy and lowers false positives to 86, supporting the narrower claim that structured verdict checking improves this evaluator on this set (Paper Tables 3 and 5; Section 4.3).

The evaluator is strongly backbone-dependent: accuracy on the same 879 sessions ranges from 65.64% with GPT-4o to 87.37% with Gemini 3 Pro, while false positives range from 279 to 86. On 100 visually ambiguous sessions, five non-expert raters individually agree with calibrated ground truth at 49-60% without guidelines and 56-67% with guidelines; GRADE reaches 72-73%. Guidelines help both humans and GRADE, but neither result eliminates judgment ambiguity (Paper Tables 4-5; Section 4.3).

The paper manually categorizes all failed sessions from the top three frontier and top three GUI-specialized models and identifies latency timeout/click misalignment, memory-induced loops, and protocol-induced visual-capability degradation; exact failure-category counts are not reported (Paper Figures 5-6; Section 4.4). Benchmark scores and evaluator agreement demonstrate behavior under the reported protocol, not task-set correctness, unrestricted data quality, release completeness, causal attribution, or safe training reuse.
