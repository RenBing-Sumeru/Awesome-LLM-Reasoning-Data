On VerifierBench's binary setting, where Invalid is merged with Incorrect, CompassVerifier-3B/7B/32B report average accuracy of 85.0/87.5/90.8 and average F1 of 80.4/83.4/87.7. CompassVerifier-7B's 83.4 F1 exceeds Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's 42.1 and the specialized Tencent-Qwen2.5-7B-RLVR's 67.1 in the same table. The strongest 32B result is uneven by domain: F1 is 80.8 on mathematics versus 94.8 on knowledge.

The ternary evaluation preserves Invalid as a separate class. CompassVerifier-3B/7B/32B report average accuracy of 83.7/85.8/89.4 and macro-F1 of 80.6/83.4/88.3. This is the more relevant evidence for applications that need to distinguish bad answers from malformed tasks or outputs. The released test labels are human gold judgments, but the paper does not report annotator counts, inter-annotator agreement, confidence intervals, or independent reproduction.

The 7B ablation reports 84.0 accuracy and 79.8 F1 for the base data; formula augmentation alone reaches 86.7/82.8, error-driven augmentation alone 86.4/82.0, and both 87.5/83.4. On the hard subset of the separate VerifyBench benchmark, CompassVerifier-32B reports 89.7 accuracy and 81.1 F1 with its model-specific prompt, and 86.8/74.3 with the VerifyBench prompt. This supports prompt robustness within two benchmark settings, not universal out-of-distribution reliability.

In the GRPO experiment, Qwen3-4B-Base scores 2.7/1.8/34.1 avg@32 on AIME24/AIME25/MATH500. Math-Verify reward reaches 8.9/7.2/63.1; CompassVerifier-7B reaches 21.2/17.3/82.2 and CompassVerifier-32B 21.2/17.2/83.3. These are downstream policy results under one Open-S1 filtering and training setup. They do not independently prove label correctness, corpus quality, or that the unreleased 96,832-example training set can be reproduced.
