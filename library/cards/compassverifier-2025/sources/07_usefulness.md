For the data-construction and open-release recipe track, CompassVerifier is a strong worked example of turning a heterogeneous response pool into both a verifier benchmark and reward model. Its most reusable elements are the staged difficulty funnel, explicit A/B/C contract, expert review of residual disputes, error-pattern-driven augmentation, and separate evaluation of binary and ternary behavior.

An implementation should preserve more than the public seven-field row: source benchmark/version, generator checkpoint and decoding config, every judge prompt and vote, human rationale and adjudication, duplicate/filter decisions, augmentation template, teacher output, and final split. Keeping Invalid separate from Incorrect enables audit of broken questions and reward-hacking outputs before deciding whether a downstream policy reward may merge them.

The public benchmark and checkpoints support verifier comparison, response filtering, and controlled RL experiments. The unreleased training corpus should not be described as an open data release. Before reuse, pin artifact revisions, inspect license inheritance, evaluate calibration and false-positive costs by domain/answer type, test prompt shift, and independently audit a stratified sample. Benchmark and policy gains are evidence of utility under reported settings, not proof of data quality.
