The work combines three artifacts that are often separate: a difficult outcome-verifier benchmark, a staged recipe for obtaining human-calibrated labels from a million-scale model-response pool, and compact verifier checkpoints usable for both evaluation and RL reward. VerifierBench also makes Invalid a first-class label rather than silently counting truncated, repetitive, refused, or flawed records as ordinary wrong answers.

The data recipe is more distinctive than model scale. It uses easy-consensus removal to concentrate test difficulty, converts moderate consensus into training supervision, derives adversarial templates from observed human–judge disagreements, and augments formula equivalence and prompt/long-context invariance. Compared with a general LLM-as-a-judge, the released 3B–32B models offer a fixed task contract; compared with rule matching, they cover heterogeneous answer formats. The contribution remains outcome-level and reference-dependent, not a verifier of reasoning faithfulness.
