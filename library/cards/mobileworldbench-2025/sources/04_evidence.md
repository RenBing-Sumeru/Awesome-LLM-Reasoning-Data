All model results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced. Fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct on MobileWorld increases MobileWorldBench generation overall from **11.84 to 12.39** and QA accuracy from **67.32 to 71.40** (Paper Tables 1–2). These gains are conditional on the released annotation pipeline, two-epoch SFT recipe, benchmark prompts, judge version, and model decoding. They do not prove that every transition label is correct or that the data represents complete agent behavior.

In the separate downstream AndroidWorld experiment, success is **46.9** for the baseline, **50.8** with zero-shot semantic world modeling, and **54.3** with the fine-tuned world model (Paper Table 3). The planner proposes eight actions, predicts their semantic next states, and scores them with a VLM value model. This result supports planning utility for a learned one-step predictor, but AndroidWorld is an online downstream experiment—not MobileWorldBench's released environment or feedback contract.

Release-level counts are directly auditable. `benchmark/gen.csv` has 250 rows and `benchmark/qa.csv` has 1,787 rows, including 1,162 Yes and 625 No labels. The official benchmark-image snapshot lists 958 files, but both expected images for the generation row at `google_apps/episode_12551800716657978063/step_2363_raw.jpg` and `step_2364_raw.jpg` are absent. Therefore **249 of 250 generation rows have both images**, and the affected row has neither.

The construction funnel is also explicit: 500 QA source transitions produce 4,000 candidates, GPT-4o filtering leaves 2,458, and human filtering leaves 1,787. For generation, action descriptions for all 250 sampled transitions are human checked and retained. These checks document selection stages, but the released QA CSV omits record-level source/split/annotator/filter provenance, and no error rate for generation references or GPT-4o rubric judgments is reported.

Reproduction evidence exposes a judge-version confound. The paper identifies `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`; the released scoring script defaults to `gpt-4o-mini` without explicit configuration. Scores generated by those settings should not be treated as interchangeable. Human pairwise comparison over 3,000 sampled output matches is supportive analysis, but does not replace a fixed, independently calibrated evaluator.
