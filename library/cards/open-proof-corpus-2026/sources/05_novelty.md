Open Proof Corpus is distinguished by combining positive and negative natural-language proofs with expert verdicts, written rationales, optional localized error spans, solver identity, source provenance, and selection context in one public record. Unlike answer-level math releases, it exposes why a proof is rejected; unlike formal corpora, it directly studies informal frontier-model proofs. Its best-of-N component further permits analysis of selector behavior against human-graded candidate groups, although exhaustive eight-candidate grading covers only a limited subset.
