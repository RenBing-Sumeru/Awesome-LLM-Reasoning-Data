Thought Anchors makes a sentence-conditioned **distribution of continuations** the primary unit of trace attribution. Earlier forced-answer approaches interrupt a trace and ask whether the answer can already be produced; the proposed comparison instead lets downstream reasoning unfold with or without the current sentence. This distinction allows an early planning decision to matter even when the correct final answer cannot yet be emitted at that position.

The method also separates ordinary resampling importance from an explicitly counterfactual subset. Rather than treating every replacement as a meaningful intervention, it conditions on replacement sentences whose all-MiniLM-L6-v2 cosine similarity to the original is below 0.8. This is a concrete and auditable semantic filter, although the embedding model and fixed threshold remain approximations rather than a guarantee of causal semantic difference.

At the artifact level, the release is valuable because it preserves the substrate beneath the score. Users can inspect base solutions, sentence boundaries, continuation arrays, extracted answers, correctness, sentence-function labels, direct dependencies, and multiple importance statistics. Correct and incorrect base traces and failed-answer outcomes coexist. That makes the corpus useful for studying when a trace changes direction, not merely for ranking sentences by a published scalar.

The work links three analysis levels: black-box continuation distributions, receiver-head attention patterns, and attention-suppression sentence graphs. Their partial agreement is scientifically distinctive, but they should remain separate evidence channels. Receiver attention is not itself proof of causality, resampling is expensive and sensitive to the replacement distribution, and masking can be out of distribution. The novelty is the coordinated toolkit and released intervention data, not a solved theory of reasoning causality.

This is also not a tree-search or best-of-\(N\) data-selection method. The keep/remove banks are sampled to estimate influence, not searched to find the highest-reward answer; no policy, reward model, process reward model, or RLVR agent is trained. The paper reports evaluation and audit uses. Describing the release as ready-made SFT, preference, or reinforcement-learning data would broaden its demonstrated use beyond the evidence.

The publication and release status must remain bounded. The paper is an arXiv preprint submitted to ICLR 2026 and displayed as under review; no acceptance decision is verified. The code, dataset, and project page are official and public, but the release lacks a semantic version, complete count manifest, record-level generator lineage, and decontamination report. Openness therefore improves inspectability without making the artifact audit-complete.
