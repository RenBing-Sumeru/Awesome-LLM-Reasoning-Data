The contribution is not a single common training corpus or a frontier-model report. Its distinctive value is one autoregressive world-model recipe spanning language state transitions and visual future prediction while exposing three materially different feedback contracts. The same release surface therefore lets a reader compare exact symbolic agreement, changed-item F1, and perceptual frame metrics without treating them as interchangeable.

It also makes a useful disclosure distinction: the author collection releases derived language data and checkpoints, whereas ByteSized32, WMA/WebArena, RT-1/Open X-Embodiment, and DINO-WM are named upstream inputs with incomplete unified lineage. That boundary is more informative for reuse than a paper-title-only record.
