Earlier large math-distillation corpora used proprietary teachers or provided far fewer open traces, while direct reference-conditioned prompting leaked answers through trivial copying. OpenMathInstruct-1 changes the auditable generation decision by masking computed values in reference solutions, scaling an open teacher, executing code, and sampling balanced subsets by problem rather than by trace. NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks acceptance, 93% MATH and 99.9% GSM8K coverage, public failures, and controlled ablations are quality signals, although code-interpreter traces and final-answer rejection are not themselves new. The direction signal is that an open teacher plus prompt and selection engineering can approach closed-teacher math distillation; reuse must still test semantic faithfulness, source overlap, and per-problem concentration.
