Process reward models require labels for individual reasoning steps, but human annotation is expensive and can have low agreement, while Monte Carlo rollout labels require repeated model calls and inherit sampling noise. FoVer asks whether formal verification tools can supply cheaper, auditable step labels on formal tasks and whether PRMs trained on those labels transfer to natural-language reasoning.

The data object is FOVER-40K: formal-logic and Isabelle theorem-proving traces paired with Boolean correctness labels aligned to ordered steps. The final paper reports 40,000 labeled steps: 10,000 Llama 3.1 8B FLDx2/Z3 steps, 10,000 Qwen 2.5 7B FLDx2/Z3 steps, and 20,000 Qwen 2.5 7B Isabelle steps generated from GSM8K-level sources. Conversation-form fields convert those labels into `correct` or `incorrect` token targets for full-parameter PRM training.

This is a data-construction study, not proof that formal verifiers solve semantic alignment. Z3 and Isabelle certify propositions in constructed formal environments; they do not establish that an LLM-generated Isabelle statement faithfully represents its source word problem. The official dataset variants also describe themselves as materials for a previous paper version, so the exact release corresponding to final ACL Table 1 remains only partially resolved.
