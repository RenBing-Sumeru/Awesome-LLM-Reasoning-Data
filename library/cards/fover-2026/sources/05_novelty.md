FoVer's contribution is a feedback-and-construction interface: it adapts Z3 and Isabelle from solution checking to local step labeling, packages those labels as PRM conversations, and tests whether formal-task supervision transfers to informal reasoning. This differs from human-labeled PRM data, Monte Carlo rollout labels that sample multiple continuations per step, and stronger-LLM judging.

The components themselves are established. FLDx2, GSM8K, MetaMathQA, Big-Math, Z3, Isabelle/HOL, Llama, Qwen, LLaMA-Factory, binary PRMs, and Best-of-K all predate the work. The novelty lies in connecting formal verification to reusable process supervision across both first-order logic and theorem proving, plus documenting formal-to-informal transfer and its failure cases.

The important boundary is local formal validity. The Isabelle wrapper assumes non-target steps through `sorry`; the Z3 branch excludes `assump` patterns; and Qwen-generated Isabelle statements introduce an unverified semantic-conversion layer. FoVer therefore offers a distinct programmatic label source, not a universal replacement for human review, rollout evidence, semantic judges, or end-to-end proof checking.
