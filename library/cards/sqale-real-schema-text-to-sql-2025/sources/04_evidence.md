The most direct evidence comes from executable validation and schema statistics. The final corpus contains 517,676 executable triplets over 135,875 expanded schemas, with a median schema size of 91 tables and 435 columns rather than repeated generation over a few small databases. The authors also compare table counts, column counts, foreign-key density, and query structures, showing that SQaLe covers longer join paths and more complex schemas. This evidence supports the claim that the corpus better represents large real-world database structures, but successful execution alone does not establish that every natural-language question is unambiguous or that the SQL is its unique correct formulation.
