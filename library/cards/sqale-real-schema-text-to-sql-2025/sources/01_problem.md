Existing Text-to-SQL datasets are usually built on a small number of manually designed databases whose schemas contain few tables and simple relationships. They differ substantially from enterprise databases with dozens of tables, hundreds of columns, and dense foreign-key graphs. A model can therefore obtain high execution accuracy on common benchmarks without demonstrating robustness to realistic schemas. Manually expanding question–SQL pairs is also expensive and does not readily increase structural diversity.

SQaLe asks how to synthesize executable Text-to-SQL data at scale while retaining the structural properties of real databases. It starts from schemas mined from public code, expands their database structures, generates natural-language questions and SQL queries, and filters records through syntax, schema constraints, and execution. The result is a verifiable corpus for pre-training, fine-tuning, and RLVR.
