Herald’s core idea is to exploit information that already exists around a Mathlib4 declaration rather than ask a generator to interpret an isolated Lean expression. Lean-Jixia is described as extracting the declaration’s head statement, kind, docstring, neighboring statements, dependent theorems, proof lines, and proof states. A dependency DAG orders statement informalization from lower levels upward, so translations of prerequisite declarations can be supplied when a dependent theorem is processed.

Retrieval adds a second scaffold. The authors manually annotate 1,000 theorem examples, embed their formal statements, and retrieve the nearest example by cosine similarity for the current target. Five pure-mathematics PhD students with Lean expertise review translations across six feedback rounds and turn recurring problems into more than twelve prompt principles. The construction model itself is not named, so the method is better characterized as dependency-aware, retrieval-augmented generation by an undisclosed LLM than as a fully specified reproducible recipe.

The statement corpus grows through two augmentation branches. Tactic-based augmentation converts the hypotheses and goal at proof states into new formal statements and checks these statements with Lean. Similar consecutive tactic states are randomly downsampled. Informal augmentation uses an LLM to produce logically equivalent rewrites, abstract-concept substitutions, omissions of implicit conditions, and Chinese, French, or Russian variants. Samples from both branches are balanced against the originals, producing the paper-reported 1:2:1 original:tactic:informal mixture.

Proof informalization focuses on tactic-style rather than term-style Mathlib proofs. Each tactic line is translated with the surrounding proof states and human-authored explanations of tactic logic, then the line-level descriptions are integrated into an informal proof. This yields step-level textual structure in the proof rows, although no independent correctness label is published for every natural-language step.

The release and the training view must remain separate. There are 579,883 distinct released statement rows. Reversing each pair for FL-to-NL training produces 1.16 million directional training examples, but not 1.16 million distinct released pairs. Herald Translator is fine-tuned from DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-Base 7B after a 2,000-step OpenHermes2.5 warm-up; the final mix is NL-to-FL:FL-to-NL:OpenHermes2.5 = 2:2:1, trained for five epochs at learning rate 4e-5 with cosine decay.
