Tree search, PRM/value guidance, state embeddings, TD learning, and verifier ensembles all predate FETCH. Its distinctive contribution is to treat redundant-state expansion and score-induced trajectory switching as separate inefficiencies, then combine state-pair supervision, post-trained semantic clustering, hyper-node search, adjusted TD(λ) targets, and inference-time ensembling in a plug-in wrapper. For trace-data curation, this makes two often-hidden decisions explicit: which partial states were considered equivalent, and how multiple value estimates determined the next expansion. The paper does not propose a new human-annotated process dataset or a generally valid equivalence oracle.
