FETCH adds two orthogonal components to Best-First, Beam, Tree Search, or MCTS. After expansion, a fine-tuned SimCSE encoder represents new reasoning states and agglomerative clustering merges nearby states into hyper-nodes; the hyper-node score is aggregated from its member-node verifier scores, and later expansion rotates through member nodes by score. Separately, verifier training replaces pure Monte Carlo targets with bounded adjusted TD(λ) returns, while inference can average several verifier predictions. The feedback contract therefore combines outcome-derived scalar value estimates with learned semantic-equivalence decisions. Neither signal is an oracle process label: clustering can merge logically distinct states, and a lower-variance value estimate can still be systematically wrong.
