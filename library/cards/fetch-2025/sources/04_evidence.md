The paper reports answer accuracy and average generated-token cost across Best-First, Beam, Tree Search, and MCTS. State merging generally reduces token use; for example, the reported GSM8K Best-First setting falls from 2.47 to 0.88 in the paper's normalized token metric. TD(λ) training and verifier ensembling improve several accuracy results, and ablations compare pretrained versus post-trained SimCSE, clustering choices, and variance-reduction components. Human evaluation is used to compare embedding similarity with judgments.

These are system-level checks of search behavior. They do not establish that every merged pair is logically equivalent, every value target is calibrated, or every retained trace is suitable for training. Some configurations increase computation on MATH, and ensembling requires multiple verifiers. Benchmark accuracy and token savings motivate auditing the construction recipe; they are not data-quality proof for an unreleased rollout corpus.
