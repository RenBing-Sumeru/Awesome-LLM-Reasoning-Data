FETCH is useful as a schema for search-policy and verifier audits. A reusable trace should preserve the prompt and split, every raw state and edge, state-pair supervision source, embedding and clustering revisions, distance threshold, hyper-node membership, member and aggregate scores, TD/Monte-Carlo target, selected expansion, termination reason, answer check, token count, and random seed. With those fields, researchers can measure redundant expansion, false merges, verifier variance, trajectory switching, and compute allocation separately.

The recipe may also generate SFT or verifier-training material after independent correctness and provenance checks. The repository's toy example is useful for interface inspection but insufficient to claim a released dataset. Downstream benchmark improvements establish a system use case; they do not replace record-level validation, licensing review, or contamination analysis.
