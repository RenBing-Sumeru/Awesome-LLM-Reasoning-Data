The strongest evidence concerns method behavior under fixed task and model settings; it is not an audit of every generated record.

**Why localized guidance is used.** In the 10,000-example Countdown motivation study, supplying zero, one, two, or three optimal-prefix steps raises search success from 55.96% to 85.28%, 95.27%, and 100.00%. Cross-entropy loss simultaneously rises from 0.0481 to 0.1245, 0.2412, and 0.2539. The authors use this trade-off to motivate local editing rather than direct imitation of a long optimal prefix (Paper Table 1).

**Countdown accuracy and candidate coverage.** With Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, 500,000 SoS initialization traces, three 200,000-problem Guided-ReST iterations, and optional operation-level PPO, the full system reaches about 87% greedy accuracy at the 4,096-token maximum budget on both seen and unseen targets. The paper reports a margin of more than ten percentage points over the second-best PPO baseline and comparable accuracy using about half as many tokens (Paper §5.2, Figure 3). Before final PPO, Guided-ReST versus ReST pass@1/pass@32 is 62.7/96.8 versus 62.3/76.6 on seen targets, and 61.0/96.8 versus 60.8/77.0 on unseen targets at temperature 1.0 (Paper Table 2). The larger pass@k gap supports the narrower claim that the data-generation procedure broadens correct-candidate coverage under this setup.

**Optimizer and reward ablations.** At the maximum Countdown budget, operation-level PPO reaches 87%, compared with 83% for token-level PPO. The paper reports approximately 2 times higher token efficiency in the low-budget regime and 1.5 times in the high-budget regime (Paper §5.4, Figure 5). A dense reward that adds 0.25 when an optimal subgoal is reached has little effect overall and slightly hurts high-budget performance; reward is granted only once per subgoal to limit repeated-subgoal hacking (Paper §5.5, Figure 6). This negative result distinguishes construction-time privileged guidance from reward shaping.

**Code self-repair.** With Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, 16,000 training problems, eight episodes per problem, four turns, and three SFT iterations, Guided-ReST achieves pass@1/pass@32 of 10.5/33.9 on 165 CodeContests tasks, versus ReST at 9.4/30.4. On 408 CodeForces tasks, the corresponding values are 9.7/37.6 and 9.7/35.9 (Paper Table 3). The authors describe these improvements as moderate and attribute the smaller effect to fewer training problems, incomplete episode-level augmentation, and weaker search. CodeContests is explicitly near-distribution because the training mixture includes that source; CodeForces is the more informative out-of-distribution check.

**Release evidence.** The official repository supplies runnable stages and source-level checks for masking, arithmetic verification, code execution, positive-score filtering, SFT, PPO, and evaluation. The checked commit has no tag or release and provides neither generated Guided-ReST buffers nor trained checkpoints. The reported benchmark gains therefore support the algorithm under the paper's conditions, not the quality, licensing, decontamination, diversity, or safe reusability of an unavailable frozen corpus. Results were not independently reproduced for this Card, and the main paper reports no error bars.
