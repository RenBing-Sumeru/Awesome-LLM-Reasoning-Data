Prior multimodal browsing benchmarks such as MMSearch, BrowseComp-VL, and MM-BrowseComp already combine images, questions, retrieval, or web-agent evaluation. MMSearch-Plus changes the task object through Spatial-Temporal Extrapolation: the image anchors an event or entity, while the requested answer lies beyond the displayed space or time. Annotators preserve a video or arXiv source reference and adversarially remove questions that closed MLLMs can answer without retrieval.

The second concrete change is the evaluated interface. The agent can alternate text search, image search, and human-authored SoM-indexed crop/search while carrying a threaded hypothesis state over SerpAPI results and Gemini summaries. This makes failures in visual localization, query policy, retrieval, synthesis, and stopping separately discussable. However, the public release serializes only QA/image/source-reference rows; the dynamic state and transitions that motivate the analysis are not released.

The feedback design remains answer-centric. Acceptable variants and GPT-4o semantic judgment make open-form scoring practical, but they do not produce citation verification or step-level provenance supervision. The paper's `state_action_level` evaluation surface reflects the agent's interaction process, not a released labeled state-action dataset. SoM gains likewise measure privileged human localization and do not establish automatic detection capability.

What is not new is visual QA, live search, LLM-generated summaries, LLM-as-a-judge, crop/zoom tools, reversible obfuscation, or Set-of-Mark individually. The 311-task scale and model score changes are evaluation breadth, not independent evidence of record correctness. Before reuse, verify source snapshots and rights, annotation/filter lineage, SoM release, evaluator calibration, success/failure episode retention, search/cache versions, replay locks, split policy, and formal contamination controls.
