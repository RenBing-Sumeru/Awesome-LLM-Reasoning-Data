All performance numbers are author-reported and were not independently reproduced with a pinned search environment. On the 311-task benchmark, o3 scores 15.1% without search, 36.0% with full rollout, and 37.6% with full rollout plus SoM. Gemini rises from 23.8% to 27.7% with SoM. Qwen shows a negative result: 13.5% under the image-search setting falls to 6.1% under full rollout. These results show that retrieval and localization interfaces change outcomes; they do not establish dataset quality, provenance fidelity, or trajectory reusability (Paper Table 1).

The paper isolates 23 easy items that o3 answered correctly with image-only input but incorrectly under full rollout. Among 10 sampled trajectories, 9 never invoked image search. Qwen produced 421 invalid image-search calls across 45 of 311 tasks. These observations locate tool-policy and action-format failures, but the sampled and aggregate reports do not substitute for release of every action, observation, error, retry, and final judge record (Sections 4 and 5.1).

For Gemini full rollout errors, the authors label 51.1% as “no relevant information found” and 11.5% as hallucination. The taxonomy assigns one dominant label per error, so it does not measure overlapping causes such as bad query choice, search drift, lossy Gemini summaries, wrong visual localization, and answer-judge error. The complete labeled episode corpus is unavailable (Section 5.2, Figure 6).

GPT-4o answer judgments reportedly agree completely with manual inspection, and initial GPT-4o/Gemini comparisons are described as consistent. However, the validation sample size, raw labels, disagreements, judge prompt/settings, and optional rule-based implementation are not disclosed. This supports use of the reported accuracy metric under the authors' setup, not a claim that the judge is calibrated for every acceptable answer or that it verifies step provenance.
