The primary sources are arXiv:2508.21475v3, the official ICLR 2026 Poster record, the official project, the repository inspected at commit `fd8137d43677f88c1ed8c8c75b2a22587049950c`, and the Hugging Face dataset inspected at revision `161a60d469b67cd14a0621ae2ab66d902df02df9`. The paper entry remains dated 2025 even though the verified venue is ICLR 2026.

MMSearch-Plus targets multimodal questions for which the supplied image is a clue rather than a complete answer source. Its Spatial-Temporal Extrapolation design asks for facts outside the depicted crop, frame, or moment, requiring an agent to localize an event or entity and retrieve wider web evidence. The benchmark contains 311 tasks built from public video-platform content and open-access scholarly repositories: 441 unique images, 279 unique questions, 306 unique answers, 8 primary categories, and 43 secondary categories.

The reusable public object is one image-grounded QA row with a sequence of acceptable answers, one to five images, `num_images`, a `video_url` or `arxiv_id` source reference, category, difficulty, and subtask. Hugging Face exposes all 311 rows in one split named `train`. That storage label is not a training protocol: the paper evaluates frozen models and reports no SFT, RL, distillation, reward-model, or agent-policy training on these rows.

At evaluation time, an agent additionally creates a long state-action episode over SerpAPI search, Gemini-produced summaries, crop/zoom operations, cached results, and accumulated hypotheses. Those episodes are analyzed in the paper but are not part of the public row schema. The official GitHub revision contains only `README.md` and `decrypt_after_load.py`; its pending-release checklist still names the rollout framework, evaluator, and SoM boxes, while search caches and full successful/failed trajectories are unavailable. This distinction makes the work relevant to `environment_agent_trajectory_data`: it defines a dynamic interaction surface while demonstrating why a QA table alone is not a trajectory corpus. The bilingual body reaches L4 screening depth, but accepted workflow metadata correctly remains `L3_summary_ready`.
