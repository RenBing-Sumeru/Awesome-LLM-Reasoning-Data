The central contribution is a provenance-aware multimodal search benchmark built by Spatial-Temporal Extrapolation: annotators select a video keyframe or scholarly figure/table, then ask about information beyond the visible region or moment. Candidate questions are tested on at least two closed MLLMs; no-search-solvable items are removed or obfuscated, critical regions may be blurred or masked, and items that remain trivial are discarded. Exact model pairs, prompts, outputs, rejection counts, and per-item decisions are not released.

The public data object is the 311-row QA/image/source-reference set. The paper-defined evaluation object is broader: an initial question and images feed a threaded agent state containing text/image searches, URLs and titles, Gemini webpage or related-image summaries, crops, hypotheses, and a final answer. Text search, image search, indexed zoom/crop through human-authored SoM boxes, and final answer form the action surface. The SoM boxes were human-curated and verified for the experiments, but the repository still lists them in its pending-release checklist.

The primary feedback attaches at the answer level. GPT-4o compares the final prediction with acceptable answer variants and returns binary correctness; lightweight rule matching is mentioned as optional but its implementation is not released or specified. This makes the accepted verification contract `mixed`, while the material published result remains LLM judgment. The judge does not verify whether retrieved pages support the answer, whether citations are correct, whether Gemini summaries preserve evidence, or whether each search/crop transition has valid provenance. State-action structure exists in evaluation episodes, but no step-level provenance labels or training reward are released.

Relative to MMSearch, BrowseComp-VL, and MM-BrowseComp, the concrete change is to make weak visual localization and retrieval beyond the depicted time/space jointly necessary, while preserving source references in the public rows. Live search, visual QA, LLM answer judging, cropping, and Set-of-Mark are not new separately. The direction signal is their coupling to a provenance-oriented task object—and the visible gap between that task object and the unreleased dynamic episode needed to audit the claimed provenance.
