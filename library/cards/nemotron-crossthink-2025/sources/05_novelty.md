Prior RLVR systems center on math/code where answers are naturally constrained. Nemotron-CrossThink's operational change is to make broader QA more rule-verifiable through templates, short answers, filtering, and blend design, then study source, question-format, usefulness, and difficulty choices under fixed GRPO settings. It also releases the two synthetic prompt sources used in the larger mixture.

What is not new includes GRPO, exact answer reward, format reward, MCQ/open templates, Common Crawl, and the four upstream datasets. The release is not the full paper training corpus and contains no policy trajectories. The main frontier-report value is the comparison between disclosed source counts/reward rules and missing run-level lineage, not the benchmark headline alone.
