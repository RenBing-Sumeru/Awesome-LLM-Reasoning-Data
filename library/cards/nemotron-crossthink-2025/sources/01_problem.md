The primary sources are arXiv:2504.13941v3, the NVIDIA project page, and the official Hugging Face release. RLVR works naturally when correctness is formal, but general-purpose reasoning mixes MCQ and open-ended tasks whose answer spaces, source quality, and verification rules differ. Treating them as math-style exact-answer tasks can produce brittle rewards, while math-only training may not cover law, humanities, social science, and science reasoning.

Nemotron-CrossThink addresses this as a data, formatting, reward, and blending problem. The paper trains on 588,645 prompts from six sources; the public release contains only the two synthetic components, 187,496 QA and 99,880 Math records. Released rows provide prompts and rule ground truth, not completed reasoning traces. For `frontier_reports_data_disclosure_ledger`, the important boundary is therefore between a verified public prompt release and the still-missing full blend, online rollout, reward, and checkpoint lineage.
