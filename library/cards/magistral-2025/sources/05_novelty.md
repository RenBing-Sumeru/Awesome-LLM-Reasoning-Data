The contribution for reasoning-data curation is the explicit coupling of prompt selection, online generation, executable or symbolic outcome feedback, response-shape rewards, and asynchronous optimization. Difficulty is not treated as a static property: a stronger RL-trained grader revisits the original math pool, and later Medium traces become Small supervision. This makes the report a concrete example of data and policy co-evolution.

The report is also useful because it exposes rejected choices. Partial credit proportional to passed code tests produced denser feedback but slightly worse reported LiveCodeBench performance than binary pass/fail reward, while an entropy-bonus approach was unstable across domain mixtures. These observations constrain the recipe, although they do not make the unreleased corpus reproducible.
