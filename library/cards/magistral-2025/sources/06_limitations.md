The report does not publish record identifiers, the sources of the math pool, the specific code-contest sources, final mixture weights, selected OpenThoughts/OpenR1 prompts, Medium traces, general-instruction sources, translation method, rejection logs, reward logs, or the full test suite. Small cold-start size, per-prompt trace counts after selection, full RL group size, total rollout count, Medium sampling temperature, data splits, decontamination, and train-evaluation overlap are unknown. The RL implementation and training infrastructure are described but not released as a reproducible training package.

The verifiers have bounded semantics. Symbolic and parser agreement validates a final expression rather than every reasoning step. Passing 20 sampled tests does not imply correctness on unselected inputs, and revising a test to the modal solution output can encode a shared implementation error. The paper does not calibrate fastText errors or translated-prompt quality. Outcome rewards cannot establish chain-of-thought faithfulness.

Asynchronous generation introduces a documented approximation: weights may change during a completion while its key-value cache remains from an older policy state. Queue bounds and update mechanics are described, but per-sample policy lag and its effect are not audited. Benchmark gains and ablations are model-evaluation evidence only; they cannot substitute for provenance, rights, contamination, or data-quality audits.
