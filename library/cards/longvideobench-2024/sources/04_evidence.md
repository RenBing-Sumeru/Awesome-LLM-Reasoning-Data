The paper and project page report a public benchmark with thousands of long videos and thousands of human-authored QA items, and the official artifacts expose code and Hugging Face data. The central empirical claim is that long-video understanding remains difficult for contemporary multimodal models, especially when relevant evidence is sparse across time. Row-level evidence is the multiple-choice answer key: the selected option is correct or incorrect. Evidence boundaries include video availability, sampling policy, subtitle handling, option order, and whether the model sees full video, sampled frames, transcripts, or referred-context fields. Accuracy does not by itself prove faithful temporal grounding.
