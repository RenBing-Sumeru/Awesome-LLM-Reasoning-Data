Under matched inference FLOPs, the paper reports similar average performance for the tested scaling families: Qwen2.5-1.5B Math with outcome rewards scores 35.84 on MCLM, while budget forcing on MR1-1.5B scores 35.23. The result challenges a simple hierarchy in which a visible “thinking” model is assumed to dominate candidate sampling or reward-guided selection once all compute is counted.

The stronger result is the linguistic gap. Budget forcing improves English AIME by 20 points in the reported setting but supplies only a 1.94-point average gain across the other languages; the paper says the same weak transfer pattern appears for the other tested strategies, without improved variance or consistency. This evidence is deliberately diagnostic rather than a claim that scaling never helps: it shows that gains depend on language and difficulty, and that a multilingual average can conceal both improvements and regressions for particular language–method pairs.
