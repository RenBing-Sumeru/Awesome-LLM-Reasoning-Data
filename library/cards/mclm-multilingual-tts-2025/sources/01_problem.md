Test-time scaling is usually evaluated in English, even though the same inference policy may be deployed in many reasoning languages. More candidates, a process reward model, or a longer chain can improve an English result while failing to improve a translated problem, increasing variance between languages, or consuming a different amount of compute because tokenization and generation length change. An English-only curve therefore cannot establish that the inference budget is generally effective.

This paper asks whether three common forms of additional test-time computation transfer across languages: outcome-reward-model selection, process-reward-model search, and budget forcing. The decision object is a mathematical problem expressed in one of 55 languages together with a generation, selection or continuation budget. The evaluation record keeps language, method, candidate or step count, tokens and inference FLOPs, answer, and consistency, allowing methods to be compared at matched compute rather than by unconstrained accuracy.
