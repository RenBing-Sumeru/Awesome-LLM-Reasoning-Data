The central idea is to treat language as a stress variable for inference scaling. MCLM supplies competition-level mathematical tasks in 55 languages, and the study subjects each method to an equivalent inference-FLOPs budget. Outcome reward modeling spends compute on multiple complete candidates and reranks them; process reward modeling spends it on stepwise branching and assessment; budget forcing spends it by allowing a reasoning model to generate longer. Equalizing cost prevents a method from appearing stronger merely because it ran a larger verifier or more decoding passes.

The paper also separates a method’s average gain from its linguistic reliability. A system that improves English but fails elsewhere is not linguistically general. The authors release MCLM, evaluation results, and MR1-1.5B, a multilingual reasoning model, to expose that distinction. The decisive record is thus a language-conditioned scaling trajectory: question, language, strategy and budget, generated trace or candidates, scoring path, final answer, and cross-language outcome distribution.
