The study evaluates Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and 7B configurations and a multilingual MR1-1.5B model. For outcome rewards, it generates K candidates and chooses with a reward model; for process rewards, it varies generation steps and candidates per step; for budget forcing, it constrains the reasoning-token limit of MR1. The authors estimate generator cost from model size and generated tokens, incorporate verifier cost, and compare configurations at matched inference FLOPs. This accounting is central because outcome, process, and continuation methods otherwise hide different computational surfaces.

MCLM covers multiple mathematical subsets and 55 languages. The paper also trains MR1-related variants from R1-derived thinking trajectories, including translated-data configurations, then measures accuracy, language-level variance, and Fleiss’ kappa consistency. Reproduction should preserve the exact translation and language set, generator and verifier revisions, candidate and step schedules, budget-forcing delimiters, FLOPs formula, answer parser, and whether the result is a per-language average or a pooled score.
