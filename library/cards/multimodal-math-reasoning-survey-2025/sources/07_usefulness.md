Use this survey when building a reading list or an evaluation plan for a mathematical task with visual or other non-textual inputs. Its three dimensions help specify what should be compared: benchmark choice, methodological intervention, and the challenge being targeted. It is also useful for preventing a single answer-accuracy number from hiding which stage of a multimodal pipeline failed.

Before implementing a system, turn the three dimensions into an evaluation checklist: specify the input modality, mathematical objective, benchmark, method change, and anticipated failure point. That makes a proposed comparison interpretable. The survey is an orientation and design aid, while the cited primary studies remain the sources for concrete architectures, training choices, and measured outcomes.
