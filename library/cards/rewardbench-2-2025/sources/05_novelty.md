The prior-work baseline is reward-model, rubric-judge, and preference-evaluation benchmarks. The change introduced by RewardBench 2: Advancing Reward Model Evaluation is the particular task object and feedback contract: single-turn records with prompt, chosen response list, rejected response list, counts, total completions, generating models, subset, and id. scored by five domains score whether a reward model ranks the chosen completion above three rejected completions; Ties combines correct-over-incorrect accuracy with a reward-margin calibration condition. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01937; project: https://huggingface.co/collections/allenai/reward-bench-2; code: https://github.com/allenai/reward-bench; data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/reward-bench-2; huggingface: https://huggingface.co/spaces/allenai/reward-bench; doi: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2506.01937), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
