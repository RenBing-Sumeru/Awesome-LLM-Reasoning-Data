The decisive SFT comparison holds the Llama-3-8B base family and evaluation protocol fixed while changing the instruction dataset. With 300K records, Magpie-Pro-Filtered reaches 25.08 AlpacaEval 2 length-controlled win rate and 18.9 Arena-Hard win rate, versus 9.91 and 5.4 for the 326K Tulu V2 Mix and 14.62 and 8.7 for the 652K WildChat mixture (Table 1). This supports the claim that the released Magpie recipe produces a useful instruction distribution at this scale, but teacher identity and response length remain entangled with the generation method.

The within-family comparison tests filtering more directly on the same Llama-3-8B consumer. Magpie-Pro moves from 21.65 AlpacaEval 2 length-controlled win rate and 15.9 Arena-Hard with 300K raw records to 25.08 and 18.9 with 300K filtered records, while different filter designs trade wins across benchmarks (Tables 12-13). This supports filtering as a consequential data decision, not one universally optimal threshold, and the LLM-judge benchmarks cannot certify factuality or safety record by record.
