The paper notes that structured, non-autoregressive search is less GPU-efficient than simple generation and may be less practical than brute-force majority voting. Internal agreement is vulnerable to correlated errors: frozen VLMs can be overconfident, ignore CoT cues, or generate insufficiently diverse completions. The reported STEM+B result illustrates that aggregate gains do not imply uniform domain gains. Search termination on empty or repetitive output is a heuristic, not task correctness.

The study uses one large frozen VLM, single runs, fixed iteration budgets, and no broad hyperparameter or multi-agent study. Model token probabilities, verbosity penalties, transition phrases, parser behavior, and early-exit calibration can all affect the tree. No confirmed release exposes complete trees, rejected subquestions, node scores, random seeds, prompts, or software environment. Thus the method is not currently replayable as a trace dataset, and benchmark improvements cannot serve as evidence that its intermediate reasoning is faithful or high quality.
