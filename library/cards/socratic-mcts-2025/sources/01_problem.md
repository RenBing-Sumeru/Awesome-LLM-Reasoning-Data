The official EMNLP 2025 paper asks whether a frozen, non-reasoning vision-language model can produce a longer and more useful reasoning trajectory at test time without fine-tuning, supervision, or architectural changes. Direct answering and ordinary CoT prompting may leave relevant visual or world knowledge fragmented, while token-level search is poorly aligned with the semantic decisions needed for multimodal questions. Socratic-MCTS therefore searches over which intermediate question to ask next.

The evaluation surface is a multiple-choice visual question, and the process object is a tree of subquestion–subanswer nodes, compositional rollouts, extracted answer choices, heuristic confidence weights, visit/value statistics, and exit decisions. The feedback contract is internal agreement from the same frozen model, not an external correctness verifier. The paper is a test-time search recipe and scaling study; no new training dataset or official tree/trace release was verified.
