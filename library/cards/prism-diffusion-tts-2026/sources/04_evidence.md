The paper evaluates on four mathematical-reasoning and code-generation benchmarks and three discrete diffusion language models: LLaDA 8B Instruct, Dream 7B Instruct, and LLaDA 2.0-mini. Its central comparison is with linear best-of-N style search. The reported conclusion is a performance-efficiency trade-off that matches the best-of-N result while using substantially fewer function evaluations, rather than an accuracy claim detached from inference cost.

The paper's main figure reports matched-performance speedups of 2.9 times on GSM8K, 6.5 times on MATH500, 1.8 times on HumanEval, and 1.7 times on MBPP relative to its stated repeated-search comparison. These are useful task-level results because they cover both math and code, but they measure function evaluations rather than a universal wall-clock cost. The evidence therefore supports efficient allocation under the tested model, decoding, and benchmark configurations, not a general claim about all diffusion or autoregressive systems.
