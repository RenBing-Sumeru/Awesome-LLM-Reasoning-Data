Closest repeated-sampling approaches spend a complete denoising trajectory on each candidate and normally use an external reward or verifier to choose among finished outputs. Prism changes both decisions for a discrete diffusion model: it makes partially denoised trajectories selectable search states, and it derives an intermediate feedback signal from self-evaluation. Compute can therefore be removed from a weak branch before that branch reaches a final answer.

Its additional change is local branching through partial remasking. Unlike a left-to-right tree that must extend or abandon a prefix, the method keeps high-confidence tokens and reopens only the region whose alternative is worth testing. The contribution is not a new correctness oracle: self-verification may still be wrong. Its direction signal is the explicit, inspectable coupling of a diffusion state, a self-score, a remasking decision, and a function-evaluation budget.
