GRACE is a step-level benchmark for context faithfulness. Its training split contains 6,915 trajectories and 29,612 steps with multi-judge consensus, while the test split contains 437 human-annotated trajectories from 10 models and four source datasets. Each step records a faithful/unfaithful label, one of eight error categories, and an explanation, supporting small process classifiers and step-level reward signals for reinforcement learning. Training and testing are explicitly separated: scalable labels come from judge consensus, whereas difficult evaluation examples retain human explanations, allowing scale and label reliability to be studied separately. Explanations also reference relevant context passages, enabling label auditing and interpretable verifier training. Each record links the source context, step boundaries, faithfulness judgment, one of eight errors, and an explanation. Multi-judge consensus scales training data, while difficult human labels are retained for evaluation, training, and process rewards.
