First, GRACE covers English unstructured text, two reasoning domains, and four source datasets; images, tool trajectories, and open-web evidence are outside its scope. Second, training labels come from agreement among LLM judges, but agreement does not guarantee correctness and may remove the most disputed examples; the human test set still depends on annotation guidelines. Third, public release creates contamination risk, so users should preserve hidden items and deduplicate by source question. Audits should sample cases where unanimous judges disagree with humans and report false-positive rates by error category rather than only aggregate metrics. Reuse should audit unanimously judged examples rejected by humans and report false positives by Grounding, Inference, and all eight errors; otherwise consensus filtering may systematically remove disputed hard cases.
