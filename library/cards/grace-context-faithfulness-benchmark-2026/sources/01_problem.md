Existing context-grounded QA and rule-reasoning evaluations usually detect hallucination at the response or full-CoT level. They cannot identify the first step that leaves the evidence or distinguish logical leaps, constraint violations, factual contradictions, and fabricated support. Even a correct final answer may contain unfaithful intermediate steps, so aggregate scores hide process risk. If such errors enter distillation or reinforcement learning, unsupported deductions may be recycled as positive process signals, further separating answer reliability from surface accuracy. If such steps enter distillation or reinforcement learning, unsupported deductions are recycled as positive supervision, so higher answer accuracy no longer guarantees dependence on the supplied evidence.

GRACE compares every reasoning step against the supplied context and provides a faithfulness label, error category, and natural-language explanation, with separate tracks for deductive inference and evidence grounding.
