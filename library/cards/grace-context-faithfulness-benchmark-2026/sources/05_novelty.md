Resources such as RAGTruth annotate hallucinated spans in responses, while methods such as VersaPRM judge whether a step is generally correct, but neither asks whether the step is supported by the current context. GRACE changes the feedback contract: every step must be grounded in evidence or be a valid deduction from it, with failures explained through a data-driven taxonomy. Its novelty is the combination of context faithfulness, step-level labels, and a difficult human test set. A logically valid step that goes beyond the source is therefore still labeled unfaithful, preventing general correctness from being mistaken for contextual reliability. LLM judges and step classifiers are not new. The actual change is replacing general correctness with a contract requiring support or valid deduction from the current context, together with data-driven error explanations.
