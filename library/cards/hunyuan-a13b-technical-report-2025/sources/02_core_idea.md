The disclosure is best read as four linked but distinct data contracts.

1. **Reasoning SFT.** Mathematics is drawn from educational materials and checked with a generative reward model plus automated solution verification. Code originates from open-source resources and passes a construction pipeline with critics and sandbox execution. Logic combines copyrighted and public puzzle collections with synthetic problems, automated chain-of-thought assessment, and human review. Science is filtered for difficulty and quality, verified by an advanced LLM, and selected through rejection sampling.
2. **Reasoning RL.** A 150,000-problem pool mixes mathematics, coding, logic, and science at 2:2:1:1. Ten percent overlaps SFT and 90% is described as novel. GRPO receives binary-style outcome signals from normalized answer/reference matching or code sandbox tests.
3. **All-scenarios SFT.** The mixture expands to language, writing, multilingual, instruction-following, role-play, knowledge QA, multi-turn, and agent tasks. Agent traces are synthesized by user, planner, tool, agent, and checker roles over real sandbox tools, MCP servers, and synthetic tools.
4. **All-scenarios RL.** A general reward model and more than 30 scoring services cover 16 subtopics; agent scoring combines format and reference-based tool-call correctness.

The resulting model supports /think and /no_think modes, with reasoning represented in a special think block. The report does not disclose the internal serialized record schema.
