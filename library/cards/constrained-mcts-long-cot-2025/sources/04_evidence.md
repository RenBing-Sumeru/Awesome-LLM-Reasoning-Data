Table 1 reports mean accuracy across five math datasets of 83.4 for full CMCTS, 82.5 for CMCTS-PRM, 78.7 for CMCTS-RULE, 77.2 for Native-MCTS+PRM, 76.8 for Native-MCTS, and 78.6 for Qwen2.5 72B CoT. Ablations compare action subsets, PRM, and partial-order rules, and the paper visualizes sampled states with jina-embeddings-v3 and UMAP to argue that constrained actions increase diversity. These are method-evaluation results under the authors' implementation and benchmark parsing.

The evidence supports that the combined search configuration improved the reported benchmark metrics relative to the studied baselines and that removing components changes performance. It does not prove PRM calibration, correctness of intermediate steps, completeness of the four action sets, or quality of released tree data. The paper itself acknowledges that action-set completeness is neither theoretically nor experimentally established and that evaluation is limited to mathematics. The official arXiv source is https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11169, and the paper-linked repository is https://github.com/pass-lin/CMCTS; the latter should be pinned and inspected before any completeness claim.
