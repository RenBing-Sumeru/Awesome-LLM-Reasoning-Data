
The strongest evidence is the agreement among independent official artifacts. arXiv v2 describes the complete stage order and feedback functions. NVIDIA's final model cards identify Qwen3-8B-Base and Qwen3-14B-Base as the starting weights. The intermediate-checkpoint card names SFT, RLHF, IF-RL, Math-RL, and Code-RL checkpoints and states that the final 8B follows SWE RL. The RM card states that Qwen2.5-CascadeRL-RM-72B is initialized from Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, trained with Bradley-Terry preference learning, and used in the family's RLHF stage.

The data release is also concrete. The official Stage-1 card reports 5,436,618 samples across math, code, science, and general configurations. The Stage-2 card reports 7,797,730 samples across those domains plus tool calling, instruction following, and three SWE tasks. RL-Math reports 14,476 problems; RL-RLHF reports 45,882 prompts; RM-Training reports 81,808 records; RL-SWE publishes prompt, source, instance, golden-patch, and localization fields and explicitly states that SWE RL uses no Docker execution. These are artifact facts, not proof that every record entered every reported run.

The paper's stage ablations are behavioral evidence. For the unified 8B model, reported LiveCodeBench v6 moves from 56.7 after SFT to 67.2 after RLHF and 71.5 after Code RL, then 71.1 after SWE RL; SWE-bench Verified moves from 26.1 after SFT to 37.2 after the final stage. The 14B final model reports 74.6 on LiveCodeBench v6 and 43.1 on SWE-bench Verified. These tables support claims about the trained checkpoints under the paper's evaluation setup, not the correctness, provenance, licensing, or contamination status of the training data.

Evidence remains partial at the release-accounting layer. Current collection viewer counts for Stage-1 and Stage-2 are lower than their dataset-card totals, and the public collection does not visibly supply dedicated IF-RL or Code-RL datasets. The report's statement that the full training-data collection is released therefore needs an immutable file/count/stage crosswalk before it can be treated as an end-to-end reproducibility claim.
