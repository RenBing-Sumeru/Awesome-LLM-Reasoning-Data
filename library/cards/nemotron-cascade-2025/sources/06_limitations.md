
No stage has a perfect verifier. The scalar RM is a preference proxy; the paper notes that the highest RewardBench model need not yield the best aligned policy and that out-of-distribution prompts can destabilize RLHF. The IF verifier checks explicit constraints but not overall usefulness, and the authors report degradation from naive use. Math reward checks an extracted endpoint rather than every reasoning step. Code reward inherits test incompleteness and execution nondeterminism. SWE reward is explicitly execution-free, so an alternative correct patch may look dissimilar to the human patch, while a similar patch may still fail repository tests.

Release coverage is incomplete relative to the described pipeline. The checked collection contains RLHF, Math, and SWE RL inputs but no visibly dedicated IF-RL or Code-RL datasets. The Stage-1 and Stage-2 dataset cards report 5,436,618 and 7,797,730 samples, while current viewer counts are lower. Upsampling, file revisions, viewer processing, and model-run membership may explain some differences, but an official reconciliation is unknown. Collection membership alone cannot establish exact use, mixture weight, or snapshot.

Lineage stops before the online learning record. The release does not provide complete policy rollout groups, per-component rewards, log probabilities, dynamic-filter decisions, rejected candidates, seeds, logs, optimizer states, or a record-to-checkpoint manifest. The report names verl but does not pin the exact commit and complete stage configurations. Reproducing the paper therefore requires substantial reconstruction even though intermediate weights are available.

Decontamination and rights evidence are bounded. The paper describes 9-gram filtering for math/code, URL matching for Code RL, and repository-plus-commit exclusion for SWE; exact benchmark revisions and removal ledgers are unknown, and general, instruction, safety, and tool-calling data lack an equivalent corpus-wide audit. Dataset cards declare CC BY 4.0, but upstream attribution, repository-code rights, and generated-response model terms still require record-level review.

Finally, the sequential curriculum is not a guarantee against forgetting. The paper acknowledges that conflicting rewards can overwrite earlier behavior, and its own stage tables contain small regressions alongside gains. Those benchmark changes characterize model behavior under the reported setup; they do not validate dataset quality, verifier calibration, or the release's legal and provenance chain.
