For the Data Construction & Open Release Recipes track, this paper is a compact case study in building process supervision when no correctness verifier is available. It specifies a source mixture, a generated data object, a predictive filtering signal, source-specific thresholds, an SFT target, and a downstream search consumer. Those layers make it useful for comparing weakly verified recipes with datasets filtered by executors, symbolic solvers, or expert process labels.

Practitioners can reuse the conceptual schema—source, preceding context, rationale, following context, baseline score, conditioned score, threshold, and decision—but should add what the public artifact omits: immutable source IDs, prompt/model revisions, filter scores, source-level licenses, retained-index manifests, and independent rationale-validity checks. The paper's human threshold calibration is a starting point, not a substitute for auditing the much larger Pile component.

The inference design is also relevant to test-time compute: a short learned rationale can act as latent information for ranking candidate steps without being injected directly into the agent's visible trajectory. The implicit-versus-explicit ablation suggests a useful hypothesis about robustness to noisy rationales, while the lack of a correctness verifier makes clear that search can still amplify a biased likelihood proxy.

Reuse should therefore separate three claims: the recipe is documented, the reported experiments show downstream gains, and the complete training corpus is openly reproducible. The first two have paper evidence; the third remains unsupported by the current 15,178-row release and repository state.
