Process supervision usually requires a step-level judge, expert annotations, or a task environment that can check intermediate actions. These signals are costly and are often unavailable for ordinary web text. Yet explanatory writing and worked solutions contain transitions where an unstated motive or inference could help predict what comes next. The paper asks whether those latent rationales can be inserted automatically and selected without gold process labels.

That question has two distinct parts. The construction problem is to turn preceding text, an inserted rationale, and following text into a reusable training object. The deployment problem is to use the learned rationale generator to guide an agent's next reasoning step. RATIONALYST connects them through a predictive proxy: a rationale is useful when conditioning on it makes the observed continuation more likely. This is scalable, but it is weaker than verifying that the rationale is logically sound.
