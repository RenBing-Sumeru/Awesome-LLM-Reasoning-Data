The main experiment compares implicit RATIONALYST supervision with the Llama-3-8B-Instruct baseline on seven exact-match benchmarks. The reported scores are 77.6→81.6 on GSM8K, 28.0→32.5 on MATH, 72.6→75.2 on ECQA, 58.2→60.3 on HellaSwag, 86.4→90.7 on ProofWriter, 77.6→80.7 on ARC-Challenge, and 39.6→45.3 on MMLU-Pro. The reported mean gain is 3.9 points.

The evaluation covers GSM8K test (1,319), MATH test (5,000), ECQA validation (17,944), HellaSwag validation (10,000), ProofWriter depth-greater-than-5 test (600), ARC-Challenge test (1,172), and MMLU-Pro test (12,000). Removing Pile rationales lowers all seven reported scores, with drops from 0.7 on ECQA to 4.1 on MMLU-Pro. This supports a contribution from the data mixture under the paper's setup, but it does not isolate rationale truth from additional training, source-domain cues, or the search procedure.

For task-specific training only, Table 4 reports implicit versus explicit guidance of 80.3 versus 77.5 on GSM8K and 74.5 versus 72.2 on ECQA. The authors interpret implicit likelihood scoring as more robust to imperfect rationales than directly exposing them to the agent. Table 5 also compares process and outcome verifiers, but contains an unresolved ECQA inconsistency: it gives full RATIONALYST 76.2, whereas Table 2 and the official model card give 75.2.

Table 1 supplies unusually useful construction evidence: candidate counts, retention percentages, and thresholds for all eight source groups. However, the release evidence is weaker. The official dataset viewer shows 15,178 rows in one train split with three fields, while the paper reports roughly 79K retained rationales. Without a manifest that reconciles those quantities, the experiments support the method's reported utility but not complete public availability or per-record data quality.
