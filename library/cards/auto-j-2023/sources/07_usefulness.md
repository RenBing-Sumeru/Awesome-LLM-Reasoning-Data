Auto-J is useful as a schema for judge datasets: preserve query, candidate responses, evaluation protocol, scenario ID, criteria version, teacher source, final judgment, critique text, parser status, and whether a sample was used for training or testing. It can inform reward-model or evaluator audits when explanations are needed alongside scores. For atlas use, keep evaluation-only claims separate from training-use claims: the paper trains a judge, but downstream reuse as a reward model needs a new calibration and bias audit. It is also a compact comparison point for generative judges that must support multiple protocols.
