Audio benchmarks typically measure ASR, classification, or single-turn understanding, while text-based multi-turn benchmarks discard acoustic information. This work treats spoken editing as a distinct capability and introduces memory tasks solvable only through background sounds, prosody, or nonverbal cues. Rubric judging itself is not new; the novelty lies in combining audio-native multi-turn failure modes, naturally disfluent human speech, and instance-specific evaluation contracts.
