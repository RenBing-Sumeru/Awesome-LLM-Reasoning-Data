The official ICLR paper supplies theoretical characterizations of the three regimes and reports empirical results with Qwen, Llama, and Gemma models. The experimental role is to test whether the predicted interaction between generator coverage, verifier behavior, and sampling sub-optimality appears in realistic language-model systems, rather than assuming a perfect verifier or reducing performance to pass-at-k alone.

The evidence is most useful as a diagnostic lens for interpreting a scaling curve. If a system plateaus after more candidates, the framework asks whether coverage has already saturated, the verifier’s effective region is too narrow, or the sampling algorithm is the bottleneck. The paper does not establish a universal numerical threshold for all tasks; readers should inspect its assumptions and empirical settings before mapping its regimes onto a particular reward model, evaluator, or serving budget.
