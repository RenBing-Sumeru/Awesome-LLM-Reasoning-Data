Verifier-guided inference commonly follows a simple recipe: sample candidate answers, score them, and return the candidate preferred by a verifier. In practice, performance then depends on at least three coupled factors: whether the generator ever covers a good answer, where the imperfect verifier is reliable, and how efficiently the sampling policy uses its limited trials. Existing discussions often isolate one factor and consequently make extra sampling sound uniformly beneficial.

This paper asks for a unified account of those interactions. It studies when additional inference-time computation should improve a verifier-based system, when it can have little effect, and when broader generation can increase the chance of selecting an undesirable answer. The target is not a new prompt recipe but a decision framework that makes the generator, verifier, and sampling algorithm jointly visible.
