This work’s novelty is to make an imperfect verifier a first-class object in a test-time scaling theory. It does not assume that the verifier merely adds noise around an otherwise fixed pass-at-k curve. Instead, the verifier’s region of convergence is modeled together with what the generator covers and how the sampler moves through that space, so poor outcomes can be attributed to a specific interaction rather than to “insufficient compute” in general.

The paper also distinguishes sequential from batched sampling in the same framework. That distinction matters operationally because the two procedures consume computation differently and may respond differently to verifier imperfections. The contribution is therefore a joint geometry for deciding whether to expand candidate coverage, improve verification, or replace the sampling procedure, rather than a single empirical recipe for more samples.
