The authors formalize a proposal distribution from the reference language model and a target distribution accessible only through an approximate verifier. They use optimal transport to characterize how coverage, verifier behavior, and the sampling procedure interact. The analysis considers both sequential and batched sampling algorithms, explicitly connecting their computational structures to the quality of the transported, verifier-selected output distribution.

The resulting analysis identifies a transport regime, a policy-improvement regime, and a saturation regime in the sub-optimality–coverage relationship. The work then corroborates the theory empirically with Qwen, Llama, and Gemma models. In this card’s terms, the operational records are candidate answers, verifier assessments, coverage under a sampling budget, and the selection rule—not an additional fine-tuning dataset or parameter update.
