The paper recasts verification-guided inference as a transport problem. A generator defines a proposal distribution over possible answers, while an ideal target distribution represents the outcomes one would want to sample. The only practical access to that target is through an approximately correct verifier. This framing turns vague notions such as “better verification” or “more coverage” into quantities whose interaction can be reasoned about.

Its central lens has three parts: generator coverage, the verifier’s region of convergence, and sampling sub-optimality. Coverage says what useful material the generator can produce; the region of convergence describes where verifier guidance moves choices toward desirable outcomes; sub-optimality captures the price of the sampling rule. Their combination predicts three qualitative regimes rather than one universal scaling curve.
