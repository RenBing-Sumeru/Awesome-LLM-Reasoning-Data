Direct long-CoT distillation normally treats each correct teacher response as one SFT target, and generic long-to-short work often optimizes length without exposing which internal branches were removed. DLCoT's distinctive contribution is to make approaches, verifications, clusters, redundancy, and corrected-error branches explicit construction variables, then compare deletion policies across teacher/student families. Its result argues against equating the shortest clean-looking trace with the best supervision: deleting repeated members can help, while deleting nonredundant erroneous paths can damage self-correction structure. Final-answer filtering, teacher distillation, clustering, and SFT are not individually new, and the paper does not establish a general verifier or universal compression policy beyond the tested mathematical models and datasets.
