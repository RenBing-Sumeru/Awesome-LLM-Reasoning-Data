DLCoT treats a long teacher response as a structured trace rather than an indivisible demonstration. It separates broad reasoning regions, further decomposes approach exploration and verification, labels candidate approaches as correct, incomplete, or erroneous while also flagging calculation/derivation errors, clusters similar approaches, removes redundant members while retaining at least one approach per cluster, and minimally reconstructs coherence before student SFT. The feedback contract is mixed: rule-based final-answer validation controls entry to the distillation pool, while LLM judgments and similarity checks control decomposition, labeling, clustering, and editing. The public artifact boundary matters: SODE supplies processing code and a Step 1 inference input, whereas users must run the intervening LLM calls themselves to obtain optimized training data; no completed final corpus or retained-row manifest was verified.
