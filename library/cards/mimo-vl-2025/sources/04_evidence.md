The report supports the recipe with ablations and a broad evaluation suite, but these results should be read as model evidence, not direct proof that every retained training item is high quality. The clearest data-linked result is the Stage-4 comparison: the checkpoint trained with a larger reasoning share and long-form reasoning performs better on difficult multimodal and text reasoning than the earlier checkpoint. The discussion reports gains across the tested synthetic-reasoning scaling comparisons without observed saturation, but does not establish a fully controlled single-variable causal estimate.

For post-training, MORL improves many reasoning, perception, and grounding evaluations over the SFT checkpoint, while the authors explicitly document interference among task families. Their comparison of fully on-policy training with vanilla GRPO reports better stability and exploration for the chosen setup. The repository releases SFT and RL checkpoints plus an evaluation framework with prompts and protocols, enabling capability checks. It does not release the training examples, reward models, verifier service, sampling manifests, or per-example acceptance records, so independent reconstruction of the data pipeline remains unavailable.
