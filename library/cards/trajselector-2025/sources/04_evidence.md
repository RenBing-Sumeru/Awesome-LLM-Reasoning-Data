The main Best-of-32 table covers AMC23, AIME24, AIME25, BeyondAIME, HMMT25, and BRUMO25. Averaged across those six benchmarks, TrajSelector scores 58.78%, compared with 54.17% for majority voting, 54.47% for the strongest listed PRM average (EurusPRM), 46.44% for random selection, and 71.83% for the oracle Pass@32 upper bound. Best-of-16 averages are 55.81% for TrajSelector and 53.06% for majority voting. Appendices report continued gains at N=48 and 64 and with Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-14B samplers.

The loss ablation favors the three-class buffer objective over binary classification. A separate offline data-selection experiment selects 1,000 examples from OpenThoughts-114K, fine-tunes Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct under the s1 recipe, and reports higher downstream scores for the TrajSelector-selected subset than random, s1K, and the listed PRM selectors across MATH500, AIME24, AIME25, and GPQA.

These results support the selector under the reported setup; they do not by themselves prove that its step labels are correct or that the constructed corpus is broadly high quality. The abstract says five benchmarks even though Section 5.1 and the tables enumerate six.
