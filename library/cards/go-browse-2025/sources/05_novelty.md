The nearest data-collection baselines divide into interaction-first methods such as NNetNav, whose exploration episodes do not share state, and instruction-first methods, which propose tasks from a limited observation before solving them. Go-Browse changes the collection substrate by retaining discovered URLs and shortest trajectory prefixes in a graph, resetting exploration to frontier pages, and separating global navigation discovery from local task execution. It also turns task proposal into an interactive agent behavior and explicitly collects both prefixed and root-start solver rollouts after VLM-based feasibility filtering. For this atlas, the direction signal is the coupled record of search state, proposed tasks, judgment, environment actions, and downstream training—not the claim that graph exploration alone guarantees high-quality data. Resetting to known pages resembles Go-Explore and VLM filtering is established practice; the reusable novelty lies in their web-data pipeline composition. A reuse audit should still verify exact prompt versions, graph/prefix fields, negative trajectories, Hub revisions, rights, and whether closed-model judgments can be reproduced.
