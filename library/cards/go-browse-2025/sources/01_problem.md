The official ICLR 2026 paper and arXiv v2 address a data-collection problem rather than a new browser benchmark: web agents need environment-specific demonstrations, yet human traces are costly and independent unsupervised exploration episodes repeatedly visit easy pages. Go-Browse therefore treats a website as an incrementally discovered URL graph and collects tasks and ReAct browser episodes while reusing paths to previously found pages. The resulting Go-Browse-WA object is not just a list of successful answers: the paper reports 26,749 trajectories over 3,422 tasks and 196,462 interaction steps from 100 URLs in five self-hosted WebArena clone domains, with successful and failed episodes, thoughts, browser actions, observations, rewards, graph/page metadata, and—in the raw release—screenshots, HTML-derived data, and full accessibility trees. This makes the work relevant both to rollout/search trace curation and to environment/agent trajectory data; L4 review should judge the released fields, feedback contract, rights, and reproducibility boundary, not the reported benchmark score alone.
