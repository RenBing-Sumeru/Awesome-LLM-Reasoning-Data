Systematic experiments show that stronger MLLMs can reproduce human preferences reasonably well in pairwise comparison but degrade in score calibration and batch ranking. Hallucinations and fine-grained mistakes in HARD widen the gap, while swapping response order changes some verdicts and judge explanations may cite nonexistent visual evidence. The combination of multiple protocols and difficult subsets supports the conclusion that multimodal judging cannot be summarized by one pairwise accuracy score, although dataset scale and the evaluated model generation limit extrapolation to newer systems.
