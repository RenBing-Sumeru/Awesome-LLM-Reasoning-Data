The paper constructs approximately 4,414 image-text evaluation instances and organizes them into HQ and HARD preference sets. HQ contains responses with clearer overall quality differences, while HARD emphasizes difficult cases such as visual hallucinations. It defines three unified tasks—Scoring Evaluation, Pair Comparison, and Batch Ranking—and compares proprietary and open MLLMs. Pairwise comparison aligns best with humans, whereas absolute scoring and multi-candidate ranking are less stable, showing that the claim “MLLMs can serve as judges” is protocol-dependent.
