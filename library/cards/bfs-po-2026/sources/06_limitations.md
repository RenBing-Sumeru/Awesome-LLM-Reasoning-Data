The terminal verifier parses a final answer and uses string matching, so equivalent formatting may be rejected and a correct answer can be accepted despite invalid intermediate reasoning. Minimum-length selection can remove explanatory or robustness-relevant steps, while maximum token entropy is only a heuristic for a semantically useful fork. Algorithm 1 requires at least one correct complete path before expansion; the paper inherits DAPO's nontrivial-group constraint but does not separately document operational handling of an initially all-wrong group. Experiments are limited to mathematics/STEM and 3B–8B models. The paper does not disclose a reusable per-question rollout count, training temperature, immutable prompt/tree manifest, seeds for every reported run, decontamination procedure, or artifact license. Most importantly, the linked repository remains a placeholder: implementation, models, checkpoints, generated trees, rejected branches, verifier outputs, and exact environments are unavailable.
