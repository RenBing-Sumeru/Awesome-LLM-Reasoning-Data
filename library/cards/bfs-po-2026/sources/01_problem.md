Long chains of thought raise inference cost and may contain redundant language or reasoning detours. Existing shortening approaches often train on precomputed concise chains or add a length penalty, but the paper argues that supervised shortening can reduce accuracy and that ordinary GRPO/DAPO samples complete answers independently rather than using the best answer found so far to guide exploration. BFS-PO addresses the training problem: given a mathematical question and ground-truth answer, how can an online policy search for progressively shorter correct complete paths without a Process Reward Model? For this Card's assigned Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data category, the primary data object is the training-time search tree—complete policy rollouts, selected paths, backtracking tokens, expansion branches, terminal rewards, and derived branch advantages—not merely the final answer. The method does not use tree search at inference time.
