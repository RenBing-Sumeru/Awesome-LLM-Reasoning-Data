The test-generation evidence is conditional. On the Table 1 comparison, the mixed description/code setup produces 92 instances with F2P tests, compared with 73 for Llama, 81 for Mistral, and 83 for Qwen in that experiment; this is not an independent audit of all released tests. In the downstream comparison, 900-trajectory RFT reports 15.87% for SWE-Dev versus 15.80% for Nebius, and 500-trajectory RFT reports 15.00% versus 14.87%; each configuration is independently run three times. These results evaluate a particular 7B setup and do not certify individual records. (Paper Tables 1 and 3.)

For training methods, the 7B experiment reports 16.2% with 1,000 RFT trajectories and 18.0% with 2,000; with 2,300 trajectories RFT reaches 21.2% and is reported above the RFT+OREO and OREO-only configurations. KTO and OREO reach 17.2% and 17.0%, respectively, at 1,800 trajectories. The authors' interpretation is that RFT gives the strongest result under their setup, not that offline RL is ineffective in general. (Paper §4.3, Figs. 4–5.)

On SWE-bench-Verified, the paper reports 23.4% for SWE-Dev-7B and 36.6% for SWE-Dev-32B. Under iteration scaling, 7B rises from 21.8% at 30 rounds to 23.4% at 75; 32B rises from 34.0% to 36.6%. The benchmark has 500 human-validated instances according to the paper, and evaluation uses developer-written unit tests. These are downstream model results under the stated scaffold and budgets, not a direct measure of dataset correctness, record diversity, license fitness, or freedom from overlap. (Paper §4.1, Table 4, Fig. 7.)
