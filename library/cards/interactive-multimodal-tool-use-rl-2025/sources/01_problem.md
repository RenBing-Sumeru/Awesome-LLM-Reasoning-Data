The primary source is arXiv:2509.14480 v1, first submitted on 17 September 2025. The official OpenReview record says “Submitted to ICLR 2026”; it does not establish acceptance or proceedings publication. The paper asks how to train an interactive tool-use policy when a binary terminal outcome is too sparse to identify which conversational turn caused success or failure, especially when user responses may be speech rather than text (paper §1; §2.2; OpenReview record).

Its experimental substrate is a retail sandbox derived from tau-bench. One online episode is `tau=(x1,e1,...,xT,eT)`: agent token sequences contain reasoning and tool calls, while environment sequences contain tool results or simulated-user responses, with speech tokens or placeholders in multimodal episodes. A rollout stops when the user simulator emits `##STOP##` or after 30 interaction steps. About 3,000 synthetic retail tasks supply a user-simulator instruction and ground-truth tool calls (paper §2.2; §4.1; Appendix “Training Data”).

This belongs in `environment_agent_trajectory_data` because the trainable object is an executable, state-changing episode rather than a static answer. It does not claim a released preference dataset, and it does not solve general multimodal perception: speech is used for user turns inside a tool-use workflow. The Card is content-complete for L4 review from the full paper and appendix, but the promised sandbox, tasks, rollouts, and checkpoints were not found as released paper-specific artifacts; direct training reuse therefore remains blocked.
