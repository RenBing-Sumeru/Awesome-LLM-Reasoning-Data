The contribution is Turn-level Adjudicated Reinforcement Learning (TARL): combine a rule-verified terminal outcome with GPT-4.1 judgments attached to individual interaction turns, then aggregate both sources into the scalar used by GRPO or PPO (paper §3.2).

The data and feedback object joins the policy, hidden user-task instruction, ground-truth function-call trajectory, full labeled conversation, per-turn judge scores, terminal verifier result, and final trajectory reward. GPT-4.1 assigns each turn `-1`, `0`, or `1`: `-1` marks the primary major deviation and may occur at most once per trajectory, `0` marks an issue or downstream by-product, and `1` marks a correct turn. The terminal verifier returns 1 only when the required successful database-changing calls and their arguments completely match the ground truth; otherwise it returns 0 (paper §2.1; §3.2; Appendix “LLM Judge Setup for Turn-Level Evaluation”).

The feedback contract is mixed. The rule verifier observes successful database writes and argument equality, but the phrasing-sensitive expected-output check from tau-bench is excluded from primary RL and evaluation. The judge observes task intent, ground-truth calls, policy identity, and the complete conversation, but its score is model judgment rather than a programmatic proof. TARL retains the process provenance of these labels, yet the stable variant aggregates them into one episode score applied uniformly to policy tokens while masking environment tokens (paper §2.1; §2.2; §3.2; §5.1).

Compared with terminal-only tau-bench RL, TARL adds localized adjudication; compared with direct per-turn reward placement, it deliberately returns to trajectory-level credit because direct placement was unstable. Read it alongside `verltool-holistic-agentic-rl-with-tool-use-2025` for broader agentic-RL tooling, while keeping this paper's distinctive turn-judge/terminal-rule contract separate.
