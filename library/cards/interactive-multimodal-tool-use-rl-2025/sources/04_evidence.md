On tau-bench retail, Table `tab::main_results` reports pass^1 of 42.6 for base Qwen3-8B, 51.3 for Qwen3-8B + GRPO, and 57.4 for `+Math+TARL`; the last comparison is 6.1 percentage points above GRPO under the reported setup (paper §4.2). These are author-reported task-success results using the primary verifier without tau-bench's expected-output check.

The multimodal table reports that base Qwen2.5-Omni-7B obtains 7.8 text pass^1 and 14.8 speech pass^1, while `+GRPO+Math+TARL` obtains 36.5 and 37.4. A policy trained without speech obtains 32.2 on speech evaluation versus 37.4 for mixed speech-text training; the authors interpret this gap as evidence that text-only fine-tuning erodes speech capability (paper §4.3–§4.4, Table `tab::multimodal_results`).

The reward-granularity analysis reports that trajectory-level aggregation improves pass^1 by 4.3 points over vanilla PPO, whereas direct turn-level reward assignment performs worse and destabilizes training (paper §5.1, Fig. `reward_granularity`; Table 2). An online-intervention variant allowing up to two judge interventions per reasoning step increases KL, destabilizes optimization, and does not improve performance (paper §5.2, Fig. `intervention_comparison`).

The negative generalization result is equally important: policies trained only on about 3,000 retail tasks deteriorate faster on airline evaluation as `k` increases, and the authors conclude that learned exploration does not transfer effectively (paper §2.3, Table `tab::baseline_rl_results`). These results show behavior under the paper's sandbox, reward, curriculum, and evaluation boundaries; they do not establish that the unreleased tasks or rollouts are high-quality reusable data, and no independent reproduction is reported.
