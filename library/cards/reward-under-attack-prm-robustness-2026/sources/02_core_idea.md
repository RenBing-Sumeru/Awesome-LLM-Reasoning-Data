The central artifact is PRM-BiasBench, containing thousands of controlled perturbation pairs with attack types, correctness labels, and PRM scores. Each record organizes a task input, candidate response, reference evidence or evaluation condition, and a feedback label, together with fields needed to trace provenance and labeling. The authors use static perturbations, adversarial optimization, and full reinforcement-learning attacks for quality control, making the resource a feedback dataset for training, evaluation, or auditing rather than a generic question-answer collection. Its primary use is auditing PRM robustness and reward hacking before deployment.
