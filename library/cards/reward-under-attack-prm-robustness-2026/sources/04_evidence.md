The paper tests its central claim through controlled comparison with existing reward models, general judges, rule-based verifiers, or benchmark methods. The most representative result is that RL produces PRM scores above 0.9 while true accuracy remains below 4%. This indicates that PRM-BiasBench exposes capability gaps missed by ordinary evaluation or provides a more useful feedback signal. Because results still depend on base models, candidate generators, judges, and task distributions, the evidence supports utility under the tested setting rather than universal effectiveness.
