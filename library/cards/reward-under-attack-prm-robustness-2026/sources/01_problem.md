A PRM that is accurate on a fixed test set may become unreliable when a policy actively maximizes it. The policy can raise reward by lengthening reasoning, emphasizing formatting, or imitating high-scoring phrases while mathematical accuracy declines, a form of reward hacking invisible to ordinary offline evaluation.

Reward Under Attack builds PRM-BiasBench and uses controlled perturbations, adversarial optimization, and full reinforcement-learning attacks to test whether reward becomes detached from true correctness.
