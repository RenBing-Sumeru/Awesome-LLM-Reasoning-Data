Experiments show that Hercule aligns more closely with RECON human judgments than several proprietary models and conventional automatic metrics, while retaining some zero-shot effectiveness on unseen languages. This suggests that combining target-language responses with English references is a viable compromise for low-resource evaluation, avoiding the need to build complete references for every language. Generality is tested across multiple tasks rather than translation alone. The evidence remains constrained by the 500-example scale, six-language coverage, and annotator composition, and high aggregate correlation does not guarantee reliable explanations for every individual case.
