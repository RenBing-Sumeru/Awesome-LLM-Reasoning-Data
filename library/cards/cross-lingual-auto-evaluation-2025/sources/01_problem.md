Multilingual generation evaluation faces two gaps: high-quality human assessment is expensive, and existing LLM judges are primarily trained for English, while low-resource languages lack native reference answers and stable evaluation data. Translating responses into English before scoring can lose grammatical, cultural, and task-specific details, whereas using a general judge directly introduces language-proficiency bias. The paper therefore investigates whether English reference answers can support cross-lingual evaluation of target-language responses and constructs a six-language test set with human judgments to measure whether evaluators truly align with people.
