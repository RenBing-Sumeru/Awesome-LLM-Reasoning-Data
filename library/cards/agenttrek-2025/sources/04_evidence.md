Official ICLR records confirm Spotlight status. Table 1 reports 10,398 successful trajectories with an average of 12.1 steps across 127 websites. The paper reports downstream gains: on WebArena, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with AgentTrek reaches 10.46 task success and the 32B model reaches 22.40; on ScreenSpot Web, Qwen2-VL-7B with AgentTrek reaches a 67.4 average versus 30.7 for the listed base model (Tables 4–5). These results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced for this Card.

The scale statements do not reconcile. Dividing 10,398 successes by 23,430 tutorials gives 44.38%, whereas Appendix C uses a 39.9% replay-success rate to derive $550.75 per 1,000 verified trajectories. Section 4 also says “nearly 5,000 verified trajectories,” while Appendix I again states more than 10,000. The 10,398 headline is repeated most clearly, but none of these figures should be silently substituted for another.

The current official HF dataset is public and ungated at revision `32aabe6fb48d8e2e7dae1678e6ff05ba23725b2c`. It has one default configuration, one train split, 52,594 rows, one `messages` column, one 1,808,828,438-byte JSON file, and a 430,739,136-byte auto-converted Parquet. The card calls them dialogue turns. Viewer examples are text-only system/user/assistant SFT messages containing task, current AXTree, history, action space, named think and memory fields, and one action.

The 52,594 turns cannot be equated with 10,398 episodes. No manifest maps them to trajectory IDs, tutorial/source URLs, websites, task categories, replay dates, evaluator outcomes, failure reasons, the 6,000 text-training subset, the 10,000 vision-training subset, or the model. Screenshots, videos, DOM/HTML, Playwright/network traces, and the full multimodal serialization are absent from the checked HF payload.

Release evidence also stops short of construction reproducibility. The checked code head has three commits, no tag, no GitHub Release, and no detected license. The HF dataset declares no license. The paper shows that failures existed—Appendix H gives an expired-tutorial example—but no failed corpus or decision ledger is public.
