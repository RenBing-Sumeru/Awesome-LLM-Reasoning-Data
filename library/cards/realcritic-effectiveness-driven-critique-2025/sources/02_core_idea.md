The paper builds a closed-loop benchmark of 2,093 examples across eight reasoning tasks. Each example contains a problem, initial answer, critique, and a revised answer generated from that critique. The primary metric is not textual similarity to a reference critique but improvement in corrected-answer accuracy. Comparisons across critic-solver combinations show that self-critique and iterative critique from conventional LLMs often fail to help or even harm answers, whereas reasoning-model critiques are more effective, demonstrating that critique evaluation must be grounded in downstream outcomes.
