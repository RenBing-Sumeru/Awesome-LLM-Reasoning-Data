Dynamic mobile benchmarks such as AndroidWorld, LlamaTouch, and Mobile-Env already use online interaction and state-based evaluation; SPA-Bench already broadens app coverage, and GUI-Robust/D-GARA already study anomalies. MobileBench-OL's concrete change is to assemble these concerns around physical phones, 80 Chinese apps, logged-in real-network operation, and five distinct capability slices rather than presenting another static screenshot/action corpus.

The task design separates three often-conflated factors. Base and Long-Tail test common versus less familiar app interfaces; Long-Horizon fixes hard tasks by at least 20 golden steps; GUI-Reasoning scores exploration requirements such as icon understanding, hidden-function discovery, and hierarchical navigation; Noise-Robust perturbs execution with four random disturbance types. This decomposition is a useful direction signal, but the subsets overlap in apps and tasks, so their counts are not independent evidence of coverage.

The second change is a full feedback-and-reset pipeline. Experts derive path-tolerant XPath-like rules from successful traces, validate them on mixed human/UI-TARS-1.5 trajectories, and couple them to explicit termination semantics. A separate inverse-task agent attempts to restore mutable app state between rounds. This makes verifier false positives, false negatives, reset failures, and version drift visible parts of the benchmark contract rather than hidden implementation details.

What is not new includes normalized GUI actions, screenshot/XML observations, rule-based predicates, agent rollouts, app-specific reset scripts, and retry-based pass@k. The 80-app/1,080-case scale and the integration of these mechanisms are engineering contributions; they do not by themselves prove record quality, safe automation, or a reusable training dataset.

For reasoning-data research, the main novelty is the inspectable chain from task and real-device state through action trajectory, condition-level feedback, terminal signaling, injected noise, and inverse reset. Before reuse, researchers still need a hidden/refreshable evaluation split, pinned device/APK/account/server manifest, full successful and failed trajectory release, rule-revision ledger, exact noise seeds, and rights review for third-party apps. Without them, the artifact is a strong evaluation recipe but a weak training-data release.
