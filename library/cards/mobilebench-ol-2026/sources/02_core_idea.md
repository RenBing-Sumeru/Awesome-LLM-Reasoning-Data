The contribution is an online Chinese mobile-agent benchmark that combines five capability slices with a rule-based full-episode evaluator and inverse-task reset on physical phones. Instead of comparing an action to one reference path, MobileBench-OL checks whether the trajectory reaches task-specific GUI/action conditions, allowing different navigation paths to satisfy the same goal.

The evaluation object joins four layers. A task specification provides the goal, app start page, golden-step budget, and success-rule material. The environment returns real screenshots and XML hierarchies. The evaluated agent emits normalized click, type, scroll, navigation, wait, long-press, and finished actions. The evaluator then produces complete success, sub-condition progress, step ratio, and one of early termination, overdue termination, or failure.

Its feedback contract is environmental and episode-level. XPath-like rules inspect element attributes such as text, resource-id, package, selected state, and the relation between an action coordinate and an element or parent bounding box. Full success requires every condition and an explicit Complete/finished action. The verifier can observe recorded screens, XML nodes, actions, and termination; it cannot prove unobserved server-side semantics, privacy safety, user intent beyond the encoded rule, or correctness of state omitted from the rule.

Relative to AndroidWorld, LlamaTouch, Mobile-Env, and SPA-Bench, the paper's claimed direction is broader coverage of mainstream Chinese apps on physical devices, explicit long-horizon and exploration subsets, random injection of repeat/unexecuted/delay/pop-up noise, and agent-executed inverse resets. The components are not individually new: GUI actions, dynamic environments, XPath-style checks, noise tests, and reset tasks all have precedents. The contribution is their integration into this evaluation surface and its evidence about current-agent failure.

The feedback is also a public attack surface. Goals and key-node/rule material are released without a hidden test split. A model tuned on these records could memorize tasks or optimize observable XPath conditions while missing unencoded semantics. MobileBench-OL should therefore be read as an inspectable evaluation and verifier recipe, not as hidden reward data or an automatically safe source for agent training.
