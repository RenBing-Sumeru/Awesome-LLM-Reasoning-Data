The paper treats invariance as the missing part of the reward contract. Isomorphic Perturbation Testing runs a single predicted logic hypothesis through the usual extensional verifier and through a logically identical task in which object identifiers are bijectively renamed. A real relational rule remains correct under the renaming; an instance-level enumeration passes the original examples but fails after the identifiers change. The authors call this mismatch a reward shortcut. They monitor it across SLR-Bench complexity levels and reasoning budgets, then train identical OLMo models with either extensional or isomorphic reward. Extensional reward produces a growing gap, while the isomorphic verifier removes the incentive. The result is a black-box audit of a specific verifier weakness, not a claim that all RLVR or all reasoning benchmarks are hacked.
