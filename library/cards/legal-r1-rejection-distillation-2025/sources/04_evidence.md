The evidence supports training effects, not a general claim that every retained trace is legally sound. On the Chinese evaluation, Legal-R1 improves over DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B on the reported average from 48.67 to 51.05. Large task deltas include legal multi-hop reasoning from 39.00 to 48.98 and CAIL2018 from 72.03 to 77.60. On the seven English tasks, the average rises from 73.63 to 74.63, while LAR decreases from 72.50 to 72.00.

The progressive-SFT ablation reports a Chinese average of 61.64 versus 60.19 for single-stage SFT and an English average of 74.63 versus 73.92. Results are mixed at task level: for example, progressive SFT scores 92.73 on LRC versus 94.55 for single-stage SFT. These comparisons support the claimed usefulness of the staged schedule under the paper's evaluation protocol; they do not isolate data correctness, because model, mixture, stage order, and training procedure remain coupled.

The paper's expert error analysis directly demonstrates residual process risk. For 30 sampled Legal-R1 article-recommendation errors, 70% are categorized as content fabrication, 20% as article misidentification, 6.7% as omission, and 3.3% as outdated references. The authors also identify citation factual errors, misunderstandings, and error propagation in sentencing. Those findings are compatible with answer-matched filtering: a final-answer gate cannot by itself validate citations or every intermediate legal assertion.
