The authors note that 17 evaluation tasks do not cover the complexity of real legal practice, subjective questions can admit expert disagreement, and automatic metrics can miss legally meaningful distinctions. The construction process inherits a related limitation: answer agreement is only a proxy for reasoning validity. The paper's own errors include fabricated content, wrong or outdated provisions, omissions, and citation mistakes.

Reproducibility metadata are incomplete. Exact Chinese dataset revisions and counts, record schema, model checkpoint hashes, generation parameters, per-task match functions, accepted and rejected yields, human-audit protocol, stage sizes, optimizer details, seeds, and item-level lineage are unknown. The reported total of 96,533 records “across eight tasks” is not reconciled with the five Chinese task families and 143 LegalBench tasks described elsewhere.

The public-artifact gap is decisive for reuse. At verification time the official GitHub project contains no files, release, or license. Consequently the claimed records, model checkpoint, construction code, rejected candidates, privacy transformations, and source-rights manifest cannot be inspected. The ethics statement says names are anonymized and sensitive information is replaced with neutral third-person expressions, but the implementation and record-level decisions are unavailable.
