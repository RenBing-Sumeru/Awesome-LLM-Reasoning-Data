Legal reasoning data is unusually sensitive to a mismatch between answer correctness and process correctness. A generated response can reach the expected charge, provision, sentence, or numerical result while fabricating facts, citing the wrong article, applying repealed law, or mixing jurisdictions. The paper asks whether test-time-scaling models help on legal tasks and, as one practical outcome, constructs Legal-R1 by distilling DeepSeek-R1 reasoning into a smaller model.

For data curation, the central problem is narrower than the model comparison: how can bilingual legal reasoning traces be selected at scale when source tasks provide gold answers but usually do not provide gold reasoning? The authors use final-answer agreement as a tractable acceptance signal, then supplement it with limited human sampling. That design scales, but it leaves the decisive legal validity of an accepted rationale only partially observed.
