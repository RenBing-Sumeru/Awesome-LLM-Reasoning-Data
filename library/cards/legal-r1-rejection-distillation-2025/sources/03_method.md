The input pool combines five reported Chinese task families—legal calculation, legal multi-hop reasoning, identifying applicable legal provisions, legal judgment prediction, and multi-defendant legal judgment prediction—with 143 U.S. LegalBench tasks. Seven LegalBench tasks used for English evaluation are excluded from training: LRC, CPC, NYSJE, Sara_N, Sara_E, Scalr, and LAR. Appendix prompts incorporate legal terminology dictionaries and structured task templates.

For every sourced instruction-question-answer triple, DeepSeek-R1 produces a candidate reasoning path and response. Response-to-gold agreement acts as the construction verifier. A success admits the associated path; a failure triggers another attempt within a budget of three; three failures remove the item. The paper does not specify per-task normalization, parsers, semantic-equivalence rules, retry yields, decoding parameters, or whether generation stops on the first match. Authors report randomly sampling each generated batch to inspect reasoning, cited provisions, and professional language, but give no sampling fraction, rubric, inter-rater agreement, or adjudication procedure.

Training starts from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B. Legal judgment prediction is trained first, followed by the remaining bilingual tasks. Reported settings are eight NVIDIA A800 GPUs, learning rate 1e-5, cutoff length 8192, bfloat16, and three epochs. Batch size, optimizer, scheduler, stage sizes, random seeds, checkpoint-selection rule, and exact model revisions remain unknown.
