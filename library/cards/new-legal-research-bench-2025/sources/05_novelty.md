The prior-work baseline is agent-environment, tool-use, and benchmark-harness papers in the same atlas category. The change introduced by Legal Research Benchmark is the particular task object and feedback contract: legal research question, expected legal answer or rubric, submitted response, and official score scored by legal expert answer or official Vals scoring rubric; exact judge and private-set policy are needs_audit. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_research; project: https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/legal_research), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
