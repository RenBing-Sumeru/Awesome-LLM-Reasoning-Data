Evidence comes from broad model coverage, cross-comparison among multiple evaluators, and calibration against human scores. Experiments show that instance-specific criteria improve agreement between automated evaluation and human judgment while revealing capability differences hidden by holistic scores. Substantial preference differences remain among judges, demonstrating that no single evaluator should be treated as absolute ground truth. Unified evaluation of 103 models also shows that rankings change by capability and task. Because each task has roughly ten instances, task-level estimates may have high variance.
