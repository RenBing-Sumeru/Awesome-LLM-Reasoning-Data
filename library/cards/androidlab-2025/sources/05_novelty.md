Prior Android-agent work already provided demonstrations, mobile task suites, UI representations, and programmatic rewards. AndroidLab does not introduce Android interaction, XML observations, SoM prompting, human demonstrations, completion classifiers, or LLM answer judges in isolation.

Its concrete change is system-level coupling. The paper packages a fixed-state Android operation environment, 138 operation/query tasks, heterogeneous task-specific completion checks, a two-mode trace schema, a human/reward-model cross-verification process, and a selected SFT subset. This lets readers trace how a task seed becomes a state-action target and how an evaluated episode becomes success or failure.

A second contribution is the explicit separation of operation and query feedback. Operation success derives from UI-tree and sometimes device-state predicates over subgoals; query success derives from a model judgment against a standard answer. This mixed contract exposes different false-positive, false-negative, drift, and reproducibility risks that a single aggregate SR would hide.

The scale itself is not the novelty claim: 10.5K traces / 94.3K steps describe the broad construction process, whereas training uses 726 traces / 6,208 XML steps and 6,053 SoM steps, with self-exploration actions excluded. For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is the linkage among observation modality, human trace selection, executable environment state, and terminal feedback. Reuse still requires archive hashes, split/overlap manifests, failure ledgers, dataset rights, and immutable environment versions.
