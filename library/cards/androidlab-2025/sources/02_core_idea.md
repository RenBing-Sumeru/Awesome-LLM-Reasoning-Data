AndroidLab connects a selected Android demonstration subset to a benchmark whose terminal feedback is executable or judged, making the observation/action schema, collection filter, and success contract visible in one system.

Its training object is a human-produced state-action trace in two observation modes. XML mode exposes task, history, and compressed UI XML; SoM mode exposes task, history, and a screenshot with numbered selectable elements. Both target function-call actions over Tap, Type, Swipe, Long Press, Home, Back, Enter, Wait, and Finish. LLM/VLM self-exploration contributed to the wider collection process but its operation data was explicitly removed from instruction fine-tuning; only the resulting reward model was retained for the next annotation stage.

The feedback contract has three distinct layers. During collection, a second annotator and a trajectory-level completion reward model cross-verify human traces. For benchmark operation tasks, hand-coded predicates inspect page/UI-tree fields and sometimes ADB/device state; every required subgoal must hold for success. For query tasks, GPT-4o-2024-05-13 or GLM-4 judges whether the answer supplied through Finish semantically matches a standard answer, with helper logic allowing up to five retries. Finish or the 25-step cap ends generation, but neither by itself proves evaluator success.

AndroidWorld is a separate 116-task/20-app reward-bearing benchmark discussed as prior work; AndroidLab contributes its own 138-task/9-app AVD suite and instruction-tuning pipeline. AndroidControl is not established as AndroidLab lineage, and V-Droid is later work rather than an AndroidLab component. The closest directional distinction is therefore the joint treatment of training traces, resettable Android state, operation predicates, and query judgment—not ownership of all Android-agent data or evaluation methods.
