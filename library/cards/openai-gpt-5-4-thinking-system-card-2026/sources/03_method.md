The disclosed system must be reconstructed as four pipelines.

**Training.** Three broad source classes undergo high-level quality/risk filtering, personal-information reduction, and safety classification. Reasoning models receive undisclosed RL. GPT-5.4-specific interventions include long-rollout self-reversion while protecting implicit simulated user work; following platform and developer-supplied confirmation policies in computer-use contexts; prompt-injection training whose connector/function-call evaluations are acknowledged training splits; and cyber safety training for calibrated dual-use help plus refusal or de-escalation of harmful action. Task sources, simulators, trace schemas, labels, rewards, detector logic, rollout count, optimizer, schedule, compute, and checkpoint lineage are unknown.

**Evaluation.** Challenging Production Benchmarks select difficult prior-failure cases and are not average production prevalence. Dynamic safety evaluation generates adaptive multi-turn conversations and checks every assistant message. Production-like evaluation samples deidentified recent GPT-5.2 Thinking conversations, preserves context, resamples the final turn with GPT-5.4 Thinking, and applies automatic labels; it is a point-in-time model-only estimate. Prompt-injection evaluates overlapping training splits. Other surfaces use HealthBench example-specific rubrics, GPT-4o fairness judgments, policy and task graders, CoT-Control instructions, hidden unit tests, cyber flags, environment success, medal thresholds, pass@1, and expert or manual review.

**Monitoring and audit.** The April 24 monitorability update defines 13 evaluations across 24 environments and three archetypes. GPT-5 Thinking observes CoT, actions/final output, or all messages; task-specific answer graders provide target labels; g-mean-squared and cross-fit filtering aggregate results. CoT-Control pairs more than 13,000 problems from GPQA, MMLU-Pro, HLE, BFCL, and SWE-bench Verified with synthetic instructions governing CoT format. This audit pipeline is not a training reward.

**Deployment.** High-cyber oversight first applies a topical classifier, then a safety-reasoner monitor using a threat taxonomy. Flags can trigger asynchronous message blocks, actor-level enforcement, trusted-access controls, and human review. System-message policy and account controls operate around the model and can change independently of training.

Evaluation budgets remain attached to their surfaces: CVE-Bench uses three rollouts, zero-day prompting, no source access, 34 of 40 runnable tasks, and pass@1; Cyber Range uses 16 trials and passes a scenario if any succeeds; external cyber work allows up to 1,000 turns with compaction every 100K tokens. Monorepo-Bench starts from a pre-change branch, permits command-line and Python actions, and succeeds only if all human-written hidden unit tests pass. MLE-Bench uses a selected 30-competition subset and a bronze-medal predicate. None of these settings is disclosed as training sampling or reward.

Reproduction requires immutable living-page revisions, simulator and monitor checkpoints, prompts, labels, traffic frame, environment commits, hidden tests, budgets, sampling, grader calibration, global membership, and item-to-checkpoint lineage. They are not released.
