GPT-5 and GPT-5.2 already disclosed the same three broad source classes, family-level reasoning RL, prompt-injection overlap for named evaluations, production-derived safety evaluation, and later CoT monitorability. Repeating those claims is not the GPT-5.4 novelty.

The first concrete delta is trajectory ownership. GPT-5.4 training explicitly targets long workspaces where an agent must undo its own changes while protecting implicit simulated user work. This creates an ownership-aware full-episode object rather than a simple response label. Unlike later Codex conflicting-edit disclosures, the report emphasizes self-reversion after long rollouts but still withholds simulator, detector, reward, and trace records.

The second delta is policy composition. Computer-use training moves from a single confirmation rule to platform-level policy plus developer-supplied policy in privileged messages. Cyber safety training is paired with the first High-cyber mitigations for a general-purpose GPT model, but model training and deployed two-tier oversight remain separate.

The third delta is evaluation construction: adversarial simulators create dynamic conversations; a production-like pilot freezes deidentified GPT-5.2 context and resamples only the final turn; prompt-injection overlap is stated; and the April living-page update documents monitor, answer-grader, positive-count, and CoT-length confounds across 13 evaluations and 24 environments. The novelty is disclosure of interactions and failure boundaries, not a released monitor or dataset.

RL, simulated users, policy graders, hidden tests, programmatic environments, CoT monitors, and deployment classifiers are not individually new.
