- The long-rollout intervention does not disclose task sources, simulator identity, change-ownership representation, episode length, trace fields, successful and failed samples, preservation detector, reward scale, aggregation, calibration, false positives, false negatives, or terminal rule. A superficial agent could preserve a diff while neutralizing the user's intent, while legitimate cleanup could be misclassified as reversion; this is a curator inference.
- Dynamic adversarial conversations and production-like final-turn resampling are evaluation data, not disclosed safety-training trajectories. Production-like estimates reconstruct sampled context, omit the full deployed safety stack, are point-in-time, and can drift with traffic, model snapshots, labeling pipelines, and product policy.
- Connector and function-call prompt-injection evaluations are splits of training data. Their scores cannot establish generalization to unseen attacks, and the report does not provide membership hashes, overlap rates, attack diversity, false-negative analysis, or a separately held-out prompt-injection suite.
- Monitorability has label and evaluator mismatch. GPT-5 Thinking monitors CoT, while task-specific or model answer graders define outcomes. Anti-Scheming and Memory contain sufficiently problematic mismatch, monitor mistakes, short CoTs, and few positives that OpenAI plans to drop them until revision.
- CoT monitorability depends on monitor checkpoint, observation surface, CoT length, positive-sample prevalence, grader quality, g-mean-squared aggregation, and cross-fit filtering. Lower scores can reflect any of these. CoT-Control's synthetic format instructions and strong length confounding make low controllability an audit signal, not proof that a model cannot hide reasoning.
- Policy graders, automatic production-like labels, task answer graders, GPT-4o fairness ratings, example-specific rubrics, hidden unit tests, cyber flags, pass metrics, medal thresholds, and expert review are distinct evaluation contracts. Combining them into one verifier or treating them as the reasoning-RL reward would be unsupported.
- Cyber evaluation non-overlap is narrow. Its deidentified production plus synthetic rare-case mixture is intentionally challenging and not representative of ordinary traffic; classifier selection and embedding clustering can create coverage and sampling bias. It does not establish global train/evaluation separation.
- Programmatic environments are incomplete proxies. CVE-Bench runs 34 of 40 tasks, Cyber Range uses an any-success rule across 16 trials, and Monorepo-Bench hidden tests may miss semantic or security failures. Scores depend on tools, scaffolds, repeated-attempt budgets, environment versions, and graders.
- The two-tier cyber classifier and safety-reasoner monitor, asynchronous blocks, actor controls, trusted access, and human review are deployment safeguards. Their false-positive and false-negative rates, thresholds, versions, escalation delays, and interaction with product traffic are not released; safety outcomes cannot be attributed to model training alone.
- The living document is mutable. A March 5 cover now contains a March 17 mini appendix and April 24 CoT update. Without immutable revisions and retrieval dates, comparisons can silently backdate later evidence or mix model snapshots.
- No training corpus, long-rollout records, simulated user work, dynamic or resampled conversations, CoT traces, graders, monitors, hidden tests, weights, reward code, global decontamination ledger, source licenses, or item-level checkpoint lineage is released.
