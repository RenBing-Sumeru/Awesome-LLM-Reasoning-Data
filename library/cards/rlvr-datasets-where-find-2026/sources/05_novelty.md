The closest baseline is ordinary dataset-level lineage, where a release cites only its direct predecessor and repeated prompts or older sources disappear behind aggregation. ATLAS changes the audit unit to canonical prompts with chronological occurrence lists and atomic-source labels. Combining exact reuse, semantic recovery, and iterative source expansion at 1.45M-instance scale makes lineage an input to construction rather than only a literature description.

SCA adds a source-conditioned utility view. A base checkpoint and a source-trained checkpoint define correctness transitions that are transferred to records from that source. This is more operational than ranking sources by size or one final benchmark score, but it remains conditioned on model scale, checkpoint, verifier, and training recipe and does not identify the causal contribution of one row.

Q and DAPO++ connect audit to action. Q separates static properties from dynamic RLVR utility, and DAPO++ uses leakage and learnability decisions to repair an existing 17K-prompt set. For `data_construction_open_release_recipes`, the useful direction is the linkage among lineage, selection rules, replacement, reward, and evaluation.

SHA-1 matching, Sentence-BERT retrieval, manual review, GRPO, VERL, Math-Verify, DAPO-Math-17k, Stack Exchange problems, Mean@N, and Pass@N are not new contributions. The release also does not establish a new provenance-completeness standard because its final lineage, SCA, removal, and replacement ledgers are absent. The novelty is the integrated RLVR workflow and source-conditioned framing, not a new mathematical verifier or proof that benchmark gains certify data quality.
