The contribution is an integrated four-layer workflow rather than a single new dataset. Atomic-source Tracing via Lineage-Aware Search (ATLAS) canonicalizes public RLVR records into final question-answer pairs, indexes exact prompt reuse chronologically with SHA-1, recovers transformed matches through Sentence-BERT retrieval plus author review, and iteratively adds candidate sources. Its intended output is a lineage dictionary with occurrence lists and atomic-source assignments.

SCA treats each atomic source as an intervention unit. For source s, a shared base checkpoint is compared with a checkpoint trained on s. Source instances receive one of four binary-correctness transitions: 00 fails before and after, 01 becomes correct after source-specific RL, 10 degrades, and 11 is correct under both. These are model- and checkpoint-conditioned learnability labels, not immutable labels of mathematical quality.

The score Q combines three dimensions: S1 covers static properties such as answer consistency and verifiability, MCQ and reuse penalties, SCA learnability, diversity, and contamination; S2 uses Math500 Mean@4 as a sampling-efficiency signal; S3 uses Math500 Pass@4 as a capability-boundary signal. Q ranks datasets. It is neither the Math-Verify rollout reward nor a judge that certifies individual rows.

DAPO++ turns the audit into a construction action. Detected leaked rows are removed from DAPO-Math-17k and randomly replaced with non-MCQ Stack Exchange rows labeled learnable by SCA. The repository releases the resulting 17,000-row prompt-and-ground-truth file, but not the complete evidence chain behind every retained or inserted record.
