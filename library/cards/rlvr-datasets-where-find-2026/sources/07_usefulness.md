For `data_construction_open_release_recipes`, the work decomposes an RLVR data pipeline into explicit audit points: normalize records, group exact reuse, recover transformed ancestry, preserve chronological occurrences, audit evaluation overlap, estimate source-conditioned learnability, define a dataset-level score, repair a training set, and evaluate the repair under a fixed reward and optimizer. Its main reusable lesson is that provenance, verification, selection, and evaluation should be connected by records rather than summarized only at dataset level.

A reusable lineage record should retain canonical prompt and answer hashes, upstream IDs and revisions, source URLs, timestamps, every occurrence, transformation type, exact and semantic match scores, candidate ancestors, final source decision, reviewer and adjudication, uncertainty, and license. The final lineage dictionary is necessary to audit the reported 1.45M-instance map.

A reusable SCA and Q record should retain base and source checkpoint revisions, the source training subset, GRPO settings, Math-Verify and parser versions, rollout seeds, per-checkpoint correctness, the 00/01/10/11 label, aggregation weights, diversity and contamination features, the Math500 checkpoint-selection trace, and every Q input. These scores should be recomputed when the model, verifier, parser, mixture, or evaluation revision changes.

A reusable DAPO++ decision ledger should preserve every original DAPO row, leakage evidence, keep-or-remove decision, complete eligible replacement pool, random seed and draw order, rejected candidates, selected Stack Exchange row, MCQ and SCA gates, final row ID, transformation, source license, and immutable checksum. The public Parquet is useful as a rule-reward prompt schema and as the exact 17,000-row training object at the verified commit, but it cannot explain why each record entered the set.

Metric contracts must remain separate: Math-Verify is the rollout reward; SCA is a checkpoint-conditioned learnability proxy; Q is a six-dataset ranking score; SRank is an aggregate reference ranking; Average* is downstream evaluation excluding Math500. Benchmark improvement is not proof that released data are correct, uncontaminated, licensed, or generally high quality.
