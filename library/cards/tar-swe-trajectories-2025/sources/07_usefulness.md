Within environment_agent_trajectory_data, this work is a concrete audit recipe for turning otherwise opaque software-agent logs into an inspectable episode ledger. A maintainer can separate task and benchmark provenance, thought, tool/action and arguments, result/observation, terminal outcome, action category, feedback-use relation, iteration/token budget, and success/failure retention. That schema is useful even when the original agents cannot be rerun.

For evaluation design, the paper shows why terminal patch success should be paired with process diagnostics. Repeated identical actions, fix-without-test behavior, ignored tool results, and thought-action misalignment can be measured as failure indicators rather than hidden in a single resolved bit. A derivative study can preregister these labels, double-code a subset, report agreement, and test whether the indicators generalize across agent and repository families.

For release audits, the Card suggests an executable checklist: publish one machine-readable episode manifest; retain raw and parsed logs plus failures; bind every analysis row to an instance and artifact hash; pin agent, model, benchmark, repository, tool, and parser revisions; specify reset and terminal predicates; release annotation guidelines and adjudication; reconcile lists across RQ outputs; and declare data-specific rights. These checks directly answer the track's questions about state/action/observation structure, failure preservation, and replayability.

The appropriate reuse class is evaluation/audit reference only. The released annotations can support exploratory behavior analysis after rights and manifest review, but the evidence does not establish safe training reuse. In particular, relationship labels are not process rewards, test-passing outcomes do not validate intermediate reasoning, and the paper reports no SFT, PRM, RLVR, reward-model, or policy-training experiment.

A stronger follow-up would reproduce the 120-episode sample from pinned raw logs, preserve the 8.3% uncategorized actions as an explicit rejection set, resolve the RepairAgent manifest conflict, publish numeric inter-annotator agreement, and replay episodes under fixed environments. It should also keep evaluation tasks separated from any later training conversion and audit exposure to SWE-bench Lite and Defects4J.
