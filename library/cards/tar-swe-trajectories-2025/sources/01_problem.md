The primary records are the ASE 2025 Research Papers entry, DOI 10.1109/ASE63991.2025.00234, and arXiv:2506.18824 v2 dated 2025-10-08. The paper asks how software-engineering agents reach a patch and why individual runs succeed or fail, rather than treating the final resolved rate as a complete account of agent behavior. Its official GitHub repository releases parsed trajectories and annotations for inspection.

The concrete gap is methodological: RepairAgent, AutoCodeRover V1, and OpenHands CodeAct emit different logs, tool interfaces, and stopping behavior, so their internal decision processes are difficult to compare directly. The study maps them to a common episode representation, T=[(t1,a1,r1),...,(tn,an,rn)], where a thought is natural-language reasoning, an action invokes a tool or external operation, and a result is the returned observation. It then analyzes structure, action sequences, and semantic coherence for successful and failed runs.

The analysis sample contains 120 episodes and 2,822 iterations: 40 RepairAgent runs on Defects4J, 40 AutoCodeRover V1 runs on SWE-bench Lite, and 40 OpenHands CodeAct runs on SWE-bench Lite. Approximately 10 successful episodes are retained per agent. Released episode material is distributed across parsed thought/action/result text views and CSVs containing instance IDs, iteration and token counts, resolved status, eight-way action categories, and five adjacent-component relation families; it is not one normalized replay record.

This belongs in environment_agent_trajectory_data because the central object is an agent episode whose feedback comes from tools, benchmark environments, and a terminal success predicate. It is not a new benchmark, agent-training method, reward model, or causal comparison of the three systems. Its supported role is evaluation and failure audit: the paper does not train a policy or demonstrate that these traces are safe training data.

The source material supports L4-depth screening of the object, construction pipeline, labels, and audit risks, while the accepted metadata remains L3_summary_ready. The boundary matters: the repository is real and inspectable, but exact replay, immutable versioning, record-level lineage, dataset rights, and the RepairAgent manifest mismatch remain unresolved.
