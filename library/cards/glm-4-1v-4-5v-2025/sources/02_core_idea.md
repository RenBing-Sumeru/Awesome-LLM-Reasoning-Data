The report treats multimodal reasoning as a staged data-and-feedback system. A shared vision encoder/adapter/decoder architecture is first trained on broad multimodal mixtures. A cleaned long-CoT SFT stage teaches explicit thinking and answer formatting. Multi-domain RL then combines Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for tasks with checkable outcomes and RLHF-style model rewards for open-ended or semantic tasks.

RLCS—Reinforcement Learning with Curriculum Sampling—is the central selection mechanism. Before RL, pass@k results from several established VLMs or earlier checkpoints are merged with expert human labels to assign difficulty tiers. During training, rollout pass@k and subcategory performance update the difficulty estimate. Sampling ratios are reweighted every iteration to reduce exposure to examples that are currently too easy or too hard and emphasize the mid-range where correct and incorrect attempts coexist.

A second selection layer, ratio-EMA sampling expansion, responds to all-correct or all-incorrect rollout groups that carry no useful GRPO gradient after KL and entropy losses are removed. The system estimates the recent invalid-sample rate, oversamples the next rollout batch, then retains a subset with a more balanced correct/incorrect composition. The report explains the mechanism but does not disclose its exact coefficients, rollout counts, selected examples, or per-iteration curriculum history.

The feedback contract is explicitly heterogeneous. Rule-based components extract special-token-delimited final answers and perform exact matching; mathematics uses SymPy and tolerances; OCR uses edit distance; grounding uses IoU; GUI tasks mix action prediction, IoU, functional and semantic checks; charts, documents, VQA, spatial tasks and video may fall back to LLM judgment. Format and style rewards address box usage, language mixing, repetition, fluency, and instruction compliance. These are primarily final-output or scalar signals, not verified step-by-step reasoning labels.
