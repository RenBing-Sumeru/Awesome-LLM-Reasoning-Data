The report provides unusually concrete construction evidence for a frontier VLM: named source families, initial-pool or annotation counts, filtering rules, response tags, difficulty-label sources, domain-specific reward logic, and several training schedules. Examples include the >10B image-text initial pool, 220M OCR images, 40M natural-image grounding annotations, >140M GUI QA pairs, a 50M instruction mixture, 120K pre-training steps, and 10K long-context steps. These quantities describe different stages and cannot be added into one final released dataset size.

The feedback evidence is also specific. The report documents reward hacking when weak LLM judges accepted vague ranges or approximate descriptions, and shows that a weak verifier in one multimodal subdomain can raise reward while degrading both that task and unrelated STEM benchmarks. This motivates box-token extraction, domain-specific verifier logic, unit tests, and format/style rewards. It supports the claim that reward design mattered to the reported training; it does not provide false-accept rates or prove that the final verifier suite is calibrated.

Across 42 public benchmarks, the current report compares the 9B GLM-4.1V model and 106B-total/12B-active GLM-4.5V with other open and closed systems. Illustrative reported changes from GLM-4.1V to GLM-4.5V include MMMU Pro 57.1 to 65.2, MathVista 80.7 to 84.6, ChartQAPro 59.5 to 64.0, OSWorld 14.9 to 35.8, AndroidWorld 41.7 to 57.0, and Design2Code 64.7 to 82.2. The report also states that RL experiments on GLM-4.5V produce gains up to 10.6 percentage points. These are model-evaluation results, not direct measurements of corpus quality, licensing, or contamination.

The cross-domain experiment starts from the GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking SFT checkpoint and compares STEM, OCR & Chart, Grounding, GUI Agent, and mix-all RL data. Single-domain training often improves other domains; mix-all is best in three of five evaluated groups, but not grounding or GUI agents. This supports heterogeneous transfer while preserving a counterexample to universal synergy. Exact training sample counts and the per-domain curriculum records are still absent.

Artifact evidence must be recorded separately. Official Hugging Face cards expose GLM-4.5V and GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking safetensor weights under MIT, and the repository links the GLM-4.1V-9B-Base weights. The Apache-2.0 GitHub repository contains inference, examples, integrations, and the released `glmv_reward` package with a lockfile and tests. It does not expose the pre-training/SFT/RL datasets, complete model-training or GRPO infrastructure, curriculum sampler state, policy rollouts, human preferences, production reward logs, or reward-model weights.
