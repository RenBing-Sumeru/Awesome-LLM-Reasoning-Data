HoneyBee's useful novelty is the experimental decomposition of multimodal reasoning data. Rather than treating “more examples” as one operation, it measures source context, unique-image coverage, questions per image, traces per question, and caption assistance as different construction decisions. That decomposition matters because 16 solutions for one question and one solution for 16 distinct images have the same row count but very different diversity and verification properties.

The selected caption-and-solve variant also makes the supervision object explicit: the target trace is a concatenation of an independently generated detailed caption and an independently generated solution. The paper tests alternative ways to condition caption and solution generation and finds this separable version strongest in its setup. At test time, that factorization enables reuse of one caption across multiple solution samples, connecting a data-format decision to an inference-efficiency experiment.

Another contribution is negative evidence. Several plausible interventions—visual perturbations, synthetic text-rich images, removing questions solvable without the image, adding distractors, selecting long traces, and balancing difficulty—do not improve the controlled average. Publishing these comparisons helps prevent the construction track from turning every intuitive filter into an assumed quality improvement.

None of the components is a universal verifier. Teacher distillation, self-consistency voting, exact-image hashing, pointer-based packaging, and caption-first targets all have precedents. The contribution is the controlled combination and the reported large-scale release, together with enough detail to distinguish known-answer filtering from proxy-answer filtering. The public artifact remains weaker than the conceptual recipe because it omits generation logs and a complete reconstruction manifest.
