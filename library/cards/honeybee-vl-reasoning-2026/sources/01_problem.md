Vision-language reasoning datasets are often described by a total row count, even though their effective training signal depends on several separable quantities: which image/question contexts are chosen, how many distinct images appear, how many questions are attached to each image, how many solutions are sampled for each question, and whether the target trace explicitly describes the visual evidence. HoneyBee asks which of these choices improve supervised vision-language reasoning when model scale, training, and evaluation are held comparatively stable.

The paper also confronts a release-design problem. The final records combine material derived from ViRL with re-annotated OpenThoughts3 questions, but some upstream images and questions are not redistributed. A reusable HoneyBee record is therefore not just a question and rationale. It is a lineage-bearing object with `q_source`, `q_id`, an optional image path, an optional question, and a generated `cot`; some rows must be reconstructed from upstream datasets before they become trainable examples.

The study is relevant to data construction because it distinguishes recipe evidence from data-quality evidence. Downstream gains can show that one mixture trained the tested PLMs better than another, but they cannot certify every generated question, caption, rationale, answer, or upstream right. The central audit question is consequently whether the public rows preserve enough provenance, verification state, and version information for another group to reproduce or safely adapt the recipe.
