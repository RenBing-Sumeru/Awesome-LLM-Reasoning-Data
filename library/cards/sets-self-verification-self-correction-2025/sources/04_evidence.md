The paper reports scaling curves and ablations across planning, reasoning, mathematics, and code-output prediction, using several model families. It states improvements of up to 10.9 percentage points in the evaluated configurations and analyzes correction rounds, temperature, model size, difficulty, self-verification sampling, API calls, and financial cost. The curves generally compare SETS with SELF-REFINE, best-of-N plus majority vote, best-of-N plus self-evaluation, and best-of-N plus self-verification.

This evidence supports the claim that jointly varying m and n can produce a better accuracy–output-token frontier than the tested alternatives for some models and tasks. Because each plotted frontier takes the best hyperparameter configuration for a dataset and budget, it is not an online allocation policy and may benefit from evaluation-set-specific selection. The results do not establish that individual self-verification statements are correct or that correction histories are high-quality data. The official TMLR publication status and arXiv record are verified; no official code or trace release was confirmed.
