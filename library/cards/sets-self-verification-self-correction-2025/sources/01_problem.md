The official TMLR record and arXiv v5 paper study how to allocate test-time compute when repeated sampling quickly saturates and a single SELF-REFINE chain often stops after the model declares its answer correct. Existing hybrids can require separately trained verifiers or revision models. SETS asks whether one unchanged language model can combine parallel exploration with sequential self-improvement through prompts alone.

The process object is an initial set of m candidate solutions, a self-verification response for each current solution, zero or more self-corrections carrying the prior solution–feedback history, an early-stop decision, and a final exact-match majority vote. The method uses no additional model training and produces evaluation-time traces rather than a new training corpus. Its feedback is the model's own judgment parsed by a rule, not an externally calibrated correctness reward; benchmark ground truth is used only for evaluation and scaling-curve measurement.
