Repeated sampling explores several independent solutions but cannot improve a branch; SELF-REFINE can revise one branch but stops when self-verification succeeds and cannot freely spend a larger budget. SETS treats these as endpoints of one design space: m controls parallel diversity and n controls sequential repair. It uses the same model and prompt interfaces for generation, verification, and correction rather than introducing a trained task-specific verifier or revision model.

The direction signal for this atlas is the explicit episode structure and budget decomposition. A SETS trajectory distinguishes initial diversity, feedback, revision depth, early stopping, and final cross-branch aggregation. Those records would support audits of whether gains come from more samples, stronger self-verification, or productive correction. The individual operations are established; their training-free composition and compute-frontier study are the paper's contribution. Final benchmark accuracy is not evidence that intermediate self-feedback is faithful.
