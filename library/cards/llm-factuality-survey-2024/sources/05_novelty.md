Instead of treating every incorrect answer as the same kind of failure, the paper offers a field-level organization around challenges, causes, improvement possibilities, and evaluation limitations. Its contribution is not a claim that it solves factuality with one mechanism; it gives readers a structure for separating different questions that are often collapsed in descriptions of hallucination. When a paper reports an improvement, this structure helps ask whether the change concerns the model's generation behavior, the quality or scope of the measurement, or both. It also makes clear that a better result under one judgment setup does not by itself establish broad factual reliability.
