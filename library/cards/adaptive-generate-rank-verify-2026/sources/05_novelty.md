Prior adaptive inference work frequently allocates only samples or stopping time. This paper makes the full generate-rank-verify pipeline the decision object and prices the expensive terminal verifier separately from cheap ranking. Its contribution is therefore a budget policy over two resources, together with a distribution-aware benchmark, an online approximation, and a statement of the ranking assumption that makes the comparison meaningful.

The work does not claim to improve the generator or reward model itself. Its novelty is the auditability of allocation: the paper can say when a candidate was generated, what score put it in the verification set, what verification cost was paid, and why a policy must fail without usable score-label structure. This is especially relevant where hidden tests make each verification call scarce.
