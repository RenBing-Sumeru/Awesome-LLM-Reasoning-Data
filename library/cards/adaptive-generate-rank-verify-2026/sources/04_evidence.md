The central theoretical evidence is a constant-factor expected-cost guarantee relative to a distribution-aware optimum, conditional on monotonicity between reward rank and verification success. This is more informative than an accuracy-only curve because it states which hidden property of the cheap score permits verifier-budget savings and provides lower bounds when that structure is absent.

Empirically, the paper tests the ranking assumption and compares ADAP with fixed and difficulty-adaptive policies on math and coding. The reported top-ranked samples are several times more likely to be correct than uniformly selected samples in the 83 coding and 22 math validation sets. The experiments support the claimed allocation advantage in those settings, but not a guarantee for a reward model whose ranking is nonmonotone or adversarial.
