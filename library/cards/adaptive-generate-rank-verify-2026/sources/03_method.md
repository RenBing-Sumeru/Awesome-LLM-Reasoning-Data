ADAP receives a prompt, a candidate generator, a cheap reward score, and a costly binary verifier. It explores shells of increasing generation and verification counts, ranks candidates by the cheap score, and pays for verification only on the current top set. A run terminates only when the verifier returns positive, so the returned answer has an explicit terminal contract rather than merely a high predicted score.

The theory first derives the distribution-aware threshold policy by dynamic programming, then bounds the shellwise online policy under monotonic score-conditioned success. Experiments cover mathematical reasoning and competitive programming, comparing against fixed non-adaptive policies and difficulty-adaptive baselines. The paper separately validates that reward ranking is empirically monotone on 83 coding and 22 mathematics problems.
