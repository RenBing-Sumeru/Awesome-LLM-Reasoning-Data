Inference-time reasoning systems often generate many candidates, score them cheaply, and then use an exact but expensive check such as hidden-test execution or answer verification. A fixed generate-then-verify plan wastes calls on easy prompts and can also verify too few candidates when a correct answer is rare. The central difficulty is that the score distribution and the probability that a score passes verification are unknown for a new prompt.

This paper asks for a policy that decides both how many responses to generate and how many top-ranked responses to verify before returning a certified positive. The target is a cost-sensitive search rule whose accounting includes reward scoring and verifier calls, rather than treating the verifier as an unlimited free selector after generation.
