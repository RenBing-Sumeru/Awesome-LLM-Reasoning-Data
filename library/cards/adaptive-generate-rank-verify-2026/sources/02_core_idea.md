The paper casts the setting as generative active search. For each prompt, the generator and reward model induce a score distribution and a score-conditioned probability of success. If both were known, the optimal policy would verify exactly when that success probability exceeds a break-even threshold determined by verification cost and the value of continuing to sample.

In practice those quantities are unknown, so ADAP searches over dyadic generation and verification scales. It repeatedly enlarges the candidate pool and verifies top-ranked members. Under the assumption that a higher reward score is no less likely to pass the verifier, the authors prove that its expected cost is within a constant factor of the distribution-aware optimum; they also show why some score-label structure is necessary.
