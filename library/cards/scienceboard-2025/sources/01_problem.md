Scientific computer use is not well represented by static question answering or ordinary desktop benchmarks. Real workflows require specialized applications, domain-specific file formats, visual and textual reasoning, multi-step manipulation, and verification of intermediate I/O and final application state. A plausible screenshot or natural-language answer may still hide a scientifically incorrect configuration.

ScienceBoard combines a scientific-software environment with 169 human-curated evaluation tasks across algebra, biochemistry, theorem proving, geographic information systems, astronomy, and scientific documentation. The tasks include 38 GUI, 33 CLI, and 98 hybrid GUI+CLI workflows. Difficulty labels are 91 easy, 48 medium, 28 hard, and 2 open problems; the paper reports a nine-step average human execution and 124 seconds average time.

The episode is an interaction loop in an Ubuntu VMware VM. Given a natural-language goal, an agent observes screenshots, accessibility trees, or both; maintains recent interaction memory; and emits GUI, CLI, answer, or API actions until `DONE`, `FAIL`, or an attempt limit. A task-specific evaluator then inspects key workflow I/O and exposed VM/application state. The benchmark is evaluation-only; released trajectories do not establish a general training license or complete training corpus.
