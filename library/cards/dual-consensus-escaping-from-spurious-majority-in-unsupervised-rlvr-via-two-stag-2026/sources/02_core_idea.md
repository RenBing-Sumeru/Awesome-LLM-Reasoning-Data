DCRL treats agreement as a robustness test rather than a simple popularity count. The current policy first acts as an anchor and samples its ordinary dominant answers. A temporary copy then takes one unlearning step that suppresses high-confidence tokens, becoming an explorer that samples alternative trajectories. Candidate answers are scored by the harmonic mean of their anchor and explorer frequencies, so a choice needs support both in the ordinary distribution and after it is flattened. The elected answer receives reward 1, the anchor-only majority retains 0.5, and others receive 0; a consensus-rate gate decides when exploratory rollouts enter GRPO updates. The method uses no external model or label during training, but it still assumes the two distributions do not share the same wrong prior.
