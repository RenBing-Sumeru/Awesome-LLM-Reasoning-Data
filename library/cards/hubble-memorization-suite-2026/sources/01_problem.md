Controlled memorization studies are often too small to resemble modern LLMs, while observational studies of large models cannot identify why a text was memorized. This leaves copyright, privacy, and benchmark-contamination interventions difficult to measure causally.

Hubble releases matched open models trained with deliberate, randomized text insertions. It turns duplication, placement in training, and data type into known treatments, so researchers can measure memorization and test mitigation methods rather than infer training exposure from a black box.
