Diffusion models usually generate a sample by following one predetermined denoising schedule. Even when an intermediate trajectory looks promising, inference cannot readily direct extra computation to it; conversely, weak trajectories still consume the same number of steps. This limits task-directed control in planning, control, and compositional image generation. The paper draws its decision boundary around test-time control rather than retraining the diffusion model: given a base generative distribution, a finite inference budget, and a score-producing verifier, how can the generative space be searched principledly so that valuable candidates are retained or refined while unpromising ones are abandoned? Its relevance is that it turns “spending more steps” into an inspectable search and budget-allocation problem.
