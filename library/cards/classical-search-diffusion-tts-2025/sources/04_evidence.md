Evidence comes from three settings with different properties. In long-horizon maze planning, local refinement reduces trajectory-wall collisions, and adding global search improves success as the inference budget grows; the project page reports over 80% success in difficult settings with global search. In offline reinforcement learning, the method guides an action distribution with a pretrained Q-function and obtains performance comparable to methods that require additional training on D4RL locomotion tasks. Image experiments use visual verifiers for object counts, attribute binding, relations, and class conditions, showing search to be more efficient than Best-of-N, which only draws parallel samples and selects the highest score. These gains nevertheless depend on the verifier providing reliable rankings at intermediate states, so output quality and total wall-clock cost must be reported together.
