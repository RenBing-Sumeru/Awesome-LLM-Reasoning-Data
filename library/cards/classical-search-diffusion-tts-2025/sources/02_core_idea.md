The central idea is to view diffusion sampling as both local optimization and global mode exploration. Locally, the method reweights the base diffusion distribution with verifier scores and uses annealed Langevin MCMC to refine the current sample toward higher-scoring regions. Globally, it treats candidates at different noise levels as nodes in a search tree: breadth-first search retains and expands promising branches, whereas depth-first search backtracks and re-noises a trajectory when its score is insufficient. The verifier can be a collision cost, Q-function, classifier, or visual-attribute detector. The essential point is not merely selecting a final sample, but allowing intermediate scores to decide where the next unit of compute goes, balancing quality, coverage, and cost.
