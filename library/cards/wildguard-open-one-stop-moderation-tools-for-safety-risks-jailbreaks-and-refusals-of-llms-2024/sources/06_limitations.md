A classifier trained on a fixed taxonomy can miss emerging harms, indirect intent, multilingual pragmatics, or attacks that do not resemble its synthetic and collected sources. Refusal labels are especially value-laden: concise boundary-setting, partial safe completion, redirection, and policy explanation may be judged differently by users and annotators. Aggregate scores can hide false refusals for benign users and false negatives on rare harms. Audit confusion matrices by category, language, prompt length, adversarial family, and task; red-team against adaptive attackers rather than treating one benchmark pass as security assurance.
