Open-ended evaluations such as AlpacaEval and MT-Bench generally use researcher-designed prompts and uniform judge rules, whereas Arena relies on uncontrolled online pairwise voting. WildBench combines these paradigms: tasks come from real-user logs but are curated into a fixed and reproducible offline set; evaluation uses both model comparison and task-specific checklists with multiple baseline anchors. It also explicitly addresses verbosity bias. The novelty is not the first use of real prompts or LLM judges, but the integration of difficult real-task selection, instance-specific criteria, pairwise and pointwise metrics, and length calibration into an auditable protocol.
