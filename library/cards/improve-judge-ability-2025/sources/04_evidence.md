**Claim.** The two stages, not merely a larger judge dataset, yield a strong open judge with 40K training records. **Controlled setup.** On RewardBench, the paper compares Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, SFT-only, DPO-only, and the same 32B base trained by SFT then DPO; evaluation uses greedy decoding (Table 1; Figure 3). **Result.** The final 32B model scores 92.7, versus 87.0 for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, 87.7 for SFT-only, and 91.8 for DPO-only; it matches the 92.7 score of a 70B SFR judge trained on 680K records. **Boundary.** This supports the specified data and protocol on RewardBench, not a causal claim that every gain is due to filtering or that it transfers to all domains.

On an internal policy model, DPO data annotated by GPT-4o and RISE raise AlignBench average from 7.20 to 7.40 and 7.60, respectively. This supports signal utility but is not a public independently reproducible experiment. Other models in Table 1 use leaderboard scores, while the authors evaluate only the RISE and Qwen series; cross-model comparisons therefore also reflect release differences.
