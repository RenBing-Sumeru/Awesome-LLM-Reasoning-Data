Earlier generative judges commonly use SFT to imitate verdicts or apply preference training without a judge-specific warm-up, often using 600K–900K records. RISE changes the training decision: it reserves teacher-inconsistent, hard prompts for self-sampled DPO pairs while using order-consistent GPT-4o critiques for SFT style adaptation. The contribution is this staged data contract and its tests of general ability and downstream policy training, rather than the invention of SFT, DPO, or pairwise judging.

Relative to simply imitating a teacher verdict, it retains critique text and makes the second phase compare the model's own correct and incorrect judgments. That makes error localization, formatting adaptation, and preference selection separately auditable, though it remains dependent on teacher and source-preference labels.
