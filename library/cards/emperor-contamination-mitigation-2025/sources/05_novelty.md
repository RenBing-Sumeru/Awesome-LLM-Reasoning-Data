Earlier mitigation work commonly judged success by aggregate accuracy drop or matching, which can conceal changed questions and offsetting item errors. This paper changes the decision object to paired item-level outcome vectors under both clean and deliberately contaminated models. Its actual novelty is the joint fidelity-resistance contract and controlled contamination pipeline, not the individual text perturbations. It also makes explicit why leaving a benchmark untouched has perfect fidelity but can still have poor resistance.
