The ICLR paper reports that coordinated offline selection, online explorability filtering, and replay can retain competitive mathematical reasoning performance while using a 20% prompt subset and reducing rollout cost. Comparisons and ablations examine the contribution of the selection signals and online allocation stages. This evidence supports DEPO as a compute-efficient policy for the reported models, verifier, tasks, and budgets. It does not establish that the selected subset is universally higher quality, that reduced compute transfers to other verifiers, or that the unavailable sample histories can be independently reproduced.
