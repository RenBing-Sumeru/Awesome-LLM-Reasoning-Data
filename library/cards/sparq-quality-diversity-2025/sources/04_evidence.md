For the main search comparison, static uniform improves the reported MATH pass@1 of the MATH-SFT Gemma-2-9B student from 38% to 47%. With 100K generated problems and 96 inference samples on AIME, the paper reports 20% for the SFT baseline and 25% for static uniform. Dynamic diverse generation discovers more skill-set combinations—5,000 after 100K generated problems versus 4,250 for static uniform—but its final MATH result is 44%, below static uniform’s 47%.

Fixed-budget filtering isolates a different question from generation policy. Joint quality-diversity filtering reaches 45.5% MATH with 32K SFT examples, within 1.5 points of static uniform trained on 500K tuples. Diversity-only filtering improves AIME pass@96 from 19% for random filtering to 25%; the gap becomes visible from 16 inference samples onward. Higher mean paper-defined quality correlates with better MATH performance for training sets of at least 8,192 examples.

The paper also audits the proxy. Gemini-2.5-flash produces alternative solutions for 1,000 generated pairs, and intended-answer agreement is used as a model-based validity label. The resulting bins show that lower solve rate correlates with lower validity. Appendix B then tests whether 16 child mutations can identify invalid parents; valid and invalid groups have the same mean child-parent score difference of 0.03 and about 28% zero-score children, so the proposed perturbative signal fails to separate them.

These results show how the recipe behaves under the reported Gemma-2 models, learned skills, and answer checker. They do not establish that every retained row is valid, diverse in a semantic sense, uncontaminated, or legally reusable. No archive or selected tuples were released for independent recounting.
