Most synthetic reasoning pipelines either keep questions fixed and sample additional solutions or generate new questions without an explicit search archive. SPARQ changes the searched object to problem statements and intended solutions. A current student’s solve rate supplies a difficulty-shaped quality signal, while model-assigned skill cells supply a behavioral descriptor for quality-diversity selection. Successful verifier rollouts are then recycled as SFT traces.

The four archive policies make the quality/diversity tradeoff operational. Static versus dynamic determines whether descendants can become future parents; uniform versus diverse determines whether selection is over individual examples or skill cells. This design exposes a concrete direction signal: the policy that produces the most measured skill coverage is not the one that gives the strongest downstream model, and recursively selecting difficult items can amplify invalid parents by reward-hacking the quality proxy.

The paper also tests recursive problem self-generation with Gemma-2-2B and Gemma-2-9B acting as both generator and student, reporting relative gains after 300K children. What is not new should remain explicit: numerical answer checking, SFT, evolutionary mutation, skill tagging, and solve-rate curricula all have prior lineages. SPARQ’s contribution is their assembly and systematic ablation for open-ended math-problem data, not a released dataset or correctness verifier.
