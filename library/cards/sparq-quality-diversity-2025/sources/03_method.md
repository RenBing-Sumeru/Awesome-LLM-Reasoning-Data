The working set begins with the 7.5K MATH training pairs. The student is pretrained Gemma-2-9B fine-tuned on those seeds for three epochs. Unless an ablation changes the role assignment, Gemma-2-27B-it generates child problem-solution pairs and Gemma-2-9B-it labels their skills. Appendix F prints prompts that pass the parent problem and solution to the mutation model, request a novel problem plus solution with an intended numerical answer, and ask the classifier for at most three relevant skills.

Each mutation step samples 64 parents. The generator writes children, the classifier assigns skill cells, and the student produces 16 solution rollouts per child. Numerical final-answer agreement with the intended answer produces binary verification labels. Solve rate aggregates them; quality is (1-)solve-rate inside inclusive [0.1, 0.9] and zero outside. Every child enters the archive. Depending on the variant, the working set stays fixed, holds the globally highest-quality items, or maintains capped high-quality populations within skill cells.

Each main method runs for at most 5,000 mutation steps, giving 320K child problem-solution pairs and roughly 5M student verification solutions. Because most children have solve rate zero, the authors report an average of about 80K retained unique problems and 500K problem/successful-solution tuples per method. The multiple successful solutions per problem bias tuple counts toward easier retained questions. An ablation removes 75% of successful traces for problems with solve rate at least 0.5, shrinking one 500K-tuple set to 350K with mixed downstream effects.

For fixed-budget analysis, the authors take a 300K static-uniform archive, remove zero-quality pairs, keep one successful student solution per question, and compare quality, diversity, joint QD, and random sampling at budgets from (2^{12}) to (2^{15}). Downstream training uses one epoch, batch size 16, learning rate 2e-6 with cosine decay to 2e-7, on an 8x8 TPUv4 slice. Initial SFT settings beyond three epochs, generation hardware, temperatures, seeds, parser rules, and answer-normalization details are unknown.
