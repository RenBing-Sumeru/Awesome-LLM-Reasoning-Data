SPARQ separates mutation, policy-relative verification, and archive management. In the main configuration, Gemma-2-27B-it receives a parent problem and solution and emits a novel child problem plus intended solution. A MATH-SFT Gemma-2-9B student then attempts the child 16 times. A rollout succeeds when its extracted numerical final answer agrees with the intended answer. If the agreement fraction is (p), quality is (1-p) when (p\in[0.1,0.9]) and zero otherwise. With 16 attempts, this effectively retains children solved between 2 and 14 times.

Gemma-2-9B-it assigns each child no more than three skills from the 100 most frequent skills in the seed set. Those skill sets define diversity cells. Static variants keep mutating only the seed working set; dynamic variants insert high-quality children and evict low-quality members. Uniform variants sample individual parents uniformly; diverse variants first sample a skill cell uniformly. All generated children enter the archive, but only positive-quality children survive final filtering.

The final supervision is student-authored. Every retained problem is paired with each successful student verification, rather than with the generator’s intended solution. Thus solve-rate outcomes serve both as a construction score and a source of reasoning traces. This is a quality-diversity data curriculum, not an RLVR optimizer: the reported downstream update is SFT, and the numerical checker operates only at final-answer level.
