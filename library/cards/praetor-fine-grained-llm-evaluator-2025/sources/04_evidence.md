The authors compare Praetor with existing specialized evaluators and general instruction-tuned models on several judge benchmarks, separately examining pointwise, pairwise, Chinese, and English settings. Praetor achieves leading results on multiple tasks and its generated critiques can guide response revision, suggesting that instance-level criteria and multi-task training do not sacrifice basic discrimination. However, the training data, model construction, and teacher supervision all differ from baselines, so the gains cannot be attributed solely to criterion customization.
