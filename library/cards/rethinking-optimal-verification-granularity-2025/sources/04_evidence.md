Profiling shows that more than half of two-step PRM score changes are below 1% of the score range in one reported setting, motivating the claim that some adjacent checks are redundant. Across model pairs, the best g changes with generator capability and budget: with Qwen2.5-Math-7B plus Skywork-o1-1.5B, g = 3 reaches about four percentage points higher peak MATH-500 accuracy than g = 1 at medium-to-high compute, while the weaker non-math Llama generator benefits from denser verification.

On MATH-500 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Skywork-o1-1.5B, the validation-tuned adaptive methods report gains of up to 3.1 points over Beam Search and 3.6 over Best-of-N, with compute reductions above 52%. At n = 128, validation-tuned CM-g reports 89.9% accuracy using 11,086 modeled FLOPs versus 24,021 for the beam-search baseline. A three-seed appendix checks stability for one model pair.

These are comparisons of search policies under a paper-specific FLOPs proxy, step delimiter, PRM, and benchmark. They do not verify that every PRM score is calibrated, every retained prefix is correct, or every rejected branch is low quality; benchmark accuracy is not record-level trace-quality evidence.
