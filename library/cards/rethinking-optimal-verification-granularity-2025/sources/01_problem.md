Verifier-guided test-time search usually chooses one fixed intervention scale: score every reasoning step as in beam search, or wait until complete answers as in Best-of-N. Frequent checks can prune errors early but repeatedly run an expensive process reward model; sparse checks reduce verifier cost and permit broader exploration but can let bad prefixes grow. The paper asks how verification frequency should depend on generator strength, verifier strength, task difficulty, and compute budget.

The evidence object is a search trajectory, not a released prompt-to-trace corpus: a math problem, newline-delimited reasoning steps, candidate prefixes of g steps, scalar PRM scores, beam/branch decisions, rejected paths, generation and verification compute, and the selected final solution. MATH-250 is used to choose policies and MATH-500/AIME to evaluate them; these benchmark records do not themselves constitute a reusable VG-Search trajectory release.
