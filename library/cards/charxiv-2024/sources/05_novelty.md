Prior chart QA benchmarks often use simplified chart styles, template-like questions, or distributions that allow brittle shortcuts. CharXiv changes the object to real scientific figures and separates basic descriptive reading from harder reasoning. The direction signal is that multimodal reasoning evaluation must carry source-image provenance and answer-verification metadata, not just a final score. The quality signal is human selection and answer verification plus a public evaluator. What is not new: image QA, chart QA, and LLM-as-judge scoring already exist. Before reuse, inspect dataset license, chart-paper provenance, split exposure, evaluator prompts, and whether public examples have entered model training corpora.
