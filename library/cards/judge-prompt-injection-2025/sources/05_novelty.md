The closest prior practice uses manually written injection strings or gradient jailbreaks that assume a known full prompt. JudgeDeceiver changes the attack object from inducing a generic unsafe completion to forcing a judge's discrete candidate choice when competing responses and the target position are hidden. Its shadow-candidate construction and index-token loss target that uncertainty, while the low-perplexity objective is an adaptation for detection evasion rather than a new general text-attack optimizer.

It retains discrete gradient optimization from prior adversarial-prompt work; what is new in this framing is the position-robust verdict objective and its evaluation against swap-style testing.

A high win rate at one fixed position could reveal ordinary position bias; the paper instead asks whether the attack survives deliberate repositioning.
