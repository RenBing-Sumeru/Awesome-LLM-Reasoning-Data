Reasoning models can improve difficult answers by thinking longer, but long chains often contain repeated calculations, restated conclusions, and late reflections that do not change the final answer. A fixed decoding limit treats every request alike: it allows easy requests to overthink and may still stop difficult requests before their solution is complete. This is costly in batch serving, where redundant thoughts occupy decoding capacity and delay other users.

The paper asks whether redundant or insufficient reasoning can be recognized while a chain of thought is being generated, without retraining either the reasoning model or a large reward model. Its operational aim is to make continuation conditional on the state of the current trace: stop or compress thoughts that already establish an answer, but retain or guide computation when the trace has not yet reached a usable conclusion.
