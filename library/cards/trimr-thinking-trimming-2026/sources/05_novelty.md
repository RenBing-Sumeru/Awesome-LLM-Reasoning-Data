Previous efficiency methods often impose a fixed length rule, train a new reasoning policy, or rank completed answers with a costly reward model. TrimR instead uses small, local checks during generation: whether a sub-thought has a conclusion and whether newly produced conclusions repeat earlier ones. This makes trimming responsive to the current trace rather than to a global length budget alone.

Its second contribution is to treat that local controller as a serving component. The paper combines overthinking detection, underthinking intervention, repetition truncation, and asynchronous execution in one training-free system. The distinctive contribution is not merely shorter reasoning text; it is a concrete policy for spending test-time computation where the trace has not already demonstrated answer convergence.
