The final ICLR paper evaluates TrimR on MATH500, AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and GPQA, with QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, and Pangu-R-38B among the reported reasoning models. It also evaluates the serving design on Ascend NPUs and vLLM under large-batch workloads. The comparisons therefore concern both task outputs and the operational consequences of trimming intermediate reasoning.

The authors report reasoning-runtime improvements of up to 70% with negligible accuracy impact under their reported settings. This is evidence for the stated deployment objective, but it is not evidence that every long trace is wasteful: results depend on the verifier's answer-existence and equivalence judgments, the segmentation markers, model-specific thinking style, and the workload mix. Replication should preserve these conditions and publish accuracy, latency, tokens, verifier overhead, and throughput together.
