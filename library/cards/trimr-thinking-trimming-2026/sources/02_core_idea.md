TrimR treats a long reasoning trace as a sequence of sub-thoughts rather than one indivisible completion. A lightweight instruction-tuned verifier checks whether a sub-thought already contains a conclusion and whether successive conclusions are equivalent. Repeated settled conclusions are evidence of overthinking; failure to reach a conclusion within the available budget is evidence that the current path may be underthinking. These observations become control signals, not training labels.

The key idea is to make a small verifier an online traffic controller for a larger reasoning model. It does not claim to certify the mathematical truth of every thought. Instead, it determines whether further tokens are likely to be redundant, whether a trace has failed to make progress, and how a gentle or forceful prompt should intervene. This connects local thought structure to a test-time decision about preserving or releasing compute.
