The reconstructable pipeline is:

`public or App-Store-derived task instruction -> manual instruction check -> emulator rollout -> per-step candidate extraction -> human/current-verifier correct-action label -> entropy-guided human correction -> P3 pair expansion plus sampled reverse-action pairs -> Q-LoRA verifier training -> batched candidate scoring -> Android execution and memory update -> benchmark terminal evaluation`.

**Inputs and collection.** Task instructions come from the cited *On the Effects of Data Scale on Computer Control Agents* source and *Android in the Wild*, plus LLM-synthesized tasks based on App Store descriptions. Synthetic instructions are manually verified, but the synthesis model, prompt, decoding, source counts, revisions, and acceptance rubric are unknown. Tasks run on an Android emulator cluster spanning more than 90 apps in seven categories. Round 1 is a human cold start: annotators choose the correct action from an extracted list at each step, yielding 9K cumulative pairs. The paper reports about four minutes per task and five hours total, without task or annotator counts (Paper §5.2, pp. 8–9).

**Iterative annotation and selection.** In subsequent rounds, the preceding V-Droid checkpoint executes tasks described as out of domain. Entropy of the candidate-score distribution triages likely mistakes; humans inspect and correct error regions, then execution resumes toward success. Cumulative pair counts reach 27K, 55K, and 110K. AndroidLab and MobileAgentBench apps are excluded from training, but AndroidWorld is explicitly in domain. Operational entropy thresholds, human-review rubric, alternative-valid-action policy, retries, failed-task retention, duplicate handling, and immutable splits are unknown. The repository preview's random 99/1 split is not evidence for the paper run.

**Pair construction and feedback.** At a state with N candidates, one labeled action is paired against each of the other N-1 actions. `chosen` and `rejected` are complete verifier prompts rather than action IDs. Logistic preference loss increases the correct score over the alternative. A separate 2.5% subset is built by executing wrong actions and training on mapped reverse actions; random downsampling prevents the observed repeated-back collapse. This supplies state-action-level pairwise process supervision and a learned scalar score, not trajectory-level RLVR reward.

**Model and execution.** The verifier is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct-4-bit plus an MLP score head, trained with Q-LoRA rank 16, learning rate 1e-4, 10 warm-up steps, reduction to 1e-6, and 20 epochs in the final round. The 110K run reportedly takes about 90 hours on 16 Nvidia A100 40GB GPUs. At inference, candidates are scored in batches with vLLM prefix caching; 50.3 actions are evaluated per step on average, using two RTX 4090 GPUs for the reported latency unless otherwise stated. The public one-GPU `train.sh`, maximum length 2800, LoRA alpha 32, and dropout 0.05 describe the preview or released adapter and are not proven to reproduce every reported setting (Paper §§5.3–6.2; HF revision `4b2ed82d9f10486dd02376b1ccbad92842e3dc7a`).

**Outputs and replay boundary.** The highest-scoring candidate is executed, producing the next UI state. GPT-4 summarizes the action and UI change into working memory; action completion for open/type/answer uses another undisclosed LLM. Iteration stops when `complete task` is selected, then AndroidWorld, AndroidLab, or MobileAgentBench applies its own success contract. Reproduction must pin arXiv v5, GitHub commit `8d549027634abe65a5721fe6bc3b5e84475db2f6`, the HF revision above, Android/emulator/app and benchmark versions, UI serialization, GPT-4 snapshot/prompt/settings, the action-completion model, seeds, batch/optimizer/scheduler details, and the complete data manifest. Most of these are unavailable.
