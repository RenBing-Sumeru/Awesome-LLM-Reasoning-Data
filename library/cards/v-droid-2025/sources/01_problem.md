The stable Atlas identifier is `v-droid-2025`, reflecting the paper's first arXiv year, but the bibliographic identity used here is the camera-ready one: *V-Droid: Advancing Mobile GUI Agent Through Generative Verifiers*, accepted at ACM MobiCom 2026 with DOI `10.1145/3795866.3796681`. The arXiv abstract page still displays the earlier title, *Advancing Mobile GUI Agents: A Verifier-Driven Approach to Practical Deployment*; arXiv v5 dated 2026-02-21 carries the final title and publication year. Keeping the assigned ID while pinning the v5 identity prevents title/year drift from silently mixing results (arXiv v5, p. 1; MobiCom 2026 accepted-paper page).

The paper addresses a specific mobile-agent bottleneck: a general LLM asked to generate a free-form Android action can hallucinate, emit an invalid coordinate, or spend substantial decoding time, while a hand-written action policy does not scale across apps. V-Droid instead extracts the currently available actions and asks a learned verifier to score whether each candidate helps complete the task. The research question is therefore how to construct step-level feedback from Android interaction traces and use it to choose actions, not how to release a new Android benchmark or train a policy by environment reward.

The internal data object is richer than the public artifact. A successful episode has a goal, per-step UI state, working memory, candidate-action set, one labeled correct action, rejected alternatives, verifier scores and entropy, the executed action, next state, occasional correction, and a completion signal. P3 transforms each state with N candidates into N-1 correct-versus-alternative pairs. The repository exposes only five `{chosen, rejected}` prompt pairs; it does not release the reported 110K pairs, trajectories, task/app manifests, score vectors, corrections, terminal evidence, or immutable splits. Accordingly, `artifacts.data` remains `null` (Paper §§4–5; official repository at commit `8d549027634abe65a5721fe6bc3b5e84475db2f6`).

This fits `environment_agent_trajectory_data` because the preferences are derived from executed multi-step Android traces, and `preference_reward_feedback_data` because supervision is a per-state pairwise ordering learned as a scalar action score. The Card reaches L4 content depth for screening through the full v5/v1 papers and pinned official artifacts, while preserving its accepted `L3_summary_ready` curation level. It supports analysis of the recipe and partial model/code replay, not a claim that the training corpus is available, licensed, independently reproduced, or quality-certified by benchmark scores.
