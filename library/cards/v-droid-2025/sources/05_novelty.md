The closest data-side baseline is AndroidControl/On the Effects of Data Scale, from which V-Droid draws task instructions: it provides human Android trajectories and evaluates next-action prediction under data scaling. Android in the Wild supplies another public task source. Existing mobile agents also use accessibility trees, action histories, LLM planning, and benchmark terminal success. Against this baseline, V-Droid's concrete change is not a new environment but a different feedback object: one correct action at a trace state is contrasted with every extracted alternative and learned as a reusable scalar scoring interface.

The second change is the human-agent construction loop. A fully human 9K-pair cold start trains the first verifier; later verifier rounds execute new tasks, entropy prioritizes likely errors, humans repair selected regions, and the corrected trace expands the next training set. This couples collection, selection, and verifier improvement across 9K, 27K, 55K, and 110K cumulative pairs. The entropy signal is a triage heuristic rather than a correctness label, so the novelty is the allocation mechanism, not a guarantee that automated annotation becomes reliable.

The third change is explicit process training for recovery. Wrong actions are executed to produce erroneous states, a fixed inverse-action mapping proposes recovery, and a small sampled preference subset teaches the reverse action. The reported collapse under excessive recovery data makes the 2.5% mixture a meaningful selection decision rather than simple data scaling. It also supplies a reusable audit question for other trajectory datasets: whether corrective examples improve recovery or merely induce a high-frequency fallback action.

Several components are inherited or integrated rather than novel: Android Accessibility Service, rule-based candidate extraction, Llama-3.1-8B, Q-LoRA, logistic pairwise loss, vLLM prefix caching, GPT-4 working memory, and AndroidWorld/AndroidLab/MobileAgentBench. P3 also does not expose an environment-verifiable reward and does not train an RL policy by RLVR; it trains an action verifier/reward model from step preferences.

For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is the explicit mapping from executed trajectory state to pairwise process preference to online scalar selection, together with an uncertainty-guided annotation loop. Before treating it as a reusable open recipe, a researcher still must obtain the 110K records, check multiple-valid-action false negatives, pin the HTML/XML serializer, reproduce GPT-4 memory, reconcile paper/code/model revisions, and resolve code/data rights.
