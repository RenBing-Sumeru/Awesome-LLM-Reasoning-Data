For the assigned `environment_agent_trajectory_data` track, V-Droid is most useful as a construction and audit recipe, not as a downloadable trajectory corpus. A collector can preserve the internal episode object that the paper implies—goal, UI state and serializer version, working memory, complete candidate set, one or more valid actions, score vector and entropy, label source, execution result, next state, correction, terminal evidence, and app/environment provenance—then explicitly compare it with the much thinner released `{chosen, rejected}` prompt object.

For `preference_reward_feedback_data`, the method supplies a concrete process-feedback baseline: expand one decision state into correct-versus-alternative pairs, train a scalar verifier, and compare it with multi-positive preferences, state-change verifiers, terminal-success labels, or calibrated uncertainty. A useful replication would record alternative-valid-action adjudication and measure false-negative rates rather than assuming all unselected actions are wrong. Entropy accuracy/AUC and the repeated-back collapse provide ready-made audit slices for selection and reward-hacking analysis.

The four-round human-agent loop can inform annotation budgeting. Researchers can test a fully human cold start, uncertainty-guided correction, random versus diversity-aware erroneous-state sampling, and fixed versus learned recovery actions, while holding the task/app mixture and verifier checkpoint constant. That controlled design would separate the effects that V-Droid's 9K-to-110K curve currently combines. Store failed, timed-out, abandoned, and unflagged trajectories rather than retaining only corrected-to-success paths.
