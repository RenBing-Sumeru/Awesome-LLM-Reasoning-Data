The core evidence is the official dataset release and the paper's split counts: 7,787 total questions, 5,197 Easy, and 2,590 Challenge. The benchmark's audit value comes from row-level answer keys and the reproducible subset definition using baseline failures. The paper reports that then-current systems were far from human-level performance on Challenge, supporting the claim that shallow methods did not solve this surface. The evidence boundary is historical: modern models may have seen ARC directly, prompt format can move scores, and aggregate accuracy does not show whether a model used scientific reasoning or memorized the answer.
