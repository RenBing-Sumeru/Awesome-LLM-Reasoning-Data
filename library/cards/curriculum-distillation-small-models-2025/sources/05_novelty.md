The prior-work baseline is direct or statically mixed teacher-trace distillation. The paper's own single-stage controls train separately on Instruct, NoThink, NoRethink, Think, or the condensed Think solution; Mix-Long randomly combines Instruct and Think outputs at 4:1. Curriculum Distillation changes the selection interface in two ways: every retained prompt has a correct output under all four modes, and the chosen target mode depends on both MATH difficulty and whether training is in Stage 1 or Stage 2.

The direction signal for reasoning-data research is this factorization of the recipe. Teacher capability, response mode, final-answer acceptance, task difficulty, and training order become independently nameable variables rather than one undifferentiated “strong-teacher” dataset. The matched prompt pool also makes mode comparisons less confounded by different prompt sets, although the same intersection rule introduces selection bias toward prompts solvable across all modes.

Teacher distillation, rejection sampling with an answer checker, MATH difficulty labels, and two-stage SFT are not individually new. The paper's concrete contribution is their conditional composition: learnable Instruct traces and dense NoThink solutions establish a foundation, then NoRethink traces externalize reasoning on Level 5. Before reuse, a builder would still need to test whether the fixed Level 5 boundary, prompt-defined modes, and answer-only verifier transfer beyond these Qwen-derived 3B students.
