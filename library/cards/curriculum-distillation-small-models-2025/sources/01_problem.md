The official EMNLP 2025 paper asks why direct reasoning distillation into 3B students underperforms even when the teacher is stronger. Long Think traces from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B can be too complex for a small student to imitate, while its compressed NoThink answers can omit the procedural scaffolding that makes general mathematical reasoning learnable. The decision problem is therefore not merely which teacher is strongest, but which teacher response mode should supervise each difficulty band and at what point in training. (Paper Sections 1 and 4.1, pp. 7412-7417.)

The constructed object begins with the 7,500 MATH training problems and their five difficulty levels. For each problem, the authors seek correct Instruct, Think, NoRethink, and NoThink responses from two 32B teachers, retain the Think response's condensed solution as another target, and use the surviving problems to assemble two stage-specific SFT mixtures. This is a data-construction/open-release-track entry because teacher identity, trace form, final-answer filtering, difficulty, and curriculum order are all construction variables; it is not evidence that long reasoning is intrinsically faithful or that the unreleased corpus is safe to reuse.

The Card reaches the paper's L4 evidence boundary through the official proceedings PDF, DOI, BibTeX, affiliations, and Responsible NLP Checklist. That boundary stops short of reusable artifacts: no appendix, generated dataset, code, retained-index manifest, project page, or student checkpoint is supplied by the verified official materials.
