- **Answer-only feedback:** Math-Verify checks the extracted final answer, not intermediate-step validity or faithfulness. A fluent Think or NoRethink trace can pass with invalid, redundant, or post-hoc reasoning.
- **Intersection-selection bias:** Requiring all four conditions to succeed removes 1,055 of 7,500 MATH problems. The reported curriculum is therefore trained on a subset that may be unusually robust across both teachers and all elicitation modes.
- **Prompt-defined modes:** NoRethink bans a short keyword list and NoThink injects a completed-thought template. These interventions can change surface wording without cleanly isolating an internal System-1/System-2 mechanism; that interpretation remains an author hypothesis.
- **Missing failures and lineage:** The 6,445 retained indices, per-attempt generations, Math-Verify outputs, rejected traces, full prompts, seeds, checkpoint revisions, and curriculum manifests are not released. It is impossible to audit false acceptances, failure retention, or version drift row by row.
- **Release and license gap:** No official generated dataset, construction code, project page, or student checkpoint was identified. The paper is CC BY 4.0, but the generated corpus's redistribution terms and the compatibility of its MATH-derived content are not established; the checklist marks the license question not applicable.
- **Reproducibility gap:** The main text reports optimizer settings but not hardware, GPU hours, stage-specific steps, or checkpoint selection. This conflicts with the official checklist's “yes” response for computational budget and infrastructure reporting.
- **Narrow scope:** The evidence covers English mathematical reasoning, MATH difficulty bands, two Qwen2.5 3B students, and Qwen-derived 32B teachers. Code, general instruction following, multimodal tasks, multilingual settings, other capacities, and learned difficulty measures are untested.
- **Evaluation and attribution:** Decontamination or overlap checks for AIME, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, and OlympiadBench are not reported. Benchmark gains combine the intersection filter, teacher choice, trace mode, curriculum order, and two-stage optimization, so they are not proof of corpus quality or a clean causal estimate of any one component.
