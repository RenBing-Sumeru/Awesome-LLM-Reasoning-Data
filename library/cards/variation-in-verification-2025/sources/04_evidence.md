Across the tested matrix, the authors report that easier problems raise TPR while TNR has no consistent difficulty trend. Errors from stronger generators are harder to detect, and verifier generation capability correlates with balanced accuracy in a difficulty-dependent pattern. Case studies show two mechanisms: a verifier can solve a hard problem incorrectly and reject a correct response, while a strong generator can make an early error yet present an internally consistent trace that the verifier accepts.

The TTS experiments report one mathematics slice where verification with fixed GPT-4o reduces the Gemma2-9B versus Gemma2-27B gap from 10.3 points to 2.5 points, closing 75.7% of the original gap. This is a result under the paper's retained-pool metric and selected difficulty range, not proof of general verifier quality or data quality.

The official code repository supplies verification and visualization code. The two MIT-labelled Hugging Face cards expose candidate traces, labels, sampling metadata, verifier rationales, and verdicts. Their existence supports release claims, but does not validate label accuracy, source rights, decontamination, or completeness.
