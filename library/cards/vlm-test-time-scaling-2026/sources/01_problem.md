Text-only test-time scaling does not automatically transfer to models that must preserve information from an image while producing a long reasoning chain. Prior work has reported mixed or negative results for small vision-language models, leaving it unclear whether more prompting, sampling, aggregation, or refinement is useful, which model scales benefit, and whether the gain survives beyond a single visual benchmark.

The paper asks whether standard language-model scaling strategies work directly for vision-language models without changing weights. It also asks when more inference becomes harmful. A useful answer must compare accuracy and runtime across reasoning and perception tasks, because a method that improves mathematical visual reasoning but encourages unsupported visual assumptions is not a generally safe compute-allocation policy.
