The work evaluates a common family of training-free scaling operations instead of proposing a new model. Single-pass methods ask for a chain of thought, structured decomposition, planning, image description, or a scene graph. Multi-pass methods sample several chains and vote or aggregate them, or sequentially refine a previous chain. This permits an apples-to-apples comparison of what kind of additional computation is spent before an LVLM emits its final answer.

The central finding is conditional rather than universal. Capable small LVLMs often gain the most from test-time scaling and can match or surpass a much larger baseline at a suitable budget. But extra reasoning can make a model overthink perception tasks. Attention analysis suggests that visual information is incorporated early in the chain, after which later text is increasingly driven by an internal representation rather than fresh visual grounding.
