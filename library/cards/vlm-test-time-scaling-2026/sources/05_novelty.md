The paper's contribution is a broad transfer test for test-time scaling, not a new multimodal reasoning prompt. It places language-model methods and VLM-specific baselines in the same evaluation over model scales, task types, and measured runtime. That comparison overturns a simple extrapolation from small text-only models: a small but capable LVLM may benefit sharply from extra inference even when earlier work suggested small language models would not.

Its other contribution is to treat visual grounding as part of the compute-allocation decision. A method is not successful just because it produces a longer chain or a higher reasoning score; it must preserve the image evidence needed for the task. The paper does not establish that attention is a causal verifier of grounding, but the attention and chain-quality analyses offer a concrete explanation for why a larger budget can improve logical visual tasks while harming straightforward perception.
