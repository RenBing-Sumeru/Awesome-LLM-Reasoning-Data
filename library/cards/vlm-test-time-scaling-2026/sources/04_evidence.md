The reported Qwen results show large gains for capable small models. On WeMath, Qwen3-VL-2B rises from 35.46 without scaling to 64.25 with self-consistency, while its average runtime rises from 0.1 to 40.2 seconds. Qwen3-VL-4B with self-consistency reaches 74.25 on WeMath and 62.95 on LogicVista; at about a nine-second budget, the paper reports that the 4B model can match the 32B baseline on HallusionBench and outperform it on LogicVista and WeMath.

The evidence also bounds the claim. On RealWorldQA, several reasoning-heavy methods reduce accuracy for some Qwen sizes, and self-refinement is especially unstable on certain Qwen2.5-VL settings. Across the study, simple CoT is identified as the strongest general runtime-performance compromise. The authors connect these patterns to attention behavior: as chains become verbose, attention to image tokens declines after an early visual-encoding window, increasing the risk of unsupported text-only reasoning.
