The arXiv abstract reports 250 carefully selected PyTorch ML workloads and shows the main empirical boundary: frontier models can often produce syntactically plausible kernels, but optimized correct kernels remain rare. The abstract reports that the best tested model solves 87% of tasks correctly, but only 10% are optimized with an average speedup of 1.12x. The official blog and repository provide the dataset, prompts, evaluation scripts, and leaderboard environment. The decisive instance-level evidence is executable: the generated kernel compiles, produces numerically matching outputs against PyTorch on randomized tests, and is timed against the reference. The evidence boundary is hardware- and stack-specific; speedups are not portable across GPUs, CUDA versions, compiler flags, tensor shapes, or timing policy.
