The closest baseline is not generic chain-of-thought distillation but WebDancer-style agent RFT/RL over ReAct search trajectories. WebSailor retains familiar components—Qwen-Agent, Search/Visit, rejection sampling, SFT masking of observations, group-relative policy optimization, and an answer judge. Its contribution is how these components are recomposed around high-uncertainty task generation and efficient agent rollout selection.

At the data-source layer, the paper replaces relatively direct question collection with rare-entity random walks, non-linear web graphs, subgraph sampling, and information obfuscation. At the trace layer, it does not directly imitate the expert LRM’s verbose thoughts: it preserves only successful behavior and reconstructs a short explanation for each step. This creates a distinct supervision object—successful state/action/observation paths paired with newly authored local rationales—whose faithfulness must be audited separately from answer correctness.

At the optimizer layer, DUPO modifies DAPO-style dynamic sampling for expensive multi-turn environments. It removes trivially all-correct cases before training and fills zero-variance slots by duplicating eligible samples already present in the batch, avoiding sequential rollout collection for replacement QAs. The paper attributes an approximate 2–3× speedup to this engineering change; the underlying group-relative objective, token-level loss, clipping, and reward composition build on prior work.

For reasoning-data research, the directional novelty is the coupling of task uncertainty, trace reconstruction, and online selection. It suggests that training-set “difficulty” should be represented not only by answer success but by search topology, number of interactions, and variance under repeated rollouts. Reuse still requires checking whether the reconstructed thoughts are faithful, whether ambiguity creates judge noise, and whether success-only filtering teaches brittle survivor behavior.
