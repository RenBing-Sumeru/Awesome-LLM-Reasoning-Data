Strict-substring deduplication is transparent but deliberately narrow. It cannot remove paraphrases, translations, templated variants, or semantically equivalent tasks. The paper does not provide a systematic overlap report against all 17 evaluation benchmarks, even though some source mixtures include competition and historical benchmark material.

The three reward families have different failure surfaces. Exact extraction can reject equivalent expressions or reward a correct final token without valid reasoning. Code checking retains at most eight randomly selected tests per item, so under-specified tests can admit incorrect programs. The 1.5B science verifier can make false-positive and false-negative entailment judgments, but the release does not publish calibration measurements or adjudicated error samples. Binary rewards reveal no partial-credit or reasoning-process validity.

Difficulty filtering inherits model and verifier biases. Removing every task missed by Qwen3-30B-A8B can discard genuinely hard or novel questions, while pass-rate gaps can reflect memorization, decoding variance, or reward bugs rather than learnable difficulty. The published row-level pass rates help analysis, but rejected candidates, stage-level counts by source, seeds, and decision reasons are missing.

License lineage needs record-level review. The dataset repository carries an MIT label and the code is Apache-2.0, while the paper names upstream material under MIT, Apache-2.0, CC BY 4.0, and CC BY-SA. The aggregate label does not demonstrate that attribution, share-alike, platform, and model-output obligations are satisfied for every row. The two released model repositories are separately labeled CC-BY-NC-4.0.

Release-state ambiguity also matters. Dataset revision `2e2790a962a3c099bfb5ea61389cbf98a5ea439b` is pinnable, but the live Reasoning360 main branch has evolved beyond the paper and has no release tag. Dataset Viewer exposes one train split, although online and offline evaluation Parquets coexist in the repository. A spot-check also surfaced mojibake in a LeetCode row; its prevalence is unknown.

Finally, the reported two-epoch direct-RL runs do not test an SFT-then-RL alternative, longer training, or fine-grained scaling and online mixture policies. On-policy responses, rejected rollouts, reward distributions, and failed verifier traces are not released, limiting audits of reward hacking and training dynamics. Coverage is English-centric and STEM-heavy despite the six-domain framing.
