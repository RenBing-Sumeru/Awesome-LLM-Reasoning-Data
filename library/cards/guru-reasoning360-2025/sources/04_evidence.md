The paper separates a controlled transfer study from full-mixture training. For the transfer study, GURU-18K samples 3K examples from each of the six domains and compares single-domain RL with uniform mixed-domain RL on Qwen2.5-7B-Base. Mathematics, code, and science transfer more readily across domains, whereas logic, simulation, and tables benefit more from in-domain data. Uniform mixed training often matches or exceeds single-domain training, but the authors' explanation that pretraining exposure drives these differences is an interpretation, not a measured property of the corpus.

A difficulty ablation compares raw and filtered mathematics. Filtering raises reported scores by 2.8 points on MATH500, 6.3 on AMC, and 5.9 on AIME24, while cross-domain effects are mixed: HumanEval falls 9.2 points, LiveCodeBench 0.4, and HiTab 3.0, whereas MultiHierTT rises 3.5. Response length also does not increase uniformly: the paper reports growth mainly for mathematics and science in the mixed setting, with domain-specific dynamics elsewhere. These results support the claim that selection and mixture choices alter training behavior; they do not establish that every retained row or verifier decision is correct.

For full training, the paper evaluates GURU-7B and GURU-32B on 17 benchmarks spanning the six domains plus IFEval and LiveBench. It reports aggregate averages of 41.17 and 52.68 and improvements over selected open RL baselines. Evaluation uses temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95, with pass-at-k or average-at-k variants depending on the benchmark. These scores show that the released recipe can produce useful checkpoints, but benchmark performance is not a direct data-quality audit.

Artifact evidence is comparatively strong. The pinned dataset revision is public and inspectable; the current code tree contains preprocessing, model filtering, reward routing, training, and evaluation components; two public model repositories expose immutable revisions; and the repository links public training logs. However, the current code main revision postdates the paper and has no paper-era release tag, so exact run replay remains uncertain.
