Open reinforcement-learning research for reasoning has centered on mathematics and code, where exact answers and unit tests make reward construction relatively straightforward. That concentration leaves two linked questions under-tested: whether gains learned in one reasoning domain transfer to others, and whether a single RL-trained model can improve broadly without sacrificing domains that require different output forms and checking procedures.

GURU frames the bottleneck as a data-and-feedback problem rather than only a model problem. A useful cross-domain corpus must join each prompt to a verifier that can score newly sampled policy responses. It must also control duplication, malformed examples, trivial questions, and rewards that are too weak to guide learning. A uniform question-answer file is insufficient because a symbolic answer, a program, an open-ended science explanation, a simulation output, and a table-derived answer demand different correctness contracts.

The paper therefore asks how to construct and test one RL-ready mixture spanning mathematics, code, science, logic, simulation, and tabular reasoning. The audit question is narrower than the reported benchmark ranking: what exactly is released, how each row was selected and checked, and which missing artifacts prevent replaying or challenging those decisions?
