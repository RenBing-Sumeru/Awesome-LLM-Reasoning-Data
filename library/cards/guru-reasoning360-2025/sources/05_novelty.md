GURU's main contribution is not a new response format. It packages heterogeneous reasoning tasks as one trainable relation between prompts and executable feedback contracts. Rule matching, symbolic normalization, code execution, and model-based science judgment are routed within a shared schema and optimization pipeline rather than flattened into a single answer-comparison rule.

The construction recipe also applies one empirical difficulty language across domains: 16 response trials from a weaker model and 16 from a stronger model. Pass-rate thresholds jointly target triviality, impossible or malformed questions, and anomalous weak-greater-than-strong behavior. Domain-specific gap thresholds preserve some local adaptation instead of pretending all reward channels have identical calibration.

Relative to mathematics- or code-only RLVR releases, GURU adds open-ended science, synthetic symbolic logic, code-input/output simulation, and hierarchical table reasoning. Relative to a model report, it releases the training mixture, row-level pass-rate fields, verifier inputs, preprocessing and reward code, checkpoints, evaluation tooling, and logs. The novelty is therefore an inspectable cross-domain construction-and-feedback package, not proof that mixing always transfers positively or that the final corpus is contamination-free.
