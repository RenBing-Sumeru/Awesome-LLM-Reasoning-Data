For direct experimentation, GURU is a ready-to-load six-domain GRPO mixture with the references and reward payloads needed by its domain router. The published weak- and strong-model pass rates support curriculum construction, difficulty reweighting, disagreement analysis, and controlled resampling without first recomputing 32 screening responses per candidate. The repository's reward modules can also serve as concrete starting points for rule, execution, and model-judge infrastructure.

For the Data Construction and Open Release Recipes track, the card is most useful as an end-to-end case study: upstream source selection leads to conservative deduplication, domain heuristics, empirical difficulty screening, verifier routing, and downstream training. The release makes those layers visible enough to compare alternative semantic deduplication, verifier calibration, test generation, and mixture policies. The controlled GURU-18K setup provides a natural baseline for studying positive and negative cross-domain transfer.

For auditing, the release is equally useful as a boundary case. It shows how apparently rich openness—data, code, models, and logs—can still omit the ledgers needed to explain why an individual candidate was rejected, which source license governs a row, or what rewards a policy actually received. A careful reuse should pin dataset revision `2e2790a962a3c099bfb5ea61389cbf98a5ea439b`, preserve `data_source`, `extra_info`, reward payloads, and both pass-rate fields, and independently audit evaluation overlap and upstream licenses.

The corpus should not be treated as a generic reasoning-trace dataset. It is best suited to answer-level RLVR and feedback-contract research; projects requiring archived policy trajectories, process supervision, preference pairs, calibrated scalar rewards, or multilingual coverage need additional data.
