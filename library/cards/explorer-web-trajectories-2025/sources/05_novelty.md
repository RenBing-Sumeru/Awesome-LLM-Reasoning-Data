Prior web-agent data work commonly begins with fixed human tasks, homepage-only/self-instruct proposals, synthetic grounding examples, or tutorial-guided replay. Explorer changes the construction order: low-level actions reveal real page affordances, the task is revised to remain consistent with those actions, and only after execution does a summarizer recover the final high-level intent. This makes task creation, behavior generation, and environmental grounding one coupled process.

The new data operation is the evolving task state. One accepted record preserves a path from abstract proposal through action-conditioned refinements to a final description, paired with multimodal page state and a learned success decision. The raw-to-accepted funnel, 60-process/50-hour collection run, cost model, and separate scroll-filtered SFT subset make the recipe more operational than a paper that reports only a final trajectory count.

The paper does not invent Playwright, ReAct-style interaction, set-of-mark screenshots, GPT-4o generation, LLM-as-a-judge filtering, Phi-3.5-Vision/Qwen2-VL backbones, or SFT. Its scale and integration should not be described as a new deterministic verifier or a complete open-data release. The learned verifier sees visible evidence rather than ground-truth server state, and the original live pages are not frozen.

For the atlas, the direction signal is exploration-driven task synthesis: allow the environment to shape the instruction instead of requiring a complete task before any action. Before reusing this pattern, inspect whether task revision creates hindsight labels, whether generator and verifier errors are correlated, whether failed paths are retained, whether page content can be redistributed, and whether per-record timestamps and snapshots make the lineage auditable. The 81% human-verifier agreement and missing corpus snapshot are central boundaries, not footnotes.
