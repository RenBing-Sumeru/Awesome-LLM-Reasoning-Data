The acceptance label is noisy and circular. GPT-4o participates in proposing, refining, summarizing, and judging; correlated blind spots can survive every stage. The 100-example audit records both false acceptance (0.14 of the normalized matrix) and false rejection (0.05), while the paper does not release all raw verifier outputs or stratify errors by site, task type, length, or safety boundary. Successful-only retention removes failures and near misses needed to recalibrate the filter, study recovery, or construct preference data.

The authors' qualitative failure analysis identifies grounding errors during refinement, unresponsive websites, summarization hallucination, technical barriers such as login/media/bot detection, and episodes incomplete at the step limit. The verifier can also mistake visible final-page evidence for actual completion because hidden server state and side effects are outside its observation. Curator inference: a model may exploit stylistic regularities shared by the generator and judge without learning robust task completion.

Live pages drift, disappear, personalize results, change layout, and trigger bot defenses. URLs alone cannot replay a record without timestamped page snapshots, browser/dependency versions, viewport, locale, cookies/session state, network logs, and exact prompts/model snapshots. Mind2Web-Live results further exclude 21 inaccessible tasks for the main table and select the maximum of three runs, so accessibility variance and reporting policy affect comparison.

Release coverage is incomplete. No official immutable 94K snapshot, raw 175K pool, 40K sample, approximately 30K final training set, membership mapping, rejected episodes, checksums, or corpus-specific license was identified. Temperature, seeds, maximum collection horizon, per-site counts, decontamination, source duplication, and train/evaluation overlap are unknown. The MIT repository license covers code, and ACL's CC BY 4.0 covers the paper text; neither establishes redistribution rights for captured screenshots, HTML, accessibility trees, task text, or third-party page content.

The paper states that no personal information is used during execution, that agents stop at CAPTCHA/login/payment, and that a subset is monitored for access-policy compliance. It does not publish a corpus-wide privacy/redaction audit, site-level terms/robots ledger, consent record, or request-rate log. These are missing disclosures, not evidence that the authors performed no controls.
