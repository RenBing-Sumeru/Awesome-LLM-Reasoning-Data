The strongest evidence about the data itself is the documented construction funnel and the verifier audit. Table 3 distinguishes 175K raw attempts from 94K accepted episodes and explicitly assigns the 49K unique URLs, 7.7 average steps, 720K images, 830M tokens, and 33.3M elements to the accepted set. Table 9 further reports 53K unique proposals, 94K final task descriptions, and 81K unique final descriptions. These counts establish scale and observed description diversity under the authors' procedure; they do not establish legality, decontamination, replayability, or item-level success.

On a random human-reviewed sample of 100 generated trajectories, Table 10 reports 81% agreement with the task verifier. Its normalized confusion matrix contains 0.14 human-labeled failures predicted successful and 0.05 human-labeled successes predicted failed. This directly demonstrates both false acceptance and false rejection. It is a calibration sample for the filter, not proof that all 94K accepted records are correct.

Under the authors' 83-task accessible subset of Mind2Web-Live, Table 5 reports full-task success of 18.1% for Explorer-4B versus 2.4% for Phi-3.5V, and 19.3% for Explorer-7B versus 14.5% for Qwen2-VL-7B. Appendix A states that these values are the maximum over three runs; on all 104 tasks, Appendix Table A.1 reports both Explorer models at 16.4%. On Multimodal-Mind2Web, synthetic-only Explorer-4B and Explorer-7B reach average step success of 37.4 and 43.0; Table 6 also shows that adding Mind2Web training changes the comparison. On MiniWob++ zero-shot, Table 8 reports 46.74 and 53.26 accuracy versus 45.28 for AgentTrek-7B and 36.96 for the Qwen2-VL-7B baseline.

Figure 3 reports monotonic improvement for 25%, 50%, and 100% data subsets averaged over three runs, but the ablation changes data amount within the selected training recipe rather than independently validating trajectory quality. Table A.3 also shows model-backbone sensitivity: full-task success is 4.8 for LLaVA-Mistral-7B, 13.3 for text-only Phi-3-mini, 18.1 for Phi-3.5V, and 19.3 for Qwen2-VL-7B. All benchmark results are author-reported; live-site selection, learned judging, backbone, and training setup constrain attribution.

The official repository contains synthesis, SFT, and evaluation code under MIT terms and showed no tagged release when checked. It provides command placeholders for local trajectory paths rather than an official corpus download. Code availability therefore supports recipe inspection, not a claim that the 94K episodes, rejected attempts, checksums, data card, or dataset license are public.
