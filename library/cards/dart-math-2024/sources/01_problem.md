The official NeurIPS 2024 paper studies a construction defect in rejection-tuned mathematics data: sampling the same number of raw responses for every prompt yields many accepted responses for easy prompts but poor coverage of hard prompts. In the paper's MetaMathQA-MATH analysis, 51.1% of Level-5 MATH training queries receive no augmented response, even though sufficiently many DeepSeekMath-7B-RL trials can eventually find a correct answer for most sampled test questions (Paper §2, Figure 2).

DART-Math asks how a fixed inference budget should be distributed across the original MATH and corrected GSM8K training queries before supervised instruction tuning. One final training record is a `query` plus a natural-language `response`; the richer accepted-response pools also retain source and response IDs, ground-truth and extracted answers, correctness, generator and decoding metadata, while query-info tables retain sampling counts and pass rates. The feedback is terminal-answer agreement, not a judgment of every reasoning step.

This belongs in Data Construction and Open Release Recipes because its central intervention is the allocation and filtering pipeline that changes which verified traces survive. It does not introduce new questions, step labels, an interactive environment, code-execution reasoning, or an RLVR objective. The Card reaches L4 by grounding the recipe, release schemas, controlled VRT comparisons, code limitations, and open audit questions in the paper, appendix, repository, and official Hugging Face artifacts.
