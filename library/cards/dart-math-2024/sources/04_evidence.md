The cleanest evidence is the equal-size comparison against VRT in Table 2. With 0.59M retained examples and greedy natural-language evaluation on six benchmarks, Llama3-8B VRT averages 35.2, Uniform 38.7, and Prop2Diff 39.7. Mistral-7B moves from 34.3 under VRT to 37.4 under Uniform and 38.8 under Prop2Diff. On MATH specifically, Prop2Diff improves Llama3-8B from 39.7 to 46.6 and Mistral-7B from 38.7 to 45.5. These are author-reported SFT results under the training/evaluation setup in Appendix B, not an independent reproduction.

The result is conditional rather than universal. For DeepSeekMath-7B, VRT/Uniform/Prop2Diff averages are 48.3/49.2/49.4; for Llama3-70B they are 48.5/49.1/49.3. Prop2Diff also lowers GSM8K relative to VRT for Llama3-8B (81.1 versus 81.7), DeepSeekMath-7B (86.8 versus 88.2), and Llama3-70B (89.6 versus 90.3). The authors therefore report larger gains on general 7–8B bases and difficult benchmarks, with smaller effects on strong or math-specialized bases and easy GSM8K (Paper §4.2, Table 2).

Coverage evidence isolates the intended distributional effect. Appendix Table 4 reports 99.6% coverage of MATH training queries for both DART-Math sets, versus 84.9% for the equal-size VRT baseline and 82.8% for MetaMath-MATH-AnsAug; Level-5 coverage is 99.1%, 62.9%, and 48.9%, respectively. Figure 4 further reports that removing the one-response coverage rule costs about eight GSM8K points at small data scale, while the gap shrinks as the dataset grows.

Official artifacts corroborate the release object: the checked Hard and Uniform revisions expose 585,392 and 590,705 two-column SFT rows, and the pool/query-info datasets expose accepted-response lineage and sampling statistics. They do not prove that all rationales are correct, licensed, diverse, or uncontaminated. Benchmark gains support the tested allocation recipe; they are not evidence of process-label quality.
