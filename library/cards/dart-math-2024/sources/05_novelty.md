Earlier rejection-tuning pipelines such as the MetaMathQA augmentation analyzed by the authors sample a fixed number of raw responses per query, then let the verifier's varying pass rate determine how many examples each prompt contributes. This makes the retained distribution an accidental function of prompt difficulty: hard prompts can disappear even when the generator could solve them with more trials.

DART changes the selection target. Uniform equalizes accepted responses per query, while Prop2Diff makes accepted count proportional to the generator-relative failure rate and separately enforces coverage. The new data object is not a different kind of rationale and the feedback interface remains final-answer checking; the concrete contribution is making allocation, target attainment, and per-query sampling metadata first-class parts of the construction recipe.

ToRA and MARIO had already spent extra computation on unsolved problems, and DART reuses standard CoT generation, rejection filtering, and SFT. The paper's Appendix A argues that those predecessors primarily optimize coverage, whereas DART explicitly controls the final difficulty distribution. This is a methodological change rather than merely a larger corpus: both DART datasets remain around 590k, and the comparison holds retained size fixed against VRT.

For reasoning-data research, the direction is important because it separates three quantities that are often conflated: raw generation trials, unique prompts covered, and accepted training responses. Reuse still requires checking whether the difficulty estimate transfers to another generator, whether parser errors correlate with difficulty, and whether gains persist after aligning raw synthesis compute—not only final dataset size.
