The contribution is to control the distribution of accepted reasoning traces by query difficulty rather than to replace the answer checker. Vanilla rejection tuning fixes the number of raw trials per query and retains whichever candidates pass. DARS-Uniform instead samples until every query has a target of `k_u` accepted responses; DARS-Prop2Diff assigns an accepted-response target proportional to difficulty, capped at `k_p` for the hardest queries, while attempting to preserve at least one synthetic response for every query. The final recipes use `k_u=40` and `k_p=192` and produce DART-Math-Uniform and DART-Math-Hard (Paper §3.2–§3.3).

Difficulty is measured by failure rate under DeepSeekMath-7B-RL. The paper body defines this as the proportion of incorrect responses; the official repository operationalizes it as `1-pass_rate`. That signal controls more sampling, not a scalar training reward. A regex/SymPy pipeline observes the generated text's extractable final answer and the reference answer, but cannot observe whether intermediate equations are valid, whether the rationale is faithful, or whether a correct result was reached through a shortcut.

The closest baseline is equal-raw-budget vanilla rejection tuning (VRT) at the same roughly 590k retained examples. MetaMathQA is the motivating public dataset whose accepted responses skew toward easier MATH prompts, while ToRA and MARIO previously increased sampling to improve coverage. DART's concrete difference is to target the retained per-query distribution explicitly and publish final SFT sets plus accepted-response pools and per-query sampling statistics; unlike process-supervision work, its native label remains answer-level.
