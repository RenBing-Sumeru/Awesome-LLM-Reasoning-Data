For the assigned Data Construction and Open Release Recipes track, DART-Math is a reusable blueprint for studying how a fixed generation budget becomes a training distribution. A controlled replication can hold prompts, generator, verifier, retained size, and SFT hyperparameters fixed while varying equal-raw-budget VRT, equal-accepted-count Uniform, and difficulty-proportional Prop2Diff. Researchers should report both raw synthesis compute and retained examples, because the paper's 590k-size control does not equalize the roughly 150 million raw trials spent by the reported DART run.

The final `query`/`response` sets are directly usable for answer-level math SFT, subject to upstream-license, overlap, and snapshot review. The pools and query-info tables are more useful for construction research: they support reallocation experiments, generator-relative difficulty studies, checker audits, and analyses of coverage versus repeated responses. They do not support process-supervision claims because they contain no validated step labels.

A stronger reuse protocol would pin source and release revisions; keep all raw candidates, extracted answers, checker decisions, and retry counts; validate a stratified sample with a second symbolic or human checker; compare multiple generators; and publish semantic-overlap results. Prop2Diff should be implemented as a deterministic script whose target calculation, rounding, raw cap, and seed are versioned rather than reconstructed manually from prose.

Reuse class: suitable for guarded SFT and construction-policy experiments after license/snapshot checks; suitable now as an audit and replication reference. It is not safe to treat the rationales as process-correct labels, and benchmark performance alone does not clear contamination, lineage, or verifier-robustness concerns.
