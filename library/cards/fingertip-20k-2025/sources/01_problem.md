The accepted OpenReview record identifies this work as an ICLR 2026 conference paper, and the official venue page lists it as an ICLR 2026 Poster. The stable atlas ID retains the 2025 preprint suffix, but the canonical publication year is 2026. The paper asks how a mobile agent can anticipate what a particular user wants to do and then execute that intent in a way conditioned on the user's history, rather than treating every phone task as a context-free instruction (Paper §§1–3; OpenReview and ICLR records).

FingerTip 20K studies this question through longitudinal human Android demonstrations collected during daily use. The paper reports 21,437 episodes from 95 mainland-China users across 506 apps, averaging 11.1 steps. One episode is a participant's current intent plus timestamp and scenario, user-linked context, ordered JPG screenshots, accessibility-tree XML observations, and JSONL GUI actions ending in an added `finish` marker. It is a human demonstration, not a unique task template, prompt, search tree, or model rollout (Paper Table 1, §4.2, Appendix A.4).

The work belongs in `environment_agent_trajectory_data` because the reusable object is a state–action episode tied to a real phone substrate and then consumed by two agent-facing tracks: proactive suggestion and personalized execution. It is both a data release and a benchmark, but not a packaged replayable environment. The public artifacts do not pin app/APK versions, accounts, initial device state, Android images, reset logic, or deterministic replay manifests.

The Card reaches L4 content depth while retaining L3 metadata status because several audit gaps remain unresolved. Most importantly, the official GitHub `total.csv` contains 20,000 rows from 83 users and 482 app identifiers, not the paper's 21,437/95/506 corpus; no official mapping explains the reduction. The evidence supports screening for offline SFT, benchmark evaluation, and release auditing, but not a claim that every retained demonstration succeeded, that the public index equals the full paper corpus, or that live execution can be reproduced deterministically.
