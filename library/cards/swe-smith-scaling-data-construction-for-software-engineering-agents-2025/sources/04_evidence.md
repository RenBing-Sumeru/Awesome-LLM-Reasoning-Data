The strongest evidence concerns construction scale and executable yield. Across all routes, 50.1% of generated candidates become retained tasks. Yield differs substantially by route: 96.9% for already validated patch combinations, 56.0% for LM Modify, 35.0% for LM Rewrite, 40.2% for procedural mutations, and 33.8% for PR Mirror. The dataset averages 381 tasks per repository and reaches 2,277 for pandas. These figures show how the environment-first design amortizes setup; they do not establish that all retained issues are equally realistic.

For the training study, the final 32B model is fine-tuned on 5,016 successful episodes drawn from 17,906 attempts. It reports 40.2% Pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified with one attempt and without a separate selector. Ablations show that more trajectories and more represented repositories tend to improve downstream resolution, while task difficulty ratings do not correlate cleanly with training usefulness. PR Mirror trajectories are strongest among the compared construction routes, LM Rewrite and procedural tasks remain competitive, and LM Modify is weaker.

The paper also provides negative evidence useful for audit. More than 25% of SWE-agent-LM-32B trajectories contain a repeated action sequence of length at least ten, versus under 4% for Claude 3.7 Sonnet. Repetition length ten is associated with an 89% failure probability, and 53% of student-model failures reach cost or step limits, often before modifying source code. A later scaffold intervention reduces repetition but slightly lowers resolution to 38.4%, suggesting repetition is often a symptom rather than the sole cause.

Benchmark improvement demonstrates that the selected trajectories can train a useful model under this scaffold. It is not a per-row quality certificate, does not validate the generated issue prose, and does not turn the visible-test tasks into an evaluation benchmark.
