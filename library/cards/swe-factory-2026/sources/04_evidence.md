On the 671-task benchmark, the accepted paper reports that GPT-4.1 mini produces 435 outputs and 337 fail-to-pass-valid environments at $0.047 and 26.3 minutes per issue. Kimi-K2 produces 424 outputs and 321 valid environments at $0.056 and 30.2 minutes; DeepSeek-V3 produces 358 outputs and 282 valid environments at $0.037 and 23.0 minutes. These are construction yield and cost results under the reported configuration, not per-record release verification.

Two ablations identify necessary components. On 44 tasks with binary test resources, all three Builder models fall to zero fail-to-pass cases when binary-file downloading is disabled. Without execution feedback, valid counts fall to 2/671 for GPT-4.1 mini, 18/671 for Kimi-K2, and 5/671 for DeepSeek-V3. The evidence supports binary recovery and execution feedback within this benchmark; it does not isolate every agent role or guarantee transfer to other languages and build systems.

The exit-code parser is evaluated on 1,217 constructed instances, of which 1,201 provide usable before/after log pairs. Human review yields TP=923, FP=0, TN=262, and FN=16: precision 1.00, recall 0.983, and F1 0.991. Four false negatives come from marker identifier typos and 12 from `set -e` early termination. This is strong parser evidence, but the annotations and corrected record IDs are unavailable, and nonzero-before still admits error-to-pass.

The model study reports improvement for all five SFT models. Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-Instruct rises from 5.8% to 21.0% resolve rate on SWE-bench Verified. This result shows that the reported task/trajectory/SFT combination can be useful; it does not prove that every one of the 2,809 trajectories is successful, correctly linked, uncontaminated, reproducible, or legally reusable.
