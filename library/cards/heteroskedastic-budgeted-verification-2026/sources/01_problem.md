Verification can make a language-model system safer or more accurate, but executing tests, invoking tools, or checking long derivations is costly. A fixed verification budget therefore creates a routing problem: the system must decide which candidate outputs deserve expensive feedback. Many existing policies rank all candidates with one uncertainty or confidence score, implicitly treating equal scores as equally useful regardless of task difficulty or verification cost.

This paper asks whether that global comparability assumption is sound. It examines budgeted verification as a controlled test-time setting in which costs, allocation decisions, and discovered errors are observable. The concern is not simply that uncertainty may be weak on average, but that the same score may identify errors well in one cost regime and nearly at random in another; a globally shared policy can then direct scarce verification away from error-dense cases.
