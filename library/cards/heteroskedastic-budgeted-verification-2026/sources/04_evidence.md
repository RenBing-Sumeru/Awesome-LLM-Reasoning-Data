The experiments use 500 MBPP programming problems with unit-test feedback and 5,000 MATH problems with answer matching. They evaluate Qwen3-8B, LLaMA3-8B, and GPT-4o-mini at 10%, 20%, 30%, and 50% verification budgets over ten seeds. On the heterogeneous MBPP settings, CST consistently improves over the global threshold: at a 20% budget, the reported gains are 5.1 percentage points for Qwen3, 15.1 for LLaMA3, and 6.5 for GPT-4o-mini.

The evidence is deliberately not uniformly positive. On Qwen3-MATH, CST loses to the global threshold at low budgets, including a 7.6-point loss at 20%. This negative control supports the paper's narrower claim: stratification helps only when the chosen partition captures consequential differences in feedback quality. Additional controls find that partial stratification recovers some performance, whereas stronger global adaptation does not reliably outperform the static threshold; an oracle signal reaches near-perfect hit rate, showing that the framework can exploit reliable signals.
