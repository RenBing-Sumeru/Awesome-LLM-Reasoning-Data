The central idea is to separate a weak predictor from a structurally mismatched allocation rule. The paper calls the mismatch heteroskedasticity: uncertainty quality changes across observable cost strata. A single global threshold or globally learned score pools these regimes, even when their score-error relationships differ, and can consequently over-audit cheap regions while under-auditing costly regions with many errors.

The proposed diagnostic is intentionally modest. Instead of adding a more sophisticated learner, cost-stratified thresholding partitions inputs into four ex-ante cost strata and maintains a separate running threshold for each one. It keeps the same fixed uncertainty score and receives no extra labels or gradient updates. If this simpler, structurally aligned policy beats a stronger global learner, the result points to signal comparability rather than optimizer power as the relevant bottleneck.
