Read the paper as a caution about what an uncertainty score means after it is placed inside a budgeted decision loop. The strongest practical lesson is the negative-control result: cost-based stratification is not inherently good, and its benefit depends on whether cost exposes a real change in signal reliability or error prevalence. When reusing the idea, retain the common budget and compare against both a static global threshold and a stronger global learner; otherwise an apparent gain may be confounded with additional capacity or looser spending.
