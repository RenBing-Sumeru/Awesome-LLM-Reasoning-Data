Its main novelty is a failure analysis of test-time allocation rather than another high-capacity allocation algorithm. The paper turns the usually implicit assumption that uncertainty scores are globally comparable into a falsifiable object, then uses a progression from global thresholding to global adaptation, partial stratification, and non-learning stratification to distinguish three explanations: weak signals, optimization instability, and structural heterogeneity.

The cost-stratified threshold is also a useful methodological contribution because it is intentionally weaker than the learned alternatives. Its gains cannot be attributed to extra feedback, a richer model, or more optimization. Together with the theoretical distortion bound, this makes the empirical finding actionable: before investing in a stronger verifier router, practitioners should inspect whether the score has comparable discriminative value in the observable regimes used for budgeting.
