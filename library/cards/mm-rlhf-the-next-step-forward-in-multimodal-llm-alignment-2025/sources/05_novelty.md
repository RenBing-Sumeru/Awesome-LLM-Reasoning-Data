The contribution is the scale and granularity of human multimodal comparisons together with critique-first reward modeling and dynamic sample weighting. DPO or RLHF alone is not the novelty.

Existing MLLM alignment often targets isolated problems such as hallucination or safety and lacks large, fine-grained human preference data covering general capability and safety. The paper asks whether broad human comparisons can systematically improve multimodal models.
