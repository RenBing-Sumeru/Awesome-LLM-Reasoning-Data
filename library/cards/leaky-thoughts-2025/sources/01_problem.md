Personal LLM agents must share data in appropriate contexts but keep it private elsewhere. Existing privacy evaluations focus on tool actions or final answers, leaving the reasoning trace—often assumed internal—outside the threat model even though it can repeat sensitive facts.

This paper evaluates reasoning models in probing and web-agent settings, measures leakage in both traces and answers, and tests whether more test-time reasoning changes the utility–privacy trade-off. It releases AirGapAgent-R, an open reconstruction of an unavailable contextual-privacy benchmark.
