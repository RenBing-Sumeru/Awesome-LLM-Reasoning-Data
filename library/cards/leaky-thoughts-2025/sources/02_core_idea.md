The paper makes the reasoning trace a first-class privacy surface rather than treating a safe final answer as sufficient. It compares vanilla models, chain-of-thought prompting, and large reasoning models; then varies the reasoning-token budget and tests accidental disclosure and prompt-injection extraction. Its main public data object is AirGapAgent-R: 4,160 prompts built from 20 synthetic profiles, 26 data fields, and eight sharing contexts, with labels for whether disclosure is appropriate. The dataset is English, hosted on Hugging Face under ODC-BY, and supports contextual-privacy evaluation rather than training. The central claim is not simply that reasoning leaks, but that scaling it can make answers more cautious while exposing more private information in thought.
