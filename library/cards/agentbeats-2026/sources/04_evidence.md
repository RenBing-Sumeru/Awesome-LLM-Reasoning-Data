The field study reports 298 judge agents in 12 categories and 467 subject agents from independent participants over five elapsed months (paper §7, Figure 5). The official competition page dates Phase 1 from 2025-10-16 to 2026-01-31 and Phase 2 from 2026-03-02 to 2026-06-02. The paper describes a planned six-month competition still in progress at writing, so these counts are a platform snapshot rather than a frozen dataset release.

Repository analysis provides construction evidence but not a public inclusion manifest. After inaccessible or malformed submissions were excluded and the remainder deduplicated, Python appeared in 99% of judge-agent repositories and 98% of subject-agent repositories. Prompts were detected in 78.3% of judge repositories; prompt-bearing judges averaged 241 prompt lines and 11.6k characters (paper §7, Table 4). The exact repository list, exclusion ledger, deduplication keys, and prompt-scanner snapshot were not published, so those statistics cannot be independently rebuilt from an immutable paper artifact.

The coding case supplies an executable feedback example. It evaluates four model-harness pairings on 731 SWE-Bench Pro public instances, 89 Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks, and 1,222 DevEval tasks retained after ground-truth-test validation. The main experiment cost is reported as about USD 6,000 (paper §8.3). In the harness-swap experiment, native pairings win five of six comparisons with an average 5.3-percentage-point solve-rate advantage; Terminal-Bench 2.0 contains a time-budget exception (paper §8.5, Table 7). These are author-reported configuration comparisons, not independent reproduction.

Trajectory inspection also exposes a negative signal. For DevEval, Gemini 3.1 Pro with OpenCode has a reported tool-call error rate of about 2%; Appendix Table 10 gives 2.44%, more than ten times the other agents. The authors attribute its cost anomaly to inefficient exploration and harness incompatibility. Harness logs are not fully comparable, however: tool sets differ, mini-SWE-agent omits token counts, and a shell call can hide read/edit/search behavior (Appendix A). The causal reading is therefore an author interpretation from non-uniform traces.

Public-score fidelity is explicitly limited. The study does not reproduce Anthropic's SWE-Bench Pro memorization screen, cannot use Qwen's unreleased corrected benchmark form, and uses Terminal-Bench thinking-effort settings that differ from public vendor reports (§8.4). Appendix A includes aggregate statistics for both succeeded and failed runs, but no record-level trajectory, artifact, score, or failure archive tied to arXiv v2 was verified. The evidence supports the protocol and reported evaluation findings; it does not establish trajectory completeness, verifier calibration, licensing, exact replay, or training-data quality.
