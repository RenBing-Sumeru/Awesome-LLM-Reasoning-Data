- **Identity and version boundary:** the accepted paper is arXiv:2606.13608v2 from 2026-06-14. The differently titled ICML Position Paper is not verified as the venue version of this record. The earlier official SDK ends at v1.2.6 from 2025-11-17, is marked deprecated, and predates the paper; current tutorial, template, gateway/Amber stack, documentation, and dashboard evolve independently.
- **No v2 replay bundle:** no immutable commit/image manifest, benchmark snapshot, full configuration, prompt, model/API version, timeout, result bundle, or record-level replay archive was verified for Tables 6–10. CI and local execution recipes show that configured assessments can run, not that historical results can be exactly reconstructed.
- **No released trajectory corpus:** Appendix A aggregates successful and failed runs, but no paper-linked archive exposes per-instance messages, tool logs, artifacts, scores, retries, exclusions, and failure reasons. A universal result schema is absent; current tutorial results may be free-form JSON. Full-episode and state/action descriptors therefore identify possible fields, not a standardized dataset release.
- **Judge heterogeneity:** programmatic tests, environment predicates, semantic prompts, and LLM judges do not share one error model or reward meaning. Predicates can omit requirements or accept shortcuts; semantic scoring can exhibit rubric bias, false positives/negatives, or prompt injection. The paper recommends isolating semantic scoring in a sub-agent but does not adversarially audit or calibrate all 298 judges.
- **Environment and observability risk:** remote agents may lack complete logs or standardized reset behavior. Runtime injection can fail or perturb coding containers, requiring preflight and remote-shell fallback. Authentication/control-plane support protects access but does not prove deterministic state, reliable replay, or complete observation.
- **Model, harness, and metric drift:** several models and production harnesses are proprietary. APIs, defaults, reasoning effort, tools, and provider behavior can change. Harness logs are structurally unequal, mini-SWE-agent omits token counts, and shell calls can conceal multiple semantic actions, limiting cross-harness trace comparison.
- **Benchmark exposure and fidelity:** there is no platform-wide contamination audit or universal split. The study does not reproduce Anthropic's SWE-Bench Pro memorization screen and cannot use Qwen's unreleased corrected benchmark form. Public tasks and differing reasoning-effort settings make vendor-score matching and future exposure claims configuration-dependent.
- **Selection and retention unknowns:** the frozen 298-judge/467-subject inventory, repository exclusion ledger, deduplication keys, prompt-scanner snapshot, per-instance rollout counts, seeds, retries, and failure policy are undisclosed. Aggregate inclusion of failures does not establish that every failed run was retained.
- **Licensing and privacy:** the manuscript is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, but checked core/reference repositories expose no verified common license and no umbrella grant for benchmark tasks, submitted agents, trajectories, logs, or results. Remote/hosted modes may process credentials, private services, code, and execution traces; consent, retention, deletion, and redistribution policy is incomplete.
- **Use boundary:** the paper evaluates and audits agents; it does not train a policy, reward model, process model, or RL agent on AgentBeats episodes. Proposed RL integration cannot justify training reuse. Benchmark performance does not establish data quality, judge validity, or generalization across assessment categories.
