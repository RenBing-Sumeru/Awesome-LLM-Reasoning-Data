Most Lean theorem-proving benchmarks consist of static competition problems or carefully curated small corpora, which differ substantially from the projects mathematicians maintain in practice. Static test sets are also vulnerable to training contamination, so high performance on benchmarks such as miniF2F does not establish that a prover can understand large dependency graphs, match project versions, and solve open obligations that matter to the community.

SorryDB asks how real project `sorry` statements can be collected continuously and evaluated inside their original repository environments. It turns unfinished `Prop` declarations from public Lean projects into reproducible tasks, requires a model to submit a replacement proof, and lets compilation in the original project determine whether the goal is actually closed.
