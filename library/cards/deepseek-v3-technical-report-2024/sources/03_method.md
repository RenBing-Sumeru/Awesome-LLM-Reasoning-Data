The reasoning-data pipeline creates two sample forms for each instance: a problem paired with its original response, and an instruction prefix plus the problem paired with an R1 response. The instruction prefix asks for reflection and verification. A domain expert for code, mathematics, or general reasoning is trained with SFT and RL; high-temperature RL sampling blends patterns from the original and R1-generated responses over hundreds of steps. The expert then generates candidate answers, and rejection sampling selects the final concise, high-quality SFT traces. For creative writing, role play, and simple QA, DeepSeek-V2.5 generates responses and human annotators verify correctness. DeepSeek-V3-Base is fine-tuned on the 1.5M-instance mixture for two epochs with cosine learning-rate decay. Multiple samples are packed into a sequence, but sample masking prevents cross-example attention.

RL uses two feedback classes. Rule rewards check deterministic math answers in a required format and compile/run code against test cases. A reward model initialized from DeepSeek-V3 SFT checkpoints judges free-form answers against references and scores tasks without definitive ground truth. Its preference records include both the final reward and a chain of thought leading to that reward, intended to reduce reward hacking. GRPO samples a group of outputs per prompt and estimates the baseline from within-group rewards rather than a separate critic. RL prompts span code, math, writing, role play, and QA. For open-ended questions, the report also uses majority-voted judgments from DeepSeek-V3 itself as constitutional feedback.
