The paper's evidence begins with scale and capability: it trains a 3.5B-parameter model on 800B tokens, then increases recurrent depth at inference rather than changing model weights. The reported result is that performance improves with additional compute, especially on mathematical and coding reasoning tasks, demonstrating that the model can use varying internal budgets rather than being fixed to its training-time depth.

The abstract reports that the model can reach a computation load equivalent to 50B parameters and describes some reasoning gains as dramatic. It also presents zero-shot per-token adaptive compute, KV-cache sharing, and speculative decoding as ways to reduce the cost of this approach. The result supports a distinct latent-compute scaling mechanism, but it remains evidence for the reported proof-of-concept architecture and benchmarks; it does not show that an ordinary pretrained transformer can obtain the same effect by simply looping its existing layers.
