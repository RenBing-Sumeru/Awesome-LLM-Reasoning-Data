The closest token-based approaches scale a reasoning model by emitting or sampling more textual intermediate work. This paper changes the computational surface itself: the additional work is hidden-state recurrence, and recurrent depth can be increased before output generation. Consequently, an inference budget is not tied to chain-of-thought length or to a separate verifier, candidate selector, or specialized rationale dataset.

The reusable novelty is the explicit separation between visible language and internal iterative computation. Adaptive depth, cache sharing, and speculative decoding further make the depth schedule a serving decision rather than only an architecture diagram. The paper does not claim that latent states are intrinsically interpretable or that all reasoning should be hidden; its contribution is a trained model design in which additional internal iterations can be measured and allocated independently of the displayed answer.
