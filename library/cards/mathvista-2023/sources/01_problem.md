MathVista was released as arXiv:2310.02255 in 2023 and accepted as an ICLR 2024 oral paper according to the official project page. It targets a gap in foundation-model evaluation: many math benchmarks are text-only, while many visual QA benchmarks do not require substantial mathematical reasoning.

The evaluation surface is 6,141 image-question examples derived from 31 multimodal datasets, including 3 newly created datasets. Each instance combines a visual context, a natural-language question, metadata for task/reasoning/category, and an answer target.

The decision boundary is visual mathematical reasoning benchmark, not a training corpus and not a formal verifier. It belongs in the atlas because it ties math answers to diagrams, charts, tables, scientific figures, and visual reasoning surfaces that text-only math benchmarks miss.
