The closest baseline is conventional CoT distillation, which supervises the student on the whole rationale in one sequence. CWT changes the supervision unit to a chunk and lets the current student's loss influence chunk boundaries; STT adds a counterfactual deletion test in which answer correctness decides whether a chunk remains visible. This differs from methods that simply ask a teacher to shorten text and from methods that hide all reasoning in latent states.

The direction signal for reasoning-data curation is the explicit treatment of omission as a construction decision. A faithful derivative release would preserve not only compressed text but also the source rationale, boundary revisions, model checkpoint used for each loss estimate, deleted alternatives, answer checks, and final retention labels. What is not new is distillation from an LLM, staged reasoning, or rationale compression in general. The paper's quality signal is downstream task and latency behavior; it is not direct validation of the correctness or faithfulness of chunk labels.
