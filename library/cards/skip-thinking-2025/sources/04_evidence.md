The paper reports ablations and comparisons across seven tasks and multiple GPT-2 and T5 student sizes. Its tables compare full-thinking training, average chunking, Search-Based Chunking, and Skip-Thinking Training, while latency analyses show that speedup depends on how many chunks a task can omit. The authors also note that Last Letter Concatenation retains almost the full process because its subtasks have little removable intermediate reasoning, so Skip-Thinking does not uniformly shorten every task.

These experiments support the narrower claim that the proposed training and deletion procedure can trade explicit rationale length against task accuracy in the studied settings. They do not establish that each skipped chunk is genuinely non-reasoning, that the teacher rationales are faithful, or that the derived records form a high-quality reusable dataset. The official ACL Anthology paper and citation are verified; no official code repository, rationale package, chunk labels, trained model release, or item-level construction log was confirmed.
