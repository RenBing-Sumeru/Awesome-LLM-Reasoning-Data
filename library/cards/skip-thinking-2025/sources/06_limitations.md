The authors identify a specific optimization limit: SBC is greedy and can settle on locally rather than globally optimal chunk modes. More broadly, the student's loss is model- and checkpoint-dependent, so the same rationale may receive different boundaries under another student or training stage. Answer preservation after deletion is a weak causal test: a model can guess correctly, exploit shortcuts, or rely on redundant wording even when a removed chunk contains useful reasoning.

The study uses teacher rationales from text-davinci-002 with a length cap and task suite inherited from an earlier protocol. Teacher errors, style artifacts, toxicity, source licensing, prompt overlap, and contamination are not fully documented. Selective deletion can hide uncertainty or dependencies, and some tasks show little acceleration. No confirmed code or trace release exposes boundaries, loss histories, rejected deletions, random seeds, or licenses. Benchmark accuracy and latency gains therefore cannot be used as proof of data-object quality or replayability.
