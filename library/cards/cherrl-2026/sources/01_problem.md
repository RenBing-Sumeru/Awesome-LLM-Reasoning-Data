Rubric-based reinforcement learning turns an LLM judge's rubric score into the optimization target, but a policy can exploit a hidden judge preference while genuine task quality stops improving or declines. In real training, true quality and the individual bias being exploited are unobserved and entangled, so neither the cause nor the onset of reward hacking can be reliably identified.

CHERRL addresses this audit problem with a controlled experimental environment rather than a new reward model or a mitigation method. It injects one known judge bias, tracks an unbiased and a biased score separately, and supplies a reference onset for controlled runs. The evaluation surface is a training trajectory containing prompts, responses, scores, bias signals, and onset labels; the public code and environment support reproduction.
