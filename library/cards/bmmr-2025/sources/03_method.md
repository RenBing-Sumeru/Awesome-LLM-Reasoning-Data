The construction can be audited as seven stages.

1. **Taxonomy and collection.** BMMR adopts the four-level UNESCO ISCED-F 2013 hierarchy. It includes 8 top-level disciplines, 16 second-level disciplines, 40 third-level areas, and 300 fourth-level subfields. More than two million college-level examples are collected from open print and digital sources, with 29 reported image types. Exact source lists, record IDs, acquisition dates, and rights are not disclosed.
2. **Integrity and hierarchical tagging.** Questions and answers are checked separately for completeness, then rematched to avoid multi-question ordering errors. GPT-4o assigns a top-level discipline, chooses among its second-level children, then emits third- and fourth-level tags from constrained candidate sets. The paper does not report a labeled validation set or tagging-error analysis.
3. **Safety, objectivity, and reasoning checks.** GPT-4o removes preference-dependent and potentially unsafe items. Three further passes reject common-sense-only questions, reasoning paths without multi-step inference, and samples whose text makes the image unnecessary. Prompts, exact rejection rates, repeated-run agreement, and rejected IDs are unknown.
4. **Transformation and augmentation.** Objective non-multiple-choice items retain their original format. Multiple-choice questions whose answer does not depend on options are converted to open-ended form. Option-dependent questions remain multiple-choice and are supplemented by “fact verification” tasks built from true and false statements within each second-level discipline. Row-level links from source question to transformed variants are not released.
5. **Quality cascade and balancing.** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct samples 32 answers per item and the result is compared with the annotated ground truth. Open-ended items with agreement from 0.2 to 0.6 and multiple-choice items from 0.3 to 0.6 are retained. Items below the lower bound pass to Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and then GPT-4o under the same rule. Remaining low-agreement cases go to 40 discipline-diverse annotators, who verify both reasoning path and final answer; only manual passes survive. Thresholds are dynamically adjusted and overrepresented disciplines downsampled. BMMR-Eval difficulty levels are based on sampling accuracy.
6. **Split and model training.** The final split is 88,991 Train and 20,458 Eval. The paper fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B and InternVL2.5-8B/38B/78B for one epoch. Peak learning rates are 1e-5 for the smaller three and 2e-5 for InternVL2.5-38B/78B; global batches are 64, 64, 64, 128, and 384. ViT is frozen throughout, and the projector is frozen only for the two Qwen2.5-VL runs. These experiments demonstrate an SFT use, not the authorship or correctness of each released trace.
7. **Verifier and evaluation.** For BMMR-Verifier, multiple models generate 32 rollouts per sample and rules label trajectory correctness. After filtering and difficulty rebalancing, the paper trains on 140k question–response pairs for one epoch with batch 64, learning rate 2e-5, warmup 0.05, and MS-Swift. Newlines define steps. The text says binary labels are appended after every step, but does not fully specify how a trajectory-level rule outcome becomes a distinct label for each intermediate step. A separate 50k-trajectory pool supports comparison with GPT-4o labels and 1,000 human-annotated cases. Main answer evaluation is greedy; Gemini-2.5-Pro, o3, and o4-mini are evaluated on a distribution-matched 5.4k TestMini subset because of cost.

The public execution path is narrower than the paper method. `api_eval.py` sends TSV questions and base64 images to an OpenAI-compatible chat endpoint at temperature 0. `bmmr.py` extracts boxed answers and applies multiple-choice matching or normalized numeric/symbolic equivalence, then aggregates by language, CoT setting, and discipline. No public command reconstructs the model-agreement cascade or BMMR-Verifier training/inference. Reproduction therefore requires a new ledger for every source, transformation, model sample, rule decision, human decision, split assignment, and verifier step label.
