- **Reference-trace authorship is unknown.** The pipeline starts from question, reasoning-path, and answer triples and later mentions revision, but it does not identify the original trace source, model or human author, revision history, or rejected alternatives per item.
- **Released Train metadata is minimal.** The viewer exposes only `id`, `conversations`, and `image`. Language, question type, UNESCO labels, difficulty, source document, transformation lineage, agreement scores, human decisions, and license fields are absent from the public row schema.
- **The selection cascade is not replayable.** Exact prompts, model revisions, temperatures, seeds, dynamic threshold changes, stage-specific counts, discarded IDs, and source-specific survival rates are unknown. Selecting intermediate-agreement items also changes the observed difficulty distribution by design.
- **Process targets are under-specified.** The paper obtains a rule-based correctness label for a complete trajectory, then describes adding binary labels after every step. It does not fully explain how intermediate steps receive differentiated labels, how partial correctness is treated, or how outcome leakage is prevented.
- **Verifier artifacts are missing.** The base checkpoint, 140k training pairs, 32-way trajectories, rule outputs, per-step labels, training code, weights, calibration data, and inference implementation for BMMR-Verifier are not released in the checked official artifacts.
- **Agreement is not accuracy.** High consistency with GPT-4o and human labels can reflect shared errors. The 1,000 annotated cases, annotator identities by discipline, adjudication, inter-annotator agreement, confidence intervals, false-positive/false-negative rates, and robustness to step segmentation are unavailable.
- **Public evaluation is outcome-only.** The GitHub grader extracts boxed answers, matches choices, and normalizes numeric or symbolic forms. It cannot detect a correct answer reached through invalid reasoning and does not reproduce the process scores reported in the paper.
- **Human-work reporting needs reconciliation.** The method reports 40 annotators for data verification and college students for 1,000 verifier annotations, while the NeurIPS checklist marks crowdsourcing and human-subject research as not applicable. The annotation arrangement may fall outside those checklist categories, but recruitment, compensation, consent, training, workload, and oversight are unknown.
- **License claims conflict.** The final-paper checklist says BMMR is CC-BY-4.0 and all sources are public domain; the live Hugging Face page is tagged MIT, and the code repository is Apache-2.0. No record-level rights matrix substantiates redistribution of books, examinations, websites, existing datasets, images, or derived traces.
- **Contamination is not audited.** Rewriting and augmentation are intended to reduce memorization and guessing, but no exact, near-duplicate, or semantic overlap ledger covers source collections, BMMR-Train, BMMR-Eval, model pretraining data, or other benchmarks.
- **Statistical and comparison limits remain.** Main results have no error bars. Costly proprietary models are evaluated on a 5.4k distribution-matched TestMini rather than the full Eval set, and the paper reports both full-set and subset results in the same broad discussion.
- **Coverage is finite.** BMMR contains only Chinese and English and does not cover every UNESCO subject. The paper also focuses on static images rather than video, audio, embodied environments, or interactive feedback.
- **The release is mutable.** No immutable package pins paper, code, Train, Eval, verifier, prompts, model revisions, and hashes together. The checked GitHub repository has no formal release.

These limitations do not negate the value of the corpus. They bound what may be claimed: downstream improvements show that the released Train object can affect models under the authors' setup, not that every row is correct, legally reusable, uncontaminated, or process-faithful.
