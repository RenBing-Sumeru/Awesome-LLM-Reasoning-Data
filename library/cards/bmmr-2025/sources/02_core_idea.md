The contribution links three data-and-feedback layers without making them the same object.

First, the dataset layer begins from more than two million college-level examples gathered under a UNESCO taxonomy. The paper describes source material as open print and digital information, including books, examinations, quizzes, websites, existing datasets, and repurposed sources. It starts preprocessing from question, reasoning-path, and answer triples, but does not identify who authored each reasoning path or how much of it was generated, copied, or revised. The final 109,449 questions include 58,740/10,685 multiple-choice items and 30,270/9,773 open-ended or fill-in-the-blank items in Train/Eval respectively.

Second, the curation-feedback layer combines GPT-4o tagging and screening with answer-agreement sampling. GPT-4o performs hierarchical discipline labeling, safety/objectivity filtering, and three checks for domain knowledge, multi-step reasoning, and image–text dependence. A cascade then asks Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct, and GPT-4o to sample 32 answers per item. Open-ended items with agreement in `[0.2, 0.6]` and multiple-choice items in `[0.3, 0.6]` are retained at a stage; lower-agreement cases escalate to stronger models and finally to 40 annotators, who check both the reasoning path and final answer. Difficulty and discipline balance are derived from this process.

Third, the verifier-feedback layer constructs a separate learned scorer. The paper samples 32 trajectories per input from multiple unspecified models, assigns each trajectory a rule-based correctness label, rebalances and filters the resulting pool, and reports 140k question–response pairs for verifier training. It appends `+` or `−` labels after steps and trains BMMR-Verifier with cross-entropy. At inference time, the probability of `+` scores the next step; response scores can average all steps or use the final step.

The release boundary is uneven. The live Dataset Viewer confirms 88,991 training rows with `id`, `conversations`, and `image`, and the raw Hub tree contains the full and mini Eval TSV files. The GitHub code loads Eval records, generates answers through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and scores normalized final answers. It does not expose the verifier model, training corpus, step labels, or process-scoring implementation. Consequently, the publicly reusable feedback contract is final-answer evaluation and the described curation recipe; BMMR-Verifier remains a paper-described artifact rather than a released supervision package.
