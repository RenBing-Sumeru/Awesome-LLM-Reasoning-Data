The paper's strongest evidence is scale and diversity: 204 tasks, 450 authors, and 132 institutions, with model behavior analyzed across many capabilities. Row-level evidence is the task scorer applied to an example, while paper-level claims come from aggregate scaling and task-family analyses. The reported conclusion is not that one model "solves reasoning" but that performance varies substantially by task and often improves predictably with scale while remaining below human performance on many tasks. Evidence is bounded by static public tasks, task-author quality, prompt policy, scorer heterogeneity, and contamination risk.
