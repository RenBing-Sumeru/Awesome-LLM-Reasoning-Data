The novelty lies in making a process verifier a controller of inference allocation at the step level. Many approaches either sample alternatives in parallel and rank completed answers, or iteratively improve a single complete response. Here, verification is used earlier and more locally: it helps choose where a trace should receive corrective computation, so the unit of intervention is an individual reasoning transition rather than the entire trajectory.

The second distinctive element is the explicit hybridization of local sequential repair and parallel search. These are often presented as competing ways to scale inference, but the paper treats them as complementary operations that can be scheduled together. The contribution is thus not a new verifier alone; it is a decision architecture in which step evidence determines when exploration should branch and when a promising branch should be repaired.
