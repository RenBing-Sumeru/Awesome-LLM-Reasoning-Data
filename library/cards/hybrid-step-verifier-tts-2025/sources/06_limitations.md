The method is only as selective as its process verifier: miscalibrated scores can direct compute toward harmless wording differences, overlook a decisive early error, or repeatedly revise a correct step. Combining parallel candidates, verification calls, and local refinements may also make the actual latency and cost substantially different from a simple sample-count budget. The reported five-model experiments support the stated setting, but deployment decisions still require task-specific checks of verifier reliability, stopping rules, long-context behavior, and the trade-off between one deeper repair and one additional independent candidate.
