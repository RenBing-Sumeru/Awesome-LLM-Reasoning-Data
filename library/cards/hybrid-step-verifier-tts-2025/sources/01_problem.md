Test-time scaling can improve reasoning by producing more candidates, searching more deeply, or repeatedly refining a draft. Training-based variants add another layer of cost because they require continued optimization before deployment, whereas training-free methods must decide how to spend inference computation on each individual problem. Purely parallel sampling can miss the chance to repair a bad local step, while purely sequential refinement can repeatedly polish one unpromising trajectory.

The paper asks whether a process verifier can make this allocation more selective. Rather than treating an entire answer as good or bad only after it is complete, the intended decision is made at the step level: identify where a reasoning trace warrants intervention, refine that segment when useful, and combine this sequential repair with complementary parallel exploration without retraining the underlying language model.
