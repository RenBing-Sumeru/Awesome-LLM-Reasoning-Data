The central idea is Conditional Step-level Self-refinement. A process verifier supplies evidence about the quality of individual reasoning steps, allowing the system to target computation at a questionable local transition instead of reflexively regenerating the entire solution. Refinement is therefore conditional on a visible process signal, not merely on a final-answer score or a fixed number of revision rounds.

The paper then makes this local repair mechanism hybrid: it combines sequential refinement with classical parallel scaling methods at step granularity. The resulting policy does not force every prompt into one inference pattern. It can preserve alternative reasoning directions while also revisiting weak steps inside an otherwise promising trace, making verification guidance the bridge between exploration and correction.
