For the data-construction/open-release track, GRIP is most useful as a graph-conditioned synthesis recipe plus a partial audit sample. A builder can adapt the sequence—extract atomic concepts, normalize a vocabulary, construct explicit and implicit relationships, choose combinations by graph type, route solution teachers by difficulty, and apply separate problem and solution gates—without assuming that the reported 2.1M corpus is available or verified.

The official 24,957-row sample supports schema inspection, duplicate checks, spot review, and experiments on question/solution text. It does not support faithful branch-level analysis because it lacks relationship type, graph path, difficulty, source seed, judge outputs, and rejection records. Training reuse should remain conditional on an explicit license and an audit of correctness, contamination, provenance, and duplication; the README's future-release promise is not a current reuse grant.

The design suggests controlled research questions for this track: compare rewriting with graph recombination at fixed generation budget; measure validity and novelty by hop distance; disclose yield before and after every gate; test independent symbolic or human verification against correlated model voting; and retain failed candidates as negative/audit data. A strong replication should also separate the effect of more samples from the effect of implicit graph relationships.

Reuse class: **recipe and audit-subset reference; not an audit-ready full training release**. The Card supplies enough evidence to reproduce the study design at a high level and to inspect the official sample, but not to recreate the authors' graph, selection decisions, or full corpus.
