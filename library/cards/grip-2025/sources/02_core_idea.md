GRIP's specific contribution is to replace seed-question rewriting with concept-relationship conditioning. Qwen2.5-32B extracts up to 5 key concepts per MATH seed; Qwen2.5-7B and BGE-M3 filter and consolidate them into roughly 10K concepts. Co-occurrence creates weighted explicit edges in a Key Concepts Relationship Graph. One-hop pairs, two-hop paths, hub-centered three-hop paths, and fully connected communities of 3 or 4 concepts then define generation conditions. The original seed question is not placed in the new-problem prompt. (Paper Sections 3.1-3.3; Appendix D.)

The data object is a concept-conditioned problem followed by a natural-language solution. An unnamed model predicts difficulty; Qwen2.5-Math-7B solves low- and medium-difficulty problems, while Qwen2.5-Math-72B handles high-difficulty problems. The released subset serializes only two concept strings plus the question and solution, so graph distance, community membership, difficulty, generator revision, source seed, and judge outputs cannot be recovered per row.

The feedback contract is whole-item model judgment. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct-7B, and DeepSeek-Math-RL each score problem quality; their scores are weighted by reported mathematical ability, and the average must reach 0.85. Each model separately gives the solution 0 or 1, and one negative vote rejects the pair. The paper does not publish the problem-score weights. This contract selects for judge-approved logical/presentational completeness and solution correctness, but it is not a formal verifier of every derivation step. (Paper Section 3.4.)

The closest prior constructions rewrite seeds or generate from externally supplied topics. GRIP's change is the graph-derived conditioning surface and its use of implicit concept relations, combined with difficulty routing and open-model voting. Concept extraction, LLM generation, rejection filtering, and SFT are not individually new; the novelty lies in how they are composed into a scalable mathematical-data recipe.
