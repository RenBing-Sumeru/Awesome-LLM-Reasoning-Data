The official NeurIPS 2025 paper addresses a construction problem: direct rewriting can keep synthetic reasoning questions close to a small seed set, while large noisy-pool filtering or proprietary teachers can move cost elsewhere. GRIP instead asks whether a seed corpus can be abstracted into mathematical concepts, organized as a relationship graph, and recombined into many new training questions. Its demonstrated scope is mathematics, not a general proof that graph synthesis transfers to every reasoning domain. (Paper Sections 1 and 2.2; Appendix F.)

The source object is all 7,500 question-solution pairs in the MATH training split. The reported target object is GRIP-MATH with exactly 2,123,345 synthesized question-solution pairs, rounded to 2.1M in the main text. The paper uses these pairs for supervised fine-tuning of six 1.5B-8B base models. This Card treats downstream gains as evidence that the selected data can provide training signal under the reported setup, not as proof that every problem or derivation is correct. (Paper Sections 3.5 and 4.1-4.4; Appendix B.)

The release boundary is partial. The official NeurIPS supplemental ZIP contains 24,957 rows with `KC1`, `KC2`, `question`, and `solution`; its README describes them as a random approximately 25K sample limited by company IP restrictions. It does not include the full corpus, graph, code, rejected candidates, judge records, model checkpoints, or a dataset license. The work belongs in the data-construction/open-release track because its main contribution is a reusable recipe plus a limited official sample, with material release and audit gaps still visible.
