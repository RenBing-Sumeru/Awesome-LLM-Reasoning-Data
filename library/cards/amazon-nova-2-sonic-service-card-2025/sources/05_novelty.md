For this atlas, the contribution is not a new speech-data algorithm or open corpus. It is a provider-authored disclosure that connects a speech interaction object, broad source categories, named alignment stages, runtime filters, and evaluation layers in one document. That makes it possible to compare what is operationally visible with what an independent data audit would require.

The report is more specific about deployed I/O and evaluation than about post-training records and feedback. Preserving that asymmetry is the useful direction signal: service cards can expose control surfaces and risk tests while still withholding the examples, reward contract, and lineage needed to reproduce the model. The presence of RLHF is not itself novel and does not imply an auditable preference dataset.
