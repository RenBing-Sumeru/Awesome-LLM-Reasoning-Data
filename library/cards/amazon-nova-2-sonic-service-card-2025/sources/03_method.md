The official Service Card says the core transformer was trained on licensed, proprietary, and publicly available multilingual and multimodal data, then developed through pretraining, SFT, reinforcement learning, and RLHF. At runtime, Bedrock receives speech or text plus configuration, applies input-policy filters, generates text, and renders speech; a triggered filter instead returns an error or canned response. The built-in safety filters cannot be configured or disabled.

Development testing combines public and proprietary datasets, automated assessors, human evaluation, and third-party red teaming. Named examples include MLS, FLEURS, VoiceBench/IFEval, and voice-converted BFCL conversations, but the Service Card does not state that evaluation records were used for training. Source identities and mixtures, source-level rights, deduplication, preference protocol, rewards, filter thresholds, rollout sampling, optimization details, checkpoints, splits, and decontamination remain unknown.
