For knockout aggregation, the system first samples N independent solutions for an input. It organizes them into a binary tournament and asks the same black-box language model to decide pairwise winners; repeated comparisons can be used for each match. The paper separates two risks: no sampled candidate is correct, and a comparison mistakenly eliminates a correct one. Its theorems combine those risks to show exponential or power-law decay in failure, depending on which quantities are scaled.

For league aggregation, each candidate is compared with several opponents and is ranked by mean win rate rather than a single elimination event. The reported experiments use GPQA, MMLU-Pro-S, and MATH-500, and include Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, GPT-4o, and QwQ-32B in different settings. The budget is total language-model calls for candidate generation plus comparison; correctness is measured on the benchmark, so a reproduction must retain both the initial samples and every comparison decision rather than logging only the final answer.
