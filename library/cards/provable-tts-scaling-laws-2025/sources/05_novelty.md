The closest alternatives either select candidates with an external verifier or aggregate them by a rule such as majority vote without a general reliability guarantee. This paper changes the inference object into a two-stage generation-and-comparison protocol and analyzes its full failure probability. The selection signal is produced by the same black-box model through pairwise judgments, but the paper specifies what that judge must do well for scaling to be meaningful.

The league variant further changes the decision from a sequence of irreversible single matches to an average over opponents, making the comparative evidence for a candidate more robust to an individual bad judgment. The new contribution is not a proof that language models are perfect judges, nor a substitute for task-specific verification. It is an explicit bridge from a comparison contract and a call budget to a measurable error-scaling claim that can be tested before the procedure is reused.
