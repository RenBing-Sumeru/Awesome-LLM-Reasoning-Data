The theoretical evidence identifies the conditions rather than claiming unconditional improvement. For knockout selection, a correct answer must be generated with nonzero probability and a correct candidate must beat an incorrect one with probability above one half in pairwise comparison. Under those assumptions, the stated failure bound decays exponentially or by a power law as total calls grow. For league selection, the paper states a stronger average-win-rate separation condition and derives exponential decay.

The empirical section tests the procedures across GPQA, MMLU-Pro-S, and MATH-500 with multiple language models, including Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, GPT-4o, and QwQ-32B. It reports increasing accuracy with candidate count in the studied settings and examines both knockout and league variants. This supports the practical relevance of the assumptions on those tasks, but it does not measure a universal theorem for every model: a deployment must still check pairwise-comparison reliability and the cost of the extra calls in its own environment.
