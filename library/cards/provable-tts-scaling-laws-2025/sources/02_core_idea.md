The paper replaces a single scalar verifier with structured self-comparison among candidate answers. In the knockout algorithm, the system samples N solutions and repeatedly pairs candidates; the model compares each pair, a winner advances, and the remaining winner is returned. If the model has a nonzero chance of generating a correct candidate and compares a correct-versus-incorrect pair better than random chance, the analysis bounds the failure probability as test-time calls grow.

The league algorithm avoids eliminating a good candidate after one unlucky loss. It evaluates each candidate by an average win rate against several opponents and returns the strongest aggregate scorer. Under a more robust condition on the average win rates of correct and incorrect solutions, its failure probability also decreases exponentially. Both methods expose the same auditable objects: candidate pool, comparison protocol, votes or win rates, aggregation decision, and number of calls, without adding a separately trained reward model.
