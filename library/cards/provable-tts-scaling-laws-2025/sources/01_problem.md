Repeated sampling is an obvious way to spend additional inference compute, but it does not say how the final candidate should be selected. Best-of-N needs a reliable verifier and can be misled by an adversarial-looking answer; majority vote can also become worse as N grows when the most frequent answer is wrong. Thus a larger call budget alone is not a scaling law: the selection mechanism must use the generated candidates in a way that remains reliable as their number increases.

This paper asks for black-box inference procedures whose probability of returning a correct answer can be driven toward one by spending more test-time calls under explicit assumptions. The relevant record contains independently generated candidates, their pairwise comparison outcomes, the tournament or league aggregation decision, and total call count. The question is therefore both operational and theoretical: how should calls be divided between generation and comparison, and under what conditions does that division improve rather than destabilize the final answer?
