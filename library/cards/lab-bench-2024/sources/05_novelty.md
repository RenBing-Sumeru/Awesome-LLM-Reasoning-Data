The prior baseline is science QA that mostly tests factual recall, exam-style reasoning, or broad expert questions. LAB-Bench changes the evaluation object: many tasks require interacting with research artifacts such as figures, tables, supplementary files, protocols, sequence strings, cloning workflows, or biological databases.

The direction signal is that domain benchmarks can encode research workflow operations while still using a simple answer-level feedback contract. The quality signal is the explicit category/subtask taxonomy, reported human coverage, public/private release policy, canary string, and official dataset-card changelog.

What is not new: multiple-choice scoring, expert-authored QA, and public benchmark releases are established patterns. Before reuse, inspect data lineage, figure and article licensing, database snapshot dates, answer-key generation, hidden/private split policy, and whether tool use is allowed.
