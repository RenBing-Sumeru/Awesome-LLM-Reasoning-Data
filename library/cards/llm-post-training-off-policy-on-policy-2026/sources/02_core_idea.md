The paper first separates post-training by trajectory provenance: off-policy learning consumes externally supplied trajectories, while on-policy learning uses rollouts generated by the learner. It then gives three behavioral roles. Support expansion makes useful behavior reachable; policy reshaping improves behavior in already reachable regions; behavioral consolidation preserves, transfers, or amortizes useful behavior across stages and model transitions.

This is a taxonomy for reasoning-data work because a label such as “SFT” does not determine either provenance or effect: the same objective can expand support or reshape a policy. The paper treats preference optimization as commonly off-policy reshaping, recognizes that online variants approach learner-generated states, and frames distillation as consolidation rather than merely compression. Closest comparisons are broad post-training surveys and specialized preference or reasoning surveys; this Card uses the paper for the provenance-and-stage lens.
