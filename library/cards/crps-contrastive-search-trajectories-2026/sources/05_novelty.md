CRPS changes the supervision-extraction step after search. Instead of selecting one successful MCTS path or locally repairing a failed path, it uses search-distribution statistics to construct a positive/negative contrast, verbalizes both global strategy and local divergence, and conditions a stronger analyst to generate a fresh solution. Negative evidence influences the final positive demonstration without being exposed to the SFT learner as a negative label.

The hard/soft distinction is consequential. Hard negatives represent correctness failures that the search visited heavily; soft negatives represent inefficient but correct reasoning. The analyst can therefore ask two different questions: why an approach fails, and why a valid approach is unnecessarily long. The synthesis prompt converts those answers into explanatory choices inside an ordinary completion.

MCTS, UCT, rejection sampling, answer verifiers, self-refinement, teacher distillation, contrastive prompts, and SFT are not new individually. The paper's contribution is their decoupled composition: a specialized explorer maps a trajectory distribution, a stronger analyst compares selected paths at two granularities, and a final verifier filters newly synthesized traces. CRPS is not contrastive representation learning and does not train the target model with a contrastive loss.

The process-supervision claim needs a boundary. Step segmentation and local critiques create step-addressed intermediate artifacts during construction, but public CRPS-30K rows expose only final solutions and terminal answers. Critique fields are model-generated and not independently verified. CRPS is therefore evidence for process-informed construction, not proof of a released step-level ground-truth corpus.

Compared with selection-only search data, the method provides a more expressive recipe but a narrower release than the full construction object. Code and final problem/solution pairs are official; search trees, chosen positives and negatives, critiques, parser repairs, verification decisions, and rejected syntheses are not part of the confirmed dataset release. Reuse studies must preserve this distinction rather than treating public rows as replayable contrastive episodes.
