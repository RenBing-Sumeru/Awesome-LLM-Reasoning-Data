The closest common practice is to use a pretrained VLM to score behavior in one robotic environment, or to evaluate temporal value ordering only on successful expert videos. Such settings provide little supervision for real failures and near-misses across robots and tasks, and they do not adequately test whether a model distinguishes completion states relative to the specified instruction. RoboReward’s primary novelty is not the first use of VLMs as reward models. Instead, it combines counterfactual task relabeling, temporal clipping of the same trajectory, and a five-level progress rubric into a unified data-construction contract with a human-verified test set. It also evaluates 22 models across diverse embodiments under one protocol, shifting the contribution from a task-specific deployment method to a reusable dataset, benchmark, and general-purpose reward model.
