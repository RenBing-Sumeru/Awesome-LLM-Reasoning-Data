First, automatically generated labels depend on GPT-5 mini correctly interpreting fine-grained spatial relations, action outcomes, and task constraints. If the validator mistakes an object hovering above a target for a completed placement, an incorrect score may remain in the training set. Reuse should therefore estimate false-acceptance rates for each reward level rather than treating automated acceptance as ground truth. Second, the method predicts a discrete reward only at the end of a complete episode, so it does not directly provide action-level or per-timestep credit assignment; long-horizon tasks may require an additional process-reward mechanism. Third, each benchmark example is accepted or rejected by one human annotator, and inter-annotator agreement is not reported. The real-robot RL study also covers only two WidowX tasks, so it does not establish universal reliability in open-world robotics.
