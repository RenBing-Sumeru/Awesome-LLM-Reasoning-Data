1. **Aggregate and clean trajectories.** The authors collect real-robot videos and task instructions from OXE and RoboArena and normalize surface-level errors in the instructions. Successful OXE demonstrations receive a reward of 5, while RoboArena’s human progress scores are mapped to the 1–5 rubric.
2. **Generate negative examples.** For each successful video, the pipeline produces counterfactual tasks associated with rewards from 1 to 4. It also truncates videos at early, middle, and late endpoints to create failed, partially completed, and near-complete outcomes.
3. **Validate and split the data.** GPT-5 mini checks whether each instruction, video, and reward are consistent with the fixed rubric; invalid sets are regenerated or discarded. Splits are constructed by original task description to prevent the same task from crossing train, validation, and test sets.
4. **Train and evaluate models.** The Qwen3-VL vision backbone is frozen while its fusion and language layers are fine-tuned to predict end-of-episode rewards. Every test example is then manually verified. Reproduction requires fixed model versions, prompts, data sources, and splits; the complete generation-call budget and random seeds are not fully reported.
