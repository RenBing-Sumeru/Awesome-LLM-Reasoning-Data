Real-world robotic reinforcement learning requires accurate reward signals that can be queried repeatedly, but existing solutions usually depend on humans labeling individual episodes or on hand-crafted reward functions tuned separately for each task. These approaches are difficult to scale across robots, scenes, and language instructions. Although general-purpose vision-language models can inspect a rollout and judge task completion, their reliability on real robotic failures, near-misses, and partially completed tasks has not been systematically established.

The paper addresses this problem by constructing the RoboReward dataset and RoboRewardBench, which represent supervision as real-robot videos, natural-language task instructions, and discrete end-of-episode progress rewards from 1 to 5. It further augments success-heavy demonstrations through counterfactual instruction relabeling and temporal clipping, then trains general-purpose vision-language reward models that can supply rewards for robotic reinforcement learning.
