The report is detailed about pipeline logic but sparse about inventory. Dataset sizes, exact source lists, collection dates, deduplication rules, language/geography distribution, teacher identities and versions, prompt templates, number of candidates per task, acceptance yields, human-review rates, and trajectory schemas are unknown. Although the SWE source is restricted to public repositories with permissive licenses, rights and privacy treatment for community posts, web pages, occupational data, office documents, financial material, workbooks, decks, and product-feedback signals are not comprehensively enumerated.

Artifact-aligned rewards reduce but do not remove reward error. Tests may be incomplete; model-enriched specifications can alter intent; LLM-generated tests can share blind spots with generators; pairwise and rubric judges remain subjective; visual scorers may reward presentation over factual content; and completion-time shaping can conflict with robust investigation. The paper provides no independent false-accept/false-reject estimates or reward weights. Dense rewards for “well-structured reasoning” also risk imposing stylistic proxies.

Reproducibility is limited because training data, Docker images, teachers, reward code, Forge, data pool, and acceptance logs are not released. The model page uses a modified-MIT label and the repository links a license, but those terms do not automatically cover source data or generated workspaces. Reported benchmark improvements are confounded by checkpoint, data, scaffold, infrastructure, and inference changes.
