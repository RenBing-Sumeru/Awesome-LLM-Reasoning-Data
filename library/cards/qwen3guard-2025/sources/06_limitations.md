The report itself warns that adversarial prompt engineering may bypass guardrails; safety annotations may encode cultural or demographic bias; and Stream's partial-context architecture can produce more false positives or false negatives, particularly out of distribution or in low-resource languages. It also notes that the model is not geolocation- or policy-adaptive.

The disclosure ledger adds separate reproducibility limits. The 1.19M corpus has no released records, source manifest, rights mapping, seed set, complete prompts, labeler settings, or item-level lineage. The reported auto-label F1 is not a released calibration or error audit. Stream's rollout ensemble, count, threshold, judge prompt, and token-label data are incomplete. The public Qwen3GuardTest dataset is an evaluation artifact, not the training corpus. Finally, the reported GSPO filter/reward setup applies to a downstream Qwen3-4B experiment and must not be generalized into the guard's own training recipe or a validated universal safety filter.
