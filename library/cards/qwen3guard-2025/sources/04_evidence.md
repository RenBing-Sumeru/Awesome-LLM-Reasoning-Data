The technical report evaluates Gen and Stream across English, Chinese, and multilingual prompt/response benchmarks. It reports F1 tables for strict and loose handling of controversial cases, and it releases Qwen3GuardTest as an evaluation artifact. The report also evaluates streaming detection latency and reports an exact-hit measure near 86.0% for direct responses; with thinking content, it reports detection within the first 128 tokens in about 66.8% of cases. These are reported evaluations, not a substitute for an independently reproducible data or calibration audit.

For the separate safety-RL application, the reported hybrid reward raises the WildGuard-evaluated safety rate from about 60 to above 97 while aiming to control over-refusal. The authors explicitly avoid Qwen3Guard as that experiment's safety evaluator and use Qwen3-235B-Instruct-2507 plus WildGuard instead. This supports a bounded claim that the report tested guard-based rewards; it does not establish universal reward calibration, dataset-filter reliability, or transfer to other policies and deployments.

Official Qwen sources provide a release repository, an official model collection, checkpoint model cards, and a report-linked public evaluation dataset. They do not provide the 1.19M training records, the complete policy/label specification history, or end-to-end construction and training code.
