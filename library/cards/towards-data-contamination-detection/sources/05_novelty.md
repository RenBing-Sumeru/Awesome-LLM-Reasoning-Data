Earlier contamination studies often evaluated a detector as though it could provide a definitive benchmark-cleanliness decision. This paper instead examines the assumptions, agreement, and failure modes of the detectors themselves on modern models and harder benchmarks. Its contribution is not the concept of training-data contamination or a general-purpose oracle. It provides evidence that detector disagreement and answer-augmentation failures are part of the empirical picture, while the reported outcomes remain conditional on the study's access assumptions, benchmark formats, and post-training settings.
