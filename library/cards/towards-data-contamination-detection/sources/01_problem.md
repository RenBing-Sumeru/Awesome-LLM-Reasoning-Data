Benchmark performance can reflect either generalization or memorization of evaluation material. The paper studies whether contamination-detection methods that were commonly validated on earlier models and conventional benchmarks remain reliable for modern LLMs and more difficult evaluation datasets. Its unit of analysis is a benchmark item together with a model response, likelihood signal, or other detector output. The study concerns the interpretation and robustness of contamination tests; it does not introduce a new reasoning-data release or recover an LLM's undisclosed training corpus.
