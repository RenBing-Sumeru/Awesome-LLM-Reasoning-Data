The central contribution is an empirical comparison of existing contamination-detection approaches under modern evaluation conditions. The authors apply five approaches to four state-of-the-art LLMs and eight challenging datasets. They report non-trivial limitations in detector assumptions and practical use, limited consistency between techniques, and difficulty detecting contamination introduced through answer augmentation during instruction fine-tuning. The paper therefore treats a detector output as a method-specific signal rather than as a universal oracle for whether a model encountered an item during training.
