Closest prior work uses general preference pairs, terminal success, or step accuracy without representing the domain-specific states and failure modes of multilingual reward-model evaluation. This paper changes the record structure and feedback contract by centering M-RewardBench and combining inputs, candidates, environmental or evidential state, and labels in one auditable example through constructing same-language or cross-language chosen–rejected pairs from multilingual instructions and responses and verifying them with human review and quality rules. The contribution is task-specific data construction, capability decomposition, and practical evaluation rather than sample count alone.
