Claim: selecting label source by HyPER yields a more useful reward-model training set than using people only, GPT-4 only, or a random mixture under the same human budget. Controlled setup: the paper holds the reward-model objective and RewardBench evaluation fixed while changing the label-source allocation; the random baseline is budget-matched. Result: the HyPER-selected mixture improves RewardBench by 7–13% over either exclusive source and improves Best-of-N reranking by 2–3% (Section 4). Boundary: this comparison supports the released routing procedure on the tested preference datasets and base models; it cannot identify how much of the gain comes separately from tags, PPM fit, or candidate simulation.
