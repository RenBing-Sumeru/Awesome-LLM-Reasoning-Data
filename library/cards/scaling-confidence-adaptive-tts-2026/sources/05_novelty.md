Prior adaptive self-consistency methods usually infer whether to stop from answer frequency, entropy, or a hand-designed stability rule. This paper changes the decision signal itself: it first trains a verbalized confidence signal under an objective that jointly prices correctness and calibration, then makes that signal the explicit input to the stopping decision. The reference-accuracy reward is the additional guard against a model learning low-confidence wrong answers.

The paper is not simply another confidence-estimation study, because its empirical endpoint is sample allocation under a fixed quality target. It is also not a general solution to uncertainty estimation: the method depends on eliciting a discrete confidence report and on the correctness checks used during training. Readers should distinguish its measured adaptive-sampling gain from any broader claim about factual reliability or safety.
