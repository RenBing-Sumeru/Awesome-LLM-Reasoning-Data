Most test-time scaling studies treat a task as one stage: select a model, draw more answers, or make one chain longer. Real workflows are often pipelines, such as retrieval followed by answering, requirements followed by code and tests, or decomposition followed by tool use. A budget spent at an early stage can improve the evidence available later, while a poor early result may force a later stage to consume more samples or a larger model.

This paper asks how to allocate a fixed inference budget across such dependent stages. A decision is a vector of per-subtask model choices and sampling budgets, constrained by total cost. The objective is not simply to maximize one local score, but to maximize final task performance while accounting for model-size preference, diminishing returns from repeated sampling, and the way an upstream choice changes the downstream task. Exhaustively evaluating this combinatorial space is costly, so the paper focuses on how to search it efficiently.
