The paper evaluates six datasets across four workflow types and compares AgentTTS with random search, Bayesian optimization, zero-shot LLM allocation, MLCopilot, and AgentHPO. It reports that AgentTTS reaches top results on most tasks with fewer trials. On 2WikiMultiHopQA, the reported test score is 0.72, exceeding the next best result of 0.70; the table also reports 2.5 hours of search for AgentTTS versus 8.3 to 12.5 hours for the listed LLM-based baselines that produced a score of 0.70.

The ablations support the paper’s three planning inputs: removing model-preference initialization prevents the reported search from reaching an optimal configuration, while omitting diminishing-return or dependency guidance delays the best trial. These results demonstrate search efficiency under the authors’ model pool, FLOPs normalization, 50-example search set, and task metrics. They do not establish a universal allocation policy, because the location of an optimum depends on model prices, fusion behavior, task ordering, and the evaluation environment.
