AgentTTS relies on repeated execution of candidate workflows, so the search itself can be costly and the quality of its result depends on a representative small feedback set. FLOPs normalize model and token costs but cannot substitute for wall-clock latency, API availability, queueing, tool failures, or actual price. The agent’s textual guidelines do not prove causal explanations, and same-model fusion can become a bottleneck as candidate count grows. The method also assumes the workflow stages and allowable model pool are known; changing a retriever, tool interface, ordering, or evaluator changes the search surface and requires a fresh allocation study.
