Self-improvement methods such as STaR and rejection-sampling fine-tuning keep model-generated solutions only when a terminal check marks them correct. On difficult tasks, most sampled solutions may be wrong, so this discards the majority of the generation budget and loses information about recurring failure patterns. Separately trained outcome verifiers can reuse wrong solutions, but a verifier trained once on a frozen generator may not see the harder or more varied errors produced by later generators.

V-STaR asks whether the same iterative sampling process can improve both sides of the system: a generator that learns from successful self-generated solutions and a verifier that learns to distinguish successful from failed solutions. The setting is intentionally narrow and auditable—GSM8K mathematics with reference answers and MBPP code with executable tests—so every complete candidate receives a binary terminal label.

The key data question is not simply whether incorrect outputs are retained. It is how terminal labels become preference pairs, how the candidate distribution changes across iterations, what score the verifier actually exposes, and whether that score remains reliable under larger Best-of-k search. Final-answer and test labels are cheap, but they do not validate intermediate reasoning or guarantee robust program semantics.
