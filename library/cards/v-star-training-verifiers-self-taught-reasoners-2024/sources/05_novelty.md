V-STaR connects two previously separate uses of sampled reasoning. STaR-style self-training sends only correct solutions back to the generator, while outcome-verifier training keeps both correct and incorrect solutions for ranking. V-STaR accumulates both objects from a sequence of improving generators, so later rounds contribute a changing distribution of positives and negatives rather than one frozen-generator snapshot.

Its verifier objective is also distinctive for this setting. Instead of adding a binary ORM head, it converts programmatic outcome labels into same-problem correct-over-incorrect preferences and uses DPO to train a generative verifier. The resulting sequence likelihood is used directly for Best-of-k ranking. This makes rejected generations reusable without claiming that they are demonstrations to imitate.

The ingredients are not individually new: STaR, RFT, ReST-style iterative self-training, outcome reward models, DPO, unit-test verification, and Best-of-k selection all predate the paper. The contribution is the specific two-buffer iterative recipe, the synthetic pair transformation, and the matched evidence that refreshing generator errors improves the verifier under the tested math and code settings.
