The paper reports experiments on GSM8K and MBPP, with transfer evaluation on a 150-problem numeric-answer, no-LaTeX, Level 1 MATH subset and the full HumanEval test set. Across 7B and 13B runs, the reported absolute improvement over prior self-improvement or verification baselines is 6-17 percentage points on math and 4-12 points on code. These numbers measure the combined generator, verifier, and inference-budget recipe, not the quality of every label or preference pair.

Budget-matched comparisons support the iterative collection claim. V-STaR outperforms a one-iteration generator-plus-verifier baseline that draws all 48 candidates from the initial SFT generator. On MBPP, verifier improvements across iterations are larger than generator improvements; a fourth iteration adds only 0.3 points, indicating diminishing marginal returns in that setup.

Under the authors' LoRA implementation and matched training data, the DPO verifier ranks candidates better than their ORM-style baseline as search grows: ORM stops searching effectively beyond 4 candidates on GSM8K and is worse than DPO beyond 16 on MBPP. This is an objective-and-implementation comparison inside one study, not evidence that every DPO verifier dominates every ORM.

The optional verifier-in-loop MBPP variant reports 53.2 Best-of-64 and 46.34 Pass@1 after 3 iterations, but the authors conclude that it gives no substantial gain. Test-time curves also show that Best-of-k saturates at k of at least 16 and that V-STaR's advantage over majority voting narrows at larger k. These findings expose both the value and the limit of additional inference compute.

Reproducibility evidence is incomplete. The accepted COLM paper, full appendices, DOI, and paper license are verified, but no official implementation, generated buffer, pair manifest, project page, checkpoint, or Hugging Face release was confirmed. Results therefore cannot be independently tied to exact records, configurations, or model hashes.
