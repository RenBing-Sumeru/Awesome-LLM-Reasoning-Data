Mechanistic studies show that a CoT may not reflect the model's actual decision process, but practical auditing still lacks an operational question: can we determine whether one specific trajectory is faithful and identify the cause and step evidence of unfaithfulness? Plausibility, importance, and answer correctness are not equivalent to faithfulness. This distinction is crucial in high-risk settings: a medically or legally correct CoT may still be a post-hoc rationale for an answer produced from other signals. In medical, legal, or knowledge tasks, a correct and plausible CoT may be a post-hoc rationale for an answer obtained through other signals. Treating plausibility as faithfulness creates false transparency in process monitoring.

FaithCoT-Bench formulates instance-level faithfulness detection as a discriminative task and compares multiple detector families on expert-annotated data with fine-grained evidence.
