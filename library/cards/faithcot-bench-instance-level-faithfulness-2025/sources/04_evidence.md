The paper systematically compares 11 detection methods on FINE-CoT and finds that none dominates across all models and domains. Knowledge-intensive tasks are harder than formal or verifiable ones, and unfaithfulness becomes more subtle as model capability increases, causing detector performance to decline. Counterfactual methods offer causal signals at high cost, whereas LLM judges are cheaper but often confuse plausibility with faithfulness. These differences show that the benchmark measures complementary capabilities rather than one text-classification skill. The paper also compares internal-access and purely black-box settings, showing that greater access does not automatically yield stable transfer and that detectors remain task-dependent. The experiment supports the claim that current detectors do not reliably transfer across models and domains, especially for knowledge-intensive tasks and stronger generators. It does not make expert labels complete mechanistic ground truth.
