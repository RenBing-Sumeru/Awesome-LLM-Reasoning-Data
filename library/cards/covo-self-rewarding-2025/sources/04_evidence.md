The paper evaluates CoVo across mathematics, commonsense, and science benchmarks with greedy Pass@1 and Math-Verify, and reports gains for several model/dataset combinations but not universal dominance. For Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, CoVo reaches 68.2 on MATH-500 versus 65.4 for few-shot CoT, 67.4 for GRPO, 67.6 for Reinforce++, and 67.8 for TTRL. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, CoVo's 51.2 is below GRPO's 51.8 and Reinforce++'s 51.6 on MATH-500, while it is highest among listed methods on OlympiadBench (19.6), GPQA (32.1), and CommonsenseQA (74.4). Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct results are similarly mixed: CoVo scores 76.8 on MATH-500, but 92.5 on GSM8K versus TTRL's 92.8 and 57.0 on MMLU-Pro versus EMPO's 57.4. (Paper Table 1; Appendix Table 6)

The paper directly tests the proposed proxy. Across MATH-500, MMLU, GPQA, and CommonsenseQA, Table 2 reports correct trajectories with much higher mean consistency (0.787–0.889) than incorrect ones (0.215–0.274), and lower mean volatility (0.227–0.386 versus 0.761–0.867). These are correlations measured with benchmark correctness labels after sampling; they support discrimination in the tested distributions but do not turn the training reward into an external verifier. (Paper Table 2)

Table 3 supplies component evidence. For Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, vector intrinsic reward plus curiosity obtains 68.2/88.7/47.5 on MATH-500/GSM8K/AMC-23; vector intrinsic alone obtains 68.0/88.4/42.5, and curiosity alone obtains 61.8/80.6/32.5. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, the corresponding three configurations score 51.2/79.6/25.0, 51.6/79.5/22.5, and 46.2/69.5/15.0. The joint reward is not best on every task, so the ablation supports complementarity more narrowly than a universal improvement claim. CoVo reward calculation also costs 49.22 seconds, or 24.6% of a measured training step, versus 4.38 seconds/2.8% for majority voting and 2.06 seconds/1.3% for ground-truth reward. (Paper Table 3; Appendix Table 8)

These author-reported scores show that the training procedure can change evaluated task accuracy. They do not validate every self-reward, prove prompt quality, demonstrate decontamination, or establish that consistency tracks truth for every item. Evaluation correctness is supplied separately by Math-Verify and benchmark ground truth, and no independent reproduction was found in the verified artifacts.

Artifact evidence is mixed. The official repository contains the OpenRLHF implementation and example training commands. The official Hugging Face repository contains README.md, train.jsonl, and test.jsonl. However, the hosted builder reports a schema-cast failure because the train records use `problem` and `solution`, while test records use `problem`, `answer`, and `level`. No released artifact was found for paper-run trajectories, distance matrices, answer groups, rewards, filtered examples, seeds, logs, or checkpoints.
