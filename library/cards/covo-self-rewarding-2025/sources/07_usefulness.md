For the **Data Construction & Open-Release Recipes** track, CoVo is useful primarily as a construction recipe rather than as a finished reward dataset. It specifies how to convert a prompt batch and multiple on-policy traces into intermediate data objects: extracted answer candidates, state-to-answer likelihood distances, consistency and volatility measures, answer-group vectors, scalar rewards, and Reinforce++ advantages.

Researchers can use the recipe to compare self-reward designs, study whether path-derived signals correlate with external correctness, test alternative segmentation and semantic answer-equivalence rules, or measure reward drift as the policy changes. The public implementation also makes ablations of filtering, intrinsic aggregation, and curiosity feasible.

Any reuse should preserve three partitions: prompt data, online self-reward records, and benchmark evaluation. The released JSONL files cover only part of the first partition; the second must be regenerated and audited; benchmark labels and Math-Verify belong only to the third. A serious reconstruction should record source IDs, prompt hashes, model and tokenizer revisions, seeds, extraction rules, raw trajectories, candidate groups, distance matrices, component rewards, filters, and checkpoints.
