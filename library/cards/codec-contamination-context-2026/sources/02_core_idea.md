CoDeC treats a negative change in a target sequence's average log-likelihood after prepending random same-dataset examples as evidence of contamination: unseen context usually supplies useful information, whereas context that triggers an already memorized distribution can disrupt prediction. The method needs token probabilities but neither training data nor a learned detector; it contributes an audit score rather than a released benchmark dataset or training corpus. The score is the fraction of targets with a negative shift, enabling comparison across models and datasets; it is not a per-record membership probability.
