Loss and membership scores assess a sample's likelihood or rare-token behavior, normally on an absolute scale that needs calibration. CoDeC instead changes the input by adding same-dataset demonstrations and uses the direction of the target's confidence shift. The change is therefore an intervention-based, percentage-valued dataset score. Its novelty is not in-context learning itself, but using its failure to help as a contamination signal that can also surface exposure to augmented or related distributions. This relative change also avoids treating absolute likelihoods from different architectures as one scale and makes the intervention behind a comparison explicit.
