MATH-500 inherits MATH's final-answer-only limits: a correct final answer can hide invalid reasoning, and an equivalent answer can be misgraded if normalization fails. The subset is small enough that category imbalance or repeated public exposure can matter. It is public and heavily reused, so contamination risk is high for modern models. The paper's results also mix evaluation with verifier-guided selection budgets; a raw model score, a majority-vote score, and a PRM-reranked score must be labeled separately.
