Prior error-detection benchmarks test unimodal reasoning chains and therefore cannot tell whether a process judge grounds an error decision in an image. PRMBench-V keeps the controlled error-insertion idea but adapts it to visual reasoning, adds a visual-perception category, and gives every seed question coverage over the same nine error types. Its distinct contribution is this balanced, step-level multimodal diagnostic surface, not the general idea of using process rewards or best-of-N selection. The paper also tests BR2-PRM, whose new decision is to aggregate dimensions using benchmark-calibrated uncertainty rather than a plain mean.
