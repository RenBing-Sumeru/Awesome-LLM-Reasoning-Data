Nemotron 3 Ultra is notable for releasing several layers that frontier reports often conflate: an official checkpoint, named data collections, recipe code, stage-level construction details, selected source and licence filtering, and explicit private-data boundaries. The release therefore supports more than a title-level description of post-training while still not establishing full provenance.

Its feedback disclosure is also more concrete than a generic statement that RL was used. It specifies 16 rollouts per unified-RLVR sample, batch and generation budgets, specialist-teacher dense signals for MOPD, and a SWE hidden-test reward caveat. These facts make the feedback surface partially auditable without turning it into a complete reusable RL recipe.

For this atlas, the important distinction is between evidence of released artifacts and evidence of full-report reproducibility. The former is strong here; the latter remains limited by private/vendor inputs, incomplete environment configurations, incomplete reward calibration, and missing item-level lineage.
