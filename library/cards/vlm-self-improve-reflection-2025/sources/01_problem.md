Multimodal chain-of-thought self-training needs more than a way to produce additional text: it needs an evidence-backed rule for deciding which generated solutions enter the next training round. In visual reasoning, a model can reach the reference answer while misreading an image, confusing symbols, or using an invalid calculation. Final-answer accuracy is therefore useful as inexpensive feedback, but it is not equivalent to step-validity or rationale faithfulness.

R3V studies whether a Vision-Language Model can bootstrap from a small GPT-4o rationale warm-up and then improve primarily from its own repeated full-solution rollouts. The concrete problem is how to retain answer-correct attempts, make selected failures useful without treating them as desired outputs, and use multiple attempts at inference without adding a separate verifier model.

This framing creates two audit questions. First, what exactly survives from the positive and negative pools into direct SFT, refinement, and selection records? Second, does the released artifact preserve enough candidate, label, decision, and checkpoint lineage to reproduce or inspect the claimed loop? The paper specifies the recipe, but the official release does not freeze the self-generated rollout corpus or its construction ledger.
