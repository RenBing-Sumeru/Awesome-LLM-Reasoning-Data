The public dataset revision checked by the card contains 28,253 rows and five columns: 25,427 train and 2,826 test. The paper reports a seed-42 90/10 split and no exact-code overlap between splits. These counts verify the released runnable-reference object, not the size of the self-play corpus. The release omits upstream row IDs and the original instruction/Python solution, so semantic duplicates and source-level leakage cannot be audited from the rows alone.

Verifier yield is the central construction result. Across the main run's 1,750 nominal attempts per branch, only 34 SEQ examples passed Liquid Haskell (1.94%), while 903 SINQ examples passed the divergence check (51.6%). Thus the mixed E0 training pool has 937 verified pairs and is overwhelmingly negative despite balanced attempts. A pure-SINQ E1 run yields 1,839 pairs; the SEQ-heavy E2 run yields 62 SEQ plus 78 SINQ pairs; the volume-matched pure-SINQ E3 yields 156 pairs.

The reported downstream results support a training-effect hypothesis but do not by themselves establish dataset quality. For example, Bob improves from 50.2% to 57.4% accuracy on EquiBench OJ_A and from 56.5% to 69.8% on OJ_V; PySecDB accuracy rises from 67.6% to 68.8%. Transfer is weak or mixed on low-level categories and CodeXGLUE. In controlled comparisons, E0 beats higher-volume E1 on several structural equivalence settings, while E2/E3 are small (140/156 verified pairs), so the paper's claim that SEQ contributes distinctive reasoning evidence is suggestive and ablation-backed, not a general proof of data quality.
