SciBench asks whether large language models can solve open-ended, college-level scientific problems that require domain knowledge, multi-step reasoning, and numerical computation. The primary paper is "SciBench: Evaluating College-Level Scientific Problem-Solving Abilities of Large Language Models," first submitted to arXiv on 2023-07-20 and revised as v3 on 2024-06-28; the arXiv record and project page identify it as an ICML 2024 paper.

The evaluation surface is a set of textbook-derived mathematics, chemistry, and physics problems with reference answers or solutions, source metadata, model prompts, generated answers, and scoring/evaluation scripts. This is not a training recipe, not a process-supervision trace dataset, and not an interactive science-agent environment. Its feedback contract is answer-level correctness under a pinned evaluator, with a separate error-analysis protocol for diagnosing failed answers.

The concrete gap is that many earlier science-reasoning benchmarks emphasize high-school topics or elementary algebra. SciBench targets collegiate scientific problem solving: open-ended free-response questions, equations and theorems, units, calculus or differential equations, and in some cases visual context. It is valuable as a benchmark surface because public code, data, project page, and Hugging Face records make the problem records and scoring assumptions inspectable.
