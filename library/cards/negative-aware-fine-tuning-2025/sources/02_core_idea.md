For a question q, the current generation policy is split conceptually into a positive policy conditioned on reward 1 and a negative policy conditioned on reward 0. Their mixture, weighted by the question's empirical success rate, reconstructs the old policy. NFT parameterizes an implicit negative policy using the same trainable positive model and the frozen old-policy likelihood, so maximum-likelihood training on a negative answer changes the positive model without storing a second network. Positive answers use the usual likelihood-ratio term; negative answers use the implied negative likelihood ratio.

The practical token-level objective stores the old token probabilities, estimates success rate from 16 binary labels, filters groups without both reward outcomes, clips the negative likelihood ratio with a straight-through maximum to avoid invalid logarithms, and weights harder prompts more strongly. The default negative clip is 1.0. In strict on-policy conditions and with the specified prompt weighting, the paper proves the NFT and GRPO gradients coincide; off policy, their clipping behavior differs. Compared with RFT, the contribution is retaining incorrect generations as supervised optimization signals; compared with GRPO/DAPO, it provides a maximum-likelihood interpretation rather than a new verifier.
