For the rollout/search/test-time trace track, NFT provides a concrete schema for accepted/rejected online groups: dataset item and revision; rendered prompt; old and updated checkpoint; 16 candidate traces; extracted final answers; binary verifier result and verifier version; per-question success rate; positive/negative partition; truncation; token-level old-policy likelihood; implicit-negative ratio; clip event; prompt weight; mini-batch and rollout step; and subsequent evaluation. All-positive/all-negative filtering should be recorded rather than silently dropping those groups.

These records can support research on learning from failures, verifier-error sensitivity, SFT/RL objective equivalence, and hard-prompt curricula. They also enable auditing whether a negative signal reflects a mathematical error, formatting failure, truncation, or verifier bug. Official code and weights make implementation studies possible, but absent raw trace logs mean the paper's original online dataset cannot be reconstructed exactly. Negative answers should not be published as generic bad demonstrations without their prompt, verifier, old policy, and sampling context.
