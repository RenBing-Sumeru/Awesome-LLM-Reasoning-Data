NFT assumes a reliable answer-level reward and estimates a question's success rate from only 16 samples. A false negative can directly suppress a correct trajectory, while a false positive enters the positive likelihood term. Treating every truncated answer as negative conflates correctness with length. Groups that are all correct or all incorrect are filtered from the update, so the training distribution is shaped by current-policy difficulty and verifier behavior. The strict on-policy equivalence result does not cover long off-policy reuse, non-binary rewards without additional assumptions, or other clipping choices.

Experiments are limited to Qwen math models and DAPO-Math-17k; transfer to code, open-ended judging, or noisy reward models is unknown. The 7B and 32B conditions use different context and compute scales. Official code is available, but the repository page shows a small commit history and should be pinned. Code/data use Apache-2.0 while NVIDIA model weights use a non-commercial license. Full online rollout logs, verifier decisions, dataset revisions, decontamination analysis, and per-run seeds are not established as released. The current arXiv v3 author list and older project BibTeX also differ, so citation metadata needs manual reconciliation.
