RFT performs supervised maximum likelihood only on verifier-accepted answers; GRPO and DAPO assign group-relative advantages to both outcomes through policy-gradient objectives; preference methods require paired or ranked responses. NFT derives a negative conditional policy from the old policy, success rate, and trainable positive policy, then optimizes both accepted and rejected generations with a token-level supervised objective. It needs one trainable model because old-policy probabilities are cached.

The theoretical connection to GRPO clarifies that the supervised/RL distinction is partly an objective parameterization choice under binary on-policy data. The track-level direction signal is the explicit preservation of rejected trajectories, per-question reward mixtures, and old-policy likelihoods as valuable construction records. NFT does not introduce DAPO-Math-17k, VeRL, binary answer verification, dynamic sampling, or the Qwen models; its novelty is the implicit-negative likelihood and associated practical clipping/weighting. Benchmark gains are optimizer evidence, not proof that negatives are semantically valid, diverse, uncontaminated, or safe to reuse.
