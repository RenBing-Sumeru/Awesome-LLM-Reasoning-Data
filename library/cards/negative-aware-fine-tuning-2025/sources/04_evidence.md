Using identical data, infrastructure, and broad hyperparameters, Table 1 reports an average of 51.7 for NFT versus 48.3 for RFT and 51.2 for DAPO on the 7B model across six math benchmarks. On the 32B model, NFT averages 59.2, RFT 52.8, and DAPO 59.9. The paper reports three to four independent 7B runs for training curves and shows that removing negative-data learning reduces validation performance. Ablations favor harder-question weighting and find that overly aggressive negative penalties hurt performance; the default clip is 1.0.

The paper's theoretical result establishes gradient equivalence with GRPO only in the strict on-policy case under its stated weighting and binary-reward assumptions. Empirical parity outside that boundary is experimental, not a general equivalence theorem. Benchmark results support the optimizer recipe, but do not show that every negative trace is informative or correctly labeled. The official weights and code improve reproducibility, yet raw online trace and verifier provenance remain incomplete.
