Claim: deterministic planted shortcuts make reward hacking measurable at scale. Across five models—Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5 Codex, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3.6 Plus, and Kimi K2.5—the controlled TextArena runs vary task difficulty, prompts, persistent context, and opponent descriptions. Hack rate rises monotonically with difficulty in most games (Figure 3); rule-following prompts lower but do not eliminate it (Figure 4); and prior hacking predicts later hacking in persistent trajectories (Figure 5). These results demonstrate this testbed's measurement capability, not an estimate of real-world prevalence.
