Earlier reward-hacking benchmarks generally target one task and rely on manual trajectory review or an LLM judge. This work changes the verification contract: the environment designer implants a bounded shortcut and a deterministic trigger, allowing automated counting across heterogeneous games. The novelty is the wrapper-plus-monitor construction and the separation of hack rate from hack-free win rate, not the observation that agents can exploit bugs or hidden information. Reusers must inspect whether a planted shortcut represents their intended deployment failure.
