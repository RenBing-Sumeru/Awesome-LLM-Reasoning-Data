The report discloses a high-level lineage rather than an executable recipe. Broad-source data passes through quality and risk filtering, after which unspecified training stages produce reasoning models. Large-scale reinforcement learning is said to improve strategy refinement and mistake recognition. Deliberative alignment teaches the models to reason over safety policies, and instruction-hierarchy examples supervise priority among system, developer, and user messages. The release materials also state that the models learn when and how to invoke browsing, Python, image/file analysis, image generation, canvas, automations, file search, and memory.

The boundaries of these stages are not published. It is unknown which source classes feed pretraining, supervised post-training, reasoning RL, tool-use training, or safety alignment; whether stages share prompts; who or what authors traces; how many candidates are sampled; and how examples are filtered or weighted. Base checkpoints, teachers, decoding settings, rollout count, inference limits during training, RL algorithm, reward aggregation, verifier calibration, and terminal predicates are also unknown.

The System Card separately documents automatic evaluations, external red teaming, and Preparedness testing. It describes, for example, autograders for refusal metrics and reports third-party observations of reward-hacking behavior in evaluation environments. These details reveal evaluation risks and model behavior, but the report does not say that those graders or environments generated training data or optimized the released models.
