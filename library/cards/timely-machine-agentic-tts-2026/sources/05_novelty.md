The novelty is to make the resource being scaled correspond to the resource a user actually experiences. Previous token- or call-budget methods can control a proxy for work, but a tool agent's deadline depends on tool latency as well as generation. The paper formalizes that difference with a per-step latency ratio and tests it under controlled delays, turning a systems detail into a test-time allocation variable.

It also makes time awareness an internal policy skill rather than an external scheduler. The contribution is not merely a new timed benchmark: the cold-start and Timely-RL recipe teaches a model to read elapsed-time feedback and adjust its reasoning. This does not establish optimal scheduling for arbitrary tool stacks, but it gives a direct training and evaluation contract for whether extra wall-clock budget is converted into useful agent behavior.
