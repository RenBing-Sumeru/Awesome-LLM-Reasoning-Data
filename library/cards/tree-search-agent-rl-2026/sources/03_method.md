Training prompts come from three reported settings. Single-hop training uses 79,168 Natural Questions pairs; multi-hop training uses 90,447 HotpotQA pairs; web-agent training uses 2,000 sampled ASearcher-35K records plus 200 WebDancer records. Evaluation spans NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle, SimpleQA, a 103-item text-only GAIA subset, WebWalkerQA, and BrowseComp. The repository provides preprocessing logic, but immutable source revisions, selected row IDs, the ASearcher sampling seed, file hashes, and official processed Parquets are unavailable.

The current policy is the trace generator; no separate teacher produces online trajectories. Experiments cover Qwen2.5 Base/Instruct and Llama3.2 Base/Instruct at 1.5B, 3B, 7B, and 14B. Released local-QA scripts default to Qwen2.5-3B, while the web-agent script defaults to Qwen2.5-14B. Each trajectory alternates tagged `think` reasoning, `search` actions, environment-provided `information` observations, and a terminal `answer`.

For local single-hop and multi-hop QA, the `search` action queries an E5 retriever over a Wikipedia-2018 corpus and receives the top three passages. Web-agent QA calls a Bing/web-search API and receives the top ten passages. An episode ends on the closing `answer` tag or EOS, or when it reaches the action budget: three tool calls for local QA and five for web-agent QA. The paper-run E5 index/corpus hashes, Bing responses, caches, endpoint versions, and replayable environment snapshots are not published.

Tree construction begins with \(M\) independently generated complete chains. Within each tree, the system randomly samples \(N\) nodes that have not already emitted an answer. Each chosen node contributes its complete root-to-node trajectory as a fixed prefix; the policy continues from that state until another complete answer leaf is formed. The process repeats for \(L\) rounds. Random expansion is important: the branch point is not selected by value, EM/F1, a learned critic, or a judge.

Default \(M=2,N=2,L=1\) creates six completed leaves at an expected token/tool budget comparable to about four independent trajectories. The paper studies expected budgets near 2, 4, 8, and 16 and varies \(M,N,L\) to expose the exploration/reuse trade-off. \(M\) increases independently rooted diversity; \(N\) and \(L\) increase shared-prefix reuse and the number or depth of local comparisons. Too little \(M\) concentrates trajectories around few roots, while serial \(L\) increases dependency and reduces efficiency.

The manager next samples \(K\) leaves per tree. It prunes leaves/subtrees not retained, packs the selected full trajectories and masks, assigns programmatic terminal outcomes, and passes selected responses plus `tree_uid` and training statistics to the optimizer. Default \(K=3\) retains all three leaves in each default tree. The returned public-facing runtime payload does not include `node_uid`, parent/child pointers, branch-point choices, or the complete topology, and the manager deletes the tree after packing.

Local QA leaves receive normalized exact match; web-agent leaves receive normalized answer-token F1; malformed structure receives a 0.2 format penalty. Selected outcomes are converted into an intra-tree normalized advantage and an inter-tree normalized advantage. The two are summed, broadcast over the selected trajectory’s policy tokens, and used in the GRPO objective. Tree-GRPO is implemented with Search-R1 and VeRL, AdamW, learning rate \(10^{-6}\), K3 KL coefficient 0.001, and the reported 180 local-QA steps or 34 web-agent steps/two epochs.

Released scripts use temperature 1.0, but complete top-p, top-k, seed, and per-record realized token/tool costs are unknown. The paper reports maximum response lengths of 4,096 for local QA and 8,000 for web-agent QA; released scripts set `data.max_response_length` to 500 and 2,000 respectively. The artifacts do not explain how the script’s per-generation limit maps to the paper-level cap, so exact budget reproduction remains unresolved.
