For each question, the authors first prepare an offline pool of 128 model responses and form a controller trajectory by sampling up to 32 of them. At round t, the state contains the top five answer frequencies, the current number of candidates, and entropy. The controller chooses stop, or asks for one, two, or four additional responses in parallel. Stopping returns a majority vote; an episode also ends when the candidate cap is reached.

The reward combines a positive or negative terminal outcome with stepwise penalties for latency and computation. The default terminal target is the running majority that would result after continuing to the 32-sample limit, rather than the benchmark’s ground-truth label; this deliberately aligns the reward with answer-pool statistics available to the controller. The paper trains the policy using PPO on 200 DAPO questions, then evaluates Qwen3 variants and GPT-4.1-nano on AIME24, AIME25, and HMMT25 against fixed self-consistency, Adaptive Self-Consistency, and Early-Stopping Consistency.
