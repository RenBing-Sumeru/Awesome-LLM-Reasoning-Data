The central idea is to make adaptive sampling a finite-horizon Markov decision process rather than a confidence heuristic. The controller’s state summarizes the observed answer pool through the top answer-count classes, the number of candidates already drawn, and answer-distribution entropy. Its action is either to terminate and return the current majority answer or to obtain a small parallel batch of additional candidates.

Correctness is not optimized alone. Continuing receives separate penalties for another sampling round and for every new candidate, while stopping receives a terminal reward based on the majority answer that the same run would produce at its fixed sample limit. A four-layer MLP trained with PPO therefore learns a cost-sensitive stopping rule without reading hidden states, logits, prompt semantics, or intermediate chain-of-thought text.
