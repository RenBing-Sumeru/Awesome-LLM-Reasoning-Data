The principal evidence is a three-benchmark comparison across four samplers. On Qwen3-4B-Instruct-Thinking, the controller keeps the average accuracy at 54.6%, essentially matching 54.8% for 32-sample self-consistency, while using 10.9 samples and 2.8 rounds on average. Under the paper’s default configurations, this is fewer samples and rounds than both Adaptive Self-Consistency and Early-Stopping Consistency at nearly unchanged accuracy.

The paper also tests whether the controller learns a portable allocation signal rather than a model-specific threshold. A policy trained from Qwen3-0.6B samples transfers to GPT-4.1-nano and Qwen3-4B-Instruct scaling curves. In a reward ablation on Qwen3-4B-Instruct, the running-majority target attains 54.6% average accuracy with 10.9 samples, whereas substituting the real label lowers accuracy to 53.7% and increases average consumption to 17.0 samples; this supports the claimed match between the state representation and reward target.
