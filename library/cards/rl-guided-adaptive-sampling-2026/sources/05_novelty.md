Earlier adaptive self-consistency methods use posterior thresholds, fixed chunks, or other designed stopping rules. This work changes the decision object: instead of asking a heuristic whether the present answer distribution looks settled, it learns a policy over answer-pool statistics, explicit sampling actions, and a reward that prices both latency and candidate count. The Lagrangian interpretation makes the policy’s two penalties legible as relaxed resource constraints rather than incidental tuning coefficients.

The contribution is not a new reasoning generator or a richer verifier. Its distinctive unit is the lightweight controller placed outside an unchanged sampler, and the evidence concerns the accuracy–rounds–samples frontier produced by that controller. Before reuse, readers should verify how final answers are extracted, whether majority voting is valid for the target task, how offline response pools were generated, and whether wall-clock cost follows the same relation as the paper’s sample and round proxies.
