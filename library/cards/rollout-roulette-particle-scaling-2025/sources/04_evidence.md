The NeurIPS paper evaluates particle filtering against greedy generation, self-consistency, best-of-N, beam search, and other reward-guided scaling baselines. It reports mathematical evaluation on MATH500 and AIME 2024, as well as broader reasoning tests including NumGLUE Chemistry and FinanceBench. The study also ablates reward models, reward aggregation, temperature, and the split between parallel and iterative computation.

The authors report a 4–16 times better scaling rate than deterministic-search counterparts, and report that Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct surpasses GPT-4o accuracy with four rollouts while the 7B model reaches the reported o1-level comparison with 32 rollouts. These are benchmark-specific comparisons under selected reward models and budgets; reproductions should report reward-model quality, total tokens, rollout count, parallel resources, and accuracy together.
