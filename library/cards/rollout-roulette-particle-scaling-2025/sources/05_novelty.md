Prior reward-guided reasoning search generally treats a high partial reward as a reason to retain a trajectory and a low reward as a reason to delete it. Rollout Roulette replaces this deterministic top-N decision with posterior sampling over a population of weighted candidates. It therefore addresses reward-model uncertainty at the allocation layer rather than assuming a better reward model can eliminate the problem.

The contribution also links classical sequential Monte Carlo concepts to concrete reasoning controls: particle weights, stochastic resampling, reward aggregation, and the division of budget between parallel and iterative computation. The novelty is not just a new search label; it changes the semantics of a partial reward from a hard pruning threshold to probabilistic evidence that can still preserve alternative solution modes.
