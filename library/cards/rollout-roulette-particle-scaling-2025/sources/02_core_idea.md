The paper recasts inference-time scaling as approximate posterior inference in a state-space model. The language model is the transition model that expands a partial trajectory, while a process reward model provides an approximate observation of step acceptability. Rather than pursuing only the single highest-scoring mode, the system keeps a weighted population of candidate trajectories representing uncertainty over acceptable solutions.

Particle filtering supplies the allocation rule. High-reward trajectories receive more weight, but stochastic resampling means they do not immediately monopolize the budget and lower-scoring alternatives can survive. The key idea is to use reward values as noisy likelihood evidence, not as a final deterministic verdict; computation is consequently shared between exploitation of good paths and exploration of paths whose value has not yet been resolved.
