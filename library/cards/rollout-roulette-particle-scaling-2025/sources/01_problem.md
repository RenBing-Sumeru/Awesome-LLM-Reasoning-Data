Reward-guided search can spend more inference computation on promising partial solutions, but beam-like methods discard candidates as soon as an imperfect process reward model assigns them a low early score. A trajectory that starts weakly can later become correct, whereas a reward-hacked trajectory can look attractive at the first few steps. Greedy pruning therefore turns an uncertain score into an irreversible allocation decision.

The paper asks how a fixed rollout budget can preserve exploration without ignoring reward information. Its target is not simply to generate more chains: it is to decide which partial trajectories remain alive after each reasoning step, so that noisy early judgments do not consume all computation or eliminate the only route to a correct answer.
