The prior baseline is terminal-outcome supervision or post-hoc failure diagnosis: an environment says that an episode failed, and a human or LLM names a plausible responsible step. Neither alone creates a process label with an executable intervention test. AgenTracer changes the data-construction interface by retaining an agent/step label only after a proposed local correction flips failure to success or an injected local mutation flips success to failure.

That creates a distinct data object: a trajectory-level failure paired with state/action-level attribution whose selection depends on counterfactual replay. It also creates a training interface in which exact agent identity and distance to the decisive step become separate reward components. For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is the conversion of sparse environment outcomes into denser process supervision without requiring manual step labels for every trajectory.

The components are not individually new. Multi-agent trajectories, model-generated critiques, fault injection, environment replay, Gaussian distance rewards, and GRPO all have prior analogues. The paper's contribution is their integration into a causal-attribution construction and training loop; scale and benchmark performance alone are not novelty evidence.

The quality signal is conditional rather than absolute: retained interventions demonstrably flip the task-specific evaluator under one replay. That is stronger than an unsupported textual diagnosis, but weaker than proof of a unique or minimal causal fault. Before reuse, a builder needs paired interventions and replay logs, evaluator and environment pins, rejected attempts, branch labels, split lineage, decontamination evidence, and an explicit license. None is recoverable from the public 127-row parquet alone.
