Appendix Table 3 supplies the clearest scale ledger. Of 4,655 raw trajectories, 2,476 become labeled pairs: coding 2,170 -> 1,288, math 1,185 -> 630, and agentic 1,300 -> 558. The reported test counts are 147, 63, and 56, totaling 266. These are paper counts, not verified release counts. Direct inspection of the pinned official parquet finds 127 coding-test rows rather than 147, 127 unique `question_ID` values, and no nulls in the seven top-level columns.

On handcrafted Who&When, Table 1 reports AgenTracer agent accuracy of 69.10/63.82 and step accuracy of 20.68/20.68 under the paper's `w/ G` / `w/o G` conditions. On automated Who&When, the corresponding agent accuracies are 69.62/63.73 and step accuracies are 42.86/37.30. These author-reported results evaluate attribution under the benchmark setup; they do not independently validate the construction labels or public release.

Table 2 gives task-family evidence. On the Agentic subset, AgenTracer step accuracy is 36.17/35.55 versus 13.49/15.31 for Qwen3-8B under `w/ G` / `w/o G`; on Math, AgenTracer reports 57.63/57.63. The comparison supports the claim that the trained tracer outperforms its base model on these reported attribution tests. It does not isolate the contribution of corpus size, DeepSeek-R1 labels, the reward shape, or GRPO because the public artifacts do not enable that ablation to be replayed.

For downstream use, Section 5.3 and Figure 3 inject AgenTracer feedback for three rounds. The text reports a 4.8-point gain for OWL+GAIA and a 14.21-point gain for MaAS+MATH-500, with GPT-4.1 used to instantiate Self-Refine and CRITIC baselines. This is evidence that attribution feedback can improve the tested systems under the authors' setup, not evidence that the full feedback corpus or an agent-training release is public.

All performance numbers are author-reported and were not independently reproduced in the artifact audit. The strongest independently checkable evidence concerns artifact shape and incompleteness: one 127-row parquet, a partial MetaGPT pipeline, three repository commits, no tags or releases, no root license, and no confirmed full data, model weights, or RL scripts.
