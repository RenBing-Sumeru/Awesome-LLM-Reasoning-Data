For `rollout_search_test_time_trace_data`, Weaver is useful because it makes the selector side of repeated sampling explicit. The reusable object is not only the winning answer: it is the whole candidate set plus the signals and fitted state that explain why one trace was retained. This supports test-time-compute studies that separate gains from more generations, more verifier calls, stronger verifier models, different aggregation, and distillation.

An auditable raw record should retain benchmark and split revision, prompt ID and text, generator checkpoint, prompt template, decoding settings and seed, candidate ID, full response, extracted answer, correctness label where legally available, and truncation status. A verification record should add every verifier checkpoint and revision, judge prompt/parser, raw score, normalized score, threshold, binary vote, filtering decision, TPR/TNR estimate, class prior, Weaver posterior, and compute cost. A decision record should retain the complete candidate ranking, selected ID, tie policy, reject-all policy, and evaluation outcome. Distillation records should also bind each query-response to its Weaver target, data split, ModernBERT checkpoint, optimizer state, and source ensemble revision.

The public code and Hugging Face collection enable selector ablations, verifier-subset studies, re-estimation under new priors, and examination of whether a compact verifier preserves selection behavior. Reusers should pin revisions and recompute thresholds and weak-supervision parameters after changing the generator, candidate pool, verifier suite, or domain. Where incorrect pseudo-labels are unacceptable, add an independent correctness gate rather than treating a high Weaver posterior as proof.

The work is also useful as an audit case: it shows why Pass@K, selected-answer success, posterior calibration, and released-data quality are different claims. Benchmark gains motivate the aggregation recipe; they do not certify the candidate traces or distilled pseudo-label corpus.
