Repeated sampling separates generation coverage from selection. For a query with K candidates, Pass@K asks whether any candidate is correct, whereas the selected-answer success rate asks whether a verification strategy actually chooses a correct one. Their difference is the generation-verification gap. The paper studies the case where correct responses are often present but a single reward model, LM judge, majority vote, or unweighted ensemble fails to identify them reliably.

The practical constraint is that available general-purpose verifiers are heterogeneous and weak. Reward models emit continuous scores with incompatible ranges and training losses; LM judges emit binary verdicts; individual verifier accuracy varies by task and candidate distribution. Supervised weighting can exploit those differences but needs labeled query-response pairs, while querying dozens of verifiers for every sampled response is expensive. Weaver therefore asks whether agreement statistics over unlabeled candidates can estimate verifier reliability well enough for response selection, and whether the resulting ensemble can be compressed.

For this atlas, the primary object is not a benchmark score alone. It is a repeated-sampling decision record: benchmark query, candidate responses, per-verifier scores or votes, normalization and threshold choices, fitted verifier error parameters, posterior correctness scores, and the selected response. The main decision boundary is test-time selection and verifier distillation, not generator post-training.
