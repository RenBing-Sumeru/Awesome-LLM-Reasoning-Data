The evidence supports a selector and verifier-compression claim, not a blanket data-quality claim.

- The NeurIPS proceedings abstract reports 86.2% average accuracy for a Llama 3.3 70B Instruct generator with smaller judge/reward models, and says the 400M distilled verifier retains 98.7% of full Weaver accuracy while reducing verification compute by up to 99.97%.
- The accessible arXiv version reports a different Table 1 average: Weaver with 100 candidates obtains 93.4% on MATH500, 72.1% on GPQA Diamond, 94.9% on MMLU College, and 90.2% on MMLU-Pro, averaging 87.7%, versus 72.2% for majority voting and a 91.9% Pass@100 oracle. These values must remain attached to that version rather than silently replacing the proceedings result.
- The arXiv ablation reports 84.5% average with a 1% development fraction versus 79.0% with a naive 0.5 threshold; extreme-marginal pruning reaches 85.2% versus 79.4% without dropping verifiers. These experiments support the importance of thresholding and filtering within the evaluated setup.
- For distillation, the arXiv text reports that ModernBERT-Large captures 98.2% of Weaver performance and reduces verifier FLOPs by 99.97%. The 98.2%/98.7% wording also differs across official versions and should be version-tagged.

The official GitHub and Hugging Face artifacts verify that code, scored benchmark generations, and distilled models exist. They do not establish that every verifier vote or Weaver posterior is correct, that released records reproduce every published table under pinned revisions, or that the transformed records are uncontaminated and license-complete. Benchmark success rate measures whether the selected answer matches benchmark ground truth; it is not direct evidence that the candidate corpus or pseudo-label set is uniformly high quality.
