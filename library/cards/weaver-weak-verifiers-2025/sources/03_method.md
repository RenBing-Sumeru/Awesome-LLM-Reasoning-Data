The reported pipeline has three stages.

1. Generation and scoring. Llama 3.1 8B/70B Instruct and Llama 3.3 70B Instruct generate repeated responses, depending on the experiment. Main comparisons use 100 candidates per query. The study covers MATH500, GPQA Diamond, MMLU College, and MMLU-Pro, with BBH and AIMO in appendix analyses. Candidate query-response pairs are scored by reward models, process reward models, and LM judges drawn from the RewardBench, Process Reward Bench, and Chatbot Arena ecosystems. The main arXiv comparison describes 33 verifier models in the 7B-72B range.

2. Adaptation and weak supervision. Reward scores are robustly rescaled by mapping the 5th and 95th percentiles to 0 and 1. Continuous outputs are binarized; LM judges already provide binary verdicts. Weaver removes verifiers whose positive marginals are inconsistent with the estimated dataset class balance. Pairwise and marginal vote statistics are matched to a conditional-independence latent model by gradient optimization, yielding verifier-specific TPR/TNR estimates. About 1% labeled development data estimates class balance, thresholds, and the correctness prior. A posterior score is computed for every candidate, and the maximum-posterior response is selected. Difficulty clustering is an oracle exploration because cluster difficulty uses ground-truth correctness; it is not the default deployable workflow.

3. Distillation. Query-response pairs are paired with Weaver posterior scores and used to train ModernBERT-Large (396M) cross-encoders. The paper reports an 80:20 train/evaluation split for this step. The generator remains frozen.

The official repository exposes generation, selection, and distillation code. Its Hugging Face collection contains benchmark generations pre-scored by multiple verifiers and distilled models. A reproducible run still needs exact dataset revision, generator checkpoint and decoding configuration, verifier checkpoint list, prompts/parsers, score normalization, threshold and filtering states, development split, fitted parameters, candidate IDs, and software/hardware revisions. Temperature is not consolidated in the paper metadata reviewed here and remains unknown.
