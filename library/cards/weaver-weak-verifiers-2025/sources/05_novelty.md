Prior repeated-sampling systems commonly select by answer frequency, one reward model, one LM judge, or an unweighted average. Weighted supervised ensembles and classical weak supervision already existed, but the former need labels and the latter assumes weak sources whose behavior can be represented and identified from agreement statistics.

Weaver's specific contribution is to adapt that weak-supervision machinery to heterogeneous verifier outputs: robust score normalization, binary conversion, class-balance-aware marginal filtering, asymmetric TPR/TNR estimation from pairwise vote moments, and posterior response selection. This makes verifier count and verifier diversity explicit test-time scaling axes rather than treating verification as a single fixed scorer.

A second contribution is operational separation between expensive label construction and deployment. Ensemble posteriors become soft supervision for a 396M cross-encoder, so the high-cost multi-verifier decision rule can be approximated by one compact model.

What is not new remains important. Weaver does not introduce repeated sampling, reward models, LM judges, process reward models, Naive Bayes, weak supervision, or knowledge distillation. It does not create programmatic correctness guarantees, remove the need for a correct candidate, or prove that more verifiers always help; the paper observes diminishing returns and correlated bias. A reuse study should compare against majority vote, strongest single verifier, matched unweighted ensembles, supervised upper bounds, and fixed-compute baselines.
