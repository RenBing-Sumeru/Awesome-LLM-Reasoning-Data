The paper releases complete model responses to SAT mathematics problems, annotation guidelines, and seven episode labels: Read, Analyze, Explore, Plan, Implement, Verify, and Transition. Both sentence and paragraph boundaries are retained, enabling analysis of state proportions, transition probabilities, and long-CoT structure. The resource supports functional process analysis and controllable reasoning rather than step correctness, value, or earliest-error ground truth. Sentence and paragraph granularities allow comparison of local behavior with longer episodes and provide hierarchical targets for automatic classifiers. The public guidebook provides boundary cases and decision rules so other teams can reproduce the annotation protocol.
