Large reasoning models produce increasingly long traces, but researchers lack a theory-grounded way to explain how those traces move from analysis to planning, implementation, and verification. Classifying CoT steps as correct or incorrect evaluates quality but not functional roles or cognitive-state transitions across models. These functional labels provide a common language for quantitatively comparing which stage a model is in rather than relying on a few qualitative examples. Without a common process language, researchers describe reflection or planning from a few examples and cannot quantitatively compare state transitions in analysis, exploration, implementation, and verification.

This work systematically applies Schoenfeld's human mathematical problem-solving Episode Theory to LRM traces and builds seven sentence- and paragraph-level process labels.
