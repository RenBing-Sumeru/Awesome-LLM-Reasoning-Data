The corpus and guidelines can train seven-class episode classifiers to analyze planning, implementation, verification, and strategy switching in new models or compare how prompts and RL recipes alter transitions. Classification F1 should be reported separately at sentence and paragraph levels with human cross-model audits. Training a PRM requires additional correctness or outcome labels because episodes describe what a step does, not whether it does it correctly. The final analysis can report episode proportions, transition matrices, and associations with correctness but should not be interpreted as clinical cognitive diagnosis. The output can also index a small set of representative trajectories for close human reading. Success checks should report sentence, paragraph, and episode-boundary agreement, seven-class F1, and transition stability. PRM training requires correctness labels rather than episode classes.
