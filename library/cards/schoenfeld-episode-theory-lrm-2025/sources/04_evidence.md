Annotation analysis reveals reproducible but distinct episode-transition patterns across large reasoning models. Some repeatedly cycle between Implement and Verify, while others enter Explore or re-plan after errors. Long outputs from reasoning models are not merely repeated calculations but include more monitoring and strategy shifts, and correct and incorrect trajectories show different state distributions. The study provides descriptive and preliminary statistical evidence rather than proving that changing an episode causally improves answers. Transition analysis also reveals that some models rarely verify after implementation, whereas others frequently return to planning or exploration. The analysis supports stable but distinct LRM state transitions and differences between correct and incorrect traces and verification frequency. It is descriptive evidence, not proof that changing an episode improves performance.
