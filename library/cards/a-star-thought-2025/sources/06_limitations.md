Construction requires an existing long CoT and its target solution. Because BIS and the future-cost term condition on that solution, the method is an offline distillation recipe rather than a directly deployable search procedure for an unseen question whose answer is unknown. It also inherits the source composition, trace-generation choices, possible contamination, and rights of s1K-1.1; the AStar-Thought release does not provide an independent provenance or decontamination audit.

The s1.1-32B validator is a model-based selector, not a formal proof checker. It can accept a path that appears capable of reaching the supplied solution despite a hidden logical gap, or reject an alternative but valid path. Moreover, reaching the maximum depth causes the current path to be returned even without validator acceptance. Selecting and reordering spans may remove dependencies or introduce discontinuities, although neighboring steps are included to reduce this risk.

The released dataset has only about 1,000 mathematics records and one visible training split; a complete rejection manifest, validator confidence calibration, per-item source lineage, and release license are not established in the checked artifacts. Data construction itself requires GPT-2 scoring and repeated s1.1-32B validation, so “low-resource” primarily describes the trained model’s inference regime rather than cost-free dataset construction. The paper studies SFT only; RL use remains future work.
