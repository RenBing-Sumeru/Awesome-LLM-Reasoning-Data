For QwQ-32B at a 512-token budget, the paper reports that direct s1K-1.1 SFT reaches 12.3 average accuracy and 2.41 ACU across MATH500, AMC23, OlympiadBench, and GSM8K, while A*-Thought SFT reaches 29.4 accuracy and 5.99 ACU. At a 4096-token budget, A*-Thought reduces average response length from 2826.00 to 1876.66 tokens relative to direct s1K-1.1 SFT, while average accuracy changes from 70.6 to 69.3. It also reports that compressed training data contains 31.31% of the original token volume and reduces training time by approximately 21.58%–24.25% across the three backbones.

Ablations and evaluations on multiple backbones support the claim that BIS-guided A* selection can improve the accuracy–length trade-off compared with the tested prompting and compression baselines. They do not establish that every retained span is logically necessary, that every validator judgment is correct, or that the upstream s1K-1.1 records are uncontaminated. The official GitHub repository, 1K-row dataset, and three trained checkpoints make the construction output inspectable, but benchmark performance is not a substitute for trace-level quality or provenance auditing.
