Formal theorem proving provides a stronger terminal check than natural-language math: a Lean proof either compiles for the supplied statement in the configured environment or it does not. The remaining data problem is harder than collecting successful proofs. A useful prover must receive sufficiently varied formal statements, learn long proof plans, recover from compiler failures, and avoid collapsing to a narrow set of proof patterns after repeated expert iteration and reinforcement learning.

Goedel-Prover-V2 treats those requirements as separate construction stages. It first builds verified whole-proof supervision, then adds records that pair a failed proof with Lean compiler feedback and a correction, expands the statement pool through two scaffolded synthesis routes, and finally applies reinforcement learning plus checkpoint averaging. The relevant objects are therefore not only final proofs: they include formalized statements, failed attempts, compiler errors, corrected attempts, extracted subgoals, synthetic variants, rollout rewards, and checkpoint lineage.

The verification boundary remains narrow. Lean acceptance establishes a proof of the supplied formal statement under a particular Lean/mathlib setup. It does not establish that an autoformalized statement faithfully represents its natural-language source, that the source is uncontaminated, or that the unreleased training records can be independently reconstructed.
