Self-Consistency Preference Optimization (ICML 2025, PMLR 267) addresses self-training for reasoning when gold answers are absent and an external reward model may be unreliable out of distribution. Standard inference-time self-consistency samples several solutions and chooses the most frequent final answer. SCPO asks whether that frequency signal can instead create preference supervision for model training.

The primary data object is an iteratively generated preference record: unlabeled seed or model-generated problem, k sampled responses with rationales and parsed final answers, answer-equivalence clusters, vote count for each cluster, one chosen response from the highest-vote cluster, one rejected response from the lowest-vote cluster, vote-margin weight, filtering threshold, iteration/model version, and optional gold-label provenance in the semi-supervised variant. The paper reports this construction and experiments but the accepted artifacts do not identify an official preference-pair dataset, rollout log, or code release. Consensus is a programmatic proxy, not a ground-truth verifier, so shared model error must remain visible.
