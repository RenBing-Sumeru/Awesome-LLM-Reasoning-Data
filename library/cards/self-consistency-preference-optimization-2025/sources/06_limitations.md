SCPO assumes a parseable, unambiguous final answer and that repeated agreement correlates with correctness. Shared misconceptions, deterministic decoding modes, answer-normalization errors, and semantically equivalent but textually different answers can distort votes. As training increases consistency, response diversity falls and rejected examples become harder to obtain, which is why later rounds require extra sampling and higher thresholds.

The main experiments cover Llama-3/3.1 8B families and math or logic tasks; extension to open-ended outputs is left for future work. ZebraLogic provides only test puzzles, and the appendix's use of test queries for transductive preference construction changes the evaluation boundary. No code, generated queries, raw responses, pair selections, random choices, or licenses are released through the accepted links. Dataset contamination and generated-problem duplication controls are not established. Aggregate performance does not certify preference-label quality.
