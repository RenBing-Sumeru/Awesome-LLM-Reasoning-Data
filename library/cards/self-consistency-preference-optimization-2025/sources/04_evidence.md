The official PMLR record identifies the paper in the Proceedings of the 42nd International Conference on Machine Learning, pages 49737–49751. Experiments report gains over the seed model and unsupervised baselines on GSM8K, MATH, and ZebraLogic. Ablations show the vote-margin-weighted loss outperforming an unweighted version in the reported settings, and threshold analysis on MATH demonstrates a quantity–quality trade-off: raising tau increases the measured chosen/rejected accuracy margin but sharply reduces retained pairs. Appendix analysis reports positive association between vote share and correctness and finds k=8 a compute/association compromise, with k=16 slightly stronger.

This evidence supports self-consistency frequency as a useful preference proxy on the studied parseable-answer tasks. It does not make plurality a verifier: correlated errors can produce a confident wrong cluster, and higher benchmark accuracy does not validate every chosen/rejected label. The transductive appendix experiment builds preference data using test queries and reports additional gains; it must remain separate from the primary train/dev/test protocol and must not be treated as leakage-free training evidence. No raw pairs, rollouts, or code were confirmed from the official PMLR page.
