SuperGLUE asks whether a general-purpose English language-understanding benchmark can remain useful after GLUE-style scores had become too easy for strong pretrained systems. The primary source is Wang et al., "SuperGLUE: A Stickier Benchmark for General-Purpose Language Understanding Systems," released on arXiv in 2019 and presented at NeurIPS 2019; the official site is super.gluebenchmark.com.

The concrete gap is benchmark saturation: the paper reports that GLUE submissions had surpassed a non-expert human estimate, so a single aggregate score on GLUE no longer separated the next generation of NLU systems well. SuperGLUE keeps the GLUE design pattern, but rebuilds the task suite around harder tasks, private test labels, an evaluation server, usage rules, human baselines, diagnostics, and a public leaderboard.

The evaluation object is not a training recipe. It is a benchmark submission made of predictions for eight main tasks: BoolQ, CommitmentBank, COPA, MultiRC, ReCoRD, RTE, WiC, and WSC, plus diagnostic predictions. The feedback contract is task-specific automatic scoring: accuracy, F1, exact match, macro-F1, and MCC-style diagnostics, with multi-metric tasks averaged before computing the overall SuperGLUE score.
