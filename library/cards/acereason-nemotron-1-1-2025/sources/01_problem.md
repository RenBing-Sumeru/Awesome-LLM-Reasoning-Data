The primary source is the NVIDIA-authored arXiv v1 report submitted on 16 June 2025. It asks two linked post-training questions: how scaling unique prompts versus responses per prompt changes a 7B SFT initializer, and whether a stronger SFT initializer remains better after a long, verifier-based RL curriculum. For this Atlas, the important object is not the leaderboard row but the disclosed chain from prompt sources to teacher outputs, rule rewards, stage selection, and released artifacts.

Three related objects must remain separate. The public AceReason-1.1-SFT train split stores `category`, `source`, `input`, and `output`; all outputs are stated to come from DeepSeek-R1. The unreleased RL object is described as a question and ground-truth answer, 8 or 16 on-policy responses, rule-derived scores, and token-level advantages. The final model is evaluated on math and code benchmarks under explicit sampling budgets, but those scores do not prove that the SFT release equals the consumed blend or that the verifier is reliable.

This paper belongs in `frontier_reports_data_disclosure_ledger` because it exposes more of the data and feedback pipeline than a weight-only model announcement while still leaving material gaps: the paper's v7 blend has 2.2M samples from a 383K-prompt pool, whereas the public SFT artifact has 3,970,332 rows and source counts totaling 514,807 questions. A related 49,585-row AceReason-Math artifact exists, but a complete code-RL corpus, tests, reward code, and immutable stage-to-checkpoint manifest do not. The Card therefore treats the work as a partial disclosure ledger, not as a fully replayable construction recipe. (Paper §§3.1–3.2; official SFT and AceReason-Math cards.)
