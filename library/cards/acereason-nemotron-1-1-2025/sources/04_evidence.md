The release evidence is concrete but narrower than the training claim. The official AceReason-1.1-SFT page reports 3,970,332 rows: 2,668,741 math and 1,301,591 code samples. Its viewer exposes only a train split with four string fields (`category`, `source`, `input`, `output`), and the card states that all outputs were generated by DeepSeek-R1 and that the dataset is CC BY 4.0. AceReason-Math separately exposes 49,585 `problem`/`answer` rows, also in one train split under CC BY 4.0. Neither artifact includes rollout groups, reward logs, stage membership, or the code-RL tests.

The SFT scaling experiment reports v1 as 18K math plus 18K code samples and v7 as 1.2M math plus 1.0M code samples. From v3 to v4, adding 16K math samples while expanding unique prompts is associated with +4 percentage points on AIME24 and +2 on AIME25. With a similar prompt set but more responses per prompt, v7 raises AIME25 from 41.3 to 49.3 over v6. Models continue improving through approximately epochs 5–6. These are seven-blend experiments under the authors' setup; they do not isolate teacher quality, guarantee the public release's equivalence, or prove data quality. (Paper §4.4, Figs. 4–6.)

Against the authors' own SFT-7B starting point, Table 1 reports the final model moving from 62.0 to 72.6 on AIME24, 48.4 to 64.8 on AIME25, 48.8 to 57.2 on LiveCodeBench v5, and 43.8 to 52.1 on v6, using the paper's avg@n protocol and default 32K inference limit. These +10.6, +16.4, +8.4, and +8.3 point changes establish performance under the reported pipeline; they do not identify which data row, verifier decision, or optimizer choice caused the gain.

The ablations supply more discriminating evidence. At Math Stage-2, training temperature 0.85 yields 67.6/56.8/52.1/47.1 on AIME24/AIME25/LCB v5/LCB v6, compared with 64.6/52.4/50.1/45.6 at 0.6 and 65.3/56.7/51.6/45.7 at 1.0; the authors turn this into an entropy-near-0.3 rule of thumb, not a universal optimum. Skipping 8K Stage-1 leads Stage-2 AIME25 to plateau at 51.8 rather than 56.7, although Stage-1 first lowers AIME25 from 48.4 to 44.6; the claimed reasoning-compression explanation is an author hypothesis. At final 32K math Stage-4, no overlong filtering outperforms filtering at a 32K evaluation cap: 71.4 vs 70.2 on AIME24, 63.5 vs 62.3 on AIME25, 53.5 vs 52.0 on LCB v5, and 48.0 vs 45.1 on v6. (Paper §§4.5.2–4.5.5, Figs. 8–10, Tables 2–3.)

The paper also reports that the AIME25 RL gain shrinks from 8.3 points at pass@8 to 1.2 at pass@128, while LiveCodeBench v6 retains a 5-point gain at pass@128. Appendix C uses 256 generated outputs per AIME item and 128 per LiveCodeBench item, resampling each k 100 times. This budget-sensitive result is evidence about sampling behavior, not about release completeness or verifier correctness. No independent reproduction is reported in the checked sources.
