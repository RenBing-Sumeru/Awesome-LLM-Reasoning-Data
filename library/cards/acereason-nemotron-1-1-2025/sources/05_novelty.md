The paper's contribution is primarily a system-level study of how supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) interact for a single 7B math-and-code reasoner, rather than a new primitive training algorithm. It exposes several useful axes that are often collapsed in frontier-model reports: SFT mixture size and composition, staged context-length curricula, rollout temperature, the decision to skip or retain an easier RL stage, and stage-dependent handling of truncated responses.

Relative to AceReason-Nemotron 1.0, the reported recipe starts from a stronger joint math-and-code SFT checkpoint and studies a larger sequence of SFT mixtures. It then applies strict on-policy GRPO in separate math and code curricula. The temperature analysis uses a heuristic target of roughly 0.3 temperature-adjusted rollout entropy, and the overlong-response experiments show that a filter helpful at shorter contexts need not remain helpful at 32K. These are empirical design signals, not universal prescriptions.

The public SFT release adds practical value beyond the paper: it exposes 3,970,332 prompt-response rows with `category`, `source`, `input`, and `output` fields and states that every response was generated by DeepSeek-R1. The associated AceReason-Math release exposes 49,585 filtered math problems for RL-related use. However, neither release establishes an immutable row-level mapping to every paper training stage.

Several ingredients are not new: distillation from a stronger reasoning model, GRPO, curriculum learning, rule-based answer checking, code tests, and overlong-response filtering all predate this work. The distinctive contribution is the combined ablation record and the evidence that interventions may reverse effect as the context-length regime changes. Because the exact training code, code-verifier assets, and stage manifests are not released, the novelty should be read as a partially disclosed recipe and empirical study, not as a fully reproducible data pipeline.
