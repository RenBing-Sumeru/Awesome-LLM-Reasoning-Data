AceReason-Nemotron 1.1 combines a DeepSeek-R1-generated math/code SFT initializer with a staged, strictly on-policy GRPO curriculum, then studies which data-scale, sampling-temperature, response-budget, and difficulty-selection choices change the resulting policy. The direction signal is the joint treatment of SFT data design and RL dynamics rather than either stage in isolation.

The public SFT row is a four-field demonstration object: domain category, upstream source label, task input, and one teacher output containing reasoning text and a final response. It carries no per-row correctness label, verifier output, reward, rejection reason, or blend version. The RL object is a group of 8 or 16 current-policy rollouts for one question-answer pair. A rule score for each response is standardized within the group and used as a token-level advantage; no KL term is used. The final-answer verifier can observe parsed math correctness or compile-and-test code behavior, but it cannot establish whether the unobserved intermediate reasoning process is valid.

The curriculum moves through math-only 8K, 16K, and 24K stages, code-only 24K and 32K stages, and a final math-only 32K stage. Later stages remove tasks solved by every rollout, so task selection itself is part of the feedback loop. This extends the stage-wise RL recipe from AceReason-Nemotron 1.0 to a stronger SFT initializer and adds controlled studies of prompt/response scaling, temperature-adjusted entropy, Stage-1 warm-up, and overlong filtering. Stage-wise GRPO, distillation from a frontier reasoner, programmatic outcome reward, and difficulty filtering are prior components; the paper's concrete contribution is their integrated comparison and disclosure, not the invention of each component.
