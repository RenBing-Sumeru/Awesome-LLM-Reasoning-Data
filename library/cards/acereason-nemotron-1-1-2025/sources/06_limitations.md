The largest audit gap is the mismatch between the paper's training-accounting units and the public release. The paper describes a 383K-prompt filtered pool and a final v7 SFT blend of 2.2M samples (1.2M math and 1.0M code), while the public SFT dataset contains 3,970,332 rows derived from 514,807 source questions. No versioned manifest maps released rows, duplicates, or sampling weights to v1–v7 or to the checkpoint actually used to initialize RL.

The released SFT rows contain only `category`, `source`, `input`, and `output`. They do not expose a correctness judgment, rejection reason, verifier trace, generation seed, blend identifier, or train/validation/test partition beyond a single `train` split. The paper says responses were filtered and simpler or shorter examples were downsampled, but it does not provide sufficient row-level provenance to reconstruct those decisions. Treating long teacher responses as a proxy for difficult questions is the authors' operational heuristic, not direct evidence of difficulty.

The RL recipe is also incomplete. The paper reports 8 or 16 rollouts per prompt, a global batch of 128 prompts, token-level strict on-policy GRPO without KL regularization, and rule-based rewards. It does not disclose the allocation of 8 versus 16 rollouts across all stages, complete optimizer and seed settings, hardware, exact math-answer parsing rules, code execution sandbox, compiler/runtime versions, hidden tests, failure logs, or immutable prompt manifests. The public AceReason-Math dataset is related to math RL, but no equivalent code-RL prompt-and-test artifact is identified.

Rule-based verification introduces known label errors. The paper explicitly notes that defective code tests can create false negatives and weak tests can create false positives. Final-answer matching also checks outcome rather than the validity of the reasoning process. Group-normalized rewards, token-level loss, difficulty filtering, and truncation handling can induce selection or length effects that are not fully measured.

The seven SFT blends and staged RL comparisons are informative but not fully controlled causal experiments: mixture size, composition, response length, checkpoint history, and curriculum stage can change together. Benchmark improvements therefore do not by themselves validate data correctness or general quality. The paper reports overlap filtering against named evaluations, but it does not supply the matching code, thresholds for every source, or an independently reproducible contamination report. Upstream licenses and usage constraints still need to be checked source by source, and the reported results have not been independently reproduced within this Card.
