For the assigned Track 12, **Frontier Reports as a Data Disclosure Ledger**, this paper is useful because it exposes enough of the SFT and RL data lifecycle to support a structured audit while also making the disclosure boundary visible. The Card can record the data objects, source families, filtering rules, sampling axes, verifier contracts, curriculum transitions, and selected ablations without pretending that the complete production pipeline is public.

The report supports four practical uses. First, it is a comparison case for how SFT scale and composition interact with downstream RL. Second, its temperature, curriculum, and overlong-filtering ablations can inform hypotheses for controlled experiments. Third, the stated math-answer and code-test rewards provide a checklist for verifier audits, especially false-positive and false-negative analysis. Fourth, the discrepancy between the paper's 2.2M v7 blend and the 3,970,332-row public SFT release is a concrete example of why a disclosure ledger should distinguish the described training object from the downloadable artifact.

Reuse should be conditional on the artifact. The paper and this Card are suitable as audit and design references. The public AceReason-1.1-SFT and AceReason-Math datasets may be considered for training or evaluation only after pinning a revision, checking upstream-source rights, validating rows, and defining a local split and contamination policy. The released model can support behavioral comparison under its own license. None of these artifacts is sufficient to reproduce the full RL recipe because the code verifier, code tests, stage manifests, and exact stage-to-release mapping remain unavailable.

The strongest next audit action is to obtain immutable manifests for the final SFT initialization data and each RL stage, together with executable verifier definitions and rejected-rollout metadata. Until then, benchmark gains should be used as evidence about the reported model behavior, not as proof that the released data are correct, uncontaminated, or broadly reusable.
