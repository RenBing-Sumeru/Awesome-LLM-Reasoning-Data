The novelty is the bidirectional use of an early process-reward signal. Prior pipelines generally make a fixed commitment to either finish many independent samples or repeatedly score and expand beams. THROW instead treats partial-trace quality as a prompt-level budget signal: the very same measurement causes compute to contract for easy prompts and expand for hard prompts.

This is more specific than generic early exit because it preserves a path for additional deliberation when it is needed. Its hybrid design makes the comparison operational: it reports policy decoding, PRM overhead, tokens, and batch-1 wall-clock latency rather than only an accuracy-at-budget curve. The result frames the practical objective as selectively moving between diversity and targeted refinement, not simply making either constituent search method cheaper.
