With Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, averaged over the reported candidate counts and math benchmarks, THROW is 1.54 times faster than Best-of-N and 14.38 times faster than beam search, with 35.7% and 80.4% fewer generated tokens respectively. It improves average accuracy by 1.1 points over Best-of-N and stays within 0.9 points of beam search. At 16 paths on AIME24, its latency is 7.31 seconds and 8,881 tokens, versus 10.78 seconds and 14,916 tokens for Best-of-N and 112.17 seconds and 57,973 tokens for beam search.

The 3B policy model shows the same trade-off: 1.51 and 11.86 times latency speedups and 37.1% and 79.3% token reductions relative to the two baselines, with a 2.4-point average gain over Best-of-N. The difficulty indicator correlates with pass-at-one difficulty at absolute Pearson correlations from 0.679 to 0.801. On MATH500, informed branch selection reaches 0.722 accuracy at 16 paths, five points above random selection, supporting the claim that the savings come from allocation rather than arbitrary pruning.
