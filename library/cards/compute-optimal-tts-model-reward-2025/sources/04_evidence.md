The official paper reports broad MATH-500 and AIME24 comparisons and states that the best strategy depends strongly on policy, PRM, and difficulty. It highlights several cross-scale outcomes under its compute-optimal configurations: a 1B model exceeds a 405B model on MATH-500; a 0.5B model exceeds GPT-4o; a 3B model surpasses a 405B model; and a 7B model beats the cited o1 and DeepSeek-R1 comparisons on the reported tasks. These are empirical configuration results, not claims that parameter count has ceased to matter.

The evidence also identifies reward compatibility as a mechanism. A case study in the paper reports that one PRM favored short responses and produced incorrect answers for a Llama-3.1-8B policy, while a different PRM produced correct answers but used more computation. This makes the comparison useful for test-time allocation: accuracy, reward behavior, candidate length, search method, and compute should be examined together. Replication should use the released code and project settings while rechecking claims against the exact model versions available to the evaluator.
