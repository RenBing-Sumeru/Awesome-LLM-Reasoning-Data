Best-of-N sampling, beam search, and diverse verifier tree search can all spend more computation at inference, but their relative value is not fixed. A process reward model may guide one policy model accurately yet mis-score another model's traces because of distribution mismatch. Likewise, a strategy that works on easy arithmetic may waste budget or prune useful alternatives on hard olympiad problems. Choosing a search recipe by model size alone therefore hides the interaction between generator behavior, verifier reliability, and problem difficulty.

The paper asks what a compute-optimal test-time strategy looks like once those interactions are made explicit. It compares policy models, process reward models, difficulty levels, and inference methods under mathematical reasoning budgets. The target decision is a reward-aware choice of search method and hyperparameters for a particular policy and task, rather than an assertion that one universal sampling or tree-search method should receive every extra rollout.
