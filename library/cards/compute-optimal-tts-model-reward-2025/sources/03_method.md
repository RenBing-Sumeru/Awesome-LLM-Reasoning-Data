The study frames reasoning as trajectories generated by a policy model and assessed by a process reward model. It evaluates three external inference procedures: best-of-N generates N full responses before scoring; beam search repeatedly scores steps and retains a subset; diverse verifier tree search divides search into multiple independently explored subtrees. For each prompt and available budget, compute-optimal scaling selects strategy hyperparameters intended to maximize the probability of the correct answer.

Experiments vary policy models from 0.5B to 72B across two reported families, process reward models from 1.5B to 72B, and MATH-500/AIME24 difficulty conditions. The paper distinguishes on-policy PRMs, trained on the same policy's responses, from offline PRMs trained on other policies; this distinction is used to analyze reward distribution shift. The released repository provides an implementation surface for the compared search procedures and the project page documents the reported evaluation settings.
