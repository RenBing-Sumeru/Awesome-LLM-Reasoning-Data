The reported experiments show consistent gains over the compared test-free baselines across four language models. The paper emphasizes that the method selects among candidates without an additional LLM selection pass. On LiveCodeBench with locally served models, its execution time is about one fifth of earlier test-time methods; for API-based models it uses roughly five percent of their time, and it consumes around four percent of the input tokens used by those methods. These comparisons support an efficiency claim, not merely a pass@k opportunity claim.

The evidence also identifies why coverage matters. Random inputs can repeatedly exercise common paths and fail to expose semantic disagreement, whereas coverage-guided inputs search less common branches before candidates are clustered. The method can be combined with reasoning models and other test-time procedures because its selection stage is model agnostic. Its pass@1 result remains the appropriate deployment metric: the final program is selected without access to the benchmark's hidden tests.
