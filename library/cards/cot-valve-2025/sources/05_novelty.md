Earlier controls mainly specify a target length in the prompt, use summary tokens, repeatedly sample until different lengths appear, or prune text after generation. CoT-Valve instead locates a parameter-space direction associated with long-versus-short reasoning and uses coefficient arithmetic to generate multiple lengths from related model states. MixChain turns those controlled variants into same-question trace groups; CoT-Valve++ supervises intermediate coefficients, and CoT-Valve+P makes the data curriculum progressively shorter. The direction signal is useful because it links a data field, trace length, to a controllable model update and exposes paired alternatives for analysis. Parameter interpolation, LoRA, chain compression, and answer matching are not new by themselves, and the paper does not establish that length is a causal measure of reasoning quality or that the mechanism generalizes beyond the tested model relationships.
