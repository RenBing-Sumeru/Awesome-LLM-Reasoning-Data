Long chain-of-thought can improve difficult reasoning but raises inference cost, and prompt-only length requests do not reliably produce a requested budget. CoT-Valve, published at ACL 2025, asks whether one model can expose a controllable long-to-short reasoning continuum without training a separate model for every length. For the atlas, the relevant data object is MixChain: each question is paired with multiple answer-bearing reasoning traces at different lengths, plus token counts and, in one released subset, answer-correctness flags. The decision boundary is therefore not merely whether shorter outputs score well, but whether the trace lineage, length-control parameter, correctness check, and retained failures are sufficient to reproduce or audit the compression recipe.
