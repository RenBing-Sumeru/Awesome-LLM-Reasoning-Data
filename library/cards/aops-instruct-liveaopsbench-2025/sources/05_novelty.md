Earlier mathematical instruction collections already used competition problems, community solutions, LLM generation, or solution rewriting. Numina includes about 30K questions from the AoPS contest collection, and Omni-MATH uses 4,428 AoPS-derived evaluation questions across timestamps. LiveCodeBench had already demonstrated timestamp-based rolling evaluation in code. AoPS-Instruct and LiveAoPSBench therefore do not introduce LLM rewriting, substring decontamination, or live evaluation in isolation.

The concrete change is to make a broad community forum one shared, continuously growing substrate for both training and evaluation. The training path separates the first-post question, a selected later user's source answer, and a Qwen-authored rewrite; the evaluation path adds a timestamp cutoff, proof/non-answer exclusions, a lower n-gram threshold, two independent rewrites, final-answer agreement, and human sampling. Posting time and source role become construction fields rather than incidental metadata.

The work also distinguishes training selection from evaluation selection. Proof questions remain eligible for AoPS-Instruct but are excluded from LiveAoPSBench because the judge observes only parseable terminal answers. One incoming discussion can yield several SFT pairs when multiple replies contain solutions, whereas the benchmark deduplicates final-answer candidates. This makes data-object semantics and feedback observability explicit instead of treating all forum text as one homogeneous corpus.

Some of the contribution is scale and engineering: 1.07M crawled topics, staged open-model inference, exact-match filters, and evolving publication. The directional contribution for reasoning-data research is the coupled protocol: a source timestamp can define a training cutoff and a later evaluation window, while separate filters reflect different reuse risks. That protocol remains valid only when every snapshot and model cutoff is pinned.

Before reuse, researchers must still verify which artifact is author-maintained, whether Figure 2 or the body/code identifies the actual extraction model, whether the public files preserve enough lineage for correction and attribution, and whether a future model could have trained on the released benchmark. Those checks distinguish the methodological contribution from unsupported claims of permanent contamination resistance.
