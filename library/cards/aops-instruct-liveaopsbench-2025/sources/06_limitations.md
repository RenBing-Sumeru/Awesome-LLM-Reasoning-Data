The verifier stack is answer-level and fallible. Community responses can be incomplete or wrong; the extractor can select the wrong reply; and rewriting can add, omit, or distort a step despite a prompt that forbids adding information. Cross-model agreement preserves shared errors and compares final answers rather than proofs. The paper itself shows an incomplete cubic solution and an incorrect counting rewrite, so a correct benchmark score cannot certify the training rationales (Paper Figures 16-17).

Coverage is structurally limited. LiveAoPSBench removes proof questions and items without concrete boxed answers, while the source processing is text-only. This excludes an important part of Olympiad reasoning and underrepresents diagram-dependent geometry. The human audit covers 386 benchmark questions, not the full benchmark and not 647,255 training pairs; its 5% incorrect and 3% no-answer annotations also show residual selection/judge error.

Reproduction is incomplete at the checked versions. The author repository has no tagged release, and commit `56f6bc1e670170e301f1c9ebc172124defd21e90` lacks the two shell scripts invoked by its README. Figure 2 labels QA extraction as Qwen 32B, while the body and current `parse_aops.py` specify Llama-3.1-70B; the paper's Qwen2.5-14B topic classifier also differs from current `classify_aops.py`'s Llama-3.1-8B default. Paper-run decoding, retries, seeds, optimizer, and full environment are unknown. The public AoPS-Instruct artifact is explicitly third-party; equal row counts do not demonstrate identical crawl dates, source edits, model versions, filters, or records.

Public provenance is insufficient for source-level audit. The extraction prompt records answer user and post number, but the default model-ready releases omit canonical topic/post URLs and IDs. The benchmark exposes an index, and the third-party default training set exposes only chat messages. Curator inference: this weakens independent source verification, contributor credit, targeted correction, and takedown handling even if identifiers were omitted for privacy. A protected provenance map could address those needs, but none was verified.

Content-rights evidence is unresolved. AoPS terms say AoPS does not claim ownership of submitted content and that users grant AoPS a broad sublicensable license. The reviewed paper, repository, and dataset cards do not document a corresponding authorization from AoPS or contributors to the dataset publisher. Curator inference: Apache-2.0/MIT labels on code or uploads do not by themselves establish rights in underlying user solutions or reposted problem statements. This is an audit gap, not a conclusion that a release is unlawful.

Timestamp splitting lowers but does not eliminate contamination. Reposted or paraphrased old problems can appear after the cutoff, and 8/10-gram filters miss semantic overlap. The 2023 retrospective split falls inside the AoPS-Instruct source period and is not automatically held out for an AoPS-trained model. LiveAoPSBench-0824 and the current 5,328-row full-2024 release are different snapshots; an unpinned `LiveAoPSBench-2024` score is not reproducible, and a public snapshot can enter future training.
