The family RL environments cover mathematics and scientific reasoning, competitive coding, instruction following, software engineering, search, chat, general agentic tool use, and long-context tasks. The report says all of these task families are trained simultaneously. It describes asynchronous RL that decouples training from inference, uses the models' throughput for rollouts, and applies GRPO with masked importance sampling to account for rollout-policy differences.

For Nano, official model documentation describes a mixture of public, crawled, synthetic, private third-party, and NVIDIA-internal sources. It states that synthetic post-training records can include distilled trajectories, solutions, and translations from strong teacher models and agent systems. It describes quality controls that remove malformed examples and repeated reasoning, use automated compiler, numerical, and language checks, and apply targeted content filters. Exact prompts, per-source mixture weights, teacher routing, yields, rejected records, and assignments to Nano, Super, or Ultra remain unknown.

The public release boundary is material. Nano weights and selected final pre- and post-training data are released; the model card says a sample is ungated, while remaining code, math, and multilingual data require gating and approval. NVIDIA developer recipes are open-source-data reference implementations, and NVIDIA states that their results differ from technical-report benchmarks that used additional proprietary data. Private third-party and NVIDIA-internal data are listed in the Nano documentation but not made publicly accessible.
