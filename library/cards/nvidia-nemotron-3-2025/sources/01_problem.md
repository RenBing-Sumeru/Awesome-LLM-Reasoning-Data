A frontier model family can be described as open because it releases weights, software, recipes, and some data, while still leaving the full training system impossible to reconstruct. For a multi-environment reinforcement-learning pipeline, reproducibility depends on the allocation of sources to models, task and environment revisions, rollout groups, reward definitions, rejected samples, and data rights—not only on the presence of public checkpoints.

NVIDIA Nemotron 3 exposes more of this surface than many model reports. Its family white paper names simultaneous multi-environment RL, its public Nano release documents data classes and parts of the post-training pipeline, and NVIDIA publishes developer recipes and datasets. However, the official materials also distinguish publicly releasable data from gated material and from private third-party or NVIDIA-internal data, and they do not provide an immutable family-wide training manifest.
