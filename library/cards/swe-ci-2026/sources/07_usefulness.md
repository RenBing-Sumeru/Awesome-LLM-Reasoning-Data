SWE-CI is immediately useful for evaluation and audit. Its 100-task paper protocol, 20-epoch state sequence, executable tests, archived repository states, and 115 GB trajectory release support studies of regression, requirement synthesis, code-edit policy, verifier failure, and long-horizon agent health. The task images and hashes also make it more reproducible than a benchmark that only publishes issue text.

The evidence does not support labeling the release as a ready-made training recipe. The paper demonstrates evaluation, not SFT, RL, or reward-model optimization. The trajectories could become inputs to those methods, but only after defining a stable split, checking that failures and retries are represented, removing secrets and incidental personal data, clearing upstream and model-output rights, pinning environments, and preventing test/target leakage into evaluation. The defensible current training-use labels are evaluation and audit; any agent-training use is a new downstream protocol.
