The paper evaluates 20 models from 8 providers and reports consumption above 10 billion tokens. Most models have a zero-regression rate below 0.25, and only 2 Claude Opus models exceed 0.5. Regression frequency correlates positively with iteration count for 12 of 20 models, while regression magnitude correlates negatively for 11 of 20. These findings support the claim that sustained correctness remains difficult even when local patches improve tests.

Style evidence points in the same direction: 15 of 20 models beat human oracle code on Pylint, but every evaluated model loses on the deeper MI maintainability metric. Most models resolve fewer than 50% of tasks, and the largest gains usually occur in iterations 1–4. These are evaluation results, not proof that EvoScore fully measures maintainability. They depend on target-test coverage, deterministic execution, complete episode retention, and correct separation of semantic failure from initialization, dependency, timeout, API, or container failure.
