The core contribution is a simple length-control layer for AlpacaEval-style automatic evaluation. Instead of only reporting the raw fraction of pairwise wins against a baseline, the method fits a model of auto-annotator preference that includes the output-length difference, then predicts the counterfactual preference when that length difference is zero.

The data object is still the AlpacaEval pairwise comparison record: instruction, two outputs, generator identities, randomized order, auto-annotator preference/probability, and metric fields. The feedback contract is an LLM-as-judge preference plus a statistical adjustment, not a deterministic answer key. Closest comparisons are original AlpacaEval, AlpacaFarm/Aviary-style automatic preference simulators, MT-Bench, and Chatbot Arena. The direction label is automatic-evaluator debiasing: keep the cheap scalable judge, but audit and reduce a known confounder before treating the score as model quality.
