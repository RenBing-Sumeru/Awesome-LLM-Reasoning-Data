The prior-work baseline is not absent: AlpacaEval, AlpacaFarm, Aviary, MT-Bench, and Chatbot Arena had already made pairwise or judge-based evaluation central to instruction-following model comparison. What changes here is the reporting contract. The score is no longer only "how often did the judge prefer this model"; it is also "how often would the judge prefer it after controlling for output length."

The direction signal is important because many benchmark fixes require new human data or hidden tasks, while this one treats evaluator bias as a measurable confounder in an existing public harness. The quality signal is the improved leaderboard correlation and explicit reduction of length gameability. What is not new is the idea of LLM-as-judge, pairwise preference, or AlpacaEval itself. Before reuse, inspect whether the chosen judge, baseline, prompt, cache, and dataset version match the reported setting.
