In one sentence, ThinkLite-VL spends offline search compute to estimate target-policy difficulty, keeps prompts first solved only after more than five MCTS iterations together with prompts still unsolved after 50 iterations, and then performs one GRPO stage on that model-specific subset.

For a prompt `x`, image `I`, and current reasoning prefix `s_t`, the matching Qwen2.5-VL policy samples three candidate next steps at temperature 0.5. MCTS selection is driven by visit counts; simulation continues until a final answer or a ten-step cap. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct then receives the question, ground-truth answer, and generated rationale/answer and returns a single true/false sentence. A true decision stops search and records `K`; otherwise search continues to the 50-iteration budget. The 7B pass produces 5.4K late-solved plus 5.6K unsolved prompts, while the 72B pass produces 7.5K prompts. (Paper §3.2, Appendix Tables 6–7)

The feedback contract has two boundaries. For construction, the text-only critic can compare generated text with a supplied reference answer and can reject an obviously inconsistent rationale, but it cannot inspect the image, prove intermediate steps, or distinguish genuine difficulty from an incorrect label, ambiguity, parser sensitivity, or its own judgment error. For GRPO, the paper states the objective and 32 rollouts but does not disclose the operational reward, answer extraction, normalization, or judge fallback. The end-to-end contract is therefore mixed and terminal, not a verified process-label contract.

The data object after selection remains the original multimodal task and answer. MCTS continuations, critic outputs, `K`, and failure status are construction evidence that should accompany the selected row, yet the public Hard-11K package is not documented as a full decision ledger. This distinction matters: downstream model gains test whether the selected prompt set was useful under the authors' training setup; they do not validate every search trace or ground-truth answer.

The closest comparisons in the paper are MM-Eureka's zero-shot accuracy filtering, offline self-consistency with 50 rollouts and an accuracy-below-0.2 rule, online self-consistency retaining current-policy accuracy between 0 and 0.9, random equal-size subsets, and the unfiltered 70K pool. ThinkLite-VL changes the selection feature from one-shot or flat rollout success rate to explicit tree-search effort and repeats the measurement separately for each target scale. The model-specificity result is essential: the 7B and 72B selected sets overlap by only 5.4K, and cross-training on the other scale's set underperforms training on the matching set.
