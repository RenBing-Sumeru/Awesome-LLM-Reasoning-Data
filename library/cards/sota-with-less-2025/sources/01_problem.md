ThinkLite-VL asks a data-allocation question rather than a model-architecture question: given a heterogeneous pool of visual-reasoning prompts, which small subset should receive expensive reinforcement fine-tuning so that the target policy learns from neither trivial nor uniformly impossible tasks? The official NeurIPS proceedings list the work in Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 38, Main Conference Track; the official virtual-poster and OpenReview records identify it as a 2025 Spotlight. The arXiv record was first submitted on 10 April 2025. (NeurIPS proceedings; arXiv:2504.07934)

The candidate pool contains 69,997 rows from eight sources: Geometry3K (3,001), GeoQA (5,010), Geos (66), FigureQA (10,000 randomly sampled), ScienceQA (10,332), OK-VQA (9,009), IconQA (10,000 randomly sampled), and TabMWP (22,579). The paper describes a source row as image, ID, prompt, and answer. The live 70K Hub viewer exposes `image`, `problem`, `answer`, `id`, `choices`, and `ground_truth` in a single train split, but it does not expose upstream source/split IDs. Five sources are converted to open-ended answering; ScienceQA, GeoQA, and Geos remain multiple choice. (Paper §3.1, Figure 3; official 70K dataset viewer)

The construction object is richer than the released training row. For every target policy and source item, an auditable selector record would join the source identity and normalized prompt/answer to MCTS reasoning steps, the text critic's true/false decisions, the first accepted iteration `K` or unsolved-after-50 status, the threshold rule, and the final selected/rejected decision. The paper uses this process to form an 11K subset for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and a 7.5K subset for Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct. The selected prompts, not the MCTS traces, enter GRPO; this is answer-level RLVR and prompt selection, not released step-level supervision or teacher-trace distillation. (Paper §§3.2–3.3)

This belongs in `data_construction_open_release_recipes` because its main intervention is a reproducible-in-principle recipe for source mixing, search-based difficulty measurement, model-specific filtering, and downstream RFT. It does not establish a general-purpose difficulty score independent of policy, critic, prompt, or budget, and it does not show that MCTS traces themselves are suitable training demonstrations.

The evidence boundary is unusually informative but incomplete. The paper and appendix provide the selector logic, prompts, data counts, ablations, and model comparisons; the official repository exposes the MCTS script and an eight-shard launcher; the official Hub collection exposes the 70K pool, 7B hard subset, and model checkpoints. However, the 72B-selected data, complete item-level decision ledger, failed searches, upstream row manifest, exact GRPO reward, decontamination report, and source-license matrix were not found. The Card is therefore suitable for mechanism screening and audit design, not an assertion that the released data can exactly reproduce the reported run.
