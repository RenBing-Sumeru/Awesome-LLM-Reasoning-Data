The strongest evidence is within-policy comparison under matched or explicitly varied data budgets, not the state-of-the-art label.

- In Table 2, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct averages 59.69 across eight benchmarks, the random-11K GRPO baseline 60.89, and ThinkLite-VL-7B 64.18. At 72B, the corresponding values are 68.25 for the base, 69.91 for random 7.5K, and 72.67 for the MCTS-selected 7.5K. The reported matched-size margins over random selection are therefore 3.29 and 3.06 average points. MathVista testmini is 75.1 for ThinkLite-VL-7B and 79.7 for ThinkLite-VL-72B, but those benchmark scores are model outcomes, not record-level data validation. (Paper Table 2)
- Table 3 isolates the 7B mixture: selected 11K averages 64.18, versus 62.04 for unsolved-only 5.6K, 62.38 for late-solved-only 5.4K, 60.89 for random 11K, 63.15 for self-consistency-selected 23K, and 63.13 for the full 70K. Under this one recipe, combining late-solved and unsolved prompts outperforms either component and the larger alternatives. It does not establish that every retained unsolved item is valid. (Paper Table 3)
- Table 4 compares offline and online selection. At 7B, offline MCTS, offline self-consistency, and online self-consistency average 64.18, 63.15, and 62.34; at 72B they average 72.67, 71.01, and 70.12. The authors also note that online self-consistency converges faster despite negligible final improvement. (Paper §4.3, Table 4)
- Table 5 tests model specificity. The 7B policy averages 64.18 on its own 11K set but 61.24 on the 72B-selected 7.5K set; the 72B policy averages 72.67 on its own set but 70.24 on the 7B-selected set. The selected sets overlap by 5.4K, with 3.6K unsolved by both. This is direct evidence against treating Hard-11K as model-independent difficulty. (Paper §4.4, Table 5)
- Appendix Table 9 varies the 7B threshold: Fullset 63.13, Iter1+Unsolved 63.29, Iter5+Unsolved 63.89, Iter10+Unsolved 62.65, Iter20+Unsolved 62.61, Iter30+Unsolved 62.39, Iter40+Unsolved 62.26, and Unsolved 62.04. The table supports inclusion of a medium-difficulty band, but its 63.89 value conflicts with 64.18 for the nominal selected 11K in Tables 2–4. Nearby prose also uses 63.89. The Card preserves the inconsistency rather than choosing one number. (Appendix B.2, Table 9; Paper Tables 2–4)
- Appendix Figure 5 reports that random-11K and full-pool models can obtain higher training reward while finishing with worse benchmarks than the selected 11K model. This is a useful negative result: training reward is not a sufficient proxy for downstream reasoning performance. The figure does not disclose raw curves or run-level logs. (Appendix B.1, Figure 5)

Artifact evidence is partial. The official collection contains the 70K pool, 7B Hard-11K dataset, 7B and 72B checkpoints, and the paper. The 70K viewer reports 69,997 rows and only a train split. The Hard-11K page provides a single Parquet and a minimal load example; its viewer is unavailable. The GitHub repository contains the selector and evaluation folders but no formal release or top-level license. No 72B-selected 7.5K dataset, all-item `K` table, rejected set, failed-trace ledger, or paper-matching training launcher was found.

All numerical comparisons above are author-reported point estimates and were not independently reproduced for this Card. The main and ablation tables show no repeated-run dispersion. Although the NeurIPS checklist marks statistical significance as satisfied with a pointer to Section 4, the checked results do not expose seeds, confidence intervals, or error bars for the core comparisons. That weakens claims about small benchmark differences even though the larger matched-size gaps remain descriptively clear.
