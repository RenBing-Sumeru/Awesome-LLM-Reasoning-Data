The private verifier evaluation set contains 776 balanced positive/negative examples: 500 from SWE-bench Verified and 276 from Multi-SWE-bench Flash, with SWE-agent and OpenHands trajectories mixed 1:1 and labels obtained by held-out tests in repository-specific Docker environments. Dockerless reports AUC 81.0 on Verified and 72.1 on Multi-SWE, gains of 14.3 and 9.2 over the strongest trained open verifier in the comparison. These are ranking results; they do not establish score calibration, a safe decision threshold, or false-positive/false-negative rates.

The SFT-selection ablation reports Verified/Multilingual/Pro scores of 58.8/41.3/31.9 using all 16K rollouts, 58.2/44.3/32.0 using a random 4K, 60.0/48.3/33.9 using the environment-selected 4K, and 60.6/47.7/35.3 using the Dockerless-selected 4K. The main environment-based evaluation reports 62.0/50.0/35.2 for Dockerless-RL-9B, compared with 59.6/41.3/32.3 for Qwen3.5-9B and 62.4/51.3/35.7 for test-execution RL. Environment-free evaluation in the same minimal-image distribution reports 53.8/42.3/30.6 for Dockerless-RL-9B. These results support the claimed usefulness of the learned score within the studied pipeline, but benchmark performance is not proof that individual selected trajectories are correct, reusable, licensed, or contamination-free.

The negative results help define the boundary. Verifier AUC rises from 78.3 with `K=0` questions to 81.0 with `K=4`, then falls or fluctuates to 79.6 at `K=6` and 80.3 at `K=8`, showing that more evidence can add noise. Environment-based SFT leads environment-free SFT by 7.0 points on Rust and 13.3 points on C; the authors associate this with unavailable compiler diagnostics while cautioning that two languages are not conclusive. Across 7,680 RL rollouts, shared rollout work averages 2,308 seconds and Dockerless reward adds 41–180 seconds, or 7.2% of total runtime, with a heavy tail to an undisclosed hard timeout. All figures are author-reported and were not independently reproduced.
