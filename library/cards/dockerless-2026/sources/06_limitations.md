The golden/reference patch is a hard dependency in both question generation and final scoring. The paper does not explain how to score a genuinely new private, enterprise, or legacy issue with no known correct patch, and showing the reference to the judge creates a similarity/leakage channel that may penalize alternative correct solutions. Candidate-patch and Q&A-context truncation at 10,000 characters can also remove decisive evidence from large repositories.

The learned score is not a calibrated terminal predicate. AUC measures ranking, while operating thresholds, calibration, false-positive and false-negative rates, reward clipping, adversarial verifier gaming, and the handling of two failed scoring passes are unknown. The Rust and C deficits show that missing compilers, dependencies, and runtime diagnostics can matter. More questions can reduce AUC, so repository evidence is neither complete nor monotonically helpful.

Operational reproducibility is blocked by missing environment controls: image digest, installed packages and indexes, repository/submodule commit manifest, dependency installation policy, network/egress, cache and filesystem reset behavior, privilege/resource limits, numeric hard timeout, seeds, command logs, and replay scripts are unknown. Although evidence sub-agents are prompt-restricted to read-only commands, OpenHands policy rollouts may execute arbitrary shell commands. No sandbox threat model, secret policy, supply-chain protection, or malicious-repository defense is reported; reproduction should use disposable, network-restricted infrastructure.

Retention is selective and unauditable. Verifier construction keeps only teacher trajectories whose judgment matches execution, removes malformed, interrupted, and out-of-range traces, and caps negatives at 4:1. SFT retains only the highest-ranked 4K of 16K, and SFT/RL discard failed verifier passes. No accepted, rejected, interrupted, timeout, compiler/dependency-failure, or all-failed trajectories and no drop manifest are released.

Source-name disjointness between verifier training and evaluation does not establish instance-, code-, or semantic-level decontamination. SWE-Rebench-v2 SFT/RL overlap, downstream evaluation overlap, task IDs, candidate-model mixture in the 776-example benchmark, and model-pretraining contamination checks are undisclosed. The arXiv distribution license covers the paper, not reuse of repositories, issue text, patches, GLM-5 traces, or agent rollouts; their licenses, provenance rights, privacy/consent handling, and security review are unknown. No paper-specific code, data, model, benchmark manifest, or project release was verified.
