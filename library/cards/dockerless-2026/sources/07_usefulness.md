For the Environment & Agent Trajectories track, Dockerless is useful as a schema for separating an episode from the feedback that later labels, selects, or rewards it. A reusable record would need the issue and immutable repository revision; base-image digest; complete state/action/observation and shell log; final patch; golden-patch availability; each generated question; every read-only evidence action and file/range citation; binary execution label; predicted verdict and both raw scoring passes; selection decision; SFT/RL membership; termination reason; and links to the resulting checkpoint.

The paper directly supports five uses in its own pipeline: SFT on the selected 4K full episodes, reward modeling from execution-labeled Q+A+Judge sequences, RLVR with the dense Dockerless score, agent training through OpenHands rollouts, and evaluation of the verifier and resulting policies. It does not provide evidence for broader training uses, nor does it release a corpus that can currently be reused for those purposes.

The main audit lesson is to preserve three feedback columns instead of collapsing them into “success”: executable held-out-test label, learned verifier judgment/score, and GRPO-normalized reward. Researchers can use the paper to design comparisons between repository-specific execution and learned surrogate rewards, to study selection bias from the 16K-to-4K funnel, and to build failure-retention or replay requirements. Until artifacts and rights are released, its practical value is as a method and audit reference rather than an open trajectory source.
