The paper's pioneer experiment uses HumanEval Plus with up to 200 candidate solutions and 100 generated unit tests per problem, plus bootstrap resampling, and reports that adding tests generally improves best-of-N selection across several policy and test-generator models. The effect is larger on harder problem quintiles, motivating dynamic allocation. In the main three-benchmark comparison, CodeRM-8B raises HumanEval Plus Pass@1 over random selection by 18.43 points for Llama3-8B and 3.42 points for GPT-4o-mini; results also improve on MBPP Plus and LiveCodeBench for the evaluated policy models. Under fixed compute, dynamic allocation provides only a modest additional gain, reported as up to about 0.5 points on MBPP Plus.

The paper also evaluates test-verifier behavior with accuracy, F1, false-acceptance rate, and false-rejection rate, and ablates false-positive filtering and training-set size. These results support the claim that test count, filtering, and allocation affect this verifier's downstream selection behavior. They are not record-level proof that every synthetic unit test is correct, diverse, uncontaminated, or useful outside the reported Python function-generation setting. Benchmark gains likewise do not establish the quality or completeness of an eventual public dataset.
