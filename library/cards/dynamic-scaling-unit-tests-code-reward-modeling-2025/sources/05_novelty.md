Generated-test reranking predates this work: CodeT, MBR-Exec, and related methods already execute generated tests to rank sampled programs, while test-time scaling work often increases the number of policy outputs. This paper combines three changes: an execution-filtered synthetic dataset for a dedicated 8B test generator, empirical scaling of the number of verifier samples, and difficulty-aware allocation of verifier compute. Its direction signal for the rollout/search/test-time-compute track is the separation of policy-sampling budget from verifier-sampling budget and the explicit attempt to allocate the latter by estimated difficulty.

The execution contract itself, supervised fine-tuning, majority voting, probing, and greedy allocation are not individually new. The paper does not establish a general scaling law, and its dynamic allocator adapts a policy-compute objective that the authors acknowledge may be mismatched to verifier compute. The contribution should therefore be read as a code-specific construction and scaling study, not as evidence that more tests universally produce higher-quality rewards.
