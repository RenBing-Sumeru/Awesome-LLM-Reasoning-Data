LEAP asks whether a general-purpose foundation model can solve long Lean theorem-proving tasks through structured interaction with the proof environment, rather than relying on one-shot generation or a specialized prover model. The authoritative paper is arXiv:2606.03303v2, submitted on 2 June 2026 and revised on 3 June 2026; no peer-reviewed venue was verified. The official project is https://imobench.github.io/, and the official release is under Google DeepMind's `superhuman` repository.

The paper also introduces Lean-IMO-Bench: 60 IMO-style problems from IMO-ProofBench, manually converted into Lean statements and divided into 30 Basic and 30 Advanced instances. A released benchmark row contains `Problem ID`, natural-language `Problem`, reference `Solution`, `Grading guidelines`, `Category`, `Level`, `Short Answer`, `Source`, and `Lean Statement`. For LEAP evaluation, that task record is paired with a generated Lean proof, the search budget, and a solved/unsolved checker outcome.

This belongs to Programmatically Verifiable Outcome Data because the decisive label is Lean acceptance of a complete proof artifact. It is not process supervision: an LLM reviewer filters proposed decompositions, but no step-level correctness labels or replayable trajectories are released. It is also not evidence for model training, because the paper performs inference-time search and evaluation rather than SFT, RLVR, or agent training. The Card reaches L4 on the strength of the full paper, appendix, benchmark CSV, result tables, license, and released successful proofs; exact framework code, model/toolchain versions, and failed traces remain unavailable.
