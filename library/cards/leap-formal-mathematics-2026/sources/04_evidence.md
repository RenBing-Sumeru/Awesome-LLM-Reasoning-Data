The paper's results are conditioned on Gemini 3.1 Pro as the LEAP backend and on unequal forms of test-time search. They have not been independently reproduced in this Card.

- On Putnam 2025, Table 2 reports 12/12 solved by LEAP with two rollouts. Gemini 3.1 Pro and Goedel-Prover-V2-32B each solve 0/12 under pass@128; Hilbert solves 4/12 with two rollouts, and Aristotle solves 9/12 with two rollouts. Table 3 shows that LEAP's successful proofs are not equal-budget events: 46 to about 3,000 LLM calls, 8 to 211 active DAG nodes, and 300 to about 2,000 proof lines per problem.
- On Lean-IMO-Bench, Table 4 reports 25/30 Basic (83.3%) and 17/30 Advanced (56.7%) for LEAP, giving 42/60 or 70% overall. Aristotle records 23/30 and 6/30; Hilbert records 11/30 and 2/30. LEAP still solves only 1/6 Basic geometry and 1/8 Advanced geometry problems.
- The iterative-revision ablation in Table 5 raises Gemini 3.1 Pro on the Basic set from 20.0% one-shot pass@128 to 36.6% with one initial sample plus up to 20 revisions, while Goedel-Prover-V2-32B decreases from 10.0% to 6.6%. This negative result limits the claim: compiler feedback is not uniformly useful across backends.
- Removing global DAG memory reduces Basic/Advanced solve rates from 83.3/56.7 to 73.3/40.0 (Table 6). Removing the LLM decomposition reviewer makes Putnam A5 fail after eight rollouts, whereas the full setup solves it with two; Figure 3 shows a formally admissible decomposition that merely recreates its ancestor goal.

The official repository corroborates artifact availability by publishing the successful Lean proofs for 12 Putnam tasks, 42 solved Lean-IMO-Bench tasks, and two open-problem cases. It does not corroborate replayability: agent code, prompts, DAG logs, compiler-message histories, and unsuccessful branches are absent. Benchmark performance therefore supports the paper's evaluation claim, not a claim that the released data or verifier pipeline is complete or training-ready.
