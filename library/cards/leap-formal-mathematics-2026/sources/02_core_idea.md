LEAP's contribution is an inference-time prover that lets Gemini 3.1 Pro alternate between informal mathematical planning and mechanically checked Lean artifacts while storing proof progress in a reusable AND-OR DAG. It first tries a direct informal-to-formal proof and compiler-feedback revisions. If that path fails, it proposes an informal blueprint, converts the plan into a Lean proof sketch, and turns the proposed lemmas into child goals only after the sketch type-checks and the state update remains acyclic. Shared lemma nodes provide memoization across branches; simple depth-first search and backtracking continue until the root is proved or the search stops. (Paper §§2.2-2.5, Figures 1-2.)

The feedback contract has two non-interchangeable parts. Lean observes syntax, types, dependencies, and whether a complete proof checks in the current environment. For a proof sketch, it verifies that the parent follows once the explicitly proposed child lemmas are assumed; `sorry` is confined to those child statements. Lean cannot determine whether the encoded theorem faithfully matches the intended informal problem or whether a decomposition is strategically useful. The LLM reviewer addresses the latter with a heuristic relevance/easiness/plausibility judgment, but it cannot certify mathematical correctness and no calibration is reported.

The closest empirical comparisons are one-shot Gemini 3.1 Pro, the specialized Goedel-Prover-V2-32B, Hilbert's recursive agentic search combining a general model with a specialized prover, and the proprietary Aristotle system. LEAP's key distinction is not a new proof checker or a new training corpus: it uses a general-purpose backend, checked blueprint decomposition, global DAG memory, and a planning reviewer at test time. Lean Blueprint, informal-to-formal planning, retrieval, compiler revision, and proof search all predate LEAP; the paper's direction signal is their integration with an exact terminal verifier and explicit compute accounting.
