Prior Lean systems offer several pieces used here: Lean provides kernel-backed checking; LeanSearch retrieves Mathlib material; Lean Blueprint represents human proof plans as dependency graphs; Draft, Sketch, and Prove connects informal arguments to formal proving; specialized systems such as Goedel-Prover-V2 and AlphaProof train formal provers; Hilbert and Aristotle add agentic search. LEAP does not make these components new, and its benchmark expansion is separate from the proof-search mechanism.

What changes is the allocation of responsibilities. A general-purpose model supplies informal reasoning, proof generation, decomposition, revision, and a heuristic decomposition review. Lean supplies the executable acceptance predicate. An AND-OR DAG supplies global proof memory, shared lemmas, anticipatory lemma planning, and acyclic dependency tracking, rather than treating every decomposition as an independent recursive subtree. The Lean-IMO-Bench release supplies a 60-instance evaluation surface whose statements were manually formalized from IMO-ProofBench. The DAG and reviewer ablations make these choices distinguishable from adding more samples alone.

For reasoning-data research, this is useful because it separates three records that are often collapsed: a checker-accepted artifact, a judge-approved search decision, and the compute used to reach it. The release only preserves the first consistently. Before reuse, verify statement fidelity, pin the proof environment and backend, normalize compute across methods, inspect benchmark exposure, and obtain the missing failed branches and reviewer outputs. Without those checks, the work is a strong evaluation/reference design but not a complete trajectory-data recipe.
