**Method and feedback.** Lean checking is exact relative to an encoded statement and active dependency environment, but it cannot establish that a manual formalization preserves the intended natural-language problem. A checked sketch can also introduce mathematically valid but strategically useless subgoals. LEAP delegates that distinction to an LLM reviewer; the Putnam A5 ablation demonstrates one benefit, but the paper does not report reviewer calibration, false-positive/false-negative rates, alternative reviewers, or robustness to prompt changes. Search remains costly and irregular: successful Putnam proofs require 46 to about 3,000 LLM calls, and future work explicitly identifies branch prioritization and compute allocation as open problems.

**Data and release.** The official repository provides the benchmark CSV and successful proof files, but not the LEAP implementation, prompts, model configuration, DAG state schema, compiler-feedback history, reviewer outputs, or failed/rejected candidates. This successful-only release blocks analyses of branch waste, verifier-triggered repair, reviewer bias, and failure retention. It also has no Lean toolchain, Mathlib lock, or LeanSearch-index manifest. Although the repository states Apache-2.0 for software and CC-BY for other materials, downstream users should retain each problem's source metadata and separately review original competition-material rights.

**Evaluation and generalization.** The results depend on Gemini 3.1 Pro, two-rollout LEAP runs, and method-specific budgets; a solve-rate comparison is not a compute-matched comparison. Geometry remains weak at 1/6 Basic and 1/8 Advanced. Pretraining overlap and decontamination are not reported, and two comparison systems are proprietary or incompletely reproducible. The case study formalizes a Color-2 planar routing subproblem of the Hamiltonian decomposition construction, not the complete open problem. Curator inference: model-service drift, Mathlib changes, or an updated retrieval index could change both acceptance and search behavior even when the paper's headline algorithm is unchanged.
