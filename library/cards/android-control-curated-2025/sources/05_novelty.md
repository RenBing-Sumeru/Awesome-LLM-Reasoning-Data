The prior-work baseline is AndroidControl: human mobile-UI demonstrations with point targets and a benchmark that can judge one annotated action as the sole valid choice. AndroidControl-Curated does not introduce the upstream demonstrations, the Android tasks, the named expert agents, GRPO, or GUI-agent grounding as such.

The first concrete change is the feedback interface: exact-point matching becomes UI-element box containment, so the evaluator is closer to interaction intent than pixel identity (Paper §2.1.1, p. 3). The second is a correction data object: consensus failures trigger LLM-proposed deficiency labels, revised tasks or ground truth, rationales, and human verification; released rows may preserve revised histories, alternative valid actions, review results, and original-versus-corrected targets (Paper §2.1.2, pp. 3–4; official `android_control_high_task-improved.json`).

The third change is a training use tied to the purified surface. Magma-R1 post-training uses 2,400 selected curated steps with a dense Gaussian grounding reward and action-balanced GRPO batches rather than relying only on sparse terminal success (Paper §2.2, pp. 4–5). This is a direction signal because benchmark purification, reward geometry, and sampling policy are treated as coupled data-engineering choices. The Table 2 deltas are evidence that those choices affect reported scores, not a record-level quality certificate.

The work is primarily an integration of evaluation redesign, label repair, a public static release, and a small post-training recipe. Before reuse, compare paper equations with the actual scorer, recover the 2.4K selection and split manifest, pin the 3B checkpoint used for Tables 1–2, inspect all rejected and corrected cases, and resolve code/data/screenshot licensing and live replay. Without those checks, the release is most defensible as an evaluation and audit reference rather than unrestricted training data.
