Many test-time methods improve reasoning by producing more full trajectories, invoking auxiliary scorers, or applying a decoding intervention at every position. These strategies spend computation uniformly even though a long answer is not uniformly uncertain. A few unstable token choices can derail a chain, while modifying confident tokens can waste compute or disturb an otherwise sound solution.

The paper asks whether inference-time effort can be concentrated only on the small locations where the model is genuinely uncertain. It needs a signal available during one decoding pass and an intervention that does not require a separately maintained unconditional model state. The objective is to improve final reasoning reliability without turning every generated token into an expensive search or guidance operation.
