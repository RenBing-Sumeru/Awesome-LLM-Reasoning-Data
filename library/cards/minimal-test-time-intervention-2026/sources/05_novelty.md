This work changes the granularity of test-time allocation. Rather than deciding how many full samples, thoughts, or branches a prompt deserves, it decides whether a particular next-token distribution merits extra steering. The key empirical claim is that reasoning uncertainty is localized enough for this token-level policy to correct important decisions while leaving most of a trajectory untouched.

It also adapts classifier-free guidance to language-model serving in a way aimed at memory rather than only output control. Reusing the main KV cache and constructing a short negative branch turns an otherwise always-on two-branch procedure into a sparse, local operation. The novelty is therefore the joint design of uncertainty detection, selective intervention, and cache-aware approximation.
