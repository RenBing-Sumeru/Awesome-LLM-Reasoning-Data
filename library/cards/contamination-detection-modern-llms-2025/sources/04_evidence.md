The strongest evidence is the before/after oracle comparison, because its newly added benchmark fractions are known. None of the five detector metrics moves consistently with those fractions. Even the 100%-added MMLU and BBH test exposures are not reliably detected, demonstrating a false-negative pathway for instruction-tuning exposure with answer augmentation. Conversely, a 0%-added test split can move after tuning on the same benchmark's train split, demonstrating a distribution-mediated false-positive pathway for test-set contamination.

Min-K% is especially diagnostic of the reliability problem: its values decrease for all tested splits after known added contamination, opposite the expected direction. This does not establish that the underlying benchmark data are clean or that Min-K% always fails; it shows that the tested statistic and interpretation do not robustly transfer to this controlled SFT setting.

Agreement across the five methods is also weak. Off-diagonal Spearman correlations range from `-0.198` to `0.320`. The paper acknowledges taking “slight statistical liberty” when aligning p-values and other metric directions for the comparison. Thus, the correlations are evidence that these implementations do not corroborate one another under the chosen aggregation—not a calibrated estimate of any detector's sensitivity or specificity.

The four-model/eight-benchmark tables provide descriptive outputs for modern production and open-weight models, but those models' true historical training exposure is unknown. A high WPQ or Local Order accuracy, low Token Overlap p-value, Min-K summary, or Canonical Order p-value cannot independently prove contamination. The paper's claims about likely contamination on particular real-model splits remain method-dependent hypotheses, not ground-truth labels.

No result in the paper demonstrates data quality for model training. Benchmark performance may rise because of memorization, generalization, or other training changes; detector performance may change because of prompt artifacts, likelihood calibration, distribution shift, ordering, or judge variance. The evidence supports a negative audit conclusion—current probes are inconsistent and fragile—not a positive claim that the released 1,650 probe rows constitute verified high-quality training data.
