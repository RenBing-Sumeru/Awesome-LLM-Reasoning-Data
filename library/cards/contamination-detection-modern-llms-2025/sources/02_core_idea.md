The paper's central move is to treat “contamination detection” as five distinct measurement contracts rather than one interchangeable score. WPQ asks whether the model can identify an original item among four LLaMA-3-generated perturbations plus a `None` choice. Local Order asks which of four candidates immediately followed a target in the dataset. Both yield quiz accuracy, but accuracy above chance may reflect fluency or semantic adjacency rather than memorized training exposure.

Token Completion Overlap provides a partial instance under a general prompt and a dataset-guided prompt. The generated suffix is compared with the reference using ROUGE-L and GPT-4 exact/near-match judgments. The released implementation bootstraps guided and general score lists to obtain a one-sided empirical p-value. This contract mixes model generation, a learned judge, lexical overlap, and a statistical comparison; it does not directly emit an instance-level contamination label.

Min-K% uses probability access. For each text it averages negative log likelihood over the lowest-probability 20% of tokens and reports the mean and standard deviation over 100 examples. A larger value is expected to indicate greater familiarity in the method's interpretation, but no threshold is given for an arbitrary dataset. Canonical Order instead compares likelihoods of ten canonical shards with 25 shuffled variants per shard and maps ten paired differences to a one-sided t-based p-value.

The controlled oracle supplies the key test of these interfaces. LLaMA-2-70B-Chat is measured before and after instruction tuning on known fractions: MMLU test and BBH test at 100%; ARC-Challenge train and DROP train at 50% with their test splits at 0%; HumanEval test at 25%; and GSM8K train at 25% with its test split at 0%. The design asks whether a detector's output moves monotonically with known added exposure.

No common verifier, reward, or judge unifies the five methods. Programmatic quiz correctness, token likelihood, a GPT-4 match judge, lexical overlap, and order statistics observe different proxies. The paper's most important result is therefore a contract failure: none of these proxies consistently tracks the oracle fractions, and weak cross-method agreement prevents their outputs from being treated as mutually confirming contamination decisions.
