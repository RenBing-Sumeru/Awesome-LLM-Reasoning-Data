The paper treats prompting as a way to study and elicit reasoning in language models rather than as a single interchangeable trick. Its contribution is a comparative synthesis of research works, paired with systematic resources for beginners and a discussion of why reasoning abilities may emerge. The central reading lens is to connect a prompting claim with the task, the model response, and the comparison that support it. That lens is useful because a prompt can change how a question is presented without making every task equally informative about reasoning. Readers can use the survey to distinguish an observed result in one setup from a broader statement about a model's abilities.
