The paper's evidence is its organized coverage of existing research rather than a newly collected benchmark score or a single controlled experiment. The official abstract promises comparisons and summaries, so a useful reading habit is to return to a cited primary paper before treating two reported results as directly comparable. Ask whether the papers use the same task formulation, prompt information, model setting, and success measure. Task construction and prompt format can change what a score means, especially when a task accepts only a final answer while another also studies an intermediate explanation. The survey is strongest as evidence for how the literature is organized and where a claim came from; it does not erase differences among the primary evaluations.
