Its method is literature synthesis rather than a training pipeline or an experiment that trains one new system. The authors introduce research works through comparisons and summaries, then provide a resource collection and discuss future directions. For each cited study, keep four objects visible: the prompt supplied to the model, the reasoning task, the resulting response, and the evaluation used to judge that response. These objects form a compact reading record for a method: changing any one of them can change what a reported result establishes. The survey therefore supports comparison across papers, while the original studies remain necessary for exact examples, model settings, and measurements.
