Read this paper when you need a defensible starting point for prompting-based reasoning research or when a paper list contains unfamiliar method names. It is especially useful for turning a vague question—such as whether a prompt improves reasoning—into concrete checks about task type, information supplied by the prompt, comparison system, and stated future direction. The linked resource collection is a practical entry point for further reading. For a project, it can help formulate a small comparison table before choosing a primary paper: what is being asked, what the prompt adds, what response is evaluated, and what conclusion the result can reasonably support.
