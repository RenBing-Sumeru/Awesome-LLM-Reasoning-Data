Min-Seek starts from the observation that long and unsuccessful reasoning often contains many recovery attempts, whereas sound reasoning is frequently more direct. Instead of trying to identify every semantically important earlier thought, it uses the complementary objective of removing history likely to destabilize the next revision. Its simple retention rule keeps the shortest reconstruction cycle produced so far, together with the prompt and first thought, as the compact context for subsequent thinking.

The second idea is a custom KV cache that stores keys without position embeddings and applies contiguous positions again before generating the next reconstruction cycle. This permits the retained thoughts to remain compact rather than accumulating their original positions. The combination makes additional sequential computation a controlled loop with bounded remembered state, rather than an ever-longer transcript that the model must attend to in full.
