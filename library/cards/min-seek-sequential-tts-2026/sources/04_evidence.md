The official paper reports experiments over five reasoning tasks and compares Min-Seek with standard generation and Budget Forcing across a broad range of induced-thought counts. Its reported finding is that Min-Seek provides higher and more stable accuracy across those lengths, rather than requiring a narrowly tuned stopping point. The paper also argues that bounding the cached cycles permits linear computational complexity under mild conditions and makes longer sequential inference practically faster than retaining all prior thoughts.

This evidence is valuable because it measures the failure mode of sequential scaling itself: whether an added revision still helps after the apparent sweet spot. It does not mean that short reasoning is universally correct or that cache pruning preserves all information. A reviewer should inspect the task-wise curves, the number of retained cycles, memory measurements, and model-specific implementation details before applying the result to architectures with different positional encodings or reasoning formats.
