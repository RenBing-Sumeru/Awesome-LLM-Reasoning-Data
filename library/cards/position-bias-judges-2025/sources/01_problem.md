LLM judges make evaluation scalable, but may select an answer because it is shown first or last rather than because it is better. Existing reports mostly test pairwise order swaps and do not separate systematic bias from sampling noise, leaving benchmark users unable to diagnose a judge.

This paper performs a systematic audit of position bias in pairwise and list-wise judging. It turns repeated prompts and candidate-order permutations into measurable reliability signals, then studies which judges, candidates, and tasks are associated with the failure.
