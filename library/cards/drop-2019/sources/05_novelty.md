The prior baseline is extractive reading comprehension where answers can often be copied from a single span. DROP changes the task surface by asking for answers that may require combining multiple paragraph facts through arithmetic, counting, sorting, or comparison while still preserving automatic answer-level scoring.

The direction signal is evaluation of symbolic or discrete operations inside natural-language paragraph QA. The quality signal is adversarial crowd construction, expert-human comparison, and a generalized metric covering spans, numbers, and dates. What is not new: QA passages, crowdsourcing, and EM/F1 scoring already existed. Reuse requires inspecting exact release size, answer schema, scorer implementation, data license, split leakage, and whether a model saw public examples during pretraining or instruction tuning.
