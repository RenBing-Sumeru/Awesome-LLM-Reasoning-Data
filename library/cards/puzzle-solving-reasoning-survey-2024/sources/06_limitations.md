Puzzle benchmarks can be narrow and may reward patterns specific to a family of tasks. Rule-less puzzles can introduce ambiguity in both the intended answer and scoring, while rule-based puzzles can still be solved by shortcuts if instances are poorly designed. Results therefore need to be tied to the exact puzzle definition and check used. The survey also does not turn heterogeneous primary studies into a single controlled comparison. Differences in model, prompt, access to tools, training data, and answer format can matter as much as the named method. Do not treat a fluent explanation as proof that constraints were respected, and do not infer broad reasoning ability from success on one puzzle format.
