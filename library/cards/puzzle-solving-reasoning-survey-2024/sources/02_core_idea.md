The paper's organizing idea is that puzzle structure matters. Rule-based puzzles provide explicit constraints; rule-less puzzles require more interpretation or creative inference. It compares prompting, neuro-symbolic approaches, and fine-tuning against that taxonomy, so a reader can ask whether a method is solving the right kind of difficulty rather than treating all puzzles alike. The split is useful because an explicit constraint checker and an ambiguous clue pose different demands. Before reading a score, identify what information is given, which conclusions must be derived, and how correctness is decided. A method that works with clear formal rules should not automatically be expected to resolve an underspecified or creative puzzle.
