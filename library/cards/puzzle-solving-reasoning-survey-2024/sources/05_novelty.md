Its distinctive contribution is a puzzle-specific taxonomy that separates rule-based from rule-less settings while connecting that split to methods and evaluation resources. The paper treats puzzle solving as a way to inspect the gap between a plausible answer and constraint-respecting reasoning, not simply as another general question-answering score. The novelty is organizational rather than a new solver or benchmark. It gives readers a way to state what is being tested: following explicit rules, combining clues, handling ambiguity, or producing a solution that can be checked. This prevents the word “reasoning” from becoming a catch-all label and makes it easier to see why two puzzle results may not be directly comparable.
