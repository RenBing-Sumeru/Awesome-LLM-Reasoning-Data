Construction starts from WOMD training and interactive-validation scenes carrying the `objects_of_interest` label. A rule program produces an ego-centric textual description of the HD map, traffic controls, present motion and position of the ego and surrounding vehicles, cyclists and pedestrians, their relative positions, and future states. The paper reports 52,000 training scenes and 11,000 validation scenes.

Microsoft Azure GPT-4 Turbo receives a prompt with four components: system role, five ordered responsibilities, global rules, and human-written in-context examples. It generates current-only environment, ego, and surrounding-agent Q&As, then uses current and future descriptions to generate one interaction Q&A per surrounding agent and an aggregate ego-intention Q&A. Generation reportedly cost about USD 12,750. Exact endpoint snapshot, temperature, sampling parameters, seeds, retry policy, parsing failures, rejection policy, and corrected-row ledger are unknown.

The repository describes per-scene JSON fields `sid`, `ego`, `cur_time`, `future_time`, `rel_id`, `rel_qa_id`, and paired `env`, `ego`, `sur`, and `int` question/answer arrays. Original agent IDs and substituted Q&A IDs coexist; the latter encode vehicles in [0,100), bicycles in [100,200), and pedestrians in [200,300). The release is split into `training.tar.gz` and `validation_interactive.tar.gz`, but the current Waymo archive requires sign-in and no immutable Card-level manifest is available.

For the visual extension, ScenarioNet and MetaDrive replay scenes as BEV and ego-view video; the paper specifies 90 frames at 10 Hz, with 10 historical and 80 future frames. Motion-LLaVA uses LLaVA-v1.5-7B with a MultiPath++ motion encoder and trains all components for one epoch. Human evaluation covers 1,610 Q&As judged by four people, but there is no automatic row-level acceptance predicate and further human cleaning was ongoing.
