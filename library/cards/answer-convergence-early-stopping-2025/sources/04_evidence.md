The paper reports that convergence often occurs before the full trace, with the distribution shifting later as task difficulty increases. Across five benchmarks and five model settings, the three methods are evaluated by final accuracy and token use; Learn-to-Stop is generally more robust on harder tasks, while answer consistency and token adjustment work best on lower-reasoning-demand tasks. NaturalQuestions results include token reductions above 40% in some settings, and the paper also studies model size, hyperparameters, and faithfulness of truncated traces.

These experiments evaluate stopping policies, not a training-data release. Matching the full-trace answer cannot show that either answer is correct, and preserved benchmark accuracy cannot certify the semantic validity of every truncated prefix or learned label. Manual faithfulness analysis is limited and does not replace record-level verification.
