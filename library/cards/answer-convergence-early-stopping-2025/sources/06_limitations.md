Convergence can lock in a wrong answer, especially on GPQA or AIME-like tasks, and the supervised labels reproduce the full model's own mistakes. Sentence boundaries and answer normalization are format-sensitive; repeated equivalent answers may be written differently, while identical strings may hide different reasoning. Think-token adjustment depends on a model exposing a compatible reasoning terminator. Activation-based stopping requires model access and a separately trained predictor for each representation regime.

Premature truncation can make the visible rationale unfaithful to the returned answer. Results cover five benchmarks and selected model families; transfer to other languages, open-ended generation, tool use, or different decoding settings remains unknown. Complete derived traces, labels, activations, thresholds, stopper checkpoints, licenses, contamination checks, and replay manifests are not established as public artifacts.
