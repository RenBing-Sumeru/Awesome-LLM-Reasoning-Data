Closest prior work uses general preference pairs, terminal success, or step accuracy without representing the domain-specific states and failure modes of video reward-model evaluation. This paper changes the record structure and feedback contract by centering VideoRewardBench and combining inputs, candidates, environmental or evidential state, and labels in one auditable example through generating chosen and rejected answers for video questions and verifying preferences across perception, knowledge, reasoning, and safety. The contribution is task-specific data construction, capability decomposition, and practical evaluation rather than sample count alone.
