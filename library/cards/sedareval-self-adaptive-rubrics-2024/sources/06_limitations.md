1. **Scale and language:** One thousand Chinese questions support controlled study but do not cover multilingual or all open domains.

2. **Criterion cost:** High-quality instance-level rubrics require expert understanding of questions and solutions, making large-scale professional expansion expensive.

3. **Solution diversity:** Predefined scoring points may omit unconventional but correct reasoning paths and unfairly penalize creative answers.

4. **Evaluator overfitting:** A dedicated evaluator may learn dataset wording and rubric templates rather than genuine quality assessment.

5. **Error propagation:** Factual or weighting errors in a rubric systematically affect both human labels and model scores.

Adaptations should explicitly allow equivalent solutions and include blind cross-domain testing.
