The paper compares the self-adaptive-rubric evaluator with direct GPT-4 scoring and other automatic evaluation paradigms on the same responses, using human scores as the reference. The SedarEval-trained evaluator achieves higher agreement with human judgments, indicating that question-specific scoring points and deductions reduce the ambiguity of generic judges. Category-level results further suggest particular effectiveness on tasks requiring explicit reasoning steps or key knowledge points. The evidence supports adaptive rubrics, but the evaluator is trained and tested on similar rubric-formatted data and may benefit from in-distribution advantages. Cross-language, cross-domain, and novel-format generalization still require independent validation.
