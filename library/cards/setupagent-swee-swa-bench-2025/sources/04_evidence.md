The final ICML paper reports 535 SWA-Bench instances from 44 repositories and 885 SWEE-Bench instances from 366 repositories (Paper Table 1, §4.2, Appendix C Table 10 and repository lists). The current official Hub revisions do not reproduce those snapshots: SWA-Bench revision `9ce2651cf73f5cf691101ef5859ce3fccfc6cc24` exposes 450 test rows, while SWEE-Bench revision `012045a7367884b1e694c5840c409a9147daf58e` exposes 798. The missing 85 and 87 rows are not explained by a public changelog, so paper-scale and current-release claims must remain separate.

A manual audit of 30 SWA instances found that all ran the intended tests, 23 used exactly the reference commands, and only 22 had fully correct setup. This is direct evidence that the 95% test threshold admits imperfect environments. The paper reports SetUpAgent success on 28.6% of SWA repositories and 58.5% of SWA instances, versus 21.6% and 71.5% for SWEE. On successful SWA repositories, the full method reaches 44, compared with 33 using CI/CD only, 15 using text only, and 11 without iterative improvement (Paper §4.3, p. 7; Tables 3–4, p. 8).

The evaluation results are version-sensitive. ArXiv v1 states up to 40% lower success and reports GPT-4o AutoCodeRover v2 at 10.2% on SWA versus 16.6% on SWE, a 6.4-point or 38.6%-relative decrease (arXiv v1 Table 6). The ICML/PMLR final abstract changes the headline to up to 60% after final Table 6 adds DeepSeek V3 at 8% on SWA versus 26% on SWE over 100 sampled instances. The 60% statement is the final-paper headline; the 40% statement belongs only to v1, and neither should be generalized beyond the stated agents, subsets, and harness.

For contamination, all evaluated models perform lower on post-knowledge-cutoff SWA tasks, but only GPT-4o reaches statistical significance; SWEE shows no comparable signal (Paper §5.4 and Table 8, pp. 8–9). This is suggestive stratification, not proof of training-corpus decontamination. Three SetUpAgent runs over 100 SWEE candidate repositories succeed on 27, 27, and 26 repositories, respectively (Appendix A.1, Table 11), which supports reported repeatability but is not an independent reproduction.

These experiments show that automated setup can expand the evaluation surface and that agent rankings/success vary with repository distribution. They do not certify every released environment, prove data quality from benchmark performance, establish dataset licensing, or reconcile the current Hub rows with the paper snapshot.
