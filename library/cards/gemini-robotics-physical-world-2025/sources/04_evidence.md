The generalization suite contains 85 tasks: 20% in-distribution, 28% visual generalization, 28% instruction generalization, and 24% action generalization. Trials are repeated in the physical world, paired models are run back-to-back in random order, and pairwise t-tests are used. This protocol reduces ordering and environment bias but does not release trial videos, scorer identity, inter-rater agreement, or confidence intervals for every result.

For six long-horizon specialization tasks trained with 2,000–5,000 demonstrations each, the author-reported average success is 79%. Lunch-box packing reaches 100% success and lasts more than 2 minutes; the spelling task succeeds on 4 of 6 unseen hand-drawn sketches. A specialist initialized from scratch on the same specialization datasets scores 0% across these tasks. These results support the value of generalist initialization, but they do not isolate backbone, distillation, generalist mixture, and specialization data.

Reasoning specialization is evaluated in 100 trials across 8 tasks, with one unique initial scene per trial and groups for one-step reasoning, semantic generalization, and spatial understanding. The report visualizes predicted future arm trajectories, but it does not release the re-labelled records, coordinate schema, label generator, or validation, so the intermediate cannot be independently reproduced.

Fast adaptation compares 5, 20, and 100 demonstrations on 8 tasks. Each task/data point uses 10 physical trials. With at most 100 demonstrations, performance exceeds 70% on 7 of 8 tasks, and two tasks reach 100%; 100 episodes correspond to about 15–60 minutes of demonstrations. These are task-dependent success results, not training rewards, and some baselines remain stronger on easier tasks.

ERQA releases 400 image/text multiple-choice questions under CC BY 4.0, including spatial, trajectory, action, state, pointing, multi-view, and task reasoning; 28% use multiple images. Its answer key and harness support answer-level evaluation. The license covers ERQA and the manuscript, not the proprietary ALOHA 2 demonstrations, reasoning relabels, Gemini weights, or training code.
