The contribution is not merely another terminal benchmark result. Terminal-Task-Gen puts broad prompt adaptation and targeted executable synthesis in one construction pipeline, then studies data filtering, mixture order, context length, and scale with the same family of student models. The episode representation preserves multi-turn commands, observations, corrections, and recovery behavior rather than reducing an attempt to a final answer.

The most consequential empirical distinction is the explicit comparison of no-filter, completion-filtered, and success-filtered SFT mixtures. In the tested setting, discarding incomplete and unsuccessful trajectories removes much of the data and performs worse. This turns negative and partial behavior into a concrete data-engineering variable rather than an unreported by-product of rollout generation.

The open artifacts also create an unusually inspectable boundary between task specification, environment, verifier, conversation trace, and trained checkpoint. That boundary is incomplete: task archives are separate from the conversation rows, outcomes are not normalized, and the seed-based stream is missing. The useful novelty is therefore the combined construction-and-ablation surface, not a claim that the release provides fully labeled failures or fully reproducible containers.
