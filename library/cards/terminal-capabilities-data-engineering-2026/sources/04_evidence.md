The release evidence and the paper evidence answer different questions. The current official Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus exposes four train-only configurations totaling exactly 366,154 rows: 226,313 `dataset_adapters`, 44,809 `skill_based_easy`, 89,343 `skill_based_medium`, and 5,689 `skill_based_mixed`. Their sum reconciles to the paper's 226,313 adapter and 139,841 skill-based trajectories. It does not reconcile to the full Table 5 total of 490,520 because the paper also uses 124,366 seed-based trajectories that are not confirmed in the public collection. The schema confirms conversations and run metadata, but no normalized success, reward, test-result, or termination field.

The filtering ablations provide evidence for the study recipe. For adapter data, the paper compares 196,940 complete-only samples with 226,313 no-filter samples and selects no filtering. For the full synthetic mixture, Table 7 compares complete-only (104,603 samples, `6.74 ± 2.20` on Terminal-Bench 2.0), success-only (83,448, `5.06 ± 2.11`), and no filter (264,207, `12.4 ± 2.29`). Under this experiment, the no-filter mixture performs better, consistent with the authors' interpretation that error states and recovery behavior can supply useful supervision.

That result does not establish the label composition of the public 366,154 rows. Table 7 covers both seed-based and skill-based synthetic trajectories, including the missing 124,366-row stream. The released feature map does not expose the completion and test-success labels needed to reproduce the complete/success/failure breakdown. It is therefore evidence that the paper's selected training recipe retains unsuccessful and incomplete behavior, not evidence that every released row has a verified outcome or that a reuser can select failures by label.

The remaining reported experiments show associations under the paper's SFT setup: single-stage mixed training outperforms the tested adapter-then-synthetic curriculum; Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B improve as the synthetic-data fraction rises through 0%, 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, and 100%; and the default 32,768-token SFT with 40,960-token evaluation performs better than the reported 65,536-token variants. Nemotron-Terminal models also improve over their Qwen3 bases on Terminal-Bench 2.0. These benchmark results support the tested recipe's utility, but they do not by themselves prove corpus correctness, verifier quality, absence of contamination, or exact replayability.
