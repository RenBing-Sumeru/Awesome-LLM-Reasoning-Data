Terminal agents need supervision that couples reasoning and shell actions to the changing state of an executable environment. Such data is expensive to collect: every episode may require a fresh container, multi-turn interaction, dependency installation, command execution, and an end-state check. Existing terminal-agent reports rarely disclose enough about task sources, unsuccessful attempts, environment construction, or filtering to determine what actually entered training.

This work addresses two linked gaps. The construction gap is how to obtain both broad coverage and terminal-specific tasks without relying on scarce human interaction logs. The audit gap is how to distinguish a paper's experimental mixture from the rows that are actually downloadable, and how to interpret a retained trajectory when the release does not expose a normalized verifier result. Those distinctions matter here because the paper studies 490,520 trajectories, whereas the confirmed public corpus has 366,154 rows, and because the paper supports retaining incomplete and unsuccessful behavior while the released row schema does not make those outcome classes directly enumerable.
