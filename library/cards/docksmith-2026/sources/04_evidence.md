On the MDE evaluation, DockSmith reports 39.72% F2P and a 58.28% Commit Rate, compared with 19.46% F2P and 34.13% Commit Rate for the base Qwen3-Coder model. F2P is the execution-correctness outcome; Commit Rate measures the propensity to submit and must not be read as correctness. The paper's representative JEG2/highline case shows a baseline reaching 50 steps while DockSmith terminates in 5, but this single case does not establish a universal generation limit. (Table 1; Appendix B.1, Table 9 and Listings 1-2.)

The filtering and curriculum ablation reports average F2P over three fixed-budget runs: 32.24 for the baseline, 34.03 with acceptance shaping, 33.13 with curriculum alone, and 34.93 with both. This supports the joint curation recipe under the reported budget, not a claim that each accepted fragment is high quality. (Table 4 and §3.4.)

Transfer is mixture-dependent. With SWE-only training, the paper reports 49.65 on SWE.V, 31.83 on SWE.M, and 10.67 on Terminal. A 1:1 Docker:SWE condition reports 51.90/33.92 on SWE.V/SWE.M, while a 1:0.5 condition reaches 14.04 on Terminal. Increasing the Docker share to 1:2 falls to 49.55/31.75/11.52. The result is evidence that Docker interaction data can help some broader software-agent tasks at selected ratios, and also evidence that more Docker data is not monotonically better. (Appendix Table 8.)

GPT-5.1-based error analysis labels 3,757 events for the baseline and 2,161 for DockSmith; critical errors decline from 2,454 to 1,468. Context Retrieval errors instead increase from 285 to 316, a 10.9% rise. Resolution analysis also remains mixed: runtime and logic recovery improve, shell and environment resolution drop by 2.4 and 4.6 percentage points, and overall resolution changes from 75.7% to 75.6%. The paper's case analysis identifies Dockerfile rollback/state inconsistency, fragmented dependency diagnosis, diagnostic oscillation, and evaluation-protocol violations. (Tables 5-6, §3.5, and Appendix B.1.)

All performance and error counts are author-reported and were not independently reproduced for this Card. GPT-5.1 labels are automated judgments rather than programmatic ground truth, and the paper gives no human-agreement study. Benchmark improvement demonstrates utility of the selected training pipeline, not row-level data quality, licensing, security, or deterministic replay.
