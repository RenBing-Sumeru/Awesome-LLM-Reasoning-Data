The analysis assumes that a solution and its verification can be costed by generated tokens and, in its simplified formulation, often uses the same model family for both roles. Real serving stacks may have different model sizes, batching, caching, latency targets, and verifier prompts, so the fitted constants and crossover budget will not transfer automatically. The fine-tuned verifier also depends on teacher-generated rationales and known-answer filtering. Results on MATH, AIME, and GPQA do not establish that the same allocation law holds for open-ended tasks where correctness cannot be reduced to a reliable terminal verdict.
