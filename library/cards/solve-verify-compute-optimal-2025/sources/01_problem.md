Extra inference computation can be spent in two different ways. Self-consistency samples many solutions and relies on answer frequency, increasing the chance that at least one reasoning path is correct. A generative reward model instead writes one or more verification chains for each candidate solution and uses its verdicts to rank candidates. Comparing the two at the same number of solutions makes verification appear efficient, but omits the substantial tokens required to generate the verification reasoning itself.

The paper asks what should happen under a fixed total compute budget: should a system generate more solutions, or should it generate fewer solutions and spend the saved computation checking them more carefully? It also asks how the optimal solution count and verification count scale as the budget grows. The relevant record is therefore not just an answer and a score; it includes each solution, each verification chain, its verdict probability, and the joint cost of producing all of them.
