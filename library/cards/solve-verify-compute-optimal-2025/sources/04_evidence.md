The COLM paper evaluates compute-matched curves on MATH, AIME24, and GPQA-Diamond, with additional model-family and scale checks using Llama and Qwen configurations. It reports that self-consistency is more compute-efficient than generative verification at most practical budgets. In the illustrative MATH setting with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the generative verifier first matches self-consistency only after up to eight times the inference compute and needs substantially more budget to move ahead.

The paper also reports that at high compute, generative verification can win: one figure describes an additional 128-fold compute increase for a 3.8% gain in the stated comparison. Its difficulty analysis finds harder MATH problems benefit more from the fine-tuned verifier, with up to 30% relative improvement over self-consistency in the reported condition. These results support a conditional policy rather than a blanket verdict: account for verifier quality, problem difficulty, and the actual cost of verification before spending the budget on it.
