The method represents a solution as a sampled chain of thought and a verification as another generated chain ending in a Yes/No verdict. For each candidate, it averages the probability assigned to Yes across V verifier generations, then uses best-of-N selection. Its simplified cost is proportional to S(1+λV), where λ is the ratio between verification and solution token lengths; self-consistency is the special case V=0 followed by majority voting. This formulation makes the token cost of verification visible in every comparison.

For the fine-tuned generative verifier, the authors first let a solution generator answer MATH training problems and ask GPT-4o to produce verification rationales with access to ground-truth solutions. They retain only rationales whose verdict agrees with the ground-truth correctness of the generated answer and balance Yes and No examples, then fine-tune student LLMs. At inference they vary S and V over increasing ranges, measure success rate against compute, derive optimal S and V for each budget, and fit power laws to those optima.
