The distinctive contribution is not merely using a generative verifier. The paper changes the comparison unit from number of solutions to total inference computation, making a verifier's own chains of thought part of the bill. This corrects a common evaluation shortcut in which a verification method is credited for using fewer candidates while its many expensive verification rollouts are uncounted.

It also treats the two sample counts as jointly optimizable scaling axes. Rather than recommending a fixed number of verifier calls, it derives empirical scaling laws for solution and verification counts and identifies a growth asymmetry favoring solution exploration. The resulting contribution is a decision framework: under a known budget, choose whether verification is worth activating and, if it is, grow solutions more aggressively than verification chains.
