The paper evaluates solution sampling and generative verification on a compute-matched basis. For self-consistency, it samples S solutions and sets the number of verifications V to zero. For generative verification, it samples S solutions, produces V verification chains for every problem-solution pair, averages the verifier scores, and chooses the highest-scoring solution. The comparison therefore charges both generation and checking to the same budget instead of counting candidate solutions alone.

This exposes two regimes. At practical lower budgets, sampling additional solutions is generally more compute-efficient because verification chains can be as long as or longer than solutions. At sufficiently high budgets, a capable generative verifier can improve selection. The paper then fits separate power-law relations for optimal S and V, arguing that both should grow but solution generation should grow faster, by a reported factor of about 1.5 to 2 relative to verification.
