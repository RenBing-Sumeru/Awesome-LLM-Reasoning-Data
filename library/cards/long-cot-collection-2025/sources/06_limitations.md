The largest audit limitation is artifact absence. The ACL paper and its metadata end the release claim with the literal word `LINK`; no paper-authorized code, dataset, model, immutable revision, row schema, split, or hash manifest is available. A Hugging Face organization named Long-CoT-Collection currently exposes no public models or datasets, but the paper does not provide it as a release target, so it is not recorded as an artifact.

Construction is also only partly reproducible. Exact o1 and GPT-4o snapshots, collection dates, generation decoding, candidate count, retry policy, token caps, random seeds, tie breaking, source-row IDs, and item-level retained/rejected judgments are unknown. GPT-4o both generates and filters the data, while answer agreement cannot detect a lucky final answer, an invalid intermediate step, or a plausible but unfaithful rationale. The 100-item o3-mini comparison has no human adjudication or reported judge-error rate.

Budget units are not directly homogeneous across hidden OpenAI token accounting, GPT-tokenized R1 text, and tagged local-model text. The authors themselves report that the 25% constraint can compress too much information into too few outlines and make reasoning confusing. They test only o1 as the reference LRM and use a 0.5B policy for RL because 16 A100 40GB GPUs could not fit a larger long-sequence setup.

Upstream Magpie licensing is named, but rights for o1-derived flow summaries and GPT-4o-generated traces, redistribution terms, decontamination, benchmark overlap, personal/sensitive content screening, and record-level lineage are not documented. Finally, benchmark and RLVR gains are pipeline-level outcomes; they do not establish that every trace is correct or that the collection alone caused the improvement.
