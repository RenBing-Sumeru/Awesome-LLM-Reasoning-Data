The paper evaluates three different propositions, none of which independently proves data quality. First, an o3-mini A/B/tie judge compares the collection with correct-answer R1 outputs on 100 sampled questions, with answer order randomized. The reported chart favors the collection on reasoning flow while R1 is somewhat stronger on strategy and step correctness; judge identity overlap, sample size, and absence of human calibration limit this evidence. Second, token distributions show that ordinary GPT-4o prompting rarely exceeds 1,000 tokens, whereas flow-guided generation yields much longer traces and a distribution closer to o1-mini.

Third, downstream models improve under the reported setup. On GPQA Diamond, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct rises from 22.7 to 36.4 and Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct from 37.6 to 39.9; on MMLU-Pro the corresponding changes are 43.7 to 44.5 and 49.9 to 51.4. MATH500 scores for models trained on the 100%, 50%, and 25% budget variants are 66.6, 60.7, and 57.6. Best-of-N curves improve, especially at larger N, and the 0.5B policy initialized on the collection is reported to obtain 2–3× larger *performance gains* from RLVR than the base initialization on MATH500 and GPQA. These results support the complete recipe under its experimental conditions; they do not isolate the collection from optimization, reward design, source mixture, or judge errors, and “2–3× gains” is not “2–3× final performance.”
