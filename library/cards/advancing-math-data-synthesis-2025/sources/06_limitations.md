The paper mixes NuminaMath and Lila with proprietary problem data. Without source proportions, item IDs, terms, or the proprietary collection policy, provenance, redistribution rights, and contamination cannot be audited end to end. The CC BY 4.0 paper and Apache-2.0 model repository do not license the unreleased mixed corpus or its derivatives.

Generator, weak-student, and strong-teacher checkpoints are unknown, as are decoding, retries, parsers, random seeds, per-seed candidate counts, and acceptance yields. LLM-produced `accept`/`refuse` and `correct`/`wrong` tags are judgments, not proofs. They may accept invalid questions, correct answers with faulty reasoning, or teacher corrections that introduce new errors. Retrospective enhancement creates artificial backtracking rather than authentic sampled mistakes.

The synthesis arms differ substantially in token exposure, especially the 13.90B-token tutorship arm. CPT and SFT also differ in learning rate, batch size, number of passes, and checkpoint selection; SFT reports the best of ten checkpoints, while CPT uses validation loss. Step count is a coarse difficulty proxy, and evaluation selects the better zero-shot or few-shot result per dataset. The public comparison model checks final answers while ignoring rationale errors, leaving step-level quality unmeasured.

MinHash byte-overlap removal addresses narrow exact-text duplication and leakage, not semantic contamination. The main text and Appendix A.1 also disagree on 14.7B versus 13.7B mathematical-corpus tokens in the controlled study. Accepted records, rejected candidates, refusal reasons, false-accept rates, seed-to-output lineage, and synthesis code are unavailable. The released checkpoint enables model experiments but cannot substitute for data, licenses, or audit logs.
