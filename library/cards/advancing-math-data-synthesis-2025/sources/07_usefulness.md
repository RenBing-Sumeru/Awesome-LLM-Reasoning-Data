For the Instruction, Demonstration, and Rationale Data track, the paper supplies a concrete taxonomy of four teaching targets: alternative worked solutions, transformed question-solution pairs with self-checks, artificial backtracking traces, and sampled student attempts paired with teacher judgments and corrections. Follow-up studies can compare these schemas without treating all synthetic rationales as interchangeable.

For the Data Construction and Open Release Recipes track, it is most useful as an ablation blueprint. A reproduction should pin source and seed IDs; disclose generator, student, teacher, and answer-judge revisions; publish prompts and parsers; equalize or factorially vary token budgets; retain every candidate, refusal, and judge decision; and report per-method yield, false accepts, and correction error. CPT/SFT comparisons should align exposure and checkpoint selection or explicitly model their effects.

The work can guide continued-pretraining mixture studies, minimal SFT controls for instruction following, difficulty sampling based on solution structure, and studies of correction traces. Authentic student-error data and symbolic, executable, or multi-judge checking would be important controls for retrospective and tutorship synthesis. Step validity should be audited separately from final-answer accuracy.

The present reuse boundary is model evaluation plus recipe reimplementation. Training-data reuse remains blocked by the unavailable corpora, proprietary-source rights, missing item lineage, and absent synthesis code. A model checkpoint is useful evidence that an artifact exists, but not a substitute for a reusable data release.
