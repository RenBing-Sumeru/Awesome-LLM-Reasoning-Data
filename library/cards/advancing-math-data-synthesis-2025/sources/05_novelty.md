Earlier math-model recipes commonly used web mathematical text during pre-training and worked solutions during post-training, while augmentation studies often isolated one reformulation or solution-generation method. This paper's useful change is a joint experimental decomposition by transformation semantics and training stage. (Paper §1 and related work.)

The most distinctive serialized object is tutorship amplification: it keeps a weak model's sampled attempt and pairs it with a stronger model's correctness judgment, error location, correction, and continuation. This differs from retrospective enhancement's artificial `[back]` trace and from response/query variants that primarily change solution paths or questions. The paper also compares training distribution and step-count difficulty around the same problem-solving pool.

The novelty is a comparative recipe and ablation framework, not an open corpus, formal verifier, or general proof that CPT dominates SFT. Teacher corrections, self-checks, alternative solutions, and error traces predate the paper. Its reusable contribution is the explicit distinction among these data objects and stages, bounded by unequal synthesis budgets and unreleased generator identities, records, and code.
