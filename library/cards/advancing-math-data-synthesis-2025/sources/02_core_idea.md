The paper separates two choices often conflated in math-data work: the transformation applied to a problem-solution seed and the training stage that consumes the resulting tokens. The authors report collecting 25 million problem-solving records from NuminaMath, Lila, and proprietary data, then selecting 14 million as augmentation seeds. Source proportions and seed IDs are not published. (Appendix A.1.)

Response diversification produces two alternative solutions to the same question. Query expansion converts a source question to a statement, generates two new questions and solutions, and self-checks them. Retrospective enhancement inserts later steps into earlier reasoning and marks retry behavior with `[back]`, producing an artificial backtracking trace. Tutorship amplification samples a weak student's solution and asks a stronger teacher to output `correct` or, when wrong, identify the erroneous step, repair it, and continue the solution. (Paper §4; Appendix G.)

These objects have different feedback contracts. Response and query synthesis use self-issued accept/refuse or correctness tags; tutorship uses another model's judgment and localized correction; no independent retrospective verifier is disclosed. The comparison then allocates problem-solving records to CPT or SFT. The released MathGPT-8B weights are the final model artifact, not a release of the 25 million source records, 14 million seeds, four synthetic families, or rejected candidates.
