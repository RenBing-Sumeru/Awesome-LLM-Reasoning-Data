The usual math-model pipeline assigns mathematical web text to continued pre-training (CPT) and reserves worked problems for supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This paper asks a narrower data-design question: whether questions paired with reasoning steps should already be consumed during CPT, whether their transformation method changes the outcome, and how the same problem-solving tokens behave when allocated to CPT or SFT. (Paper §1–§2.)

The study compares ordinary mathematical-corpus tokens, original problem-solving records, and four synthetic derivatives. Its central data objects are not just final answers: they include alternative worked solutions, transformed question-solution pairs with self-checks, artificial retry traces, and student-attempt/teacher-correction records. Each carries a different feedback signal and rationale structure.

For this atlas, the paper is both an instruction/demonstration/rationale data study and a construction-recipe comparison. The source and synthetic corpora are not released, however. Only the paper, prompts, an answer-comparison model, and the final MathGPT-8B checkpoint are public, so the work supports recipe audit and independent reimplementation rather than direct dataset reuse.
