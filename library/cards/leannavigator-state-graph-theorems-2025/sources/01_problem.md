Lean can verify formal proofs step by step, but automated theorem provers are constrained by scarce machine-checkable training data. Existing corpora often retain only the original Mathlib theorem and one human proof, remain far smaller than natural-language corpora, and are expensive to expand through manual formalization. Limited theorem coverage also exposes models to too few intermediate states, alternative proofs, and tactic combinations.

LeanNavigator avoids authoring new mathematics from scratch. It treats the execution of existing Lean proofs as a state-transition graph: starting from Mathlib4 theorems, it enumerates executable tactics, discovers states that can reach `ProofFinished`, and reinterprets those states as new theorems with proofs. The process yields 4.7 million Lean theorem–proof pairs for training stronger proof generators.
