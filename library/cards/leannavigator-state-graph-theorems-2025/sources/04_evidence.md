Both generation efficiency and downstream training support the claim. Under a two-minute budget, LeanNavigator explores about 2,035 states per theorem on average, compared with roughly 21.69 for ReProver, showing that template retrieval and batched instantiation substantially enlarge the verified search space. A Flan-T5-base prover trained on the generated corpus solves 104 of 493 MiniF2F problems versus ReProver’s 99, and 39 of 117 MIL problems versus 30. These comparisons show that large-scale state-derived data is useful within the same Lean ecosystem. However, the model, search procedure, and training data change together, so gains cannot be attributed solely to corpus size, and evidence is limited to Mathlib-related Lean benchmarks.
