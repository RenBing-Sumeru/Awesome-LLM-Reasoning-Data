Claim: fine-grained correction is data-efficient for reducing visual hallucination. With 1.4K annotated samples, the paper reports a 34.8% hallucination-rate reduction from its base MLLM and better results than concurrent LLaVA-RLHF trained on 10K annotated samples. The comparison uses the paper's models and five reported benchmarks; it does not establish that 1.4K corrections transfer to every MLLM or image domain.
