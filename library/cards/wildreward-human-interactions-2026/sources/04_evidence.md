The paper compares WildReward with conventional pairwise reward models on multiple reward benchmarks, calibration tests, and consistency evaluations, reporting comparable or superior performance. A user-diversity ablation shows that adding more distinct users improves the model, supporting the claim that the signal comes from heterogeneous real interactions rather than duplicate volume alone. Online DPO guided by WildReward improves several downstream tasks. These converging tests support usefulness, but behavioral labels remain proxies: follow-up actions do not uniquely reveal satisfaction or correctness.
