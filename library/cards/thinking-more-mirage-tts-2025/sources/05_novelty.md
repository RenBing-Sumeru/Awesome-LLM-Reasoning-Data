Earlier discussions often report a favorable point on a longer-thinking curve and implicitly treat further length as more compute applied to the same reasoning skill. This paper changes the evaluation object from a single endpoint to the entire budget-performance curve. It names the failure region, overthinking, and connects it to a measurable change in output uncertainty rather than merely noting that a long prompt sometimes fails.

Its practical novelty is the matched-budget comparison between sequential continuation and parallel independent traces with a fixed aggregation rule. The paper does not introduce a new reward model or claim that majority voting verifies a solution. Instead, it makes token allocation, trace diversity, and answer aggregation explicit variables, so a reader can audit whether a reported gain comes from extra computation, a different distribution of that computation, or a selection effect.
