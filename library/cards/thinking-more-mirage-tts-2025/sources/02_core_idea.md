The central claim is that longer sequential thinking is not a reliable proxy for better reasoning. Extending a trace can initially increase accuracy, but it also increases the uncertainty and variance of the output distribution. After a task- and model-dependent point, that dispersion outweighs the initial coverage benefit, so the same mechanism that looked helpful at a modest budget produces worse final answers at a larger one.

The proposed alternative is parallel thinking. Rather than place an entire budget in a single continuously extended chain, the method samples several independent reasoning paths whose total token length stays within that budget and returns the answer chosen by majority vote. This changes the allocation decision from “how long should one trace continue?” to “how many independent paths should share the budget, and how should their final answers be aggregated?”
