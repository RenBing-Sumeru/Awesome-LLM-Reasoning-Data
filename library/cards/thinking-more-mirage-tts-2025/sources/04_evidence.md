Across the three reported mathematical benchmarks and three distilled reasoning models, the sequential policy displays a non-monotonic curve: initial extra thinking improves performance and sufficiently long continuation degrades it. One reported GSM8K example raises accuracy from 82.2% to 87.3% as average thinking length grows from 385 to 1,100 tokens, then lowers it to 70.3% at 15,980 tokens. The same analysis reports that extending one model from 385 to 6,136 thinking tokens increases measured output entropy from 0.23 to 2.79.

The parallel comparison tests the same total token budget rather than granting its method unlimited extra computation. The paper reports that at a 16K-token budget, parallel thinking attains up to 22% higher accuracy than sequential extension, and that it maintains or improves accuracy as the budget grows across its plotted settings. This supports the narrower claim that sequential continuation can be an inefficient allocation policy; it does not establish majority vote as an optimal selector for arbitrary tasks, model families, or cost metrics.
