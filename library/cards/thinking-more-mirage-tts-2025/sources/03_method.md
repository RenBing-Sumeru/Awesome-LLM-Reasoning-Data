The authors define test-time budget control by varying the number of thinking tokens before an answer is allowed to terminate. They study sequential controls that suppress the end-of-thinking marker and append prompts such as “Wait” or “Think more,” then measure accuracy against average thinking-token use. To interpret the observed curve, they use a simple probabilistic model in which increasing policy variance can initially improve overlap with a reward region but later dilute probability mass and lower expected reward; they also measure response-distribution entropy empirically.

For the alternative allocation, a total budget B is divided among N independently generated reasoning traces rather than assigned to one trace. The final answer is selected by majority vote. Experiments compare these procedures on GSM8K, MATH-500, and AIME 2024 using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. The recorded decision surface is explicit: method, token budget, trace count, trace outputs, aggregation rule, accuracy, and average tokens.
