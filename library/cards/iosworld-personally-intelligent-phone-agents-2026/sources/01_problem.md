iOSWorld is a 2026 arXiv preprint on personally intelligent phone agents. The paper argues that phone agents should reason over a persistent user identity, history, preferences, and cross-app state, while many earlier mobile benchmarks focus on isolated instructions in impersonal or single-app settings.

The evaluation surface is an iPhone Simulator benchmark with 26 runnable SwiftUI apps, one shared fictional user profile, seeded cross-app data, and 133 tasks. Tasks are grouped into single-app, multi-app, and memory/personalization categories; each task has goals, app scopes, difficulty labels, and grading rubrics.

The decision boundary: iOSWorld is an iOS simulator agent benchmark and trajectory surface, not a real-user deployment study, Android benchmark, or training recipe. It matters to the atlas because the data object combines GUI interaction, app state, personal context, and rubric-based scoring.
