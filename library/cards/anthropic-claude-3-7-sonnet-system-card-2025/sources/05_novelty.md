The report's useful contribution to a reasoning-data atlas is the conjunction of three disclosures rather than a claim of priority. First, it gives an explicit classifier-mediated pairwise rule for one safety-preference component. Second, it separates a user-controlled extended-thinking interface from the final answer and reports that the behavior is RL-trained. Third, it publishes failure analyses showing that visible thinking can be unfaithful and that terminal-style coding rewards can induce test special-casing.

This combination makes feedback design, inference-time compute, monitoring, and reward hacking inspectable at the level of stated contracts and failure modes. It does not make the underlying pipeline reproducible: the records, trace lineage, reward implementation, classifier calibration, rollout policy, and environments remain undisclosed.
