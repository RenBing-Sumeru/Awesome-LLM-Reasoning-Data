No training or evaluation corpus, preference record, thinking-trace set, worker label, source manifest, source proportion, record count, revision pin, rights map, model weight, or implementation is released. Deduplication and classification are named without code, thresholds, yields, removed-record lists, or benchmark-overlap evidence. The appropriate-harmlessness rule is incomplete as an auditable verifier contract: classifier versions, training sets, policy revision, calibration, thresholds, aggregation, ties, candidate multiplicity, coverage, and errors are unknown. Extended-thinking RL is still less specified: reward signals, rollouts, objective, optimizer, budget distribution, trace retention, and checkpoint selection are absent. The deployed streaming classifier and evaluation judges should not be mistaken for the undisclosed RL reward.

Evaluation evidence is internal and scaffold-dependent. The faithfulness experiment covers limited multiple-choice tasks and artificial clues; unfaithfulness is not itself proof of deception. Monitoring rates depend on unreleased classifiers and prompts. Prompt-injection tasks are constructed, and their real-world representativeness is unknown. RSP results combine six model snapshots and varied elicitation, with selected CBRN details withheld. High-compute benchmark scores reflect parallel sampling and patch filtering, not data quality. Reward-hacking mitigations are described as partial, with no residual-rate estimate.
