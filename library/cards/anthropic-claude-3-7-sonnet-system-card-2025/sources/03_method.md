Training is described only at stage level. The proprietary mixture contains publicly available Internet information, non-public third-party data, data from labeling services and paid contractors, and internally generated data. Public Internet coverage is stated through 2024-11, while the knowledge cutoff is the end of 2024-10. Cleaning includes deduplication and classification. Anthropic states that Claude 3-family models were not trained on prompts or outputs submitted by Claude users or customers. Source manifests, quantities, mixture weights, filter configurations, and source-level rights are not published.

Post-training includes human feedback and Constitutional AI during reinforcement learning. The appropriate-harmlessness pipeline constructs classifier-mediated response preferences. Extended thinking is also RL-trained, but its reward contract and optimization process are unknown. These training components must be distinguished from the deployed streaming completion classifier, which analyzes thinking tags and encrypts flagged harmful content, and from evaluation-only classifiers or model judges.

Evaluation protocols are heterogeneous. The harmlessness study compares prompts drawn from the preference-data source set with separately curated out-of-distribution prompts and uses an 8,192-token thinking budget in extended mode. Other RSP studies use tools, agentic harnesses, iterative prompt refinement, helpful-only snapshots, Best-of-N, or Pass@30. The release announcement's high-compute SWE-bench setting adds parallel attempts, rejection of patches that break visible tests, and a scoring model. None of these evaluation scaffolds establishes the undisclosed training rollout protocol.
