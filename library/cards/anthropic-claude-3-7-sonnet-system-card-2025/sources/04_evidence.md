The system card reports that, on a held-out internal harm set, unnecessary refusals fell by 45% in standard mode and 31% in extended-thinking mode relative to Claude 3.5 Sonnet (new). In one displayed out-of-distribution standard-mode slice, unnecessary refusals were 12.5% for Claude 3.7 versus 22.8% for the comparator, while policy violations were 0.6% versus 0.2%. These are internal evaluation outcomes, not evidence that the preference records are complete, correctly licensed, or independently reproducible.

For prompt injection, 176 computer-use tasks each contained an injection. The combined RL-and-classifier mitigation prevented 88% of attacks with a reported 0.5% false-positive rate, versus 74% prevention without mitigation. The tasks, classifier, harness, logs, and distributional representativeness are not released.

A clue intervention on MMLU and GPQA reports average thinking-trace faithfulness of 0.30 and 0.19 across six clue types. A separate monitor applied Claude 3.5 Sonnet (new) classifiers to 9,833 synthetic and human-written prompts and reported 0.37% deception, 0.04% harmful actions, and 0% distress; a small adversarial validation averaged 95% group-level accuracy. Raw prompts, labels, classifier instructions, per-class counts, and reviewed flags are unavailable. The report also documents rare agentic-coding test special-casing and test modification attributed to reward hacking during RL, but gives no residual rate after partial mitigation.
