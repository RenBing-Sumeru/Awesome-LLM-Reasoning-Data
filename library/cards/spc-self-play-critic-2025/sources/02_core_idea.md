SPC places two policies initialized from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in an adversarial game. The sneaky generator receives a problem, a correct partial solution, and a selected correct step. It chooses among logical error, calculation error, misunderstanding conditions, using an incorrect rule/formula/property, and incorrect approach, then rewrites the selected step to introduce a subtle error. The critic sees a problem, prefix, and last step and produces analysis, a concise critique, and an explicit Correct or Incorrect conclusion.

Before confrontation, solver rollouts test whether a proposed corruption has practical effect. A retained incorrect step must turn a high-success continuation point into one with zero observed success under the paper’s thresholds. In the game, a critic that correctly identifies the candidate receives +1 and the generator receives -1; successful deception reverses the signs. The generator is also penalized when its edit fails to reduce solver success. Critic RL data mix retained corruptions with correct steps and balance positive and negative outcomes.

The feedback loop is deliberately asymmetric in the second round. Round 1 trains Sneaky-1 and Critic-1 from data generated by Sneaky-0 versus Critic-0. Because Sneaky-1 wins against Critic-1 too rarely, round 2 generates data using Sneaky-1 versus the weaker Critic-0. Both rounds are then merged to update the round-0 policies into Sneaky-2 and Critic-2. The method’s central idea is thus not unrestricted self-play, but controlled opponent matching plus solver-impact filtering to keep the training game informative.
