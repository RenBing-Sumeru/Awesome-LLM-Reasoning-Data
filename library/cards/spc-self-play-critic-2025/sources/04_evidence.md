On ProcessBench, the paper reports average step-classification accuracy of 70.8% for Critic-0, 76.8% for Critic-1, and 77.7% for Critic-2. On the balanced PRM800K evaluation pairs, the reported average rises from 71.0% to 72.8% and 75.8%. On the math subset of DeltaBench, it rises from 54.9% to 58.8% and 60.5%. Appendix C.1 states that evaluation uses 1,341 PRM800K test pairs, 3,400 balanced ProcessBench steps, and 1,542 balanced DeltaBench steps.

The ablations help isolate construction choices. Generating four critic outputs per transformed step creates same-input positive/negative pairs; removing this pairing yields 75.0% ProcessBench accuracy instead of 76.8% after round 1. Training round 2 from the poorly balanced Sneaky-1 versus Critic-1 game drops the critic to 72.0%, whereas matching Sneaky-1 with Critic-0 and mixing both rounds produces 77.7%. The sneaky generator’s solver-attack success rises from 21.5% to 33.6%; omitting failed solver attacks as negative examples reduces the reported attack rate to 12.1%.

For use at inference time, SPC checks one generated step at a time and asks the solver to regenerate a rejected step up to five times. With five-way self-consistency, the paper reports 85.2% on MATH500 and 23.3% on AIME2024 for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, versus 82.0% and 16.7% for self-consistency alone. For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Self-Consistency + SPC reaches 94.0% and 73.3%, versus 92.2% and 70.0% for self-consistency alone.

These results support that the trained critic changes benchmark behavior under the stated protocols. They do not independently establish the correctness, representativeness, licensing, or contamination status of the released training records. The release files were not row-auditable through the public link during this review.
