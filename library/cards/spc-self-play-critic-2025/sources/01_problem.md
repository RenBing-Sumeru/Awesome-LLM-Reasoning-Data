Reliable process supervision needs labels about whether an individual reasoning step is correct, not only whether a final answer matches. Such labels are expensive: a reviewer must understand the problem, the preceding trajectory, and the local step. They can also age poorly when newer solvers produce different styles and error distributions. A binary step label alone provides little natural-language feedback for diagnosing the error.

SPC addresses this as a data-construction problem. Its target object is a step-level critic example containing a math problem, the partial solution before a selected step, a correct or deliberately corrupted candidate step, a natural-language critique, a Correct/Incorrect conclusion, and a game-derived reward. A paired sneaky-generator example additionally records how a correct step is transformed under one of five error types and whether that transformation harms a solver and fools the critic.

The hard part is label validity. Calling a model-generated edit “incorrect” merely because the generator intended an error would be circular. SPC instead checks downstream behavior: the same solver completes from the original and changed steps, and a pair is retained only when the original continuation succeeds at least 75% while the changed continuation succeeds 0%. This reduces, but does not eliminate, uncertainty about whether the changed step is intrinsically wrong.

The resulting supervision is therefore mixed. Final-answer success supplies a programmatic signal for corruption filtering, while the learned critic supplies a natural-language step judgment. The Card treats these as distinct contracts rather than equating the critic’s confidence with mathematical truth.
