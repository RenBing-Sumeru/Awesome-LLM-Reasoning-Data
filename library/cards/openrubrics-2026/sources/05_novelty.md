Earlier rubrics-as-rewards work either relies on experts or asks a frontier model to write criteria on demand, so its criteria need not explain known preferences. OpenRubrics changes the construction decision: it conditions rubric synthesis on contrasting ranked responses, separates hard rules from principles, then retains only rubric-pair records that reproduce preference labels. Training a generator and judge on those retained records is not itself a new reward objective; the novelty is the preference-grounded, consistency-filtered rubric data contract.
