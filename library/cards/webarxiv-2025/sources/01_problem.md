WebArXiv studies a reproducibility problem in web-agent evaluation: live websites change, but browser agents are often compared on tasks whose answers, layouts, or UI behavior can drift after the benchmark is released. The primary source is the arXiv preprint "WebArXiv: Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Time-Invariant arXiv Tasks" (arXiv:2507.00938v2, 13 Aug 2025), by Zihao Sun and Ling Chen at the University of Technology Sydney.

The concrete problem is not general web question answering. It is whether multimodal browser agents can be evaluated on realistic arXiv navigation and information-retrieval tasks while keeping the answer surface stable enough for repeated comparisons. The paper positions WebArXiv against live-web benchmarks whose answers change and against simplified or trace-fixed settings that do not preserve realistic browsing difficulty.

The data object is a suite of 275 arXiv-grounded tasks, split into five temporally stable categories: platform and organization information, rules and account management, paper discovery and retrieval, advanced search and filtering, and deep paper content extraction. The evaluation surface is a browser trajectory plus final answer checked against a verified gold answer.

The benchmark is best read as environment and agent-trajectory data because it couples task instructions, visual/browser observations, actions, trajectory review, and deterministic outcome labels. The important boundary is that WebArXiv is an evaluation benchmark and agent-analysis substrate, not a released post-training dataset with verified reusable rollouts for training.
