This is not a data release. Its distinctive Track 12 contribution is that it makes several evaluation boundaries unusually explicit. Reward-hacking stress tests are described with hidden fuzzed tests, environment and reward-structure updates, and monitoring, but are explicitly not real-world hacking-rate estimates. White-box work is described with snapshot comparisons, synthetic and controlled prompts, sparse autoencoders, and activation steering, but it remains an internal pre-release audit rather than a released interpretability artifact.

The card further states a consequential audit limitation: evaluation awareness can change behavior and make behavioral evaluations systematically underrate deployment risk. This does not establish a deployment failure rate, but it is a concrete reason to distinguish reported evaluation evidence from a complete safety or post-training-data disclosure. The Card retains that distinction instead of converting either evaluation practice into a reusable training method.
