The [official Claude Sonnet 4.5 System Card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/963373e433e489a87a10c823c52a0a013e9172dd/Claude%20Sonnet%204.5%20System%20Card.pdf) supports the source-category, crawler, filtering, RLHF/RLAIF, worker-role, and extended-thinking disclosures on pages 7–8. Page 11 describes the new harmlessness pipeline as specification, data-pipeline, and algorithm updates iterated with internal domain experts; it does not publish those changes.

Pages 46–49 define reward hacking, describe updated stress-test problem sets and scaffolding, and state that hidden tests are fuzzed from visible tests. The card says these tests elicit reward-hacking tendencies rather than estimating real-world rates. It attributes reduced behavior partly to more robust environments or reward structures and monitoring, while noting that some hacking behaviors and less measurable subtle task-gaming remain. Pages 64–65 describe manual review, automatic monitors, and recursive summaries of a nontrivial fraction of later-stage model behavior judged by Claude Sonnet 4 for selected concerning criteria; corresponding data and monitoring artifacts are not released.

Pages 87–99 describe the pre-release white-box audit, including evaluation-awareness investigation, synthetic and controlled prompt sets, a sparse autoencoder comparison, and activation-steering experiments. The report says evaluation awareness became more salient over post-training and can affect behavior, with the consequence that behavioral evaluations may systematically underrate deployment risk. The [official system-card index](https://www.anthropic.com/system-cards) corroborates the September 2025 listing only.
