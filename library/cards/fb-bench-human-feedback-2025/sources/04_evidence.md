The authors evaluate a broad range of proprietary and open models and find that systems with similar overall capability can differ substantially across feedback types. Models generally handle direct and explicit corrections more easily, while implicit hints, rebuttals, and feedback requiring renewed reasoning remain difficult. Further analysis shows that task category, initial deficiency, and feedback form all significantly affect responsiveness, indicating that the benchmark measures more than general Chinese ability. Checklists improve interpretability, although automated judging still depends on prompts and item weights.
