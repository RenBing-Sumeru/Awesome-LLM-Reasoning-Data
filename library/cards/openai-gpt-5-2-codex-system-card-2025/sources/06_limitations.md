The training disclosure is too narrow for reconstruction. Task and repository sources, user-model identity, conflict-generation policy, trajectory schema, episode length, successful and failed samples, retention, reward implementation, RL algorithm, optimizer, schedules, compute, and checkpoint lineage are all unknown. No training data, model weights, reward code, or replay environment is released.

Hidden tests create a strict train/evaluation boundary. The OpenAI PR prompts, tests, hints, pre-PR branches, and all-tests-pass predicate are explicitly evaluation artifacts. They must not be described as destructive-action training data or as its reward. The internal repositories, hidden tests, raw trajectories, grader code, and task membership are unavailable, so even the evaluation cannot be independently replayed or audited for overlap.

The preservation reward has unresolved false-positive, false-negative, and reward-hacking risks. As a curator inference, a model might preserve a textual diff while disabling, duplicating, relocating, or semantically overriding the user's intended behavior; conversely, a necessary conflict resolution could look like destructive reversion. Without a preservation detector, semantic tests, calibration, thresholds, conflict taxonomy, and error audit, the positive signal's behavioral meaning is unknown.

Conflicting edits can vary from independent additions to direct semantic contradictions. The report does not disclose how the user model selects files, whether changes arrive before or during tool actions, how merge conflicts are represented, whether the agent sees provenance labels, or which conflict types are rewarded. This prevents judging whether the intervention generalizes to realistic collaborative development rather than a narrow synthetic pattern.

The report's sandboxes, configurable network policy, deployment monitoring, Preparedness classifications, and cyber safeguards are deployment evidence. They do not establish the training environment, data rights, or reward design. Context compaction and long-horizon evaluation budgets likewise do not reveal the length or state representation of destructive-action training episodes.

Narrow non-overlap and held-out statements apply only to named cyber or bio evaluations. There is no global code-repository decontamination, benchmark-overlap ledger, train/dev/test manifest, privacy or consent record, source-license ledger, or item-to-checkpoint lineage. Model scores also conflate policy capability, scaffold, inference budget, grader, environment, and deployment choices, so they cannot isolate the effect of the disclosed intervention.
