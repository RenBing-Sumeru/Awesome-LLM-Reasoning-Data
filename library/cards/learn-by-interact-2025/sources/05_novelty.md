The distinctive contribution is not autonomous task generation by itself, environment interaction by itself, or supervised next-action tuning. It is the combination of exhaustive contiguous-subtrajectory extraction with backward instruction construction: the system uses the behavior that actually occurred to write a locally aligned task, including when the original attempt detoured or failed.

That design changes the unit economics of agent-data synthesis. One costly raw interaction can yield many training objects, and imperfect full episodes need not be discarded wholesale. The count expansion from 85,905 episodes to 1,456,808 candidates demonstrates this mechanism at scale. It also makes the record contract more local: the object says “perform the behavior in this segment,” rather than necessarily claiming “solve the seed task.”

The accompanying agentic retrieval is also tailored to sequential environments. Observation-based retrieval looks for examples containing the current observation, while model-based retrieval formulates a query using the task, interaction history, and current state. Their combination can change demonstrations as the agent moves through the environment, unlike fixed instruction-only retrieval.

The paper does not establish novelty for self-instruct, synthetic trajectories, LLM judging, dense retrieval, LoRA, or next-action SFT in isolation. Nor does backward construction make execution feedback equivalent to verification: the committee accepts semantic alignment, while original-goal success and safety may remain unknown.

For reasoning-data curation, the important direction signal is that **goal labeling can be treated as a post hoc data-construction operation**. This makes lineage unusually important. A reusable record should bind the seed document, original instruction, full raw episode, selected span, constructed instruction, construction prompt/model, original-task outcome, committee votes, and final downstream conversion. Without that binding, the novel recovery mechanism is hard to distinguish from label laundering or correlated duplication during audit.
