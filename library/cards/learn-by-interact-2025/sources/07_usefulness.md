For the **Data Construction & Open Release Recipes** track, Learn-by-interact is most useful as a construction pattern and audit target, not as a downloadable dataset.

- **Interaction-to-SFT recipe.** Teams with a controllable environment can seed tasks from operational documentation, retain full state/action logs, derive span-level instructions, and train next-action models. Each derived row should preserve raw-episode and span IDs so related examples remain groupable.
- **Failure-recovery study.** Compare three ledgers: episodes that solve the original task, episodes that fail but yield useful relabeled spans, and episodes rejected entirely. Evaluate both original-goal success and constructed-goal executability to determine whether backward construction recovers skill or merely changes the label.
- **Verifier audit.** Separate mechanical inactivity checks, semantic committee judgments, and environment execution. Release every judge input/output and a human-audited stratified sample, including unanimous accepts, unanimous rejects, and disagreements.
- **Correlation-aware scaling.** Measure gains by unique raw episodes and source documents as well as derived-example count. Split by episode and document before studying sample scaling, because 100,000 overlapping spans are not equivalent to 100,000 independent interactions.
- **Agentic retrieval research.** Evaluate observation-only, model-query, and combined retrieval under fixed token and model-call budgets. Track retrieved span provenance, repeated-example rate, state-match errors, and whether demonstrations expose evaluation answers.
- **Release-contract design.** A credible open implementation would pin source snapshots, environment/container images, prompts, model/API revisions, accepted and rejected records, raw-to-derived lineage, licenses, checksums, and evaluation splits. Paper counts alone are insufficient.

Reuse class: suitable as a conceptual recipe for SFT and agent training, and as a case study in backward labeling and sequential retrieval. Direct corpus reuse is blocked because no official machine-readable data or implementation was found. Preference training, reward-model training, and RL replay are also unsupported because the paper does not release comparison groups, scalar rewards, log probabilities, or policy-run state.
