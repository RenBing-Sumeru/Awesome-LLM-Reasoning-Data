Competitive code-reasoning data needs more than a problem and a plausible-looking solution. A useful record must expose tests that can detect algorithmic and implementation errors, yet many public competition problems provide only sparse public examples and newly synthesized problems have neither trusted solutions nor expected outputs. rStar-Coder asks how to expand difficult programming problems while constructing an executable acceptance signal for the resulting solutions.

The paper begins with 57,215 problems collected from TACO, APPS, CodeContests, CodeContests-Python-Submission, OpenR1 Codeforces, USACO 2011-2023, and IOI 2002-2023. Cross-source deduplication and removal of items without reference solutions leave 37,754 seeds. GPT-4o transforms these seed problem-solution pairs into 1,565,632 candidate problems; test generation, output labeling, and agreement filtering retain 380,560 synthetic problems. Table 1 reports 418,314 seed-plus-synthetic problems, summarized elsewhere as 418K, and the paper reports a 580K long-reasoning SFT mixture.

The data object is configuration-specific rather than one uniform row. SFT exports pair questions with QWQ-32B long-reasoning responses and code. RL-oriented exports separate questions from input-output tests. Seed records may include starter code, verification booleans, and test provenance flags; synthetic SFT rows preserve the seed question and platform-level source but lack a `question_id` for a direct join to testcases.

The work reaches L4 because the final NeurIPS paper and appendix, five-config Hugging Face release, and supplemental construction scripts are public. It remains `partial`: the public counts do not reconstruct the paper mixture, source-level rights and original URLs are missing, and the supplemental archive does not run end to end as shipped.
