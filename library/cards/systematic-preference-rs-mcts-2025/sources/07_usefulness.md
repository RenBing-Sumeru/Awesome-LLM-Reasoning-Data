For the assigned Data Construction and Open-Release Recipes track, this paper supplies a useful template for auditing preference datasets as constructed objects. A release inspired by it should identify the source prompt, generated constrained prompt, constraint IDs and keyword arguments, every candidate response, per-constraint outcomes, aggregate score, chosen/rejected decision, and pair reuse. MCTS records additionally need parent and sibling identifiers, shared-prefix boundaries, actions, token log-probabilities, visits, Q-values, rollout rewards, self-evaluations, terminal reasons, and selection history.

The study is also useful for designing matched ablations. Keep pair count and unique-prompt count fixed; vary score margin separately from absolute quality; distinguish prompt difficulty from low pair yield; and compare independent RS candidates with shared-prefix MCTS siblings under explicit compute budgets. Preserve unselected responses and ineligible sibling pairs, because they reveal selection pressure and enable alternative pairing rules.

Use its findings as hypotheses for future curation: moderate prompt difficulty may outperform maximal difficulty, mixed contrast can sometimes help, and RS gains may saturate as N increases. Do not generalize these claims beyond the tested 8B model and verifiable constraints without new evidence. Do not claim an MCTS benefit without matching data quantity, prompt coverage, verifier contract, and compute, and do not treat downstream benchmark gains as proof that the preference records are high quality.

Because no reusable artifact was confirmed, this Card is a recipe and audit checklist, not a training recommendation that can be executed directly. Reuse requires a new implementation plus independently declared upstream terms, release licenses, split manifests, and provenance.
