The main novelty is experimental control over preference-data structure rather than a new preference optimizer. The same synthetic constraint environment supplies a graded verifier, and the study varies shared-prefix structure, chosen/rejected correctness, score margin, prompt difficulty, and sampling scale while holding training-pair and unique-prompt counts fixed for comparisons. This makes data curation itself the experimental object.

RS and MCTS serve as contrasting data generators. RS offers efficient independent sampling and tends to yield more high-contrast pairs. MCTS spends more compute to create sibling pairs with a common prefix, yields more low-contrast pairs, and can create many pairs from its tree structure. The study therefore links search topology to the structure and yield of the resulting preference data instead of treating tree search only as an inference technique.

A second important design choice is reward separation: MCTS may use a mixed verifier/self-evaluation signal to decide where to search, yet pair acceptance is rechecked with the same code verifier used for RS. That separation improves the fairness of the comparison, but it does not make the self-evaluation calibrated or the final verifier comprehensive. The contribution is a controlled recipe and ablation study, not an open data release or a general theory of human preference.
