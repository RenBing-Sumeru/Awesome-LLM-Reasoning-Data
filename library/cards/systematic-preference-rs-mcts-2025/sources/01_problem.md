Preference-learning results depend not only on the optimizer but also on the data object presented to it. A chosen/rejected pair can differ in absolute quality, score margin, prompt difficulty, sampling budget, and whether the two responses share a prefix. When several of these factors change together, a downstream gain cannot be attributed cleanly to one data-design choice.

This paper creates a controlled instruction-following substrate in which response quality can be checked against 23 programmatic constraints. It then compares two automatic pair-construction procedures: rejection sampling (RS), which selects independently generated full responses, and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), which selects sibling branches that share a partial-response prefix. The central question is not whether MCTS is universally better, but what shared prefixes, pair contrast, absolute response quality, prompt difficulty, and sampling scale contribute under matched training-data sizes.

For the Data Construction and Open-Release Recipes track, the audit question is equally important: which prompts, verifier decisions, candidate responses, tree states, rewards, and selection records exist conceptually, which hyperparameters are reported, and which reusable artifacts are actually open.
