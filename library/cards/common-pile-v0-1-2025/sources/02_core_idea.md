The paper's core move is to treat collection and curation of public-domain and openly licensed text as a reproducible dataset release rather than an opaque pretraining mixture. It reports 30 sources across varied domains and releases the Common Pile v0.1, creation code, training mixture, and checkpoints for its Comma v0.1 validation models.

For this atlas, the central data object is not a model score but a provenance-bearing document record: source, asserted rights status, processing path, and position in a mixture. The feedback contract is unknown because the release is text for pretraining rather than labeled reasoning supervision. The closest comparisons are datasheets, provenance initiatives, and web-corpus releases; the Card's category rationale is that its audit questions generalize to prompts, traces, answers, and feedback data used later in post-training.
