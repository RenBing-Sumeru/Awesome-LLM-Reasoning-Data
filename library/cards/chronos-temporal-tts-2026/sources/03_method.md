For each generated trajectory, Chronos derives a token-level temporal signal from the top-k next-token probabilities. It focuses on a final token window and passes the sequence through projection layers, parallel convolutions with several kernel lengths, residual multi-scale blocks, and a classifier trained against final-answer correctness. Short filters model local confidence fluctuations; longer filters model dependencies across larger portions of the trace.

At deployment, many candidate trajectories are sampled for a prompt. Chronos scores them, keeps the highest-scored proportion, and weights each retained answer in a majority-voting rule. The paper trains on AIME problems from 2000–2023 and evaluates in-domain and out-of-domain performance on AIME25, HMMT25, and GPQA-Diamond. The resulting decision record connects a generated path, its temporal confidence pattern, a learned score, and its final vote.
