Parallel test-time scaling improves reasoning by sampling many independent trajectories, but majority voting gives every completed path the same influence. A frequent answer can therefore win even when it is supported mainly by shallow, uncertain, or locally flawed reasoning. Token-confidence heuristics improve on uniform voting, yet commonly collapse a sequence into one average number and ignore where uncertainty occurs in the reasoning process.

The paper asks how a system can estimate the quality of an entire reasoning trajectory before aggregation while keeping the scorer lightweight. Its premise is that reasoning validity evolves over time: a local confidence collapse or late inconsistency can matter more than a globally averaged token statistic. The desired decision is to allocate greater voting weight to credible paths, not merely to the most common answer.
