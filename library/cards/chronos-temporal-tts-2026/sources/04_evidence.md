The official paper evaluates DeepSeek-1.5B, Qwen3-4B, and DeepSeek-8B across AIME25, HMMT25, and GPQA-Diamond. It reports consistent gains over Pass@1, uniform majority voting, and a token-confidence baseline. For Qwen3-4B-Thinking on HMMT25, Chronos@128 reaches 74.38 percent accuracy, compared with 55.42 percent for Pass@1 and 60.62 percent for majority voting at 128 samples; the paper reports only a 0.0005 percent increase in inference FLOPs.

These results support the narrower claim that temporal trajectory scoring can make a fixed multi-sample budget more useful. The evidence is based on final-answer correctness labels and the paper’s selected task distribution, not direct proof that every confidence fluctuation corresponds to a logical error. Review should include the effect of the tail window, retention ratio, scorer training domain, and the cost of obtaining token probabilities on the intended inference stack.
