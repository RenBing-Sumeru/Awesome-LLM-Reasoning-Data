Chronos assumes that token-probability dynamics contain transferable evidence of final correctness; this may be weaker for models with poorly calibrated logits, for tool-mediated reasoning, or for tasks where a correct answer has an unusual surface form. Its scorer is trained from terminal labels, so it can learn correlations that do not localize the actual faulty step. The tail-window length, top-k signal, retention ratio, and sampling model are all operational choices that can change results. Although the scorer is lightweight, token probabilities must be accessible, excluding text-only closed model APIs.
