Correctness is limited to final-answer agreement with the released answer key. It does not verify intermediate reasoning, detect lucky guesses, or prove robustness outside the exam distribution. Public release makes contamination a central risk for modern models, especially for older exams and widely mirrored benchmark files. Some tasks require language-specific parsing or normalization, so evaluator bugs can move scores. License and redistribution terms should be checked at the artifact level because the benchmark aggregates exam material from multiple sources.
