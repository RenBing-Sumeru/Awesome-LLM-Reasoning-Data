Correctness is relative to the encoded formal statement. A checker-accepted proof establishes the theorem as formalized, not automatic semantic fidelity to the original Putnam wording. Formalization errors, library choices, type encodings, and factored-solution design can change the task. The current official sources also expose version drift: arXiv v2 and the project/repository do not use the same aggregate formalization count.

The benchmark is public, with no stable hidden split found in the checked pages. The authors discourage publishing proofs to reduce contamination, but public statements and future proof submissions can still leak into training data. Scores do not measure proof readability, human mathematical insight, or robustness to equivalent theorem encodings. Reuse must pin licenses: Apache-2.0 for Lean/Isabelle artifacts, MIT for Coq, and separate permission context for informal Putnam statements.
