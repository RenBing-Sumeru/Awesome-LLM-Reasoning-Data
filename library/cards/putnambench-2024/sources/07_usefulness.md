Use PutnamBench as a recipe for formal-math evaluation surfaces where the row-level feedback is a verifier verdict. A reusable schema should keep the original problem id, language, theorem statement, factored-solution mode, proof-assistant version, library revision, prompt/scaffold, attempt budget, timeout, candidate proof, checker output, and final accepted/rejected status.

For the atlas, the card is useful because it shows how a public benchmark can still expose a strong feedback contract while carrying contamination risk. It is best reused for evaluation, verifier design, benchmark-hardness analysis, and proof-search diagnostics. Training reuse should be treated separately: collect trajectories and failed attempts explicitly, record whether formal proofs are public, and avoid mixing answer-given and answer-finding tasks under one score.
