The prior baseline is either informal competition-math evaluation, where final answers can be checked but proof validity is opaque, or formal theorem-proving benchmarks with narrower domains and language coverage. PutnamBench changes the benchmark object by encoding a historically difficult undergraduate competition corpus in multiple proof assistants and making proof-assistant acceptance the feedback contract.

The direction signal is strong for post-training reasoning data because it separates theorem statement, candidate proof, checker result, language substrate, and contamination policy. What is not new: proof assistants, Putnam problems, and compiler/kernel checking are established ingredients. The quality signal is the public maintained artifact plus hard reported baselines. Before reuse, inspect the arXiv/project count drift, exact language subset, license per language, whether informal MAA statements are needed, and whether proof publication or training use would pollute future evaluations.
