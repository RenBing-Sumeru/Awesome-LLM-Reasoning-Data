The authors define the Refinement Gap as selfRef@k minus maj@k: the improvement of a candidate-conditioned generative refinement pass over majority voting on exactly the same k solutions. Across instruction and thinking-model families from 1.5B to 72B, the gap rises with model scale and is only weakly related to ordinary pass@1 ability. This suggests that a model can be a capable direct solver yet remain a poor synthesizer of conflicting proposals.

Generative Self-Refinement transfers this synthesis behavior from high-gap teachers into a student. The student is trained jointly on direct solutions, so it can produce useful candidates, and on refined targets conditioned on its own candidate-like solutions, so it can repair and combine them. At inference it is still one model: it samples k attempts and then produces a candidate-conditioned final answer. The design makes the extra computation generative rather than merely selective.
