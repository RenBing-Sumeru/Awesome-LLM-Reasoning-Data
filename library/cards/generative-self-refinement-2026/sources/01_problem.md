Parallel test-time scaling normally samples several solutions and then selects one by majority vote or by a reward model. These procedures can exploit a correct proposal if it is present, but cannot construct a correct solution when every sampled candidate is flawed. Sequential self-refinement is not a complete remedy: smaller models can fail to criticize their own reasoning and may degrade with additional reflection, so more tokens do not necessarily create a useful correction process.

The paper asks whether refinement is a distinct capability beyond candidate coverage and how it can be made available to smaller models. A valid comparison must hold the candidate pool fixed, because otherwise a self-refinement gain can be mistaken for the ordinary benefit of sampling more answers. The target inference behavior is to read several imperfect attempts, diagnose their partial insights and errors, and generate a new final solution that is better than any selection rule can obtain from the same set.
