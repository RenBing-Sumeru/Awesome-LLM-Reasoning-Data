The gains are demonstrated primarily on mathematical exact-answer tasks and depend on expensive teacher-generated direct and refinement targets. The candidate-conditioned prompt consumes additional context and one extra generation after sampling, so it may lose its advantage under strict latency or context limits. A high Refinement Gap does not guarantee factual grounding, tool correctness, or safety in open-ended settings, and the all-wrong-candidate result is still a low-probability recovery rather than a reliable replacement for external verification.
