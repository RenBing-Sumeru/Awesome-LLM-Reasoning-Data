For each OpenMathReasoning problem, the student supplies four candidate responses to match its future test-time distribution. A high-gap teacher receives the question plus enumerated candidates and generates a refined target; these triples form the refinement data. The pipeline deliberately keeps difficult low-quality pools, including cases with zero correct candidates, because a training set containing only easy agreement cases would teach selection rather than correction. The teacher also supplies a direct target for the original question.

The final student minimizes an equal-weight combination of direct-solving and refinement negative log likelihood. The paper retains 184,000 instances of each type and fine-tunes Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, then runs the unified model in generate-then-refine mode. Main comparisons use four candidates and include majority voting, scalar and reasoning reward-model Best-of-N, and an 8B synthesizer. Evaluations cover five mathematics benchmarks and repeat AIME runs 32 times, with an additional input-scaling analysis from two through ten candidates.
