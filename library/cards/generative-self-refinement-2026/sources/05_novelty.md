The central novelty is to isolate refinement from sampling. Majority voting is a deterministic function of the same candidates, so the Refinement Gap makes a stronger claim than comparing a refined model with pass@1 or with a different candidate budget. It shows that parallel candidates can be used as evidence for a new solution, not merely as a pool from which to extract the least bad answer.

The hybrid objective is also materially different from distilling only polished solutions or only corrections. Direct-solving training improves the quality of the candidates that create refinement headroom, while refinement training teaches the model how to use partial and contradictory evidence. The method does not introduce a new external reward model or a stronger verifier; it changes the inference consumer itself into a learned candidate-conditioned generator, which is why it belongs to test-time scaling rather than ordinary instruction tuning.
