Multimodal judges usually choose a better response using one holistic preference or fixed rubric, while real users may care simultaneously about visual faithfulness, reasoning, concision, style, and safety and may change priorities across tasks. A judge can be accurate overall yet ignore requested criteria, fail to adapt when criteria switch, or collapse conflicting dimensions into one preference. The paper asks how multi-criterion human-annotated response pairs can measure fine-grained, switchable, and conflict-aware criterion following.
