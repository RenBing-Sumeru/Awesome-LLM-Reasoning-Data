The paper evaluates 25 open and proprietary LMMs. Even leading proprietary models struggle to maintain pluralistic criterion adherence, especially on open-ended tasks, while open models lag further under criterion switching. Critic fine-tuning with holistic judgments improves visual grounding but does not reliably transfer to criterion-level decisions. Reasoning fine-tuning and test-time scaling also fail to remove boundary inconsistencies. These comparisons support the benchmark’s distinction from holistic preference, although the validity of conflict labels still depends on how human criteria are defined.
