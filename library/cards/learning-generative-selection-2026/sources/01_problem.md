Parallel sampling can raise the probability that at least one candidate solves a reasoning problem, but the system must still identify which candidate to return. Majority voting can favor a repeated wrong answer, independent reward scores do not compare candidates jointly, and prompted generative selection is weak in small models. This paper asks whether a 1.7B reasoning model can learn GenSelect directly through RL. Its data object is a selection episode rather than a newly synthesized solution: a math or code problem, an enumerated set of two to sixteen Qwen3-1.7B candidate solutions, precomputed correctness labels, the selector's comparative reasoning, a final `Judgment [IDX]`, and a scalar reward determined by the label of the chosen candidate. It belongs in the rollout/search/test-time trace track because candidate generation and selector rollout are separate test-time-compute stages whose quality and costs must be audited independently.
