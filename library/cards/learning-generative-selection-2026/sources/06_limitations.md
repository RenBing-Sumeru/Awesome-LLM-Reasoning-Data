Every training selection prompt contains at least one verified-correct candidate, so the method does not learn abstention, candidate rejection, or recovery when all candidates are wrong. Math-Verify-based symbolic checking may mishandle equivalent forms or malformed outputs; unit tests can miss bugs, reject otherwise valid programs through runtime or formatting failures, and provide a noisier reward. Filtering to below-50%-pass problems and capping correct candidates at 50% changes the natural Best-of-N distribution, while length filtering can preferentially remove complex solutions. The paper does not disclose the code-training count, final selection-task count, candidate-generation sampling parameters, data manifest, overlap/decontamination procedure, licenses, seeds, compute totals, or item-level failures. No official code, constructed dataset, selector checkpoint, or rollout release was verified. Code gains are smaller and noisier than math gains, and benchmark performance is not evidence that the underlying records are accurate or reusable.
