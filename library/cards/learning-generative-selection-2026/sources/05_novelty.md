GenSelect itself predates this paper, as do Best-of-N sampling, majority voting, automatic math/code verification, and DAPO. The paper's specific change is to train a small GenSelect policy with RL over deliberately mixed correct and incorrect candidate sets. Selection rather than synthesis makes the reward cheap to compute because all candidate labels are known before the selector rollout; the policy may reason jointly across candidates but must return one existing index. The difficulty controls—source problems below 50% pass rate, at least one correct candidate, and a 50% cap on correct candidates—turn candidate-pool composition into an explicit construction variable. This is a useful direction signal for test-time aggregation, but the reported cross-generator transfer and score gains do not prove that the hidden constructed dataset is complete, representative, or reusable.
