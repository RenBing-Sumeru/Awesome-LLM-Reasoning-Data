The official dataset and paper agree on 207,489 trajectories over nine languages. The four mode counts sum exactly to that total. The dataset card's outcome table sums to 65,244 resolved, 95,487 unresolved, and 46,758 unknown trajectories. Therefore, 65,244/207,489 is 31.4%. The paper's approximately 40.6% pass rate is reproducible only when unknown outcomes are excluded: 65,244/(65,244+95,487) = 40.6%. This denominator distinction matters for sampling and audit.

The paper reports concrete trajectory-shape differences. In OpenHands, non-thinking and thinking modes average 94.08 and 58.22 assistant turns per trajectory; in SWE-agent they average 130.70 and 74.82. Thinking traces use more tokens per turn, so fewer turns should not be read as lower total compute or better semantic quality without further evidence.

Table 5 provides the most direct evidence for retaining non-success data in the authors' training setup. Moving from resolved-only to the full corpus changes SWE-bench Verified scores from 55.3 to 58.1 in thinking mode and 57.7 to 59.8 in non-thinking mode; SWE-bench Multilingual changes from 40.5 to 47.6 and from 49.6 to 57.1. The full dual-mode model's best reported results are 61.7% on SWE-bench Verified, 57.1% on SWE-bench Multilingual, and 36.8% on SWE-bench Pro. These are downstream ablations, not proof that unresolved or unknown rows are individually correct, safe, or non-contaminating.

Artifact evidence is stronger for availability than for replay. The HF Viewer reports 207,489 rows, 18,338,420,390 bytes of Parquet data, ten top-level fields, four completed source-mode splits, and no gated access. Revision `9c0e4579a4ee0effa3e5f7a552494a045f29377d` was current when checked. However, the public schema does not expose container hashes, task commits, test logs, teacher decoding settings, or row-level rejection reasons.
