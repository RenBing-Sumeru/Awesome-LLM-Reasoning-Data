Outcome semantics are coarse. `resolved=0` says that the patch did not resolve the task under the harness, while `-1` says outcome information is unavailable; neither identifies whether failure came from reasoning, tools, tests, infrastructure, or missing evaluation. Unit tests can be incomplete or flaky, and the release omits per-row test logs and commands. The 40.6% pass rate also excludes unknown outcomes and should not be quoted as a fraction of all 207,489 rows.

Exact replay is under-specified. Rows expose `instance_id`, repository, source-dataset name, trajectory, tools, and patches, but not explicit canonical PR URLs, base/head commit SHAs, container digests, harness versions, seeds, teacher decoding, retries, or rejection reasons. The official collection currently lacks pipeline code and an `OPEN-SWE-AGENT` checkpoint. The paper also reports framework overfitting and 5-10% looping behavior for the evaluated model in MOpenHands.

Release rights and privacy need independent review. The dataset card applies CC BY 4.0 and source repositories are filtered by permissive repository-license labels, but that does not separately establish rights for issue/PR prose, patch fragments, dependencies, teacher outputs, or retained reasoning. The ethics statement says automated filters redact PII and credentials, yet no detector specification or residual-risk measurement is provided. Long tool logs and explicit reasoning can still carry secrets, personal data, insecure commands, or teacher-specific vulnerabilities.

No broad decontamination analysis is reported between the 20,000 source tasks and SWE-bench Verified, Multilingual, or Pro. `TrajectoryScanner` addresses git-history shortcuts inside a run, not train–evaluation overlap. Because the public configurations are source modes rather than held-out splits, reusers must construct their own leakage-resistant partitions before evaluation.
