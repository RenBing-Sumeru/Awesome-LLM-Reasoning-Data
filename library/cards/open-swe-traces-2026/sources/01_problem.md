Open-SWE-Traces addresses a data bottleneck between software-agent evaluation and training. Repository-level agents act over long episodes: they read issue text, inspect a codebase, call shell or editing tools, observe environment responses, propose a patch, and receive an executable outcome. Public corpora often expose tasks or final patches without the complete interaction history needed to distill those behaviors, and filtering to successes alone removes evidence about unproductive search and recovery.

The primary source is arXiv v1, submitted on 14 June 2026, with an official NVIDIA Hugging Face release. The released data object is a full software-engineering agent episode, not merely an issue–patch pair. It connects a SWE-rebench-V2 task and repository to a teacher-generated conversation/tool trajectory, model and reference patches, and a three-valued environmental outcome. This makes the work relevant to the atlas's data-construction and open-release track: the central contribution is a published recipe and corpus for turning executable pull-request tasks into multilingual distillation records.

The decision boundary is important. Open-SWE-Traces is not a new task benchmark, a learned reward model, or a proof that every retained trajectory is high quality. Its evaluation experiments test whether the released mixture can train software agents under the authors' setup; benchmark scores are evidence about that setup, not independent validation of every row.
