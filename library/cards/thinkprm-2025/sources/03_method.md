Source and raw candidates. The paper samples problem-prefix pairs from the PRM800K train split, which derives from MATH and contains model-generated solutions with human step labels. QwQ-32B-Preview receives the problem, the multi-step prefix, and a fixed instruction to critique each step, emit a boxed label, and stop after the first incorrect step. Four teacher chains are sampled per prefix; the exact sampling temperature and seed ledger are not disclosed in the rendered paper or dataset card.

Filtering and released object. Malformed or missing boxed decisions are discarded, as are chains whose emitted step judgments disagree with any corresponding PRM800K label or exceed 4,096 tokens. Accepted text is normalized by standardizing boxed notation, removing content after the final verification decision, and adding think delimiters. Roughly 20% of raw chains passed the format and label-agreement checks, so about 5,000 generations were needed to obtain 1,000 retained rows. The release contains one 1,000-row train split with `problem`, `prefix`, `cot`, `prefix_steps`, `gt_step_labels`, and `prefix_label`; rejected chains are not included.

Training. The authors train generative PRMs based on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B, 7B, and 14B models and QwQ-32B-Preview using next-token supervised finetuning over complete verification chains. The 1.5B and 7B models use full finetuning for three epochs; 14B variants use three-epoch LoRA and QwQ uses five-epoch LoRA. The paper reports effective batch sizes of 32 for 1.5B/7B and 16 for LoRA runs, plus single-A100 or four-A6000 training setups, but several learning rates and LoRA parameters are missing from the rendered source.

Score extraction and search. At evaluation, ThinkPRM generates a verification chain up to 8,192 tokens, the implementation forces the final correctness query, and the resulting affirmative probability is used as the prefix score. Weighted-majority Best-of-N sums verifier scores for solutions that share a final answer; AIME 2024 directly ranks solutions by verifier score. Guided beam search samples candidate next steps, scores their prefixes, retains the top expansions, and repeats for at most 20 steps in the reported setup.

Verifier scaling and artifacts. Parallel scaling independently samples multiple verification CoTs and averages scores. Sequential scaling uses up to four rounds with different re-check prompts; the repository notes that parallel and sequential modes are not combined. Official artifacts include code and experiment recipes, the public 1K dataset, and ThinkPRM-1.5B/7B/14B model releases. The paper studies 65K process-filtered and 128K outcome-filtered expansions, but those datasets were not verified as part of the public 1K release.
