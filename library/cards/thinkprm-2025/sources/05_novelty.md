The prior baseline is either a discriminative PRM trained on step-label pairs or an untuned LLM-as-a-Judge that verbalizes a decision. The former exposes a scalar but not an audit trace and consumes many labels; the latter exposes a rationale but may not reliably detect errors or emit parseable decisions.

ThinkPRM's specific change is to distill verifier behavior through long, process-filtered synthetic rationales while using human labels only as rejection criteria. This changes the reusable object from a prefix-label pair to a prefix-verification-CoT-label record and lets the verifier spend variable generation compute before producing the scalar score consumed by selection or search.

The direction signal for rollout/search curation is that verifier traces themselves become a test-time data stream. Independent verifier samples, sequential revisions, score aggregation, and beam decisions should therefore be logged alongside candidate solution rollouts. The work also separates process-label agreement from outcome-only filtering and shows that a larger outcome-filtered pool need not produce the better verifier.

What is not new remains important. The paper does not introduce PRM800K, MATH, SFT, LoRA, rejection sampling, generative reward modeling, Best-of-N, beam search, or self-consistency. It does not prove generated critiques are faithful explanations, provide a calibrated universal reward, or show that more verifier tokens always help. Reuse should compare matched discriminative, untuned judge, short-CoT, outcome-filtered, random/majority, and compute-matched controls.
