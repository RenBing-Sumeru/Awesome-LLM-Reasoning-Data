ThinkPRM changes a process verifier from a prefix-to-scalar classifier into a verbalized judge. Given a math problem and solution prefix, the model generates a long verification CoT that critiques steps in order, emits a boxed `correct` or `incorrect` judgment for each reviewed step, and then produces a final solution-correctness decision. The final decision probability becomes a prefix score; the rationale is an observable verification trace, not a proof.

The supervision recipe uses rejection sampling rather than gold rationales. QwQ-32B-Preview samples four verification chains for each PRM800K-train prefix. A chain is retained only when every required boxed judgment is parseable, every generated judgment agrees with the corresponding human PRM800K step label, the chain is no longer than 4,096 tokens, and the final 1K set remains roughly balanced by prefix correctness. Humans supervise the decision boundary through labels, but they do not validate the wording or faithfulness of the generated critique.

Verifier compute can then scale in two ways. Parallel scaling samples K independent verification CoTs and averages their prefix scores. Sequential scaling extends one verification through distinct prompts such as `Let me double check`, `Let's verify again`, and `Did I miss something?`, asking the model to revise its prior judgment. Best-of-N uses verifier-weighted answer voting, while guided beam search scores candidate partial solutions before expansion.

The feedback contract is judgment-required and model-conditioned. A high score means the trained generative verifier assigns high probability to correctness after its own verification process; it is not a programmatic correctness check, an environment reward, or a model-independent label. Repeated thinking can expose alternative checks, but correlated errors, premature commitment, and step-label interference remain possible.
