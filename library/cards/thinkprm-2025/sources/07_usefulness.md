For `rollout_search_test_time_trace_data`, ThinkPRM provides a concrete example of a second model generating traces that govern the fate of first-model rollouts. Curators should treat the candidate solution, verification CoT, parsed labels, scalar score, aggregation, and search action as separate linked objects rather than collapsing them into one reward number.

An auditable training row should preserve the upstream PRM800K ID and revision, MATH source/split, problem, full solution, selected prefix boundary, human step labels and annotator metadata, teacher checkpoint, all four raw verification candidates, prompt, temperature, seed, token limit, parse result, per-step generated labels, rejection reason, selected CoT, and content hashes. The public six fields retain useful content but not this complete lineage.

An inference record should add the candidate-generator checkpoint and decoding settings, candidate/answer ID, verifier checkpoint and tokenizer, rendered prompt, verification text, stop condition, final forced-decode probabilities, prefix score, parallel sample index or sequential round, trigger phrase, latency, token count, and failure flags. A decision record should retain all candidates, answer grouping, score aggregation, beam state, pruned paths, selected path, and tie-breaking policy.

The 1K release can support verifier SFT reproduction attempts, rationale-versus-label audits, parser robustness studies, score calibration experiments, and controlled re-scoring of fixed candidate pools. It should not be treated as a programmatically verified proof corpus or as evidence that generated critiques are universally correct. Independent mathematical checking is needed wherever wrong process feedback is costly.

For atlas readers, the work clarifies that scaling candidate rollouts and scaling verifier reasoning are distinct compute axes. Benchmark gains support the usefulness of the studied verifier pipeline, while label imbalance, rationale faithfulness, contamination, licensing, and full decision lineage remain independent audit obligations.
