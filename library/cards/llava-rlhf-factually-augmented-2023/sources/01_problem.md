Vision-language assistants can give fluent answers that are not supported by the image. This paper studies that hallucination boundary in image-grounded dialogue: a response is preferred when it is less hallucinated, and, when both are grounded, more helpful.

The decision surface is an image, conversation context, and two candidate responses. LLaVA-RLHF turns that comparison into reward-model supervision and evaluates whether the resulting assistant reduces hallucination on open-ended image questions.
