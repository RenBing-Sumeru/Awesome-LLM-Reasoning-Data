Existing long-memory datasets usually come from chat logs, synthetic user events, or state updates and focus on “what was remembered.” KnowMe-Bench jointly preserves actions, contexts, and inner interpretations from autobiographical narratives and uses mnestic realignment to separate the time of recall from the time of the recalled event. Its evaluation target expands from factual retrieval to evidence-grounded person-model inference. The novelty is not merely longer context, but the combination of high-density experience representation, flashback-aware temporal structure, and three-tier auditable rubrics.
