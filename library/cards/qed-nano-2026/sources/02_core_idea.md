QED-Nano combines three post-training stages. First, DeepSeek-Math-V2 supplies long natural-language proof traces for a separate FineProofs-SFT release and QED-Nano-SFT initialization. Second, the model is optimized with asynchronous streaming GRPO using problem-specific rubric rewards on filtered FineProofs-RL prompts. Third, Reasoning Cache turns one very long proof attempt into a sequence of partial attempts and compact textual state summaries, allowing the policy to continue from an explicit account of progress.

The reusable FineProofs-RL record is deliberately smaller than that pipeline. Each row has eight fields: `problem`, `source`, `rubrics`, `scores`, `rewards`, `num_rewards`, `reward_mean`, and `reward_std`. The pinned release contains 5,227 unique rows in one `train` split, with 3,794 labeled `aops` and 1,433 labeled `olympiads`. Every released reward equals its integer score divided by seven. No proof, reasoning, message, grader-error, or assessment-text field is present.

The feedback contract is judgment-based outcome reward. Gemini 3 Pro writes a 0-7 rubric with positive checkpoints, zero-credit conditions, and deductions for each problem. GPT-OSS-20B with medium reasoning reads a completed natural-language proof and the rubric, then assigns one integer score. This signal is richer than a bare correct/incorrect label at the problem level, but it remains one scalar outcome for a full proof; the released rubric is not a sequence of verified step labels, and the judge is not a formal proof checker.

The central rollout distinction is between offline annotation and online optimization. Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 generates a nominal 128-attempt population to estimate prompt difficulty. The released arrays preserve those scalar outcomes, although 453 rows have only 62-127 values and one row has 256. Online GRPO instead samples 16 current-policy proofs for each prompt; 64 prompts form a 1,024-sample batch. Reasoning Cache adds three state-summary cycles during training. At test time, the paper compares single-turn generation with several much larger iterative scaffolds.

The contribution is therefore a coupled recipe rather than one monolithic trace release: SFT proof distillation initializes style, score-population statistics define a curriculum, rubric judgments supply RL rewards, textual cache states structure iterative reasoning, and high-budget inference explores further refinements. The audit boundary is equally important: FineProofs-RL exposes the prompts, rubrics, and scalar populations but not the generations or proof-specific feedback needed to reconstruct those populations.
