The prompt pool begins with text-only Olympiad-style problems from AI-MO/aops and AI-MO/olympiads. The construction removes items that depend on images, are trivial or ill-posed, are very short or purely computational, or come from easier contests. GPT-5-Nano supplies an additional quality pass. All 2025 competition problems are excluded, and prompts are fuzzy-matched against IMO-ProofBench and ProofBench before exact problem-text deduplication. The release does not include the fuzzy-match threshold, candidate pairs, decision log, or rejected-prompt manifest.

Gemini 3 Pro then creates a task-specific 0-7 grading scheme. The rubric expresses checkpoints that should earn credit, conditions that should receive zero credit, and deductions. For offline difficulty annotation, Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 generates nominally 128 independent proof attempts per problem, and GPT-OSS-20B-medium grades each attempt. The retained FineProofs-RL row stores the rubric, score array, score/7 reward array, count, mean, and standard deviation, but drops every proof attempt and every proof-specific grading explanation.

The pinned parquet contains 5,227 rows. Direct inspection found 4,773 rows with exactly 128 scores, 453 with 62-127, and one with 256. More specifically, the shorter counts range from 62 to 127 and are concentrated at 126-127. All rows have matching `num_rewards`, score-list length, and reward-list length; no field is null, all rewards match score/7, and recomputed means agree within `1e-6`. These integrity checks validate the stored arrays, not the mathematical validity of the unseen proofs.

Curriculum selection separates the offline population from online RL. The paper removes problems on which the base model has pass@1 at least 0.7 and problems with no successful difficulty rollout. The FineProofs-RL card additionally specifies reward mean at most 0.7 and reward standard deviation above 0.08. Retained prompts can still have zero-score attempts, but all-failure prompts are excluded. The reason for non-128 rows and the single 256-row population is undisclosed.

Online training uses asynchronous streaming GRPO. For each selected problem, the current policy generates 16 long proofs. A batch contains 64 problems and therefore 1,024 samples. GPT-OSS-20B-medium assigns the full-proof rubric score. The reported optimizer allows at most five gradient steps of off-policy lag, uses entropy coefficient 0.0001, constant learning rate `1e-6`, and no KL regularization. Actor temperature is 0.8. Released configs cap generation at 49,152 tokens; the paper describes the response limit as roughly 50,000 tokens.

Reasoning Cache changes the rollout substrate. During training, a frozen Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 summarizer compresses partial proof progress into a textual state. The next rollout conditions on the original problem plus that summary, and the released configuration uses three reasoning/summarization cycles. Sixteen candidate proofs are sampled from each cache state before rollouts are subsampled for updates. The summaries and state-aligned proof texts are not part of FineProofs-RL.

The preceding SFT branch is separate. DeepSeek-Math-V2 generates 7,500 proofs over about 4,300 distinct problems for FineProofs-SFT. Structural filtering retains outputs with closed reasoning and proof sections, including some low-scoring or incorrect proofs. This stage teaches proof-writing form but also produces length explosion, repetition, and meandering; early RL from a verbose checkpoint reportedly suffers roughly 60% overflow or truncation.

The model card reports 150 optimization steps, 12 nodes with eight H100 GPUs each across seven generator, four trainer, and one grader node, four days, and 9,216 H100-hours. Test sampling uses top-p 0.95 and top-k 20; the grader uses temperature 1.0 with medium reasoning, and the cache summarizer uses temperature 0.7. At evaluation time, Reasoning Cache, Self-Check, Nomos, RSA, and DeepSeekMath-style scaffolds expand inference beyond a single proof; the largest reported RSA setting averages about 2.05 million tokens per problem.
