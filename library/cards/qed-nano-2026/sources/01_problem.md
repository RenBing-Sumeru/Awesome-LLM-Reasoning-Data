Olympiad-level natural-language theorem proving combines long-horizon generation, sparse outcome feedback, and unusually expensive evaluation. The strongest systems often depend on undisclosed models, data mixtures, judges, and inference scaffolds. QED-Nano asks a narrower reproducible question: can a 4B open model be post-trained to write difficult proofs, and can additional reasoning be organized across training-time rollouts and test-time refinement rather than hidden inside a proprietary pipeline?

For a reasoning-data atlas, the main issue is not the headline model score. It is the relationship among four different objects: proof demonstrations used for SFT, a prompt corpus annotated with rollout-score populations, online RL samples graded by a learned judge, and multi-turn textual states used by Reasoning Cache. These objects have different generators, budgets, retention rules, and reuse boundaries. Treating them as one generic “proof dataset” would hide the feedback contract and overstate what was released.

FineProofs-RL makes this distinction concrete. Its 5,227 train rows contain Olympiad-style problem text, a coarse source label, a problem-specific rubric, arrays of scalar scores and normalized rewards, and summary statistics. They do not contain the Qwen3 proof attempts used to estimate difficulty, the online QED-Nano policy rollouts used for GRPO, or GPT-OSS-20B’s per-proof assessments. The release is therefore a prompt/rubric/score-population dataset, not a corpus of raw proof trajectories.

The work belongs in **Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data** because rollout budgets and selection rules shape both the training curriculum and the inference scaffold. Offline difficulty estimation is nominally based on 128 attempts per problem; online RL uses 16 attempts per problem in 64-prompt batches; Reasoning Cache performs three summarize-and-refine turns during training; and some evaluation scaffolds use roughly two million tokens per problem. These numbers are not interchangeable, and benchmark performance cannot establish the quality or completeness of any one data object.
