The main release limitation is the missing candidate text. FineProofs-RL stores 5,227 prompts, rubrics, and score populations but none of the proof attempts that received those scores. Successful and failed generations are both unavailable. Per-attempt GPT-OSS-20B assessments, extracted errors, grader tokens, expanded prompt inputs, and judge rationales are also absent. A user cannot recreate a score, audit mathematical content, study judge mistakes, or train on the hidden proofs from this dataset.

The nominal offline budget has unexplained exceptions. Of 5,227 rows, 4,773 contain 128 scores, 453 contain 62-127, and one contains 256. Counts and arrays are internally consistent, but neither the paper nor the dataset card explains interruption, retry, duplication, or aggregation behavior. Users should preserve the actual list length and must not assume a fixed 128 candidates per row.

Curriculum filtering changes the visible problem distribution. The online pipeline removes very easy prompts and all prompts with zero successful difficulty rollouts. This retains some failed attempts inside mixed-success score arrays but eliminates the hardest all-failure population. The paper does not release the pre-filter corpus, accepted/rejected manifest, or counterfactual training results needed to measure this selection effect.

The verifier is a learned judge. GPT-OSS-20B-medium may give partial credit and apply a detailed rubric, but it does not formally prove or refute the submitted argument. The paper’s human comparison finds generosity and a substantial discrepancy on IMO 2025 Q2. False acceptance, false rejection, style preference, rubric ambiguity, and reward gaming remain possible. No versioned calibration set, refresh policy, attempt-level confidence, or adversarial judge audit is released.

Source and decontamination lineage is coarse. Each row is labeled only `aops` or `olympiads`; stable source URLs, competition IDs, year metadata, and item-level rights are absent. The paper reports exclusion of 2025 competition problems, fuzzy matching against IMO-ProofBench and ProofBench, and exact deduplication, but not thresholds, candidate pairs, reviewer decisions, or overlap logs. Model-pretraining contamination is not resolved by prompt-level filtering.

Split semantics require care. The main FineProofs-RL repository has one 5,227-row `train` split and no internal validation or test split. FineProofs-RL-test is a separate official dataset repository with a 128-row `test` split loaded by the configs. Users must not merge those identities or infer a random split. No released manifest documents how either set relates to original source competitions at the item level.

Licensing remains partial. FineProofs-RL declares Apache 2.0, and the code and model card also use Apache-2.0; the arXiv paper is CC BY 4.0. Those top-level declarations do not by themselves establish rights to scraped AoPS posts, competition problem statements or solutions, or model-generated derivatives. The checked AI-MO/aops and AI-MO/olympiads cards do not expose a clear repository-level license. Redistribution and production use therefore require source-item rights review.

Reproduction requires immutable pins. The checked evidence uses code commit `02a4699ed380f8e980a8d44c755b8bfd4e310718`, FineProofs-RL revision `61c9ade2e04ab4ee7e257434e0826cb9b737f63e`, FineProofs-RL-test revision `f2f55ba4a125aa0cb2fde0e3e75df9ce8971774a`, and QED-Nano model revision `1016ddef8dd40552b97216ce34e3fece2fffa2bb`. The repository has no immutable release tag, so unpinned links may drift.

The training and inference results are budget-sensitive. SFT creates repetition and excessive length, while early RL reportedly truncates or overflows around 60% of attempts. Reasoning Cache summaries can omit necessary proof state or preserve an incorrect plan. At test time, RSA averages about 2.05 million tokens per problem versus roughly 93.7 thousand for a single turn. Accuracy differences therefore cannot be attributed solely to better post-training data or model parameters.

Unknowns that materially block full audit are the raw offline and online proofs, state-aligned Reasoning-Cache traces and summaries, proof-specific judge output, non-128 count explanation, exact rejected-prompt and decontamination logs, row-level provenance, upstream rights, peer-reviewed venue, and tagged paper-exact release. These gaps justify `status: partial` and L4 bilingual readiness rather than a higher workflow state.
