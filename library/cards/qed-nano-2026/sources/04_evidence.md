The strongest evidence about the release comes from the artifact itself. At revision `61c9ade2e04ab4ee7e257434e0826cb9b737f63e`, FineProofs-RL exposes one 5,227-row `train` parquet with exactly eight columns. Direct inspection found 5,227 unique non-empty problem strings, zero nulls, source counts of 3,794 `aops` and 1,433 `olympiads`, score range 0-7, reward range 0-1, no score/reward normalization mismatches, and no count or mean inconsistencies at the stated tolerance. It also confirmed the absence of proof, reasoning, assessment, error, or trajectory fields.

The paper and pinned configs support the budget distinctions. Offline difficulty scoring uses Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 and nominally 128 judged attempts per problem. Online RL uses 16 current-policy attempts for each of 64 prompts, or 1,024 samples per batch, with generations capped at 49,152 tokens in code and rounded to about 50,000 in the paper. Reasoning-Cache training uses three refinement cycles. FineProofs-RL-test is a separate repository with 128 rows in a `test` split, not a hidden split of the main 5,227-row release.

The authors also validate the judge rather than presenting it as infallible. GPT-OSS-20B-medium is selected through grading benchmarks and a strict ProofBench-style prompt. The paper reports that providing a reference proof did not improve the chosen in-distribution advantage measure, so it was omitted. Human comparison nevertheless finds that the automated grader can be more generous, with a substantial mismatch on IMO 2025 Q2. This evidence supports using the signal as a learned rubric judge, not relabeling it as formal correctness.

The experimental results show that the recipe can train a small model and that iterative scaffolds can raise proof-generation performance. They also show failure behavior: SFT can produce length explosion and repetition, and early RL can spend much of its budget on truncated outputs. Test-time comparisons are highly compute-sensitive. Table 2 reports approximately 93,690 average tokens for a single turn and 2,045,764 for RSA, so gains mix model ability, scaffold design, and additional sampling budget.

What the evidence does **not** establish is equally important. Because no underlying proof attempts or grader assessments are released with the score arrays, no external reader can verify whether a particular 0-7 score is mathematically justified, analyze false positives and false negatives at the attempt level, or reconstruct why a row has fewer than 128 or as many as 256 outcomes. Integrity of the stored statistics is not evidence of proof validity, judge calibration, unbiased curriculum selection, or complete trace release.

The appropriate evidence conclusion is therefore limited: citation identity, official artifact ownership, row schema, counts, top-level release declarations, rollout settings, and the reported training/evaluation procedure are well supported. Reproducibility of individual rewards, exact decontamination decisions, upstream rights, and raw trajectory reuse remain partial.
