Web agents must solve two coupled problems. Locally, they need to map an instruction to the correct interactive element and action in a visually dense page. Across time, they need to preserve intent over a sequence of navigation, typing, filtering, and recovery steps. Data built from static screenshots or synthetic browser rollouts often misses authenticated workflows, dynamic layouts, and the simultaneous pixel, DOM, and action evidence available during real use.

Human demonstrations can cover those states, but a bare action log is insufficient for multimodal training. A reusable step needs the page observation, structural representation, action semantics, grounding target, and its position in the trajectory to refer to the same moment. It also needs an honest success contract: matching a demonstrated coordinate or string is different from proving that the website reached the intended terminal state.

WebChain addresses the collection and training sides together. It asks how to build a large live-web corpus with synchronized visual, structural, and action traces, then how to separate spatial grounding from long-horizon planning during post-training. For this atlas, the central question is not only whether benchmark scores improve, but whether the released object, construction recipe, feedback semantics, rights, and replay requirements are sufficiently explicit for audit and reuse.
