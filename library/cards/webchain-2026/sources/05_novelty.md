The main novelty is the released data object and its scale, not merely the use of human browsing. WebChain synchronizes three views at every action: pixels for visual grounding, HTML/Accessibility structure for semantic localization, and precise action metadata for behavioral supervision. Because the same step remains embedded in a full task trajectory, the object can support both local element selection and long-horizon planning rather than forcing users to choose between screenshot-level and episode-level datasets.

The construction recipe also distinguishes collection from enrichment. Human annotators supply the actual live-site behavior; dense annotations for all visible interactive elements expand spatial supervision; synthetic rationales add a planning-oriented view. Keeping those provenance layers distinct matters, because “human-annotated trajectory” does not mean that every field—especially the Chain-of-Thought-style rationale—is human-authored.

Dual Mid-Training operationalizes that distinction by developing grounding and planning separately before long-chain optimization. Its reported ablations show that spatial initialization and rationale-based SFT have complementary effects on WCB-L. This is a useful recipe-level contribution, but benchmark gains do not certify privacy, task correctness, rights, or the fidelity of individual rationales.

For the open-release-recipe track, WebChain is particularly informative because it combines a high-volume artifact with a concrete pipeline and then exposes a gap between publication language and reusable release conditions: the data is gated under a custom license, raw formats need custom parsing, full-page screenshots are incomplete, and the linked repository is documentation-only at the checked revision.
