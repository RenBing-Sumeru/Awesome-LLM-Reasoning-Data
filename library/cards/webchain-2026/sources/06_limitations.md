The success contract is under-specified. “Human-verified” does not reveal how completion was judged across 428 domains, how disagreements or failed attempts were handled, or whether a reviewer replayed each trajectory. The experimental coordinate-in-box and lexical-coverage rewards check reference-action agreement, not the website's terminal state. Equivalent selectors, coordinates, action orders, keyboard shortcuts, and recovery paths make exact imitation an incomplete correctness criterion.

The synthetic rationale layer has separate epistemic risk. An unnamed VLM writes the rationale after seeing the task, history, current state, and recorded action. It can therefore produce a fluent post-hoc explanation that is not faithful to the human's decision process. The paper does not disclose the VLM, prompt, decoding settings, rejection criteria, or human validation rate, so rationale quality and contamination cannot be independently reproduced.

Privacy and rights remain material. Screenshots, DOM text, Accessibility trees, form fields, typed strings, account state, messages, addresses, search history, payment/shipping context, or session data may expose personal or confidential information. The paper says redaction and review were used and that no PII is released, but no per-record consent/redaction log or false-negative audit is published. The custom dataset license constrains recipients; it does not establish collection or redistribution permission for content from all 428 third-party sites. Site Terms of Service, robots/access policy, copyright, user consent, and authenticated-account provenance remain domain-specific unknowns.

Reproducibility is limited by live-site drift. DOM trees, page layout, localization, personalization, geolocation, authentication, APIs, inventory, and anti-bot behavior change over time. Selectors and coordinates can become stale even when the record was initially valid. Collection timestamps, browser and OS builds, viewport, locale, account state, archived pages, and replay diagnostics are not provided as a complete per-record manifest.

Release packaging has concrete gaps. Access is manually gated and limited by WebChain Academic Research License v1.0; it is not unrestricted open-source reuse. Full-page screenshots are only partially available, raw files need custom parsing, and no single schema spans every artifact. The HF quick-start points to `computer-use-agent-Lab/WebChain`, while the inspected repository is `webagentlab/WebChain`; the LICENSE footer names `Fan-Sicheng/WebChain`. The author-linked GitHub repository claims Apache-2.0 in its README but has no detected license file and no implementation code in its checked tree.

Finally, splits and provenance are incomplete. The release includes high/low test Parquet files and the paper describes a 1,200-step held-out benchmark, but deterministic split construction, deduplication, cross-benchmark decontamination, rejected traces, exact task/domain mixture, and immutable per-file checksums are not documented end to end. Reported benchmark improvements should therefore be treated as experiment evidence, not as proof that the dataset is uniformly correct or safe.
