For `rollout_search_test_time_trace_data`, VisualPRM is a useful two-stage case study. Training labels summarize a 16-rollout search from each prefix, while inference selects among N complete responses. The Card should therefore be used to audit both the label-generation budget and the test-time candidate budget, rather than treating the final `+/-` token or chosen answer as the complete data object.

The released original data can support multimodal PRM training, binary step classification, class-imbalance studies, aggregation ablations, and transfer tests across policy families. VisualPRM-8B can score candidate responses, and VisualProcessBench can evaluate positive/negative step detection. Users should pin the original release revision because v1.1 adds sources/prompts and the v1.1-Raw schema describes a changed dataset.

A stronger reproducible record would preserve upstream source ID and revision, image hash, question and answer, solution-generator checkpoint, initial-solution seed, exact step boundaries and merge map, all 16 continuation texts, extracted answers, checker modes/results, mc_i, binary label, and code/environment revision. For Best-of-N, retain N, policy checkpoint, temperature, every candidate, per-step scores, aggregate score, selected ID, ties, and rejected candidates.

The separate benchmark is valuable for comparing learned PRMs with prompted judges because it includes every step and neutral labels. Reusers should report class-wise F1, per-source results, and calibration, not only one overall number. They should also resolve overlap with training prompts and preserve the benchmark's policy_model and data_source fields.

This package is not evidence that any Monte Carlo label is a verified local proof step. It is better understood as estimated future solvability under one undisclosed generation/verifier stack. Downstream Best-of-N improvements motivate the scorer, but do not by themselves prove that the training dataset is clean, unbiased, licensed end to end, or optimal for another generator.
