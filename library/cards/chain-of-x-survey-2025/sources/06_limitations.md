The taxonomy describes a heterogeneous field, so it is not a controlled experiment that isolates one universal effect of chaining. A chain can be useful as a representation, a prompt, a tool interface, or a planning device, and these uses should not be collapsed into one claim about reasoning ability. Names are also an unreliable shortcut: a method may contain a chain without exposing useful intermediate information, and an apparently different method may share the same underlying decomposition. For implementation details, data, and evaluation protocols, consult the primary papers named in the relevant category. Do not infer reproducibility, faithfulness of explanations, or transfer across tasks from the survey alone; those depend on the individual system and its measurements.
