The survey's new contribution is its joint view of components and tasks. Earlier discussions often begin and end with CoT, whereas this paper treats CoT as one influential starting point for a broader family. Its value is conceptual: when a paper introduces a new “chain,” readers can ask both what the nodes are and why this task needs them. The contribution is not a common benchmark, a new training recipe, or a proof that one chain type is best. Instead, it supplies vocabulary that makes novelty claims more precise. A new method may change the node type, the transition between nodes, the task application, or only the presentation. Readers should identify which of these actually changes before treating a named variant as a distinct reasoning advance.
