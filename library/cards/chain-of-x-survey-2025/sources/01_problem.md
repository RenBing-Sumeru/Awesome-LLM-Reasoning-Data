Chain-of-Thought established the idea that a language model can work through intermediate steps, but later work uses many intermediate components rather than only natural-language thoughts. The authors call this broader family Chain-of-X. It had become hard to compare because papers use different kinds of chain nodes and apply them to different tasks, while similar names can conceal very different designs. The survey therefore helps readers avoid treating a fashionable label as an explanation of mechanism. This COLING 2025 paper is a comprehensive survey, not a new prompting method, model, or data release. Its practical problem is therefore one of orientation: before adopting a chained design, a reader needs to know what each intermediate item represents, what task it supports, and whether the reported benefit belongs to the chain itself or to the surrounding system.
