Verilog code-generation benchmarks can be memorized through pre-training or fine-tuning, making pass@k look like hardware-design ability. This risk has been studied in software but not systematically in RTL, leaving benchmark rankings and industrial adoption hard to trust.

The paper audits VerilogEval and RTLLM with two contamination detectors, then deliberately fine-tunes a controllable model and studies TED filtering. It produces a hardware-domain contamination diagnosis and mitigation trade-off, not a new code generator.
