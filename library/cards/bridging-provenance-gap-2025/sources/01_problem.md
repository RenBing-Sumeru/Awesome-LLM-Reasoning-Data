An “open” training dataset can carry a clean dataset-card license while inheriting different restrictions from webpages, platforms, model outputs, or earlier datasets inside it. Repackaging can break the documentation chain: the final release may name neither the original source nor its terms, creator, consent basis, transformation, or applicable restrictions. A team choosing data for SFT, preference learning, or multimodal training therefore cannot infer source compatibility from one top-level license string.

The problem is not limited to text. Speech and video ecosystems use different hosts, publication customs, tasks, size units, and source platforms, so a text-only inventory cannot establish how data sourcing and restrictions change across modalities. At the same time, ecosystem-level claims about geography or language require a declared definition of “creator” and careful denominators; more represented countries or languages do not necessarily mean a less concentrated distribution.

This work asks how to construct a comparable human audit of public text, speech, and video datasets, trace dataset derivations and source terms, and measure longitudinal sourcing, restriction, and representation trends. For this atlas, the resulting catalogue is an audit layer around reasoning and post-training data—not reasoning supervision, a legal-clearance service, or record-level provenance.
