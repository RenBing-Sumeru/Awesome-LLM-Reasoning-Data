Table 1 is the scale anchor. It reports 3,916 datasets in total: 3,717 text, 95 speech, and 104 video. The audit covers 798 sources, 659 creator organizations, 67 countries, 608 languages, 443 tasks, and 55 licenses. Text contributes 2.1T tokens, while speech and video contribute 775K and 1.13M hours. These are metadata and ecosystem-quantity statistics, not counts of independently verified individual source records.

The main restriction result compares dataset licenses with source terms. The paper reports that only 25%, 33%, and 32% of text, speech, and video datasets are non-commercially licensed, but some non-commercial restriction appears in sources covering 99.8%, 78%, and 99% of their respective token/hour quantities. License/source mismatches account for 79% of text tokens, 55% of speech hours, and 65% of video hours. In the detailed crosstab, less than 0.1% of text content, 5.4% of speech content, and 0.6% of video content are commercially usable under both the paper's license and source-terms categories.

These figures establish documentation conflicts under the paper's taxonomy; they do not decide enforceability or legality. The authors explicitly limit their claim to tracing lineage and the distribution of licenses and terms, acknowledge jurisdiction and use-case dependence, and state that the work does not propose one standard for every developer.

The sourcing analysis reports that 71% of video hours and 69% of speech hours come from YouTube, while general web, encyclopedia, synthetic, and social sources increasingly dominate text. For synthetic text, the paper also reports generator shares and longer average turns than natural datasets. Those findings describe provenance trends, not content quality, consent, or model performance.

For representation, the paper reports creator-geography Gini coefficients of 0.92 for text, 0.86 for speech, and 0.74 for video. Although absolute language and country counts increase, most relative concentration measures do not significantly improve since 2013. This evidence is conditional on public datasets, partial creator identity, release-affiliation-to-headquarters mapping, and the exclusion of proprietary data.

Artifact inspection confirms an Apache-2.0 repository with modality JSON directories and tooling, plus an official 38-page supplemental Attribution Card citing 455 dataset papers. It also reveals release drift: the only GitHub release is v1.1.0 from June 2024, the checked main commit is `ce6662deccf011574427b6f112df479d261960b8` from March 2025, and the README still describes the earlier 44-collection text audit. Thus the audit is available, but no frozen paper-time multimodal manifest was identified.
