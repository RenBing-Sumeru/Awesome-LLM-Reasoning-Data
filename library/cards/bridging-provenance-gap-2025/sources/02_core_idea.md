The paper extends the Data Provenance Initiative from an earlier supervised-text audit to a cross-modal catalogue. Table 1 reports 3,916 public datasets: 3,717 text datasets in 108 collections, 95 speech datasets, and 104 video datasets. Together they span 798 sources, 659 creator organizations, 67 countries, 608 languages, 443 tasks, 55 licenses, 2.1T text tokens, and about 1.9M hours of speech and video. Section 2.1 also says 3,713 text datasets, so the two text totals remain an internal paper discrepancy.

The reusable object is structured metadata at collection, dataset, or separable-variant level. Human experts trace papers, repositories, dataset cards, marketplaces, derivation links, licenses, and source terms, then encode identifiers, sources, creators, languages, geography, tasks, model generation, human annotation, derivation, license evidence, and modality-specific properties. The repository also provides taxonomies, analysis plots, downloader/filter/normalization code, and an Attribution Card.

The feedback object is a human audit judgment. Dataset licenses are grouped as Commercial, NC/Acad, or Unspecified. Source restrictions are grouped as Model Closed, Source Closed, Unspecified, or Unrestricted, and aggregation uses the strictest source status; text collections inherit the most restrictive constituent status for collection-level analysis. These labels support screening and comparison, but they are neither legal rulings nor sample-level correctness rewards.

The catalogue does not attach every training record to an original URL, person, consent decision, transformation, or license. It also does not itself supply the audited dataset contents: repository scripts can retrieve and normalize selected underlying datasets, and the official Hugging Face organization hosts several derived text artifacts, but neither should be confused with one complete multimodal paper snapshot.
