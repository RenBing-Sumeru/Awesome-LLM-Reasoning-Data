The paper first bins BrowseComp attempts by reported confidence. Both evaluated models are overconfident in an absolute sense—the 90–94 confidence bin reaches at most 53.33% accuracy—yet higher bins show substantially higher empirical accuracy, while scores below 70 are near zero. Main experiments compare accuracy and average attempts under a ten-attempt cap. BrowseConf variants use about 2.06–5.72 attempts on average rather than the ten required by fixed-budget self-consistency/CISC, with model- and benchmark-dependent accuracy tradeoffs. Threshold ablations show that stricter thresholds can improve accuracy but consume more attempts; carrying summaries or negative answers changes later interaction counts.

These are system-level calibration and routing results. They do not make a particular confidence score a ground-truth probability, verify the web evidence in an attempt, or certify any unreleased trace corpus. Pass@10 is an upper-bound-style metric requiring outcome knowledge and is not the deployed selection rule.
