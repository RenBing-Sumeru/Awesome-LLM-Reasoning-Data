BrowseConf is useful as a schema for adaptive-compute web-agent traces. An auditable record should retain query and benchmark split, attempt index, model and prompt revision, action/observation log, URL and page snapshot, final answer, confidence, prior-attempt context, threshold and validation provenance, retry/stop reason, selected answer, judge configuration, correctness, tokens, interactions, latency, and cost. These fields support calibration curves, early-stop audits, memory-policy comparisons, and counterfactual replay under alternative thresholds.

Confidence should be stored as a model claim alongside—not in place of—outcome and evidence checks. The benchmark results justify studying this routing signal; they do not show that generated attempts are high-quality training data or that confidence generalizes without recalibration.
