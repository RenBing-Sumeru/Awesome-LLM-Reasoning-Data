The final NeurIPS proceedings record identifies this work as a 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks Track paper; the official OpenReview listing marks it as a Spotlight. The paper asks whether supervised fine-tuning on larger collections of human UI demonstrations can by itself produce robust real-world control, and whether in-domain scaling behavior transfers to unseen apps, tasks, and categories (NeurIPS paper, Abstract and §§1–2).

AndroidControl addresses a data bottleneck rather than proposing a new online agent environment. It releases 15,283 human demonstrations covering 14,548 unique tasks, 833 Android apps, and 40 app categories, with an average of 5.5 steps per episode. Unlike a prompt-answer dataset, one episode binds a high-level goal and per-action low-level instructions to screenshot and accessibility-tree observations plus JSON actions (Paper Table 1, p. 3; Appendix B.2, pp. 14–15).

This object belongs in `environment_agent_trajectory_data` because the released unit is a full physical-device state-action episode, and in `data_construction_open_release_recipes` because the paper documents task seeding, human collection, filtering, split construction, and SeqIO transformation. The adjacent problem it does not cover is reproducible online execution: app versions, APKs, account/reset state, network state, companion collection software, and an executable success predicate are not released.
