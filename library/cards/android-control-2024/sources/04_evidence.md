On Random-500 IDD, the fine-tuned LT-all-r64 PaLM-2S reaches 71.5% HL and 86.6% LL step accuracy. The strongest reported zero-shot values in the same table are 42.1% HL for M3A/GPT-4 Turbo and 56.7% LL for AitW/PaLM-2L (Paper Table 4, p. 8). Appendix E.3 checks the 500-action shortcut against the full test: zero-shot PaLM-2L scores 35.2/43.0 on Random-500 versus 35.0/42.7 on full-test HL/LL, while fine-tuned PaLM-2S scores 62.6/82.2 versus 64.8/80.0 (Table 12, p. 22).

Distribution shift remains substantial. On full splits, LT-all-r64 reports 70.8% HL and 83.2% LL on IDD, but only 57.4–59.6% HL and 76.8–78.5% LL across app-, task-, and category-unseen subsets. The authors report that the OOD gap grows as more training data is added (Paper Table 5 and §4.5, p. 9). On Random-500, HL accuracy is 82.5% for Google first-party apps but 58.7% for third-party apps (Appendix E.5, Table 13, p. 23).

Step accuracy also overstates long-horizon reliability. Fine-tuned episode accuracy falls from 21.3% for five-step tasks to 7.6% for six-step tasks despite reported step accuracy in the 64–71% range; zero-shot systems complete no tasks longer than five steps. The training-only terminate action is especially difficult, with action-type accuracy of 38.6% for HL and 67.5% for LL (Appendix E.1 and E.4, Tables 8–9 and Figure 9 discussion, pp. 21–23).

The headline scale targets are extrapolations rather than observations. Log-linear fits project 500K and 1M episodes for 95% IDD LL and HL step accuracy, 2M for 95% completion of a five-step IDD HL episode, 10M and 60M for 95% OOD LL and HL step accuracy, and 150M for 95% completion of a five-step OOD HL episode (Paper §§4.4–4.5, pp. 8–9). Those estimates depend on the fitted regime and, for episode completion, an independence assumption; they do not demonstrate that datasets of those sizes were collected or trained.

All results are author-reported and not independently reproduced in the checked artifacts. They support the claims that paired instructions and more in-domain demonstrations improve reference-action agreement, and that OOD high-level control scales more slowly. They do not establish per-record trajectory quality, live environment success, causal attribution to data scale alone, contamination freedom, or unrestricted reuse rights.
