The key claim is that targeted embedding perturbation reduces contamination inflation with less clean-task damage than competing white-box interventions. The controlled setup fine-tunes Mistral, Qwen2.5, and LLaMA-3 with exact, semantic-level, or domain-level leakage from MMLU or TruthfulQA, then evaluates the contaminated and clean counterparts under occurrence o=3. In Table 2, DeconIEP obtains low residual contamination across the three families; for example, its Mistral MMLU exact-leakage residual is 0.007. In Table 3 its benign utility drop for Qwen2.5 is 0.009 on TruthfulQA and 0.029 on MMLU, versus 0.207 and 0.256 for Shortcut Neuron. The evidence is simulated leakage on listed models and benchmarks, not proof for unknown real training corpora. The clean benchmark is deliberately different from the leaked one, so the utility measure tests collateral interference rather than a second measurement of the same contamination.
