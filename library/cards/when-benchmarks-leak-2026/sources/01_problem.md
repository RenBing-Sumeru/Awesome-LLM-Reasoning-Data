Leaked benchmark items can make an LLM appear capable because it retrieves training exposure rather than generalizing. Removing suspected items with membership inference changes the benchmark and leaves residual leakage whenever the detector has false negatives; applying one broad inference intervention can also damage clean inputs.

The paper instead keeps each prompt and benchmark item fixed, and treats decontamination as an inference-time correction. It studies whether a contaminated fine-tuned model can be steered toward the behavior of a less-contaminated reference while retaining utility on an unrelated clean benchmark. The intended comparison is therefore between corrected contaminated behavior and an explicit clean counterfactual, rather than between two headline benchmark scores.
