An evaluator of an open-weight model can start with a base checkpoint, its fine-tuned derivative, and a benchmark where leakage is suspected; train the generator on a disjoint auxiliary subset, then compare residual contamination and clean utility before reporting benchmark scores. Success is a lower residual relative to the matched clean model without a large cross-benchmark utility drop. A benchmark maintainer can use the three leakage constructions to stress-test an intervention instead of merely filtering items. Do not use it when model embeddings/gradients or a plausibly less-contaminated same-architecture reference are unavailable, or when an audit must establish training-data provenance. Keep the original score, corrected score, reference identity, and BUD together in the report; otherwise a lower score cannot be distinguished from indiscriminate capability suppression.
