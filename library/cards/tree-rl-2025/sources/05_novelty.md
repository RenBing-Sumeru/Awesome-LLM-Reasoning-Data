The closest baseline in the paper is ChainRL: independent on-policy multi-response sampling with final-answer feedback. TreeRL changes two coupled interfaces. First, EPTree branches from policy-uncertain intermediate tokens instead of making all samples independent or applying conventional stepwise MCTS. Second, terminal outcomes from that current-policy tree become per-step credit through descendant values and global/local advantages.

This differs from offline tree-search data construction and static PRM use because the tree and its signal are regenerated on policy during RL. It does not introduce final-answer checking, policy gradients, tree search, or advantage estimation individually. Its direction signal for this track is the explicit trace contract: branch location, shared prefixes, leaf outcome, budget, and reweighting affect what supervision a trajectory contains.

Before reuse, compare against an independent-sampling RLVR baseline at matched generation and optimization budgets, retain all branches rather than only selected traces, and verify terminal services. The paper's performance gains alone do not identify which component transfers or whether the derived process labels are reliable.
