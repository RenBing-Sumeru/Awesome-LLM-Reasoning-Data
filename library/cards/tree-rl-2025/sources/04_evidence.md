The evidence should be read as system-level method evidence, not data-quality validation.

- **Search comparison.** On Omni-MATH-500, Appendix Table 4 reports the selected (6,2,1,2) EPTree setting at 30 leaves, 56.9 PassRate, and 22,268 generation tokens; multi-16 reports 16 leaves, 52.4 PassRate, and 19,858 tokens. Table 2 also reports entropy forking at 56.9 PassRate and 22,268 tokens, random forking at 54.8 and 24,213, and multi-chain at 52.4 and 19,858. These are compared-budget search results, not measurements of trace-label precision.
- **RL evaluation.** Table 1 reports average greedy-evaluation accuracy of 44.5 for TreeRL versus 41.6 for ChainRL for the Qwen-2.5-14B line; 29.3 versus 27.2 for GLM4-9B; and 65.8 versus 64.5 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-2.5-7B. The six reported benchmarks are MATH500, Omni-MATH-500, AIME2024, AMC, OlympiadBench, and LiveCodeBench. The authors also state that both RL methods show only minor improvement over the SFT baseline in that setting.
- **Ablation boundary.** The paper reports that combined global and local advantages with square-root reweighting performs best among its tested process-signal designs, and that using a subset of sampled responses gives less improvement. This does not isolate the effects of search topology, more leaves, more optimization traces, and process credit.
- **Artifact evidence.** The official repository is public and Apache-2.0 licensed. It contains the named 10,562,582-byte input JSONL, launcher, EPTree implementation, and remote-reward code; no raw EPTree release or TreeRL checkpoint was verified. Thus performance results do not establish that the public input file or unshared runtime trees are high quality.
