The NeurIPS paper reports theoretical analysis for self-consistency, perplexity, and RPC, then evaluates the method across seven benchmarks spanning mathematical, code, commonsense, and R1-style reasoning. The project and repository expose the reported method, data collection, and a demo. This is useful evidence because the proposed allocation rule is tested both as a confidence estimator and as a way to reduce actual model calls.

The reported result is that RPC can match self-consistency's reasoning performance with at least 50% less sampling budget on four mathematical datasets; under the same budget, the paper reports an average 1.29% improvement over the compared methods and improved confidence reliability. These figures depend on answer extraction, probability access, sample diversity, and the evaluation models, so a reproduction must compare equal total sample budgets rather than only the final accuracy number.
