Sampling-based reasoning improves an answer by generating several chains and choosing a candidate, but each extra chain consumes inference budget. Self-consistency aggregates answer frequency and is robust to path wording, yet its confidence estimate improves only gradually as samples increase. Perplexity instead uses the model's internal probability and can converge faster, but it scores whole paths in a way that may not respect the structure of the answer space and can select a fluent but wrong rationale.

The paper asks how to spend a limited number of reasoning samples while retaining reliable answer-level confidence. It frames the decision as more than producing many traces: the system must estimate which parsed answer is likely correct, decide which low-value paths no longer deserve computation, and avoid paying a large sample cost merely to make a majority vote stable.
