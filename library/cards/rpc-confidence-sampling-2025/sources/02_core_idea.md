The paper analyzes sampling-based reasoning as confidence estimation and separates total reasoning error into estimation error and model error. This distinction explains a trade-off: self-consistency operates on parsed answers and keeps a favorable model-error property, but its Monte Carlo estimate has linear convergence; perplexity can exploit internal probability for faster convergence, but path-level probability creates a less suitable model-error term for answer selection.

RPC combines the two signals. Perplexity Consistency injects internal probability into answer-level self-consistency, while Reasoning Pruning removes low-probability paths that would otherwise weaken the convergence behavior. The resulting controller is meant to spend samples on a smaller, more informative candidate set rather than treating every generated rationale as equally valuable evidence.
