Many sampling methods either vote on answer frequency or rank complete paths by a likelihood-like score. RPC makes their distinct errors explicit and uses that analysis to design a hybrid. Its novelty is not simply adding another candidate score: it assigns internal probability to answer-level agreement, then explicitly prunes the paths that would make the favorable convergence argument fail.

The paper also pairs this construction with an auditable public implementation containing separate baseline computations. This makes the inference-time decision inspectable at the level of path scores and pruning, rather than presenting an opaque reward model. The contribution is therefore a theoretical account of a test-time allocation trade-off and a concrete policy for reducing the number of expensive reasoning samples.
