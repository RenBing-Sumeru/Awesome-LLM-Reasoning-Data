The safe reuse class is **evaluation and audit reference with model/inference artifacts available; training-data reuse blocked**. Researchers can use the released weights, prompt templates, inference/search scripts, selected contest inputs, and selected predictions to study model behavior under the released configuration. They cannot reproduce the reported GRPO/RFT training or rebuild the verifier datasets from those artifacts.

For data construction, the paper supplies a useful conceptual schema: preserve the problem and natural-language proof, expert three-level score, verifier analysis, expert meta-score, generator self-analysis, self-score, sampled verifier set, sampled meta-assessments, validated-issue votes, selected label, routing decision, reward values, model/checkpoint versions, and lineage. A robust recreation should retain both asserted and missed defects, rejected proofs, disagreements, and human-routed cases rather than only positive labels.

For verifier research, use the paper to design four separate audits: scalar-score accuracy, issue-level precision for false rigor, issue-level recall for missed defects, and calibration against independent experts or formalized subsets. Include unrelated verifier families or formal checkers where applicable so multi-sample agreement is not only repeated evidence from one model lineage. Appendix A.3 should be modified if the research goal includes penalizing omitted defects.

For test-time compute, the disclosed 8-sample one-shot, 32-thread sequential, and 64-by-64 high-compute protocols support budget-matched ablations of generation count, verification count, refinement rounds, self-selection, and external selection. Results should separately report learned-verifier scores, expert judgments, and formal-checker outcomes when a formalization exists.

The paper is also a strong audit checklist for frontier reports: demand `n/m/k`, dataset counts, expert agreement, auto-label error rates, contest exclusions, source rights, GRPO/RFT code, reward logs, failed records, and checkpoint lineage. Until those are released, the appropriate use is method comparison, verifier failure analysis, and evaluation reproduction—not training-corpus redistribution or claims of formal proof validity.
