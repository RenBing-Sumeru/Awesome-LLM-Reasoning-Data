Verifier evidence is reported on an unspecified validation split of `D_v`. Adding meta-verification raises the average quality of proof analyses from 0.85 to 0.96 as scored by the learned meta-verifier, while proof-score prediction accuracy is reported unchanged (Paper §2.1.2, p.4). This isolates the intended change—more faithful issue descriptions—but the split size, confidence intervals, independent expert confirmation, and per-class false-positive and false-negative rates are absent. The evaluator is also the learned meta-verifier used by the method.

One-shot results on 91 in-house CNML-level problems use 8 proof samples per model and majority vote across 8 analyses from the final verifier. Figure 1 reports mean proof scores by category and shows DeepSeekMath-V2 above GPT-5-Thinking-High and Gemini 2.5-Pro under that verifier (§3.3.1, p.7). Sequential refinement on the 31 ISL 2024 problems uses a 128K single-attempt limit, 32 independent threads, up to 8 attempts, and 32 final-verifier analyses. Figure 2 reports Pass@1 rising from 0.15 at one attempt to 0.27 at eight attempts and Best@32 from 0.26 to 0.42; Best@32 is selected by generator self-scores (§3.3.2, pp.7–8). These are learned-verifier-conditioned results, not formal checks.

High-compute contest results use one final model for both generation and verification. Each problem begins with 64 proof samples and 64 verifier analyses per proof; the system retains 64 highest-average candidates, pairs each with 8 selected analyses, and runs up to 16 iterations or until one proof passes all 64 LLM-verifier attempts. Mathematical experts then assess the highest-scoring proofs (§3.3.3, pp.8–9). Under these conditions, Table 1 reports five of six IMO 2025 problems fully solved for 83.3%, four CMO 2024 problems fully solved plus partial credit on another for 73.8%, and 11 of 12 Putnam 2024 problems fully solved plus minor errors on the last for 118/120 or 98.3%. “64/64” is the search stopping condition for verifier agreement on fully solved candidates, not a Lean/Isabelle certificate.

For IMO-ProofBench, Figure 3 reports expert grading of DeepSeekMath-V2 Heavy at 99.0% on the 30-problem Basic subset and 61.9% on the 30-problem Advanced subset. The DeepSeekMath-V2 proofs were graded by the authors' experts under benchmark guidelines, whereas baseline numbers were sourced from Luong et al.; expert identities, blinding, agreement, and raw grading records are not released. The paper notes that the hardest IMO-level problems remain challenging.

The official repository verifies model and evaluation artifacts: prompt templates, inference/search scripts, selected contest inputs, and JSONL predictions with automatic and human ratings.
