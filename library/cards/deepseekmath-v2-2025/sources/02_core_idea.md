The one-sentence contribution is: DeepSeekMath-V2 alternates training a natural-language proof verifier and a proof generator, uses a learned meta-verifier to constrain false-rigor in issue analyses, and replaces human labels with multi-sample automated judgments in the last two iterations.

The initial verifier consumes `(problem, proof)` and writes an issue summary plus score `1` for complete and rigorous, `0.5` for sound overall logic with minor errors or omissions, or `0` for fatal errors or critical gaps (Paper §2.1.1; Appendix A.2). Its first RL reward multiplies a programmatic format indicator by `1 - |predicted score - expert score|`. This aligns the scalar label but does not supervise whether described defects exist. The paper explicitly observes that a verifier can earn full reward by predicting the right score while hallucinating flaws.

Meta-verification adds a second learned judgment surface. Mathematical experts score whether a verifier analysis correctly identifies real issues and justifies its proof score; a meta-verifier learns this task, and enhanced verifier reward becomes `R_format × R_score × R_meta` (§2.1.2). The proof generator then receives the verifier's proof score and is trained to emit both a `Solution` and `Self Evaluation`; its reward uses `alpha=0.76` for proof quality and `beta=0.24` for accurate self-assessment, where self-score agreement is multiplied by meta-verification (§2.2.2).

What these systems observe is rubric-consistent natural-language judgment. They can flag plausible gaps and reward self-correction without a reference solution, but they cannot guarantee that all mathematical dependencies were checked. Appendix A.3 further instructs the meta-verifier to accept a no-defect analysis as reasonable even when the proof itself is obviously wrong; it validates asserted defects rather than directly penalizing omitted defects. This creates a false-negative surface that repeated related-model judgments may share.

The direction is learned proof feedback for natural-language theorem proving. The closest contrast is DeepSeek-Prover-V2 or AlphaProof, where a formal assistant provides a programmatic terminal predicate. DeepSeekMath-V2 instead makes verification scalable over prose but less conclusive. Its novelty is the feedback-and-labeling loop, not a formal proof checker.
