The prior-work baseline has two poles. Final-answer RLVR offers cheap programmatic feedback but cannot judge a proof's rigor. Formal systems such as Lean and Isabelle compile proof terms and can guarantee correctness for the supplied formal statement, but require formalization and a versioned proof environment. DeepSeekMath-V2 occupies the middle: it scales learned natural-language issue finding without reference solutions, accepting judgment uncertainty in exchange for coverage of prose proofs.

The concrete contribution is a three-role feedback loop. Experts first label proof quality and verifier-analysis quality with the same `{0, 0.5, 1}` scale. A verifier learns proof judgment; a meta-verifier learns to judge the verifier's asserted issues; a generator learns both proof production and self-evaluation from those learned scores. As the generator improves, multi-sample verifier and meta-verifier judgments automatically label new hard proofs, replacing human annotation in the final two iterations.

The distinctive data change is not simply more proofs. Each stage adds a different feedback object: `(problem, proof, expert score)`, `(problem, proof, verifier analysis, expert meta-score)`, generator proof plus self-analysis and self-score, and later proof plus a set of verifier analyses, meta-assessments, validation votes, selected label, and routing decision. This is a reusable conceptual schema for learned-verifier research even though the actual records are unavailable.

GRPO, model judges, self-refinement, rejection fine-tuning, and best-of-many search are not individually new. Nor does the paper establish formal verification, independent verifier diversity, or an open training recipe. Its direction signal is the coupling of verifier scaling with data construction: increased judgment compute changes which hard proofs become positive or negative training labels.

Before reuse, readers must obtain or recreate expert protocols, per-class calibration, `n/m/k`, independent verifier diversity, automatic-label error rates, human-routing and discard logs, source rights, contest-overlap checks, GRPO/RFT code, and item-level checkpoint lineage. Without them, the paper is an auditable frontier recipe and evaluation release, not a reusable training-data release.
