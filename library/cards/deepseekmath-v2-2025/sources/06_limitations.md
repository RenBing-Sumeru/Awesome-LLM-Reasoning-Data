- **False rigor is an observed reward failure.** Initial verifier RL directly rewards the extracted `{0, 0.5, 1}` score and output format, so a verifier can match the expert scalar while inventing nonexistent defects. Meta-verification reduces this surface but is itself a learned judge trained from undisclosed expert records, not a proof oracle.
- **Appendix A.3 leaves false negatives unpenalized.** Its rubric says a no-defect verifier analysis should be considered reasonable even if the proof is obviously wrong. Meta-verification therefore checks whether asserted defects are valid; it does not directly punish a verifier for failing to assert a real defect.
- **Correlated verifier blindness can become a positive label.** In automatic labeling, a proof receives `1` when no legitimate issue is found across `n` sampled analyses. Generator, verifier, and meta-verifier share model-family and iterative checkpoint lineage, and the final model performs both generation and verification at test time. Repeated agreement can amplify a shared blind spot rather than eliminate it.
- **The automation contract is not calibratable from the paper.** Numeric `n`, `m`, and `k`, sample independence, checkpoint diversity, tie handling, label distribution, discard rate, human-routing rate, expert-audit sample size, agreement statistics, and per-class error rates are not disclosed, even though the final two iterations rely entirely on automated labels.
- **A 64-of-64 pass is not formal validity.** It means 64 LLM analyses under the learned rubric did not reject the selected natural-language proof. No Lean/Isabelle compilation, formal statement, kernel check, or proof certificate is present. Expert assessment of selected top proofs is stronger evidence than self-agreement but still lacks released identities, blinding, agreement, and full-candidate coverage.
- **Evaluation is partly self-referential.** The `D_v` quality improvement is scored by the learned meta-verifier; one-shot and sequential proof correctness use the final verifier; Best@32 uses self-scores; high-compute search selects by the same final model that generates candidates. Independent human evaluation is concentrated on selected high-compute outputs and IMO-ProofBench.
- **Contest contamination is unresolved.** Training prioritizes 17,503 AoPS contest problems after 2010, while evaluation includes public IMO 2025, CMO 2024, Putnam 2024, ISL 2024, and IMO-ProofBench. No crawl manifest, temporal cutoff, exact or near-duplicate test, solution-overlap audit, memorization analysis, or source exclusion ledger is released.
- **Training records and lineage are unavailable.** Sizes and distributions for `D_v`, `D_mv`, validation data, generated hard proofs, rejected items, human-routed items, and RFT records are unknown. Expert protocol, GRPO/RFT code, reward implementation, optimizer settings, compute, dataset versions, and prompt-to-update checkpoint lineage are also missing.
- **Artifact openness is narrower than data openness.** Apache 2.0 model weights, inference/evaluation scripts, selected inputs, and selected predictions are released. The AoPS crawl, expert labels, verifier analyses, meta-labels, automated hard-proof corpus, RFT data, and training code are not released or licensed for reuse. Weight and evaluation-code availability must not be described as an open training dataset.
