The official arXiv v1 of *DeepSeekMath-V2: Towards Self-Verifiable Mathematical Reasoning* was submitted on November 27, 2025 by nine DeepSeek-AI authors. It addresses a failure of final-answer reward for theorem proving: a numerically correct answer can conceal invalid reasoning, and many proof tasks have no short answer that a program can compare with a key. The paper therefore studies whether an LLM can learn to identify issues in natural-language proofs and provide feedback for proof-generation RL and test-time refinement.

The central data objects are judgment-bearing natural-language records, not formal proof objects. Initial verifier data `D_v` contains a problem `X`, a generated proof `Y`, and a mathematical expert score `s` in `{0, 0.5, 1}`. Meta-verifier data `D_mv` adds the verifier analysis `V` and an expert quality score `ms` in the same three-level set. Generator records add a proof, a self-analysis, a self-score, external verifier score, and meta-verification feedback. In the final two training iterations, new hard proofs are labeled through repeated verifier and meta-verifier sampling instead of human annotation.

This distinction defines the Card's boundary. DeepSeekMath-V2 evaluates prose proofs with learned natural-language judges. It does not compile Lean or Isabelle terms and does not produce machine-checked certificates. Passing 64 verifier attempts means that 64 sampled LLM analyses found no disqualifying issue under a shared learned rubric; it is a high-confidence search condition followed by selected expert assessment, not guaranteed mathematical validity.

The paper belongs in `frontier_reports_data_disclosure_ledger` because it exposes an unusually detailed generator–verifier data flywheel, explicit reward formulas, expert-label schemas, and test-time budgets while withholding the training records, auto-label parameters, calibration evidence, contamination audit, and record lineage. L4 means the full paper, appendices, official repository, released evaluation inputs and outputs, and model page support a bilingual audit Card. It does not mean the training dataset or pipeline is reusable.
