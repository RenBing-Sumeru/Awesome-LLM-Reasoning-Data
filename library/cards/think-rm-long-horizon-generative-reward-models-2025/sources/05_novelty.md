Prior methods rely on final answers, generic step labels, or a single LLM judge, leaving a mismatch between feedback and the target. This work centers supervision on paired responses, long review traces, and final A/B decisions, uses generating long comparative reasoning and filtering it by the original preference label and output format to generate and filter records, and releases hs2-naive-reasoning-binary-max. The novelty lies in the data object and acceptance rule rather than standard training algorithms.
