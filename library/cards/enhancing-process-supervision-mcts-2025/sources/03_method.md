Inputs are a set of reasoning problems and corresponding ground-truth answers. The paper assumes no human process annotation and no access to an LLM stronger than the target model. It evaluates two target policies, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and deepseek-math-7b-instruct, using MATH and GSM8K. Individual reasoning steps are delimited by two newline characters and treated as tree nodes.

For each current partial solution, MCTS performs four operations. Selection follows the child with the highest Upper Confidence Bound until reaching an incompletely expanded or terminal node. Expansion samples a new next-step child from the current LLM with non-zero temperature. Simulation continues from that child until the model emits a final answer. Backpropagation compares that answer with the ground truth, assigns binary reward 1 or 0, and updates visit counts and cumulative rewards along the visited path. After repeated simulations, each immediate child of the partial-solution root receives the paper's relative-correctness score derived from its visit count, its average reward, and the visit-weighted sibling average; a manually set scale constant controls score magnitude. Children with score 0 are omitted from the training tuples. The highest-UCB child extends the selected path, and the procedure recurs until a final step or an undisclosed maximum length.

At iteration `i`, the selected tuples generated by the iteration `i-1` policy train the new policy with score-weighted negative log likelihood plus a KL penalty relative to the preceding policy. The authors describe this as SFT while also noting that the objective can be regarded as a form of reinforcement learning. All experimental models use LoRA. Zero-shot-CoT, rejection-sampling fine-tuning, and a Step-level DPO variant built from the highest- and lowest-reward steps are the comparison methods.

The verifier/reward boundary is narrow: terminal reward comes from comparison with the supplied answer, but the paper does not specify the answer-normalization or equivalence checker; intermediate labels are statistics of model-generated rollouts rather than direct step verification. There is no learned PRM or human judge in the proposed loop, and no external execution environment is described. Reproduction remains blocked by undisclosed simulation count, UCB formula and constant, temperature, score scale, maximum length, per-iteration sampling schedule, optimizer settings, LoRA configuration, random seeds, exact answer checker, and the absence of official code and trace data.
