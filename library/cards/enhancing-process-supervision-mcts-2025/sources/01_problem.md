The official arXiv record dates the paper to 2 January 2025 and states that it was accepted by the AAAI 2025 NeurMAD workshop; the official OpenReview venue page lists it as NeurMAD Submission 15. The paper addresses a specific weakness of outcome-supervised self-training for mathematical reasoning: accepting or rejecting an entire chain by its final answer does not identify which intermediate next steps were useful, and a correct answer can arise from a flawed path. Human process labels and separately trained process reward models can supply finer feedback, but the former are costly and the latter add another learned component.

The proposed data object is not a final-answer corpus. For a problem with a ground-truth answer, it consists of tuples containing the problem, a partial solution, a candidate next step, and a search-derived relative score. The score is induced from MCTS rollouts whose terminal answers receive binary reward. These tuples are consumed by iterative, score-weighted training. For the Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data category, the decision boundary is important: this is a recipe for producing training-time MCTS traces and step labels, not evidence of an open trace release and not a test-time-compute method evaluated only at inference. No official code, generated tree dataset, trained model, or reproducibility package was verified.
