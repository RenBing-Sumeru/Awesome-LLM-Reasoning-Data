The contribution is a self-training loop that turns MCTS outcome rollouts into graded next-step supervision and trains the policy directly on those scores, without first fitting a separate process reward model. Starting from the current partial solution, the target LLM expands and simulates a search tree. A completed simulation is compared with the problem's ground-truth answer and receives reward 1 for correct or 0 for incorrect; visit counts and cumulative rewards are backpropagated. Each root child is then assigned a score proportional to its visit count and to the difference between its own average reward and the visit-weighted average across sibling children. Thus the feedback object is a search-derived relative score, not a human correctness label and not an independently calibrated probability that the step is valid.

The resulting record is `(problem, partial solution, next step, relative score)`. Zero-score steps are excluded, while the next step with the highest UCB value extends the partial solution; MCTS is repeated at the new prefix until a final step or the maximum solution length. A score-weighted negative log-likelihood term trains the next policy, and a KL term constrains it toward the preceding iteration. The process repeats with the updated policy generating the next batch.

This fits the assigned category because search trees, rollout outcomes, sibling alternatives, visit statistics, and selection scores are the relevant data lineage. Compared with MCTS-DPO's best-versus-worst preference pairs, the method uses graded scores for more sampled next steps; compared with ReST-MCTS*, it does not train a PRM to guide the search. MCTS, self-training, terminal answer checking, KL regularization, and iterative generation are prior ingredients rather than new components.
