Earlier outcome-supervised recipes such as RFT and STaR retain chains mainly according to final-answer success; automated process-supervision work uses Monte Carlo estimates or a learned PRM; ReST-MCTS* uses a PRM to guide MCTS; and MCTS-DPO turns selected high- and low-reward steps into binary preference pairs. Against those baselines, this paper's specific change is to compute a visit-weighted, sibling-relative scalar for sampled next steps and use those scores directly in a weighted language-model objective. It can preserve supervision from more than only the best and worst children and avoids training a separate PRM.

For the assigned search-trace category, the direction signal is the explicit data lineage from terminal rollout outcomes to per-step training weights: problem and partial prefix lead to a set of sampled next steps, terminal simulations, visit/reward statistics, relative scores, a selected continuation, and a new policy iteration. A faithful release could therefore expose how search budget and rollout policy shape the training labels rather than presenting only the final fine-tuned model.

The reported accuracy and transfer comparisons are an empirical signal for the complete recipe, not a direct quality audit of the generated tuples. MCTS, UCB, self-generated reasoning, binary ground-truth reward, iterative self-training, LoRA, and KL regularization are not themselves novel here. No official trace or implementation release was verified, so reuse currently means reimplementing an incompletely specified recipe rather than downloading the claimed data object.
