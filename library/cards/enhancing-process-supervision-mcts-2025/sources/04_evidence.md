Table 1 reports mean accuracy ± standard error. For Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Zero-shot-CoT scores 47.07 ± 0.15 on MATH and 80.77 ± 0.25 on GSM8K; the proposed procedure reaches 51.92 ± 0.20 at iteration 4 on MATH and 85.80 ± 0.22 at iteration 2 on GSM8K. For deepseek-math-7b-instruct, the corresponding Zero-shot-CoT results are 41.20 ± 0.27 and 78.79 ± 0.20, while the best reported proposed-method results are 44.57 ± 0.27 at iteration 3 and 82.02 ± 0.18 at iteration 2. In the table, the proposed method also exceeds the reported RFT and Step-level DPO results at the compared iterations.

Table 2 trains on one dataset and evaluates on the other. The reported cross-dataset scores exceed each model's Zero-shot-CoT values in the tested directions, although the gains are smaller than in the same-dataset setting. The paper also states that performance converges quickly: only about 2,000 training problems from MATH and about 2,000 from GSM8K are used, and adding iterations or problems can reduce performance.

These experiments support the claim that this particular generate-and-train procedure can improve the evaluated model-task pairs under the reported setup. They do not show that every retained next step is correct, that relative scores are calibrated across problems or iterations, or that the unshared trace records form a reusable high-quality dataset. The official evidence locations are the arXiv record, the NeurMAD paper PDF, and OpenReview Submission 15; none of those checked pages supplies an official code or data release.
