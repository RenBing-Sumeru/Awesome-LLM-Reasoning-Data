The relative score inherits errors and variance from the current policy's rollouts. A promising step can score poorly when later continuations fail, while a flawed step can benefit from a lucky completion; sibling-relative values also need not be comparable across roots or model iterations. Terminal checking is described only as comparing the generated final answer with ground truth, so normalization, symbolic equivalence, malformed answers, and multiple valid forms remain unaudited. Dropping score-zero steps removes part of the search evidence and may obscure hard negatives.

The five-page paper omits central reproducibility settings, including rollout count, UCB details, sampling temperature, score-scale constant, maximum solution length, exact per-iteration problem schedule, seeds, LoRA and optimizer hyperparameters, and how standard errors were obtained. It reports that further iterations or more problems can degrade performance and that LoRA may limit improvement, but it does not diagnose the collapse. Experiments cover two 7–8B instruction models and two math datasets, so broader domains and model scales are unknown.

No official code, trace dataset, tree logs, selected/rejected records, checkpoints, license for generated records, split manifest, or contamination analysis was verified. Consequently, tree completeness, prompt lineage, duplicate handling, answer-checker behavior, and score reconstruction cannot be audited. Benchmark gains evaluate trained models; they are not proof that the intermediate process-supervision data are correct, complete, or reusable.
