- The 175 tasks form one public evaluation set; no train/validation/test partition or model-pretraining/agent-development decontamination protocol is disclosed. Public task prompts and evaluator sources increase future exposure and benchmark-specific gaming risk, even though runtime evaluator code is encrypted in task images.
- Feedback validity is bounded by task-specific predicates. Deterministic checks can reward unintended state changes or miss semantic failures, while the 51 LLM-evaluated tasks inherit judge-model variance and rubric bias. No independent judge-calibration corpus or repeated-judge variance estimate is released. The observed user-renaming shortcut illustrates verifier gaming rather than merely an agent capability failure.
- Reproducibility depends on more than repository code: task images, service backups, OpenHands versions, browser behavior, model APIs, Claude-3-5-Sonnet-20241022 NPCs/judges, and run configuration can all drift. Release 1.0.0 and run paths are identified, but immutable container/service-image digests and complete per-run manifests are unknown.
- The 24.0% 2024 result and 30.3% archival result use different model and OpenHands configurations. Treating this as a controlled improvement would conflate model, harness, and paper-version changes. The experiments repository is also a living leaderboard, so an unpinned “best score” is not a stable paper result.
- The inspected 2024-12-17 baseline retains successful, partial, and zero-score trajectories, but completeness is not independently established for every historical third-party run. Missing or inconsistent failed episodes could bias downstream error analysis. There is no evidence of a selection or filtering policy designed to make these logs suitable for training.
- The main benchmark and environment-backup repositories declare MIT, and the current arXiv manuscript is CC BY 4.0, but the official experiments repository has no confirmed license. Those licenses do not automatically cover third-party services, real-world project material, screenshots, or model outputs. Training or redistribution of trajectories is blocked pending a record-level rights review.
- The imagined company uses mock/manual material together with “real-world software project data.” Per-record source lineage, rights, redaction, consent, deletion policy, and captured screenshot contents are unknown. Simulated colleagues do not make privacy review inapplicable because seeded source material may still concern real people or projects.
- Task construction uses undisclosed LLM brainstorming and extensive manual labor. The model, prompt, sampling, rejection rate, and seeds are unknown; per-task review logs are unavailable. This prevents reconstruction of the source mixture and measurement of selection bias.
- The environment covers a specific self-hosted software-company setting. Tasks are not exhaustive or representative of occupations, and no human performance baseline was collected. Generalization to other organizations, tools, languages, accessibility settings, or real workplace consequences remains unsupported; job-automation claims would exceed the evidence.
- The release is supported for evaluation and audit, not for SFT, preference learning, reward modeling, process supervision, or agent RL. Benchmark performance does not prove that its trajectories are high-quality training data, and the paper reports no training ablation using them.
