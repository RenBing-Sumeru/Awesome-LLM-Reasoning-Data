On GAIA validation Answer Accuracy, Table 1 reports Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct at `10.30` without fine-tuning and `19.39` after Tool-R1 training. The Tool-R1 Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct result is `26.67`. MAT Agent is listed with 20k QA-plus-trajectory records and scores `15.15/16.97` for the two model-size columns, while Tool-R1 uses 1.3k QA pairs and scores `19.39/26.67`. These are author-reported results, not an independently reproduced or controlled data-only comparison.

Table 2 supplies the more diagnostic evidence for the 7B setting. Vanilla GRPO scores `9.09`, below the `10.30` untuned base; moderate-difficulty filtering reaches `16.36`; adding the auxiliary rewards reaches `18.79` and reports `3.84` on GAIA Level 3. A dynamic queue without resampling scores `18.18` while reported training time falls from `41.5` to `22.3` hours, and the queue with resampling reaches `19.39` in `22.3` hours. The paper does not provide seeds, variance, or an independently rerunnable configuration.

The evaluation surface is the GAIA validation set, described as 446 tasks grounded in 109 files, with Answer Accuracy as the main metric. This supports the narrower conclusion that the full Tool-R1 recipe improves the studied Qwen2.5 agent configurations under the authors' setup and that vanilla GRPO can fail in the same setup.

The scores do not certify the 1,300 questions, cached trajectories, judge labels, execution rewards, or tool observations as high-quality data. Different base models and scaffolds confound the MAT Agent comparison; no split or contamination audit is reported; and live web or model-backed tools can change the observation distribution. Benchmark performance therefore motivates the recipe but does not prove unrestricted training-data suitability.
