The theoretical evidence distinguishes coverage assumptions rather than declaring BoN universally poor. Theorems 3.1–3.2 show that under average-case coverage, BoN's candidate budget simultaneously improves coverage and increases exposure to reward-model tail errors, yielding overoptimization and a suboptimal regret rate; Theorem 3.4 shows that properly tuned BoN can reach the optimal rate under stronger uniform coverage. Theorem 4.1 gives InferenceTimePessimism an optimal regret rate up to constants and a bound that holds for all sufficiently large (N) at fixed β, while Theorem 4.2 supplies a matching query-complexity dependence up to logarithmic factors. Empirically, Figure 1 shows the predicted proxy-reward/accuracy divergence for GSM8K with OASST, and Table 1 plus Appendix B extend comparisons across tasks, policies, and reward models. The paper also reports that many fixed-budget differences are not statistically significant and that BoN can remain monotonic when a reward model is sufficiently informative. These results evaluate selectors under the studied setup; they do not prove that the sampled responses, reward scores, or unreleased traces form a high-quality reusable dataset.
