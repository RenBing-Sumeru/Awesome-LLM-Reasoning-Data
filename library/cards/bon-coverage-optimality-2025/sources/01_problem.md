Best-of-N (BoN) spends inference-time compute by sampling (N) responses from a reference policy and returning the one with the largest score from an imperfect reward model. The paper asks why increasing (N) can raise the proxy score while lowering true task reward, and whether an inference-time selector can improve without this overoptimization. Its formal unit is one fixed prompt (x), a reference policy πref, candidate responses with πref likelihoods and scalar proxy rewards, a selected response, and an evaluation-only true reward. This is a Track 5 evaluation and construction surface: the important object is the set of rollouts and selector signals under a compute budget, not a standalone answer or a released training dataset. The paper is an ICML 2025 conference paper in PMLR 267; the official record spans pages 25075–25126.
