
The paper first models inference-time alignment through sample-and-evaluate queries: for a prompt, an algorithm may sample candidates from πref and query each candidate's policy likelihood and imperfect reward ŕ, while the true reward (r^⋆) remains unavailable to selection. BoN uses ŕ only as an argmax. InferenceTimePessimism instead targets a χ²-regularized response distribution whose unnormalized weight is πref(y|x) · relu(β^{-1}(ŕ(x,y)-λ(x))); it estimates the normalizer λ from sampled candidates and uses rejection sampling to select a response. Separating candidate budget (N) from regularization β is the core mechanism: (N) controls approximation and compute, whereas β controls pessimism about reward-model error. In the experiments the feedback contract is mixed: four scalar reward models drive selection, answer correctness supplies programmatic evaluation on GSM8K, MMLU, and MATH, and GPT-4o-mini supplies the AlpacaEval-2.0 comparison judgment.
