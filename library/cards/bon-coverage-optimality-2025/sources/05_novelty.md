Prior work had used BoN, rejection sampling, and training-time regularization, and had analyzed properties such as reward/KL trade-offs. The distinct contribution here is to study downstream true-reward regret under mismatch between an imperfect proxy reward and the unknown true reward, make reference-policy coverage an explicit limit, and give a per-prompt query-complexity account of compute. InferenceTimePessimism reuses the χ²-regularization principle associated with training-time χPO but implements the target distribution at inference time through normalizer estimation and rejection sampling. This is not a new source dataset, reward model, or benchmark. For reasoning-data curation, the direction signal is narrower and useful: a test-time selection record should retain candidates, policy likelihoods, proxy scores, (N), β, normalization and acceptance decisions, and a separately defined true-evaluation field, because the final selected answer alone hides reward hacking.
