The guarantees assume sample-and-evaluate access to πref and ŕ, including response likelihoods, a bounded reward, mean-squared reward error measured under πref, and a coverage relationship to a comparator policy. The optimal β depends on coverage and reward-error quantities that may be unknown in deployment; practical robustness therefore does not remove tuning. Pessimism can be over-conservative when the proxy reward already separates correct and incorrect responses, and the experimental fallback to BoN can cause a small performance dip when rejection sampling fails at low budgets. The main answer-based tasks use binary correctness, a regime where the paper says average and uniform coverage may coincide for the chosen optimal comparator, so they do not test finer proof or reasoning quality. AlpacaEval-2.0 uses only 128 prompts, 5 replicates, and a proprietary GPT-4o-mini evaluator, making that evidence preliminary and noisy. Bootstrapping from approximately 20,000 pre-generated responses is not identical to a fresh online sampling deployment. Official code, response pools, exact seeds, runtime environment, data license, and decontamination controls were not verified, so end-to-end reproduction and trace reuse remain unresolved.
