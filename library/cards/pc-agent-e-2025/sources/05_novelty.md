The prior-work baseline is full-trajectory collection or distillation: obtain complete computer-use demonstrations from humans or a strong proprietary model and train on those episodes. The paper's own direct-distillation comparator executes ten complete Claude trajectories for each task. A second baseline retains only the 312 human trajectories after adding synthetic thoughts.

PC Agent-E changes the unit of synthetic expansion. Rather than allowing Claude to choose an action and then continue from its own potentially erroneous state, Trajectory Boost queries nine alternative one-step decisions at every state on the executed human trunk. The resulting supervision object is therefore a fan of thought-action targets around a trusted screenshot and history, not a tree of observed transitions. Flattening both human and branch nodes into action-level multimodal SFT rows is the consequential training-use change.

Several components are integrations rather than new primitives: human GUI recording, ReAct-style thought/action formatting, Claude generation, PyAutoGUI actions, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaMA-Factory SFT, VM-based GUI benchmarks, and task-specific evaluators all predate the work. The scale increase from 312 episodes to about 27K rows is likewise a consequence of repeated branching and flattening; it is not evidence of 27K independent tasks or executed episodes.

The direction signal for reasoning-data research is the deliberate trade: synthetic coverage grows cheaply by attaching multiple labels to a human state, while transition validity and terminal success are sacrificed for those branches. This creates a clean audit question for agent data—whether diversity at a fixed state can substitute for replay-verified trajectories. Before reuse, researchers should measure branch executability, semantic correctness, action diversity, hidden teacher style, human-state coverage, dropped-failure bias, and downstream sensitivity to removing invalid alternatives.
