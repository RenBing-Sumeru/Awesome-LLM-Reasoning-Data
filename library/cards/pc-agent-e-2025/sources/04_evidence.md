The following are author-reported results under their stated settings; no independent reproduction is reported. Success rate is the WindowsAgentArena-V2 environment evaluator's task-level success rate, not the fraction of training samples judged correct.

| Comparison | Model/data condition | Benchmark and budget | Reported result | What the result supports—and does not support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base vs PC Agent-E | Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct base vs the same base after full SFT on about 27K action-level samples from 312 augmented human episodes | WindowsAgentArena-V2, 141 feasible tasks across 11 Windows apps, screenshot-only 1280×720 scaffold, default 30-step cap | 14.9 → 36.0 success rate; `(36.0-14.9)/14.9 ≈ 141.6%` relative | Supports the headline “141%” only relative to this Qwen base under these conditions. | Human-only vs Trajectory Boost | Thought-completed human trajectories vs adding nine unexecuted synthetic alternatives per human step | Same training and WindowsAgentArena-V2 evaluation family | 17.2 vs 36.0 | Supports an incremental benefit from the synthetic alternatives in the reported pipeline; it does not show that each alternative is executable or correct. |
| Direct distillation vs PC Agent-E | Ten executed Claude end-to-end trajectories per task, 3,120 total, trained with the same procedure vs alternatives sampled at human states | WindowsAgentArena-V2, reported comparison in Figure 7 | 26.2 vs 36.0 | Supports the paper's data-construction comparison. The explanation that avoiding accumulated errors causes the gap remains an author interpretation. |

The paper also reports about three hours for offline Trajectory Boost versus about 900 hours for an equivalent 3,120-trajectory direct-distillation collection, described as 300×. Hardware details and accounting methodology are undisclosed, so this is a synthesis-time claim rather than a portable cost estimate. WindowsAgentArena-V2 repairs infeasible tasks, evaluator bugs, and initialization behavior; the authors report initialization failures falling from 10–30% to below 5%. These benchmark repairs improve the evaluation surface but remain separate from correctness verification of the released SFT targets.
