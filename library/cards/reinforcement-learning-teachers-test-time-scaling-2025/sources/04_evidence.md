The reward-ranking study generates 16 explanations per question with the pre-RL teacher, builds 16 rank-specific datasets, and reports a Pearson correlation above 0.89 between reward rank and downstream student performance. This supports the reward as a within-pipeline ranking signal, but does not establish calibration across other students, models, or domains.

On AIME 2024, MATH-500, and GPQA Diamond, the full 16,710-row RLT student reports an overall average of 49.50 at 7B versus 46.60 for the Bespoke baseline; at 32B, the reported averages are 73.23 versus 71.47. These controlled comparisons hold questions and student recipes largely fixed while changing traces. They are downstream outcomes, not direct proof that individual explanations are correct or reusable.

The reward ablation is an important failure test. With the full reward, the 7B overall result is 49.50; removing the explanation-token KL term yields 34.10 and qualitative repetition or excessive length, while removing min/max reductions yields 47.43. The result shows that reward shape materially affects trace behavior. Official artifacts include code and Apache-2.0 distilled 7B/32B students, but no generated-trace dataset or clearly identified RLT teacher checkpoint.
