Prior open reasoning datasets already used teacher distillation, answer filtering, multiple candidates, LLM judges, and long-CoT SFT. OmniThought does not originate those components. Its more specific change is to retain several answer-valid candidates per problem, keep reasoning validity separate from answer acceptance, and annotate every retained trace with teacher identity plus two whole-trace descriptors intended for student-aware selection.

Reasoning Verbosity is not simply length: the paper combines a QwQ-32B judgment about whether the reasoning amount fits the problem with log-normalized token length. Cognitive Difficulty is not problem difficulty alone: it estimates the competence needed to follow and reproduce the chosen reasoning method. The downstream selection rule then uses a target capacity `mu_CD` and the CD-RV gap to sample traces for a particular student. This makes model capacity, trace method, and verbosity explicit construction variables rather than post-hoc observations.

The work also connects one release object to three training interfaces. SFT samples from the RV-CD space; DPO constructs within-problem verbosity pairs; GRPO learns scalar RV/CD predictors and adds their outputs to format and accuracy rewards. The value is the common metadata substrate and the controlled comparisons, not a claim that the three objectives share the same semantics. In particular, DPO's chosen/rejected relation is about desired verbosity, whereas GRPO's RV/CD terms are learned rewards.

The direction signal is conditional rather than universal. The reported optimum shifts from `mu_CD=5` for a 7B student to 7 for a 32B student, and the paper's 100K-to-1.1M scaling table shows diminishing gains. Reuse should therefore validate the selection rule on the target model and domain. The contribution remains partly disclosed because generator settings, row-level source lineage, verifier assets, exact selected subsets, and a frozen end-to-end construction config are not available.
