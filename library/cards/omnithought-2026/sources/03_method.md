The ACL paper describes the following pipeline.

1. **Collect problems.** Mathematics uses OpenMathReasoning, MathInstruct, DeepMath-103K, NuminaMath-1.5, and OpenThoughts2-1M. Code uses TACO, APPS, OpenThoughts2-1M, and OpenCodeReasoning. Science uses StackExchange Physics, StackExchange Biology, camel-ai/chemistry, and OpenThoughts2-1M.
2. **Deduplicate and screen overlap.** Apply normalized Indel similarity and 10-gram similarity against AIME24, MATH500, LiveCodeBench V2, and GPQA-Diamond. Thresholds, removal counts, and decision logs are not reported.
3. **Generate candidates.** DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-R1-0528, and QwQ-32B produce multiple CoTs per problem. Some candidates below 3,000 CoT tokens are randomly removed to reduce the share of simple items. Teacher allocation, requests per problem, decoding settings, seeds, and retry rules are unknown.
4. **Validate two outcomes.** The released prompt asks a judge to return independent `reasoning_valid` and `solution_valid` booleans. Code solutions are executed against tests; math and science combine rules with LLM judgment. Only an incorrect final answer forces removal, so answer-correct but reasoning-invalid traces may remain. Each retained problem is reported to have at least two accepted candidates.
5. **Annotate process characteristics.** QwQ-32B judges RV and CD on 0-9 scales. The final RV is `round(alpha * L_RV + (1-alpha) * L_norm)` with alpha=0.5, where `L_norm` is a log-normalized token length. CD rates the competence required to follow and reproduce the reasoning method. The release does not expose the raw judge RV, normalized-length component, tokenizer, or corpus extrema used in that calculation.
6. **Select for a student.** For every problem, the paper scores candidates by whether CD stays within target capacity `mu_CD` and by the CD-RV gap. It normalizes the two components and mixes them with beta=0.5 by default. More than one high-probability trace may be sampled per problem; exact selected manifests for the released models are absent.

The paper evaluates three reuse paths. On matched 10K-problem sets, SFT compares random, RV-only, CD-only, and combined selection. DPO chooses RV 3–5 traces and rejects the maximum-RV trace for the same problem, creating a verbosity preference rather than a correctness preference. GRPO trains RV/CD reward models, normalizes their predicted scores to [0,1], and adds them to format and accuracy rewards; reward weights and rollout-generation settings are not disclosed.

Appendix D provides training scaffolds: the RQ SFT, DPO, and GRPO studies use 8 A800-80GB GPUs, while strong 7B/8B and 32B SFT uses 64 A800-80GB GPUs. The paper footnote points to EasyDistill, and the current repository contains a ThoughtX README, a simple RV/CD filter, and an example training config. At commit `acbe49b5d9b16b3beb874528056e11dfdfac6d1c`, that example targets a 1.5B student with a 4K sequence length, so it is a partial scaffold rather than a frozen reproduction of the paper's construction and 7B/32B runs.
