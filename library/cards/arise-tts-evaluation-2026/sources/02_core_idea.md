ARISE evaluates transitions between adjacent scaling iterations for each sample. A transition from incorrect to correct receives a positive contribution weighted by the relative extra token cost; a transition from correct to incorrect receives a larger negative contribution that grows with wasted computation. Unchanged outcomes contribute nothing. The metric therefore distinguishes a useful correction from a costly regression instead of relying solely on the overall direction of a benchmark curve.

Its second component is adaptive resolution. The evaluation begins with a minimum number of trials and estimates variation in both correctness and token consumption. Samples or settings with greater observed variability receive additional trials, while stable cases do not consume the same evaluation budget. This makes measurement itself budget-aware and aims to reduce the instability caused by high-temperature, repeated reasoning runs.
