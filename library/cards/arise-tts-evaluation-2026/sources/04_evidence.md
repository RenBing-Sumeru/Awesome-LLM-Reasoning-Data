The official paper evaluates contemporary reasoning models in mathematics, code generation, and agentic domains. It reports that ARISE gives a fine-grained, stable measure of scaling effectiveness and exposes substantial differences in scaling efficiency that aggregate metrics can obscure. In the reported comparisons, Claude Opus obtains the strongest ARISE characteristics across the evaluated task domains, illustrating that a model’s ordinary accuracy and its quality-cost response to more inference can be distinct properties.

The evidence supports an evaluation claim rather than a new model-capability claim. It shows why negative transitions and token instability must be visible when comparing scaling curves. A reviewer should nevertheless inspect the number of compute levels, decoding settings, task-specific correctness functions, and adaptive-sampling thresholds. The metric’s ranking can depend on those protocol choices, particularly when outcomes are subjective or when additional tokens have a cost not well approximated by generated-token count.
