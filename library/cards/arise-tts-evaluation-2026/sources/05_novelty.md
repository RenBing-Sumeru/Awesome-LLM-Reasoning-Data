ARISE changes the unit of a scaling evaluation from an aggregate curve point to a transition experienced by one sample. This matters because test-time computation can have asymmetric consequences: recovering a wrong answer is useful, but losing an already correct answer after spending more tokens is especially harmful. The metric encodes that asymmetry directly instead of allowing accuracy averages or slopes to erase it.

It also treats evaluation sampling as a resource-allocation problem. Rather than fixing the same number of trials for all samples and budgets, it uses observed variance to decide where more measurement resolution is needed. The contribution is therefore a coupled design for what is scored and how confidently it is measured, making the reliability of a scaling claim part of the evaluation rather than an afterthought.
