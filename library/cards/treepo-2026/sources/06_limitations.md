Outcome verification is narrow. A boxed-answer equivalence rule scores the final result, not the validity of each intermediate segment. A trajectory may receive positive reward despite flawed reasoning, and the parser or equivalence rule may mishandle alternative but mathematically equivalent forms. Tree-relative baselines redistribute these outcome rewards; they do not repair the underlying verifier.

Retention is incomplete. Repetition-stopped, max-depth, max-length, answer-complete, and EOS paths may enter the terminal sample pool, but short-filtered paths and unallocated active branches can disappear. Root groups with all-correct or all-wrong rewards are dynamically removed, and no rejected-query manifest is published. Consequently, the public release cannot answer which branches were considered, pruned, starved of budget, used for fallback, or excluded from updates.

The persisted diagnostic format is not a full tree log. It contains arguments, timing and length summaries, aggregate depth/token distributions, and flattened input/output pairs. It omits stable parent-child edges, per-record finish reasons, branch allocation decisions, fallback ancestry, branch-level rewards, group-rejection decisions, and exact trainer batches. Even when a terminal response is retained, its full construction lineage is not guaranteed to be recoverable.

The public Hugging Face release is easy to misread. Its default viewer exposes 3,120 benchmark-aligned records in an automatically named `train` split, even though repository filenames distinguish training and evaluation Parquets. The dataset card lacks the metadata needed to declare semantic configurations and splits. It also publishes no dataset license. The code's Apache-2.0 license applies to the implementation, not automatically to the data or model weights; the model-weight license is also unknown.

Data governance remains undisclosed. There is no reported prompt-level decontamination or semantic-overlap audit across the SimpleRL/DeepScaler mixture, evaluation benchmarks, or base-model pretraining data. Upstream row-level source and rights manifests are absent. Random seeds, the hardware model for the 64-GPU online runs, failure runs, complete logs, container hashes, and independent rerun variance are unknown.

Configuration drift weakens exact reproduction. The paper permits at most two additional sampling batches during informative-group formation, while the released training script permits up to 10. Temperatures reported for the main script and a paper case study also refer to different contexts. Without an immutable manifest for each result, readers cannot determine which exact code and sampling limits produced every reported checkpoint or table.

The efficiency findings are conditional. Gains depend on how much prefix sharing the policy produces, segment length, branching factor, target width, stop behavior, vLLM scheduling, and hardware. Wider or deeper trees are not uniformly better, and reduced GPU-hours do not uniformly preserve the highest reported accuracy. The H100 offline setup and the undisclosed 64-GPU hardware should not be generalized into a universal cost claim.

Finally, the accepted ICML 2026 OpenReview record differs from the earlier arXiv/project/repository citation in title, year, and author list. The venue record adds authors and uses the accepted title. Automated citation harvesting can therefore regress the Card to the older 2025 metadata unless the accepted record remains canonical.
