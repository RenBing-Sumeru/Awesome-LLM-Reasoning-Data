The strongest direct evidence concerns sampling efficiency under specified budgets. In the paper's fixed-initial-divergence training-time comparison, sequential sampling takes 5.78 GPU-hours, while tree sampling takes 4.29, 4.82, and 5.09 GPU-hours at branching factors 2, 4, and 8, respectively. For the more-initial-divergence model, sequential sampling takes 6.40 GPU-hours and the corresponding tree settings take 3.65, 4.56, and 5.05 GPU-hours. These are reported reductions of 12% to 43%, not a guarantee that every tree configuration is both faster and more accurate.

The controlled offline study holds prompt count, rollout count, sampling parameters, and a 7,000-token trajectory budget fixed. Across Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-Math-7B, the reported geometric-mean gains are 40% in trajectories per second and 30% in tokens per second. The abstract's "up to" summary uses 40% at the trajectory level and 35% at the token level. The geometric mean and maximum are different summaries and should not be merged.

The experiments also show a budget-dependent search trade-off. The tested divergence factors 2, 4, and 8 produce different compute-performance curves, and wider search becomes favorable only at larger budgets. Table 2 does not show a uniformly dominant accuracy result at every lower-cost configuration. This supports the narrower conclusion that shared-prefix tree sampling can move the efficiency frontier under the reported setup; it does not establish that increasing width or depth is always beneficial.

The policy results supply evidence that the tree-derived advantage can be used for online RLVR, but benchmark performance is not evidence that the released data are complete or high quality. Correct final answers can coexist with invalid intermediate reasoning, and the public Hugging Face records are prompt/ground-truth rows rather than the actual rollout trees that produced the updates. Similarly, the availability of checkpoints demonstrates a release surface, not a complete provenance chain.

Artifact inspection adds evidence beyond aggregate results. The official repository contains an Apache-2.0 implementation with TreePO recipe code, veRL/vLLM integration, runtime tree structures, launch settings, tests, and diagnostics. The official Hugging Face collection contains multiple checkpoints and `TreePO_data`; the dataset server confirms the prompt/reward schema and 3,120 viewer rows. At the same time, neither surface provides immutable paper-run parent-child edges, every branch reward, all stopped or discarded paths, rejected query groups, or trainer-batch hashes.

Reproducibility evidence remains partial. The code and data were inspected at explicit commits, and the repository documents relevant development versions, but there is no tagged release or complete run-to-checkpoint manifest. The disagreement between at most two extra sampling batches in the paper and a maximum of 10 in the script is a concrete unresolved configuration drift. No independent rerun variance is reported in the checked official artifacts.
