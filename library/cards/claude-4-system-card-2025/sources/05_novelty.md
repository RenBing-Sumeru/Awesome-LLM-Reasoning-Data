The report's atlas contribution is comparative disclosure, not a new open alignment dataset. It is unusually explicit about several source classes, opted-in user data, deduplication/classification, crowd-worker functions, and the distinction between helpful-only, broadly safety-trained, and final snapshots. At the same time, it withholds the operational preference and reward pipeline.

This combination is useful because it reveals a boundary between governance evidence and data evidence. Iterative safety evaluations can influence release decisions and motivate mitigations without becoming a released training corpus. Human feedback and Constitutional AI are established approaches rather than new methods here; the direction signal is how a frontier system card connects them to model snapshots and risk-governance decisions while leaving record-level lineage unavailable.
