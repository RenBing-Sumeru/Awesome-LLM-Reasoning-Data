For every question, the method pre-collects 128 independent reasoning trajectories from a base model, segmented into 500-token intervals, together with intermediate answer probes. A controller's state contains the question, instantiated and active branches, prefixes, depths, revealed probes, and remaining budget. Its atomic actions are BRANCH, CONTINUE, PROBE, PRUNE, and ANSWER. A terminal aggregation rule turns the state into an answer, and the objective rewards exact accuracy while penalizing generation and probe cost.

The authors use AIME24 as the discovery set and hold out AIME25 and HMMT25. Claude Code proposes controller implementations over five rounds after reading prior code, accuracy-token curves, and execution histories. Each candidate is evaluated 64 times on random subsets of the stored trajectories. The final controller is compared with SC@64, adaptive and early self-consistency, and Parallel-Probe across Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 8B, then tested on a distilled Llama model and GPQA Diamond to assess transfer beyond the search setting.
