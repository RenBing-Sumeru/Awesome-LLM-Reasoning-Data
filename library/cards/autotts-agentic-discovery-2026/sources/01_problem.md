Test-time scaling methods commonly commit to a hand-written schedule: sample a fixed number of branches, stop after a threshold, or apply a manually chosen pruning rule. Such decisions determine whether inference tokens are spent exploring alternatives, extending a promising thought, querying a probe, or terminating. Because these controls interact, adjusting a few thresholds by intuition explores only a small and potentially unrepresentative part of the available accuracy-cost trade-off.

The paper asks how to discover an allocation policy rather than hand-design one. The desired policy must make fine-grained decisions over a multi-trajectory reasoning process while remaining affordable to evaluate repeatedly. Directly running a base model for every proposed controller would be prohibitively expensive, and a scalar score alone does not tell a search agent why a candidate overspent tokens or discarded useful branches. The problem is therefore both an inference-control problem and a low-cost strategy-search problem.
