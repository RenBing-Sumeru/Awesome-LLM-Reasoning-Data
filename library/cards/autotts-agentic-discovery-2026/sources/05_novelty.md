The new element is the environment-driven discovery formulation. Existing adaptive methods may branch, prune, or stop intelligently, but their policy family and thresholds are chosen before evaluation. AutoTTS treats those choices as controller code to be searched, and deliberately separates expensive generation from cheap controller evaluation. This lets an LLM reason over failures in previous scaling curves and alter the policy, rather than only tune a numeric threshold inside one fixed algorithm.

The one-dimensional beta requirement is also a substantive design decision. Without it, a code-generating explorer tends to introduce many sharp, search-set-specific knobs that make a reported curve hard to reproduce and likely to overfit. The method does not claim a universal optimum over all tree-search policies, nor does it invent the underlying actions. Its contribution is a reusable discovery scaffold that jointly exposes the actions, feedback, budget constraint, and behavioral history needed to search their composition.
