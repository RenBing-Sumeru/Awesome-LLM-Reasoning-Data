- **Winner-only model:** Bayesian Plackett-Luce consumes the player set and final winner, not pitches, rationales, near-wins, or the five elimination outcomes as graded evidence. It cannot tell whether a win came from strong social reasoning, deception, parser quirks, schedule composition, or a random tie break.
- **Matchup assumption:** the ranking has no matchup-effect term, while the paper reports an 8.3-percentage-point same-provider voting effect. This directly shows that opponent/provider composition can affect outcomes and may conflict with a single context-free skill parameter.
- **Parser and selection boundary:** sidebar choices and votes depend on parser extraction. Only completed games with a parsed final selected player enter the frozen paper set, and vote analysis requires parsed votes. Counts, IDs, logs, and reasons for incomplete, failed, or unparsed attempts are unknown, so selection bias cannot be quantified.
- **Environment validity:** players receive no explicit reward and the game is intentionally low-stakes. Winning may measure persuasion, coalition behavior, provider affinity, or game-specific adaptation rather than safe, truthful, or effective behavior in real-world high-stakes settings. The benchmark can also saturate if frontier models asymptote and repeatedly trade wins.
- **Nonuniform schedule:** models were removed from the active pool over time for low performance, unreliability, or cost, while earlier games remained. Participation frequencies and matchups are therefore nonuniform; schedule sensitivity and disabling policy need explicit audit.
- **Frozen/live drift:** the paper set is 999 games/49 models, whereas the audited live source had 1,270 games/62 ranking rows/59 displayed models. Live normalization, ordering, intervals, model pool, and counts can change. Any live claim requires retrieval date, source timestamp, and ideally an immutable hash; earlier snapshots are obsolete.
- **Replay binding:** the frozen manifest identifies logs but does not bind each game to an exact engine commit, dependency lock, OpenRouter/provider configuration, immutable model snapshot, decoding settings, or retry policy. A game-ID seed covers local random draws, not deterministic sampling from closed model APIs.
- **Compute and failure disclosure:** total calls, tokens, cost, workers, memory, wall-clock time, generation retries, and a complete failed-run ledger are not disclosed. The replication ZIP reproduces published analyses but does not generate episodes, and its pipeline was not rerun for this Card.
- **Contamination boundary:** adaptive opponents reduce dependence on a fixed task bank, but no explicit model-training-overlap or decontamination audit covers the public rules prompt, engine, logs, or subsequent use of those artifacts. “Contamination-resistant” is a design claim, not proof of zero exposure.
- **License boundary:** the frozen game-log dataset is CC BY 4.0 and the engine is MIT at the pinned commit. The anonymous replication ZIP contains no license, so its reuse terms are unknown. The continuously growing live prefix is not separately versioned with an immutable license manifest per update.
- **Use boundary:** evidence supports evaluation and audit only. Benchmark ranking and behavioral findings do not establish SFT, preference, reward-model, RLVR, or agent-training suitability, and benchmark performance is not proof of data quality.
