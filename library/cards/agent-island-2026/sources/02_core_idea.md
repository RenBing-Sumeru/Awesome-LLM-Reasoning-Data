The contribution in one sentence is: Agent Island turns adaptive seven-model social play into structured full-episode evaluation data, then maps each game's participant set and terminal winner to a Bayesian skill posterior rather than scoring a fixed prompt set.

The environment is the central mechanism. Seven distinct models are sampled without replacement, although models from the same provider may meet. During elimination rounds, private sidebars create hidden local context, public pitches expose claims to all active players, and private votes plus rationales drive elimination. The final round changes the voter set: the two finalists pitch, while the five eliminated players form the jury. Elimination and final-winner ties are resolved uniformly at random. The log therefore captures more than a transcript: it records which events each player could see, which choices were parsed, the changing active/eliminated sets, and the terminal selection.

The feedback contract is mixed. Model agents supply judgments through sidebar selections, elimination votes, final votes, and rationales. A parser extracts choices; the environment deterministically tallies parsed votes, applies a random tie break when necessary, and emits one winner. A Bayesian Plackett-Luce model then assigns Gamma(1,1) skill priors and models a winner's probability as its skill divided by the sum of participant skills. Gibbs sampling with 2,000 iterations, 500 burn-in iterations, and seed 42 produces posterior skills and uncertainty. This observes winner outcomes and participation sets; it does not independently verify the truth, fairness, persuasiveness, or reasoning quality of intermediate messages.

Relative to a conventional static benchmark, the changing opponents make each episode responsive to current model behavior and avoid a finite bank of reusable answers. Relative to a pairwise chat preference surface, Agent Island records a seven-agent, multi-round state/action history and a terminal jury decision. The accepted evidence does not establish a single closest external benchmark, so this Card does not invent one. The method combines established components—multiagent simulation, voting, parsers, and Plackett-Luce ranking—around a released, versioned episode corpus and a separately growing live benchmark.

For reasoning-data research, the important chain is `rules/config + seven model IDs -> visibility-conditioned interactions -> parsed votes and five eliminations -> final jury winner -> JSON episode -> posterior ranking and vote audit`. The terminal result supports evaluation and behavioral audit only. It is not objective correctness, dense process supervision, or a demonstrated training reward.
