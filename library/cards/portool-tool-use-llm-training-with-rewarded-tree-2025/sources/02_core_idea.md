PORTool replaces independent rollouts with bounded-branching, prefix-sharing exploration. A tree node contains the policy's reasoning/tool-call step and the executed response or error. Children are distinct next-step candidates under the same interaction history, so sibling comparison controls for the prefix rather than matching approximately equal states after the fact.

After all 8 trajectories terminate in the 21-tool environment, a fixed GPT-4o evaluator labels each complete path true, false, or unable_to_answer. Each path is judged 5 times and majority-voted. Descendant outcomes are discounted by remaining length and aggregated into one prefix-consistent step importance. A bounded formatting/execution component rewards parseable calls and successful invocation, but correctness-dominant scaling prevents it from overturning the terminal outcome signal.

Optimization mixes two views of the same tree. Trajectory-relative advantage compares whole paths and supplies coarse global feedback. Fork-relative advantage compares sibling steps sharing a prefix and supplies local credit at actual decisions. PORTool does not select only successful demonstrations: success and failure branches must coexist for relative credit, although the paper does not publish an archival retention policy or the trees themselves.
