PORTool addresses credit assignment in multi-tool agentic RL. A terminal answer label says whether a complete episode succeeded, but it does not identify which intermediate call was decisive, recoverable, or harmful. Broadcasting one trajectory advantage to every token can reinforce malformed calls inside a successful episode or suppress a necessary shared step because some downstream branches fail.

The study uses 3,607 anonymized, de-identified production mobile-assistant queries across 8 domains. Of these, 1,287 are time/location-dependent and 2,320 are invariant; 3,072 are assigned to training and 535 to held-out evaluation. Policies interact with 21 rigid-schema tools and terminate through response_gen or after 6 tool-call steps. Each training query produces 8 rollouts.

The relevant data object is not an isolated trajectory but a rewarded tree: multiple complete episodes share an identical query, history, calls, and tool responses before branching into alternative next steps. Successful, failed, and unable-to-answer descendants jointly determine credit for their shared prefix. This is a strong construction recipe, but the private queries, complete trees, labels, rewards, code, checkpoints, and executable backend are not verified as public data.
