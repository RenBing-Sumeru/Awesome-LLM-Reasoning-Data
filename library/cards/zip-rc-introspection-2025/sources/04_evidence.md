The paper first checks that the in-model joint forecast is informative. At generation start, its reward–length distributions have total variation around 0.45–0.48 against 256-rollout estimates; at completion, reward prediction reaches F1 scores of 0.80–0.91 and accuracy of 0.82–0.88 across the three models. This supports using the reserved logits as a control signal rather than treating zero overhead as a purely architectural claim.

At matched generation cost and latency emphasis, ZIP-RC sampling improves Qwen3-1.7B AIME 2024 accuracy from 53.1% under majority voting to 65.8% at similar normalized cost, and improves the mixed benchmark from 91.0% to 92.2%. The paper reports analogous gains for both LFM models and smooth Pareto frontiers under compute- and latency-weighted settings. Its strongest boundary is mixed-difficulty mathematical reasoning with a trained verifier, not universal deployment without supervision.
