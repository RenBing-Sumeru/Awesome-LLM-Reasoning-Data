ZIP-RC needs post-training data with verifier-derived reward labels, so its quality depends on verifier calibration and on the relation between training and deployment trajectories. The method assumes spare or reserved vocabulary logits can be masked safely and that the auxiliary loss does not materially damage generation. Its utility estimates rely on sample independence and diversity; when extra samples are highly correlated, neither best-of-N nor adaptive branching can recover much value. Finally, normalized FLOPs and best-case latency are proxies for serving cost, so a deployment must benchmark actual batching, KV-cache behavior, CPU-side utility calculation, and tail latency.
