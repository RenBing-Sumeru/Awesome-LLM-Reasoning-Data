Read the capability map as a hypothesis about an application, not as a substitute for application evidence. Define the task in operational terms, including its inputs, outputs, constraints, and failure consequences. Then identify which fundamental abilities the survey connects to comparable domain work and ask how each ability would be observed in your case. A general benchmark can be informative without being decisive, because it may omit the conditions that make a domain task difficult. Keep one column for a model's reported benchmark performance and another for the evidence that the relevant ability is exercised under your setting. This makes backbone selection a reasoned match between requirements and evidence rather than a choice driven by one aggregate rank.

Use a capability claim only at the level supported by the evidence. If a cited application highlights a combination of abilities, do not reduce it to a single benchmark label or assume that one ability can stand in for the others. Compare models after writing the required capabilities, their relative importance, and the conditions under which they must work. Then ask whether the available evidence actually exercises those conditions. The survey helps organize this reasoning, but it does not make a domain connection a deployment guarantee. A careful reader can use its map to identify missing tests, explain why a generic score is insufficient, and choose a backbone model for stated requirements rather than for a broad reputation alone.

Before making a choice, state what result would count as adequate evidence for the application. This may require task-specific examples or operating conditions that a broad benchmark never presents. The survey can guide that specification by linking domains and capabilities, while leaving the final adequacy decision tied to the reader's actual requirements. It is therefore a framework for sharper questions, not a universal ranking of models.

Write the final judgment conditionally: name the capability combination, the domain conditions, and the evidence used. This preserves the difference between a useful selection rationale and a promise of reliable behavior in every setting. It also makes later revision possible when requirements or supporting evidence change.
