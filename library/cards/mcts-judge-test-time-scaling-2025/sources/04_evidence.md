Across the reported five base models and three benchmarks, MCTS-Judge generally improves over the corresponding vanilla and System-1 judge baselines. The headline APPS example raises DeepSeek-Coder-V2-16B-Instruct judgment accuracy from 41.0% to 80.0%; the ablation attributes 13 points to replacing weaker MCTS rewards with the simulated-execution reward and another two points to adding LLM self-assessment over UCT alone. Scaling plots vary the number of tests, simulations, depth, and rollouts and show gains that depend on the model and axis. The paper also evaluates judging without reference code and uses an LLM panel to rate trajectory qualities.

These findings support the claim that the proposed search-and-pseudo-execution procedure can improve code-judgment accuracy under the studied protocol. They do not validate the generated tests or simulated executions as ground truth. GPT-4o creates the expected outputs, while another LLM imitates execution; correlated errors can therefore reward an incorrect trajectory. Accuracy gains and LLM-rated reasoning quality are not proof that the unreleased trajectory data is complete, unbiased, or suitable for training.
