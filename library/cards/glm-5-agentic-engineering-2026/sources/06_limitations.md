The training environments are proprietary as a collection. The report gives construction processes and aggregate scale, but not repository/commit manifests, Docker images, task records, tests, web snapshots, WKG dumps, generated questions, rejection logs, or environment version hashes. “Over 90% Docker build accuracy” for terminal construction is an infrastructure statistic, not proof of task validity, semantic correctness, or reward-hacking resistance. Web-grounded terminal tasks also use the constructing agent as the first-pass evaluator, creating correlated-error risk.

Reward definitions are incomplete. Reasoning RL uses binary results from domain-specific judges or evaluation systems, but their identities, prompts, calibration, and false-positive rates are unavailable. Agentic RL exposes group-relative trajectory reward at the system level without publishing every task reward formula or service. General RL's rules, ORMs, and GRMs are unnamed. Executable tests reduce ambiguity for some tasks, but do not make every feedback signal programmatic or trustworthy.

Benchmark reuse and contamination remain major risks. Public repositories, issues, PRs, code-host snapshots, and web pages may intersect with SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench, BrowseComp, MCP-Atlas, or related tasks. No global train/evaluation membership ledger, repository/commit holdout, solution-overlap analysis, temporal cutoff, or memorization test is reported. The corrected Terminal-Bench release is evaluation-only and does not resolve training overlap.

Lineage is stage-level rather than record-level. The paper describes movement from broad sources through mid-training, SFT, several RL stages, and distillation, but no immutable chain links a source URL or commit to a constructed environment, trajectory, TITO tokens, loss mask, judge output, filter decision, training batch, and checkpoint.

There is no disclosed dedicated safety-training stage, refusal/harmfulness dataset, red-team protocol, safety benchmark suite, or incident analysis. This is an evidence gap, not proof that no safety work occurred. General correctness and emotional-intelligence RL should not be relabeled as safety alignment.

Finally, evaluation and deployment artifacts must not inflate training disclosure. OpenHands, Terminus-2, Claude Code, MCP-Atlas, and CC-Bench-V2 are evaluation scaffolds; vLLM, SGLang, Transformers, KTransformers, and xLLM serve the released model. Neither category reproduces the private training environments, rewards, data, or production launch configuration.
