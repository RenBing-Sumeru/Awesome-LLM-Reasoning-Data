The paper's evidence is primarily benchmark evidence: reported model accuracies on Omni-MATH, breakdowns by topic and difficulty, comparisons to earlier math benchmarks, and analysis showing that top models still leave substantial headroom on olympiad-level problems. The project page and Hugging Face release provide public artifacts rather than only paper-only claims.

The decisive row-level evidence is whether an individual model answer is accepted by the benchmark's scoring route: rule-based answer checking for mechanically comparable outputs, or Omni-Judge for semantic answer equivalence. Aggregate scores are not themselves reusable labels; they depend on answer extraction, prompt policy, model version, sampling budget, and judge version. Public release also creates contamination risk for later model evaluations.
