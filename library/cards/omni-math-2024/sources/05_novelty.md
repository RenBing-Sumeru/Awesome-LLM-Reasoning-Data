The prior baseline is the family of math reasoning benchmarks such as GSM8K, MATH, and OlympiadBench, where either difficulty, coverage, taxonomy, or answer judgment can be too limited for current frontier models. Omni-MATH changes the evaluation surface by emphasizing olympiad-level breadth, subdomain/difficulty structure, and a released answer judge for less mechanical responses.

The direction signal is a move from single-number hard-math accuracy toward audited topic-level and difficulty-level diagnosis. The quality signal is the presence of public paper, project, dataset, repository, and judge artifacts. What is not new: collecting math problems, reporting model accuracy, and using automated answer checking are established practices. Before reuse, inspect source licensing, dataset revision, split policy, judge failure modes, prompt leakage, and whether the target model has seen public Omni-MATH items.
