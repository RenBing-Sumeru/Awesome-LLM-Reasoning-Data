**Executor and verifier risk.** The `paper` branch executes model-generated Python through raw `exec`/`eval` in worker processes. AST parsing, a forbidden-module/keyword list, process separation, and a ten-second timeout are not a hardened sandbox; the official README warns against production use. Two matching executions can falsely accept latent nondeterminism, state dependence, or delayed side effects. Python equality and hidden I/O tests verify observed behavior only on sampled cases, not intended semantics or general correctness.

**Shortcut-bearing curricula.** Appendix E reports that generated global variables can leak correct-answer information; final experiments retain them because removing them reduced performance. Comments and docstrings act as a proposer-to-solver communication channel, and removing them also hurts performance. These are direct author observations. The further concern—that learnability reward may favor answer-bearing side channels or verifier-specific shortcuts—is a curator inference. Since every valid task enters the buffer even at zero or perfect solver success, buffer admission itself does not enforce a useful-difficulty band.

**Safety and autonomy.** The paper reports concerning chains of thought from Llama-3.1-8B and calls for oversight and safety-aware training. An autonomous proposer can create unsafe code or content, while the denylist is not a general behavioral safety policy. The released executor should therefore be run only inside a hardened, disposable environment with strict resource, filesystem, process, and network isolation.

**Release and lineage limits.** Public seed files are initialization artifacts, not the full online corpus. Missing fixed artifacts include successful and failed proposer/solver completions, eight-attempt difficulty samples, exceptions/timeouts, reward records, evolving buffers, random seeds, checkpoint hashes, and complete run-to-W&B mappings. Five checkpoints exist, but their pages have no model cards; model-weight license, intended use, safety evaluation, and checkpoint-specific provenance are unknown. The repository has no immutable release or tag.

**Evaluation and contamination limits.** No semantic overlap analysis was found against HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench, CruxEval, math benchmarks, or pretrained-model data. “OOD” means outside the explicit AZR curriculum, not verified pretraining decontamination. No error bars, independent RL reruns, seed-level variance, or total failed-run compute are reported. Benchmark gains cannot certify task novelty, executor reliability, release completeness, or safe reuse.
