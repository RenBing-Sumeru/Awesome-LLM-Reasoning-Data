Table 1 evaluates the SFT and RL checkpoints on BrowseComp-EN, BrowseComp-ZH, xbench-DeepSearch, GAIA, and HLE. The SFT model reports 24.4, 28.3, 61.7, 66.0, and 23.9; the RL model reports 35.3, 44.1, 73.7, 74.1, and 30.6. These are author-reported pass@1-style results under the paper's tool and judge setup, not independent reproductions (Table 1; §5.1).

The within-model comparison gives the clearest supported effect: RL adds 10.9 points on BrowseComp-EN, 15.8 on BrowseComp-ZH, 12.0 on xbench-DeepSearch, 8.1 on GAIA, and 6.7 on HLE over the listed SFT checkpoint. Figure 6 further reports smaller pass@3 than pass@1 gains on simpler xbench/GAIA tasks, while both metrics rise more on BrowseComp; this is an interpretation of sampling behavior, not proof of a new reasoning capability (§5.4, Figure 6).

For scale and budget, final-paper §3.4 reports more than 30,000 SailorFog-QA-V2 instruction-tuning pairs, graph components averaging about 30 nodes and degree 2.5, and structural coverage from chains to cycles and dense clusters. Section 5.4 raises context from V1's 32k to 128k and the maximum ReAct rounds to 100; nearly 90% of correctly solved instances finish within 64k. These figures do not disclose complete-trajectory count, rollout attempts, API calls, or compute.

The main evidence limitations are material. The paper reports no training-seed variance, no ablation isolating the 30K-plus corpus from the V1 SailorFog-QA and IterBench mixture, no public reward audit, no decontamination analysis, and no independent simulator-to-live transfer reproduction. The official V2 tree remained exactly README plus two images from the 2025-09-17 release commit through the inspected 2026-02-27 commit, so experimental scale is not public release scale.
