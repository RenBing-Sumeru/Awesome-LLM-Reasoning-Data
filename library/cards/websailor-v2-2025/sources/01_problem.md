WebSailor-V2 addresses two linked bottlenecks in training deep information-seeking agents: task generators that expose too narrow a range of uncertainty, and live-web reinforcement-learning systems whose latency, failures, and changing outputs make large-scale rollout collection unstable. The stable bibliographic boundary is the ICLR 2026 Poster record; arXiv:2509.13305v1 was submitted on 16 September 2025, while the final OpenReview version adds authors and a dataset-statistics section.

The paper uses three linked data objects. SailorFog-QA-V2 supplies synthetic question-answer instruction pairs sampled from dense web-derived graphs. Unspecified high-performing open-source models solve those tasks, and rejection sampling selects successful ReAct episodes for SFT. The current policy then generates complete ReAct rollouts in an offline-Wikipedia simulator or a managed live-web environment, with one terminal scalar reward per episode (Paper §§2–4; Appendix B).

This is an `environment_agent_trajectory_data` paper because the training object includes a task, thought/action/observation history, tool-mediated environment state, terminal answer, and trajectory-level reward. It is not a public dataset or replayable-environment release: the final paper reports more than 30,000 SailorFog-QA-V2 instruction-tuning pairs, but the official V2 directory contains only a README and two images, with no V2 records, trajectories, training code, simulator, reward implementation, or model link.

The Card therefore treats WebSailor-V2 as a construction and audit reference. It can reach L4 descriptive depth because the paper specifies the high-level pipeline and tools, but canonical metadata remains `partial` and `L3_summary_ready`: the reward predicate, complete corpus, successful and failed rollout populations, record-level lineage, and replay stack are unavailable.
