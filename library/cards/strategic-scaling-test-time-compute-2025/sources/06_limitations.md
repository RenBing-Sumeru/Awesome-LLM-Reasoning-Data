The theoretical separation assumes independent response correctness and an oracle threshold that identifies correctness exactly or approximately; learned PRMs and LLM judges can violate that assumption. Early false-positive elimination can freeze a wrong answer, while false negatives waste budget. Oracle scales are not directly comparable across tasks, and the best threshold gamma may use expert knowledge or held-out tuning.

Compute is primarily counted as generations, although response lengths and oracle costs differ; token-budget experiments narrow but do not remove this accounting issue. MATH-500-Hard is constructed from model-specific failure within a budget and should not be treated as an intrinsic difficulty label. The paper does not release per-query allocations, raw scores, rejected responses, prompts as a versioned bundle, seeds, or an executable environment. Dataset licenses, decontamination controls, and prompt overlap audits are not established in the Card metadata. Benchmark improvements cannot substitute for these missing audit records.
