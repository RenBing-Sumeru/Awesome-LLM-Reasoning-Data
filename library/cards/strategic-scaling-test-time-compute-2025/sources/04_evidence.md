The paper evaluates MATH-500, AIME25, and 479 LiveCodeBench items, comparing adaptive allocation with uniform Best-of-N and a tuned two-stage baseline under shared average budgets. It reports improved coverage or accuracy in many configurations, including a 7.41-point LiveCodeBench coverage gain for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct at average budget 8 and larger gains in some other model-oracle settings. Results are averaged over four runs, and ablations cover allocation rules, thresholds, token budgets, and streaming queries.

These results support the narrower claim that adaptive cross-query allocation can outperform the studied allocation baselines under the paper's generators, oracle choices, and budget accounting. They do not establish that PRM or LLM-judge labels are correct, that the derived MATH-500-Hard subsets are model-independent, or that unreleased allocation traces are high-quality reusable data. The hard subsets are defined using whether the evaluated model succeeds within specified compute, so their difficulty status is tied to that model and protocol. The authoritative public source checked here is https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12721; no official reusable trace or code artifact was confirmed from the accepted links.
