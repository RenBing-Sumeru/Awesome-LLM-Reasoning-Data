Earlier test-time scaling commonly chooses a per-query sampling budget or applies the same Best-of-N budget to every item. Related multi-query methods use an initial uniform exploration stage and then commit to a fixed allocation. This work instead treats the entire query batch as an online allocation problem: solved-looking queries leave an active set, and their remaining budget is reassigned while evidence accumulates. The entropy variant further uses diversity of observed answer patterns to prioritize difficult instances.

The direction signal is the explicit separation of generator, allocation policy, aggregation rule, and reward oracle. It shows why a test-time trace schema needs cross-instance fields—global budget, allocation rounds, elimination events, and unused-budget transfers—not only per-response scores. It also exposes that one headline method can rely on PRM, LLM judge, self-consistency, or execution depending on the task.

Bandit theory, Best-of-N generation, self-consistency, PRMs, and execution verification are not individually new. The novelty is their cross-query allocation formulation and adaptive policy. Reported benchmark gains are a policy-evaluation signal, not evidence that the underlying responses, oracle scores, or logs form a validated dataset.
