The paper Strategic Scaling of Test-Time Compute: A Bandit Learning Approach (arXiv:2506.12721; ICLR 2026) studies allocation across a batch of evaluation queries rather than how much compute to spend inside one query. Uniform Best-of-N gives every query the same number of generations even though easy, hard-but-solvable, and effectively unsolvable items benefit differently from additional samples. The decision problem is therefore to maximize coverage or final-answer accuracy under one shared generation budget without knowing query difficulty in advance.

For this atlas, the important object is not a new training corpus. It is an inference-time allocation trace: query identifier, generated responses, reward-oracle scores or answer clusters, the current best response, active/eliminated status, per-round and cumulative compute, stopping reason, and final selected answer. The paper evaluates such policies but does not provide a versioned prompt manifest, per-query allocation logs, or a reusable trace release. Any claim about data quality or reusable lineage must therefore remain unknown.
