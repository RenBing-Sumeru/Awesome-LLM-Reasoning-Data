The contribution is a reproducible longitudinal audit of 24,291 NLG papers (1952–2025): precision-oriented regular expressions convert paper text into evaluation-modality and criterion signals, then reveal how human evaluation, LLM judges, and vocabulary changed. The released dataset contains paper IDs, metadata, and extracted signals rather than copyrighted full text; code reconstructs the corpus from ACL sources. The key audit finding is not that LLM judges replace people, but that their rapid adoption leaves a sizable unvalidated segment requiring human calibration.
