As automatic metrics and LLM judges make NLG evaluation cheap, it is unclear whether they are replacing human evaluation and which criteria the field is actually using. That uncertainty makes it hard to judge whether fast automated feedback is calibrated rather than merely convenient.

The paper audits this question with a longitudinal, paper-level analysis of ACL Anthology NLG research. It identifies human, automatic, and LLM-judge evaluation signals and tracks their use by year, venue, task, criterion, and annotation method; its output is an audit dataset and baseline, not a new evaluator.
