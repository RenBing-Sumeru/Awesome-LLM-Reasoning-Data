The prior-work baseline is fragmented comparison: MetaMathQA, OpenMathInstruct-2, OrcaMath, MMIQC, ScaleQuest, and related work introduce useful agents but use different problem generators, solution teachers, students, filters, and evaluation conditions. FLAMES changes the unit of analysis from “which published dataset scores highest?” to “which pipeline factor changes student behavior when the rest of the scaffold is fixed?”

Two agent mechanisms are new within that scaffold. Taxonomy-Based Key Concepts removes dependence on one seed problem by expanding a curated topic taxonomy into concepts and then problems. Distraction Insertion preserves the original answer while inserting an irrelevant detail, targeting robustness to misleading context. The final record type itself—problem plus teacher solution—is conventional; the new data contribution is the explicit mixture of 50% Suggester-Editor, 20% IQC, 20% Taxonomy Key Concepts, and 10% Distraction Insertion chosen from controlled comparisons.

The quality-control study is also a direction signal: it demonstrates a measurable coverage-versus-verification trade-off and documents that a plausible solvability judge rejects 30.2% of human-written MATH500. However, exact deduplication, self-consistency, learned solvability judgments, reward-model reranking, teacher distillation, and full-parameter SFT are existing components. FLAMES is primarily a factorized experimental integration plus two targeted agents, not a new mathematical verifier.

Before reuse, a builder needs the unavailable records and implementation, exact inherited prompts, taxonomy and seed lineage, accepted/rejected candidates, answer extraction rules, artifact licenses, stable model revisions, and a correctness audit independent of Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct. Without those objects, the paper is a strong recipe/audit reference but not a directly reusable open dataset.
