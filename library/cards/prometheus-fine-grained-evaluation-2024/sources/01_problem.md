Using GPT-4 to evaluate long-form responses is costly, version-dependent, and difficult to reproduce, while generic reward models usually return only holistic preferences and cannot explain scores under user-defined rubrics. Fixed benchmark judges transfer poorly to long-tail dimensions such as child readability or argumentative completeness. The paper asks whether large-scale rubric-conditioned feedback data can train an open 13B model to approach GPT-4 and human evaluation when given references and scoring criteria.
