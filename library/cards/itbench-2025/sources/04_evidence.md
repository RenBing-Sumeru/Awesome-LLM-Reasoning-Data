All results below are author-reported and were not independently reproduced. In the final paper's SRE evaluation, 42 scenarios are divided into 21 with traces and 21 without, with 10 runs per scenario/model. Table 4 reports GPT-4o at **13.81% diagnosis pass@1** and **11.43% mitigation pass@1**. The frequently summarized 11.4% figure is mitigation, not diagnosis. Models were allowed up to 128K context, and GPT-4o used checkpoint `2024-11-20`; conclusions are conditional on the paper's model versions, tool wrappers, environment state, retry policy, and scenario ground truth (Paper §4.1–4.2, Table 4, pp. 6–7).

CISO evaluation covers 50 scenarios with 8 runs per model, while FinOps covers 10 scenarios across data-insight, anomaly-detection, and alert-driven tasks (Paper §4.3–4.4, Tables 5–6, p. 8). These experiments show that the environment can exercise multiple operational domains and expose low success rates. They do not independently validate the scenario ground truth, prove that released trajectories are suitable for training, or establish that a higher benchmark score reflects better data quality.

The paper's trajectory analysis provides failure evidence rather than only leaderboard values. Instrumented runs record planning-agent and tool input/output, including ReAct thoughts. Figure 5 and §5.1 identify invalid tool choice, incorrect arguments, repeated calls, syntax errors, and execution failures. Appendix examples include both successful and unsuccessful CISO episodes; one unsuccessful case reaches maximum retry and leaves invalid Rego. These observations motivate retaining negative and interrupted episodes, but example trajectories are not a complete public corpus.

Official release inspection adds an important negative result. The ITBench-Trajectories card describes 105 complete SRE trajectories, yet the pinned repository tree contains **105 `session.jsonl` files but only 90 `agent_output.json` and 90 `judge_output.json` files**. Fifteen session directories therefore lack both final output and judge artifacts. Scored failures are also retained: an inspected `Scenario-1/1/judge_output.json` reports zero across core diagnosis scores while preserving ground truth, prediction, calculations, and justification. The discrepancy supports audit and failure-analysis use; it also prevents interpreting “105 sessions” as “105 complete scored episodes.”

Finally, the paper states that only **11 of the final 102 scenarios were public and 91 were held for submitted-agent evaluation**. This is a paper-era public/held evaluation-access split, not a model-training split. Lite's `scenarios` packaging and Trajectories' Hugging Face split named `train` likewise do not establish a randomized or leakage-controlled train/evaluation partition.
