**Inputs and construction.** The final paper assembles **42 SRE, 50 CISO, and 10 FinOps scenarios**. SRE inputs derive from incidents observed in the authors' SaaS products, CISO inputs from CIS benchmarks, and FinOps inputs from FinOps Foundation domains/capabilities. Each scenario is encoded as an `M/E/T/D` tuple; metadata includes name, description, domain, class, complexity, deployment descriptors, and ground truth. SRE ground truth can specify diagnosis entities, fault propagation and conditions, and plausible mitigation actions. Incident-level sampling, anonymization, and the exact selection rule are unknown.

**Provisioning and interaction.** The runner may select scenarios subject to agent type/level filters, provisions an isolated testbed, deploys the scenario, and injects a fault or condition. The agent receives partial telemetry or policy/cost evidence through an observation proxy, then emits domain tool calls or stop. Paper baselines use CrewAI with planning/reflection and in-context examples; GPT-4o, Llama-family, Granite-family, and other models are evaluated. SRE evaluation uses 42 scenarios—21 with traces and 21 without—with 10 runs per scenario/model; CISO uses 50 scenarios with 8 runs/model; FinOps uses 10 scenarios with task-dependent repetition. Context can reach 128K tokens; most temperatures and complete retry/tool/time budgets are unknown.

**Feedback and outputs.** After the agent stops, the benchmark records final system state or a structured result and compares it with scenario ground truth using domain metrics. The run lifecycle then deletes the scenario and aggregates results. Experiment instrumentation logs planning-agent and tool inputs/outputs, including ReAct thoughts, and the paper analyzes both successful and unsuccessful runs. The paper does not establish that all of those experiment logs were released.

**Later offline releases.** ITBench-Lite packages 65 static `scenarios`—35 SRE, 15 FinOps, 15 CISO—under Apache-2.0. SRE snapshots include alerts, Prometheus metrics, ground truth, Kubernetes events/objects, and OpenTelemetry logs/traces; they intentionally omit live observability, runtime nondeterminism, interactive debugging/human-in-the-loop behavior, and active remediation. ITBench-Trajectories, under CC-BY-NC-4.0, declares 105 GPT-OSS-120B SRE sessions from 35 scenarios × 3 runs. Expected run files are `session.jsonl`, `agent_output.json`, `judge_output.json`, and optional generated code.

Reproduction should pin the final paper, framework commit `73040c0539b39f11e63abd4e848316c841559487` / version 1.4.1, Lite revision `d0916b08ba421ce5e672e9ad68aa947d938dfef0`, trajectory revision above, scenario configuration, environment images/dependencies, model endpoint/checkpoint, prompts, budgets, evaluator configuration, and retained-file manifest. The exact paper-experiment commit, image digests, seeds, dependency lock, deterministic replay guarantee, and mapping from 42 paper SRE scenarios to the later 35 are unknown. No optimizer or policy update is disclosed.
