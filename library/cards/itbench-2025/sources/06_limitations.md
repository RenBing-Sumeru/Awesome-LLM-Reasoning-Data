- **Version and lineage drift:** The early arXiv paper reports 94 scenarios, the final PMLR paper reports 102, current framework version 1.4.1 has later scenario/telemetry/schema/ground-truth fixes, and Lite/Trajectories contain different populations. The paper-experiment commit and the mapping from 42 final-paper SRE scenarios to 35 later SRE scenarios are unknown.
- **Reset and replay:** The paper documents provisioning, deployment, injection, evaluation, deletion, and aggregation, which provides run isolation but not deterministic replay. Lite is intentionally static and omits live observability, nondeterminism, interactive debugging, human-in-the-loop behavior, and active remediation. Exact images, dependency locks, seeds, and deterministic replay equivalence are unknown.
- **Feedback fidelity:** Domain metrics can award partial credit without proving the desired operational outcome. The later Gemini semantic judge can produce false positives, false negatives, or version drift. Public ground truth/evaluators invite verifier gaming, and the paper does not report an independent evaluator-error audit.
- **Release completeness:** The Trajectories card claims 105 complete sessions, while the official tree has only 90 output/judge pairs. The reasons for the 15 missing pairs—and whether authors classify them as failed, interrupted, or corrupt—are unknown. The Hugging Face viewer also reports a schema inconsistency in `payload.summary`, so raw-file parsing and schema validation are necessary.
- **Split and contamination:** The 11 public / 91 held paper-era division is evaluation access, not a training split. Lite's `scenarios` and Trajectories' `train` are packaging labels. No randomized leakage-controlled partition, model-pretraining overlap audit, or formal decontamination protocol is documented; training on public snapshots/traces can contaminate later ITBench evaluation.
- **Safety and privacy:** Agents can emit destructive commands, and untrusted evaluation infrastructure can inject instructions or code. Containerized agents and self-contained Kubernetes testbeds reduce risk but do not replace least privilege, egress control, credential rotation, or code/log scanning. Runtime manifests may contain cluster credentials and service URLs. Incident anonymization, customer/user consent, PII scanning, and redistribution review are unknown.
- **Licensing:** The framework and ITBench-Lite use Apache-2.0, while ITBench-Trajectories uses CC-BY-NC-4.0. Rights for incident-derived evidence, CIS/FinOps inputs, generated code, paper text, and upstream dependencies are not unified; a user must apply component-level terms rather than assuming one license covers the family.
- **Generalization:** Reported scores cover the authors' constructed SRE, CISO, and FinOps testbeds and model/tool settings. They do not establish reliability in production systems, prove safety under real credentials, or certify the later release as high-quality training data.
