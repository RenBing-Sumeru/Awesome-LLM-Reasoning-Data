The prior baseline for IT operations is a static task, incident description, or benchmark answer checked without executing a full agent-environment loop. ITBench changes the data/evaluation object to an `M/E/T/D` tuple plus a provisioned episode: partial observations, tool actions and responses, terminal output/state, stop, and domain feedback. This makes environment transition and operational side effects part of the reasoning-data contract rather than external implementation detail.

A second concrete change is breadth under one lifecycle. SRE diagnosis/mitigation, CISO policy/compliance, and FinOps insight/anomaly/alert tasks share runner-level provisioning, triggering, interaction, evaluation, cleanup, and aggregation, while keeping domain-specific tools and ground truth. The paper also separates an agent's stop decision from the success predicate, which is a useful design signal for trajectory datasets because a syntactically completed episode can still leave the system in the wrong state.

The later official artifacts extend the paper in two different directions rather than forming one homogeneous dataset. ITBench-Lite converts selected scenarios into 65 static diagnostic objects; ITBench-Trajectories serializes 105 SRE session logs with tool-level ReAct interaction and mixed structured/semantic episode feedback. The observed preservation of zero-score and incomplete sessions is valuable for failure analysis, but the missing 15 output/judge pairs show why release-scale claims must be checked at file level.

The work does not newly invent Kubernetes testbeds, observability tools, CrewAI/ReAct, programmatic metrics, LLM semantic judges, or synthetic faults. The 102-scenario scale and multi-model study are breadth, not independent quality evidence. Before reuse, researchers must verify the cross-release scenario mapping, version-pinned ground truth, complete file manifest, judge configuration, reset/replay properties, contamination policy, privacy/secret handling, and license boundary; those checks determine whether the object is fit for evaluation, training, or audit.
