For the assigned `data_construction_open_release_recipes` track, the paper is useful as a compact recipe for converting model uncertainty into a trace-selection field across three construction stages. It helps a builder ask a concrete question before scaling data volume: should the retained object be a demonstration, a correctness-filtered candidate, or a successful/failed rollout, and which independent predicate authorizes it to enter training?

An auditable SFT record should preserve the source-record ID and revision, prompt, full response, scoring model/tokenizer revision, per-token entropy or a reproducible summary, HES variant and parameters, rank, retention threshold, accepted/rejected decision, and domain/length stratum. RFT additionally needs all 32 candidates, the exact correctness decision, per-query versus global pool membership, k, prompt coverage, and the final selected set. RL needs rollout logits or reproducible entropy summaries, success/failure reward, positive/negative sampler, full and reduced batch membership, policy revision, temperature, generation budget, and training step.

The recipe can support SFT subset selection, post-correctness RFT ranking, and GRPO batch composition. It should not be reused as an answer verifier or promoted to a reward without separately testing whether the policy can exploit uncertainty, verbosity, tokenization, or calibration artifacts. The most informative follow-up release would include both accepted and rejected records, because the RL ablation shows that the distribution of failed trajectories materially changes the result.

For atlas users, the paper is also a good audit example: downstream gains can motivate a selection hypothesis, but they do not establish data quality. A reusable release would need immutable lineage, complete verifier decisions, score computation code, cost measurements, decontamination evidence, and licenses for the transformed records.
