For token position t, the paper computes entropy over the model vocabulary, H_t = -sum_j P_t(j) log P_t(j). Its main relative HES retains the top p fraction of token positions within one response and sums their entropies; the default is p = 0.005, i.e. the top 0.5% of that response's tokens. An absolute variant sums positions with H_t > 1.6, and AvgHE divides HES by the number of selected positions. The selector uses the cumulative high-uncertainty mass, not the mean uncertainty of the whole trace.

The same score is consumed differently at each stage. SFT ranks all demonstrations and keeps a top fraction (principally 20% for an efficiency test or 80% for a denoising test). RFT first removes incorrect candidates, then either keeps the highest-HES correct responses per query (k = 2, 4, or 8) or constructs a volume-matched global pool. RL divides rollouts into successful and failed pools, selects high-HES positives, and samples negatives; the best reported composition is high-HES positives plus random failures.

HES is therefore a selection signal rather than a correctness verifier, scalar task reward, or semantic quality label. The feedback contract is mixed: token-distribution uncertainty ranks trajectories, while an undisclosed answer checker supplies the success/failure predicate for RFT and RL. Keeping those two signals separate is essential because the paper's own entropy analysis shows that incorrect trajectories can have higher HES than correct ones.
