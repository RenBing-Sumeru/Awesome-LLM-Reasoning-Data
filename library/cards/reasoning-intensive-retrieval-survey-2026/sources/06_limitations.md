Inferential relevance is difficult to define and measure cleanly. A passage can be semantically close but insufficient, while a useful passage can appear distant until combined with other evidence. The survey also aggregates varied domains and modalities, so conclusions from one benchmark should not automatically be generalized to another. Its categories do not remove the need to inspect the original task definition. A system may use language-model inference, structured signals, or reranking, yet the evaluation may still be dominated by topic matching. Results also depend on evidence granularity, corpus coverage, compute budget, and downstream use. Avoid concluding that a method has solved reasoning-intensive retrieval from a single metric without checking these conditions.
