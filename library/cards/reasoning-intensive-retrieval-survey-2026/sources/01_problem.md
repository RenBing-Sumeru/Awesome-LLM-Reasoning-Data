Many retrieval systems rank passages by topical or semantic similarity, but some questions require evidence connected through an unstated inferential link. This ACL 2026 survey defines that setting as reasoning-intensive retrieval and collects the benchmarks, retrieval stages, and open challenges needed to study it. It synthesizes prior work rather than releasing a new retrieval dataset. The key reader question is whether a result is relevant only because it shares words or topic with a query, or because it becomes useful after inference. That boundary matters for evaluation and design: adding more semantically similar passages may not solve a failure when the system must recognize an indirect connection.
