Collection evidence is reported under the paper's own models and tau-bench-derived setup. Table 1 reports 70% Phase-1 task-generation success versus 28% without agentic feedback, 67% Phase-2 simulation success, trajectories ranging from 1 to 29 turns, and averages of 7 tool calls and 6 user turns. These statistics support the claim that iterative validation and simulated interaction produced accepted episodes; they do not measure false-positive verifier rates or public-release completeness.

The paper's human audit sampled 200 trajectories. Table 2 reports 99.4% query clarity, 70.2% sufficient information, 77.4% achievability, 0% fatal error, 0.75% erroneous step, 0% self-correction, and 99% completion. Appendix C.1 says professional experts inspect query properties, plans, responses, tool calls, errors, and completion. The sampling procedure, number and qualifications of annotators, inter-annotator agreement, and adjudication are not disclosed, so these percentages are author-reported audit evidence rather than independently calibrated labels.

Historical model results show that the whole training recipe can produce competitive tool-use models. On the BFCL leaderboard snapshot dated 2025-04-03, Table 3 reports xLAM-2-70b-fc-r at 78.19 overall and 75.12 multi-turn, xLAM-2-8b-fc-r at 72.83 overall and 69.25 multi-turn, and GPT-4o FC at 41.0 multi-turn. On tau-bench with at least five trials, default naive user, think tool only, and no prompt optimization, Table 4 reports xLAM-2-70b-fc-r at 67.1 Retail, 45.2 Airline, and 56.2 overall, compared with GPT-4o at 52.9 overall and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (new) at 60.1 overall. These are historical benchmark results under stated conditions, not proof that the public 5k subset alone caused the gains.

The trajectory-length ablation is more directly tied to APIGen-MT construction. With the trajectory count held fixed, removing reverse-recombination data reduces Airline from 45% to 33% and Retail from 64% to 61% (Figure 3). This supports the paper's claim that longer, recombined trajectories contribute useful diversity in these two domains; it does not establish the mechanism outside the tested environments.

Mixture evidence limits attribution. Table 5 reports a Qwen-32B Retail+Airline behavioral-cloning condition at BFCL 65.0 versus a 57.8 base, while the full xLAM mixture reaches 75.8. Appendix A.2 says APIGen-MT is trained jointly with APIGen and other xLAM/ActionStudio data, and Appendix B studies APIGen-MT scales up to 8k. Therefore neither the final xLAM scores nor the 8k ablation can be attributed to the gated 5k public subset without the missing mixture and sample-mapping records.

Artifact inspection verifies a different class of evidence: the HF card declares 5,000 English rows, gated access, CC-BY-NC-4.0, one `train` split, and the ShareGPT-like fields; the file pointer fixes the checked JSON hash. It also verifies that construction-only blueprints, ground truth, state diffs, rewards, verification logs, failures, reset state, and replay metadata are absent. Benchmark performance does not repair those release gaps or prove data quality.
