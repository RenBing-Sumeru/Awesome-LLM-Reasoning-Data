The analysis first shows that the best response-length regime depends on task difficulty. For Qwen2.5 tag models, the reported best accuracy moves from low effort on GSM8K (95.53%) to medium effort on MATH500 (91.48%) and high effort on AIME2024 (42.00%). The paper also measures the number and proportion of erroneous reasoning rounds and reports that both rise as effort increases, providing a concrete mechanism for why indiscriminate length expansion can be harmful.

In the end-to-end study, the authors state that Qwen2.5-32B-TOPS outperforms other reported distillation-based 32B o1-like models over the mathematical benchmarks and is comparable to its QwQ-32B-Preview teacher. They additionally compare random correct-response selection with selecting the shortest correct one, isolating the importance of the filtering rule. The evidence supports a task-dependent-length claim on the stated models and math data; it does not show that shortest-correct selection is available or optimal for arbitrary domains without a reliable correctness signal.
