Longer chains of thought are often treated as a universal way to spend more inference compute. The paper shows why that rule is unsafe: responses with more reasoning rounds can contain more erroneous intermediate steps, and training or deploying with an excessively long effort can reduce accuracy, especially on easier tasks. The useful decision is not whether to make every response longer, but how much reasoning effort a particular problem warrants.

The paper asks how a reasoning model can learn a task-dependent test-time length policy. Its central record is a problem paired with several responses generated under low, medium, and high effort, their length and correctness, and the selected shortest correct trace. This record changes a fixed-length distillation target into an allocation target: preserve enough computation for hard questions while not teaching the model to add redundant or error-prone reasoning to easy ones.
