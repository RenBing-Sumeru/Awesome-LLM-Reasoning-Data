The first stage, format imitation, uses 1.3K NuminaMath seed problems and 3.9K QwQ-32B-Preview responses under three effort levels to train a tag model. In the second stage, that model generates one response at each effort for an additional 50K NuminaMath problems. For a problem with more than one correct answer, the construction retains the shortest correct response; combined with low-effort seed responses, this yields a reported thinking-optimal collection of about 26K samples.

The final stage performs supervised fine-tuning on this selected collection. The main system is based on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, with additional experiments on LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct. The paper evaluates GSM8K, MATH500, and AIME2024, reports mean accuracy over five random seeds, uses temperature 1.0 and a 16,384-token maximum generation length, and compares against QwQ-32B-Preview and same-base distillation models. The selection relies on knowing correctness during construction, whereas deployed generation only receives the learned effort behavior.
