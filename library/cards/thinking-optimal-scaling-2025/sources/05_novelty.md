Previous long-chain distillation typically consumes a fixed distribution of teacher responses and treats length as a generally beneficial feature. TOPS changes the record-selection decision: a problem is observed under several controlled effort labels, and the consumer is trained on the shortest response that passes the correctness contract. This makes response length a problem-conditioned allocation variable rather than a side effect of a teacher's default behavior.

The contribution is not merely pruning traces after they are generated. The selected traces teach a model to imitate distinct effort modes and then to internalize an effort-sensitive response policy through self-improvement. The closest excluded alternative would only impose a length cap at inference; this work changes what supervised examples are chosen and the behavior that is trained. Reuse requires checking whether correctness filtering and effort labels retain the same meaning in the target domain.
