TOPS needs a correctness signal to select the shortest correct trace, which is readily available for the reported mathematical construction but may be costly or ambiguous for open-ended tasks. It uses three discrete effort levels and one response per level in its stated main generation setup, so the learned policy may miss finer-grained budgets or useful rare alternatives. The paper's token-length analysis also does not replace measurement of real latency, memory, or tool-use cost; those costs must be assessed when transferring the method.
