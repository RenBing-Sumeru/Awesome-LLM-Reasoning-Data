Instruction-following models rely on reward models or LLM judges to select training responses, yet existing meta-evaluation usually provides only two responses and one holistic preference, covers few constraint types, and cannot test full ranking over a candidate set. This differs from rejection sampling, DPO, and RL, where a judge selects among multiple rollouts. The paper asks how to build a meta-benchmark spanning single-turn, multi-turn, and system-prompt steerability and assess both local constraint recognition and global ranking.
