Comparisons across open and proprietary judges show that many models strong on traditional RewardBench cannot reliably recover multi-response rankings, often overlooking local constraints or confusing language quality with compliance. IF-RewardBench scores correlate more strongly with the downstream gains obtained when those judges guide instruction optimization, suggesting that listwise preference graphs better match training use than isolated pairs. This supports external validity, although correlation is not causal proof and downstream algorithms and models remain limited.
