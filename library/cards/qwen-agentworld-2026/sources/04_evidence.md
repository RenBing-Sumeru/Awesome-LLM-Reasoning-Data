The official [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24597) and its [HTML version](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.24597v1) establish the next-observation data object, seven environments, more-than-10M aggregate trajectory claim, three trajectory channels, CPT/SFT/GSPO sequence, filters, SFT rejection sampling, and hybrid RL reward. The paper reports 7,094 retained SFT trajectories, a 92,308-trajectory RL train pool, a 9:1 rubric-to-rule reward mix, and observed self-praise reward hacking with three mitigations.

The official [Qwen-AgentWorld repository](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen-AgentWorld) describes its open-source release as the 35B model weights and AgentWorldBench. The official [35B model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-AgentWorld-35B-A3B) identifies Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base as the base and states that no outputs from external API services were included in the training pipeline. The official [AgentWorldBench dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Qwen/AgentWorldBench) exposes one test split with about 2.17K rows and fields including task, id, prompt, response, current_prompt, system_str, turn_idx, and total_turns. The [Qwen release page](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen-agentworld) likewise presents it as an evaluation benchmark paired with real observations.

These official artifacts do not release the more-than-10M training trajectories, SFT candidates, RL pool, judge identity or weights, complete executable-verifier mapping, or a 397B checkpoint. Reported benchmark scores therefore evidence model behavior under the stated protocol, not training-data quality or reproducibility.
