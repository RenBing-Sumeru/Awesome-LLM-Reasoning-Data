The paper names three trajectory channels. Dedicated infrastructure runs synthesized tasks against container, MCP, terminal, Android, browser, and desktop backends. Open interaction traces contribute terminal recordings, agent tool-call logs, and repository execution traces after a multi-agent cleaning pipeline. In-house foundation-model SFT trajectories supply a third channel. CPT also adds specialized corpora spanning industrial control, cybersecurity, law, medicine, finance, current affairs, and encyclopedic knowledge. The paper says CPT, SFT, and RL pools are strictly disjoint, but no membership manifests are released.

All records are normalized to the same action-observation schema. Filters drop trajectories with fewer than two turns, MCP/SWE calls to undeclared tools, broken GUI states, empty actions, retry cycles, and GUI turns with no state change. CPT further masks low-information observation tokens using overlap, novelty, Jaccard, and length-ratio statistics while retaining masked turns as context. One random target turn is sampled from each training trajectory.

For SFT, one of ten AutoResearch-produced prompt templates (v2-v11) is sampled, inputs above 256K tokens are truncated, and an unnamed reasoning model generates three candidates. An independent judge selects the best candidate and discards it if it misses an undisclosed threshold; 7,094 of 10,250 candidate queries remain. For RL, the reported train pool contains 92,308 trajectories. Each contributes exactly one target turn, histories above 128K tokens are filtered, and GSPO receives asynchronous rubric scores plus binary rule rewards where available. The report does not disclose decoding settings, RL rollout count, judge identity, score calibration, rule-verifier coverage, optimization hyperparameters, or the training records themselves.
