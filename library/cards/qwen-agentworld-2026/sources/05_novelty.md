The report makes the world-model training object unusually explicit for a frontier release: a five-part system-level instruction, alternating action-observation history, and a single next-observation target. It also connects specific data operations to stages: statistical loss masking in CPT, ten-template diversification and best-of-three rejection sampling in SFT, and one-turn-per-trajectory GSPO with rubric-plus-rule feedback in RL. The disclosed self-praise failure mode further shows how the reward interface can alter generated observations without improving environmental fidelity.

For the frontier-report data-disclosure ledger, the most important contribution is not a benchmark leaderboard. It is the ability to separate four layers that are often collapsed: the claimed more-than-10M training corpus, the smaller disclosed SFT and RL stage pools, the released 35B checkpoint, and the independent 2,170-sample AgentWorldBench test set. AgentWorldBench contains real-observation references and turn-level trajectory fields for evaluation, but it is neither a manifest nor a released subset of the training corpus.
