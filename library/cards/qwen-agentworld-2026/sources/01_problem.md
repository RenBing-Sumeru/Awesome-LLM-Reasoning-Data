Agent-facing language world models require more than a paper-level claim that trajectories and reinforcement learning exist. An auditable record must distinguish the interaction source, the serialized state-action-observation object, the target chosen from each trajectory, the filtering and selection rules, the feedback contract, and the released artifacts. Qwen-AgentWorld is useful for this audit because the report exposes many of those stage-level mechanics while withholding the underlying training records.

The central boundary is between training and evaluation. The report says Qwen-AgentWorld is trained on more than 10M environment-interaction trajectories across seven domains. AgentWorldBench, by contrast, is a separately constructed and released test set of 2,170 turn-level samples, each paired with a real-environment observation. The benchmark, released 35B weights, and evaluation code are evidence that evaluation and inference artifacts exist; they are not evidence that the more-than-10M training trajectories, SFT reasoning traces, RL pool, judge, or verifier layer were released.
