The training-data disclosure stops at aggregate scale and source classes. There is no trajectory inventory, per-source count, mixture weight, collection date, task-query manifest, source license ledger, item-level lineage, or released CPT/SFT/RL record. The model-card statement excluding external API-service outputs narrows one acquisition path but does not identify the generators or rights attached to each record. The claims that stage pools are disjoint and AgentWorldBench is out of distribution are not accompanied by public membership lists or a complete contamination audit.

The feedback layer is also incomplete. The SFT generator and independent judge are unnamed, and their versions, prompts, sampling settings, threshold, and error behavior are unknown. RL uses an LLM judge for most of the stated reward mass; its identity, calibration, and aggregation behavior are not released. Executable rules cover only a subset, without a coverage table or complete verifier code. Although the paper reports self-praise reward hacking and mitigates it with rule anchoring, content-type classification, and strict tag extraction, no independent robustness audit is available.

Only Qwen-AgentWorld-35B-A3B is released in the checked official artifacts; the 397B checkpoint and its starting lineage remain unavailable. Apache-2.0 covers the official repository, released weights, and AgentWorldBench, not necessarily the underlying training traces or upstream corpora. None of the reported performance results proves that those unreleased records are accurate, representative, uncontaminated, or legally reusable.
