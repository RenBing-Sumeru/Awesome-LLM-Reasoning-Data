CATS combines a success-probability estimate with conformal risk control. It treats each available reasoning-effort level as an action with a different cost and success profile, then calibrates a rule that selects an effort level only when its error risk is compatible with the requested tolerance. Easier prompts can therefore be served with less thinking, while harder prompts receive more effort or are handled conservatively.

The central contribution is a guarantee on the selected policy's error probability under the calibration assumptions, not merely a learned prediction of difficulty. This changes adaptive test-time scaling from “guess how hard to think” into a contract: the user sets a risk level, the system chooses effort subject to that level, and cost reduction is pursued only within the constraint.
