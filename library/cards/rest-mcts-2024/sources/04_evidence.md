The evidence operates at four levels and should not be conflated.

- **Constructed-target fit.** The initial value model is evaluated on 14,000 samples with absolute tolerance 0.1 and reports 69.3% accuracy (Appendix E.1). This measures agreement with constructed scalar targets, not independent human verification of every reasoning step.
- **Verifier-assisted selection.** On GSM8K and MATH500 with 256 sampled outputs, Table 3 reports 87.5 and 39.0 for self-consistency using the ReST-MCTS* value model, compared with 87.1 and 37.3 for MATH-SHEPHERD. These are solution-accuracy comparisons under one sampling setup; they do not estimate label precision or calibration.
- **Search utility and cost.** Figure 2 reports favorable accuracy/token curves for MCTS* on MATH and SciBench. Appendix Table 6 reports average runtime per question of 108 seconds for MCTS*, versus 41 for CoT plus self-consistency, 43 for ORM plus Best-of-N, and 73 for PRM plus Best-of-N. Input and output tokens both matter, and cost rises with iteration and branching.
- **Iterative training utility.** Table 2 reports two rounds across LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B: MetaMATH, and SciGLM-6B. The ReST-MCTS* second-iteration averages are 29.02, 26.50, and 35.90, respectively, but individual benchmark cells can decline; for example, LLaMA's first-iteration GPQA score is below its zero-shot starting point. The appendix also reports SciEval subsets where tree search does not beat CoT.
- **Artifact evidence.** Official APIs confirm an initial PRM dataset, iteration-specific policy and PRM datasets, and a first-iteration Llama3 policy checkpoint. Verified schemas are two fields wide. Hub license tags are Apache-2.0 for the initial PRM data and CC-BY-4.0 for the checked Llama3 policy data, PRM data, and checkpoint.
- **Sample-level audit evidence.** The first visible row in the verified Llama3 policy release reaches the correct final ratio but relies on unsupported geometric claims. This is one example, not a corpus-wide error estimate, but it concretely demonstrates that terminal correctness cannot be treated as proof of intermediate-step validity.

Together, these results support the claim that the loop can produce useful search and training signals. They do not prove that accepted trajectories are stepwise correct, that values transfer to a different policy or budget, or that the public release retains sufficient provenance for a complete data-quality audit.
