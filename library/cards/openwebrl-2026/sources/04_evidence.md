All benchmark and judge results are author-reported and were not independently reproduced. At a 30-step evaluation budget, Table 2 reports OpenWebRL-4B success of **74.1% on WebVoyager, 67.0% on Online-Mind2Web, and 64.0% on DeepShop**. These values depend on live-site state, Browser-Use Stealth Browser service, model/judge versions, retry/abort handling, and the paper's metric definition. Success excluding aborted non-agent failures is a separate statistic and must not be merged with official-score success.

Judge evaluation on the held-out 500-rollout file reports **89.8% accuracy and 92.1% F1** against GPT-4.1 labels. This measures agreement with a proprietary teacher, not human-verified ground-truth success. The judge ablation is more diagnostic: using a naive base VLM as judge produces high training reward while downstream evaluation collapses, whereas the distilled judge tracks GPT-4.1 more closely. The result demonstrates reward-hacking risk and the importance of judge construction; it does not certify the distilled judge as error-free.

Public artifact statistics are stronger evidence about the released data objects. The SFT dataset has 3,085 turn rows from 412 trajectories/tasks, all with reward 1, status `completed`, and terminal reason `task_completed`; failed demonstrations are absent. The RL-task JSONL has 2,198 specifications, but the default code snapshot trains from 2,102 tasks. Judge-13K exposes train and 500-example test files, yet lacks a public dataset card/license and was only partially indexed during audit.

The paper reports approximately 54K online trajectories for the main RL run, but official releases were found only for success-only SFT trajectories, RL prompts, judge data/weights, code, and policy/SFT/judge weights. No canonical full 54K rollout manifest with success, failure, abort, reward, mask, optimizer step, and checkpoint linkage was confirmed. Runtime generation is therefore paper evidence, not a released rollout-corpus claim.

The paper manually audits 100 failed Online-Mind2Web evaluation trajectories without the Stealth Browser service: **51% access/environment, 27% reasoning/knowledge, 13% visual grounding/interaction, and 9% task definition/judge issues**. This identifies concrete failure categories, but the 100 record-level traces were not confirmed public and the sample condition differs from official hosted-browser evaluation.
