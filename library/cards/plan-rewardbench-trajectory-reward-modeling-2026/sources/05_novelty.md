Closest prior work uses general preference pairs, terminal success, or step accuracy without representing the domain-specific states and failure modes of tool-agent planning rewards. This paper changes the record structure and feedback contract by centering Plan-RewardBench and combining inputs, candidates, environmental or evidential state, and labels in one auditable example through collecting successful and failed tool-use trajectories and constructing preferences for planning, recovery, safety refusal, and irrelevant-tool detection. The contribution is task-specific data construction, capability decomposition, and practical evaluation rather than sample count alone.
