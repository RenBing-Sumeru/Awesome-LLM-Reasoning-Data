Existing PRMs directly output step scores, provide little inspectable justification, and cannot improve by spending more inference compute. GenPRM reasons before judging each step and uses code execution to verify mathematical claims, turning a scalar classifier into a scalable generative verifier.

This gap directly determines whether mathematical step verification can be scaled: outcome-only labels cannot reveal the first failure or separate weak supervision from weak verification. The paper therefore centers its data on MATH trajectories, relative-progress estimates, code execution, and generative judgment rationales and uses that feedback for step-level training or trajectory selection. Its claims remain bounded by the task distributions represented in ProcessBench and mathematical answer correction.
