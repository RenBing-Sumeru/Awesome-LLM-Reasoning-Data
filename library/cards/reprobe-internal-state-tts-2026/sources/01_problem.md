Multi-step reasoning can benefit from sampling alternative continuations and rejecting weak steps during inference, but this requires a reliable step-level scorer. Process reward models are a common solution, yet they are often large additional language models, expensive to train, expensive to run beside the generator, and specialized to the domain on which their annotations were collected. A deployment that spends more compute on branching may therefore lose much of the gain to its verifier overhead.

The paper asks whether the generator already contains enough information to assess the credibility of its own reasoning steps. It seeks a verifier that can guide Best-of-N and beam-search scaling without re-generating textual judgments through another large model. The desired signal must be cheap, generalize beyond one reasoning domain, and remain usable while the base reasoner itself stays frozen.
