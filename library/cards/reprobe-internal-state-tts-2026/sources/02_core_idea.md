ReProbe treats a language model’s own hidden states, attention patterns, and logits as evidence about the quality of the reasoning step currently being generated. A small transformer-based probe reads those internal features from a frozen base model and predicts the probability that a step is credible. The verifier therefore reuses information generated in the ordinary decoding process rather than asking an external reward model to judge the step from text alone.

This changes the economics of test-time scaling. The system can assign scores to candidate steps and retain or reject trajectories using a module with fewer than ten million parameters, while conventional process reward models may contain billions. The paper also permits either larger-model annotations or self-supervised annotations for probe training, aiming to combine the low serving cost of uncertainty-style signals with the supervised accuracy associated with process verification.
