The ACL paper evaluates probes on mathematics, planning, and general-knowledge QA, including both in-domain and out-of-domain settings. It reports that probes with fewer than 10M parameters match or exceed process reward models much larger than themselves in step verification and test-time scaling; the paper highlights comparisons to PRMs up to 810 times larger. This supports the paper’s central claim that internal states carry usable credibility information for reasoning processes.

The evidence is not merely a classifier score: ReProbe is inserted into the decisions that determine which candidates continue in beam search or which completed trace is selected. The paper also reports automatically constructed, self-supervised training data as a lower-cost option. Nevertheless, model internals and step boundaries vary by architecture and prompting format, so the reported efficiency should be checked with the actual base model, feature extraction cost, and domain shift that a deployment expects.
