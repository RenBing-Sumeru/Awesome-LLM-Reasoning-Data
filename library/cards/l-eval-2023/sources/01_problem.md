L-Eval asks how to evaluate long-context language models on tasks that require reading thousands to hundreds of thousands of tokens, not just retrieving a short needle. The primary sources are arXiv:2307.11088, the official OpenLMLab repository, the L-Eval project page, and the Hugging Face dataset; the repository labels the work as ACL 2024 Outstanding. The evaluation object is a long document plus one or more instructions/questions and ground-truth or reference outputs. The benchmark has closed-ended tasks, where exact-style metrics are more defensible, and open-ended tasks, where the authors argue n-gram metrics are biased and should be supplemented by length-instruction-enhanced evaluation and LLM/human judgment. The atlas boundary is long-context evaluation surface, not a training corpus or retrieval system.
