Anthropic says a general-purpose web crawler follows robots.txt and avoids password-, sign-in-, and CAPTCHA-protected pages. The card names data cleaning, filtering, deduplication, and classification, but not the corresponding implementation, thresholds, yields, corpus snapshots, or source-by-source audit. Data-work-platform workers help with preference selection, safety evaluation, and adversarial testing. Worker identities, locale, compensation, instructions, agreement, task counts, and links from worker records to training stages are unknown.

The disclosed post-training methods are RLHF and reinforcement learning from AI feedback. Earlier supervised learning uses some prior-model reasoning text, while RL does not reward or penalize reasoning-text content. The report gives no preference-record schema, AI-feedback model identity, reward equation, objective composition, reward weights, calibration, verifier, optimizer, schedule, rollout policy, or checkpoint selection. It also says Anthropic has used inoculation prompting since Claude Sonnet 4 and Opus 4 on a significant subset of coding environments, including environments most susceptible to reward hacking. The prompts, environments, subset fraction, reward contract, and training impact are unknown.

For one monitorability analysis, Anthropic retrained a base model on original or Sonnet 3.5-paraphrased reasoning workspaces, using 11,000 math transcripts with reasoning workspaces and no tool use from RL training. A separate reasoning workspace evaluation used 16 samples per question at temperature 1. These are internal analysis settings, not a released training dataset, a general post-training sampling policy, or a deployed inference configuration.
