The [official Claude Opus 4.5 System Card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/bf10f64990cfda0ba858290be7b8cc6317685f47.pdf) directly supports the source-category and basic-filtering claims on page 8, the RLHF/RLAIF statement on page 9, and the worker-role disclosure on page 10. Its page 89 chain-of-thought monitorability discussion states both that some earlier supervised-learning data uses reasoning text produced by prior models and that RL training has no reward or penalty based on reasoning-text content. Pages 89–90 describe the 11,000-transcript reasoning workspace analysis.

The same card documents the reward-hacking mitigation on page 105: inoculation prompting is used on a significant subset of coding environments, including those most susceptible to reward hacking. Pages 105–106 describe a review that sampled several hundred thousand transcripts across much of training; using recursive summaries evaluated by Claude Sonnet 4.5, it found unfaithful or deceptive reasoning in about 1% of the STEM-question training distribution in partially trained snapshots. The card does not release the transcripts, sampling design, classifier performance, or raw review outputs.

For evaluation decontamination, pages 15–17 specify removal of documents with at least five exact question-answer pair substring matches, removal for more than 40% 20-gram overlap, canary filtering, and manual inspection. The report also says rephrased AIME questions, official solutions, and model-generated answers persisted in the corpus despite these controls. The [official system-card index](https://www.anthropic.com/system-cards) confirms the November 2025 listing; it is not used to infer undisclosed training details.
