The paper’s own central limitation is that Monte Carlo pseudo-labels cannot guarantee step correctness. A positive step means that at least one sampled completer recovered the answer, so an invalid prefix may receive a false positive through correction, guessing, or shortcut use. A valid but difficult prefix may receive a false negative when all 10 completers fail. The 70B/72B completers reduce but do not eliminate this model-relative error, and no formal execution audit or PRM confusion analysis is reported.

The learned selector adds further risk. Multiplying uncalibrated positive-step probabilities makes the score sensitive to trajectory length and to one low-probability step. A 0.25 pair gap is therefore not a logical margin, and the paper’s weaker DPO results are consistent with insufficient score differentiation. Curator inference: selecting only all-positive, answer-correct SFT traces can hide useful recoveries and systematic failure modes, while a DPO lower-scored trajectory may still be valid.

Release completeness blocks full audit. The SFT data omit all discarded candidates and their yield; the DPO data omit candidates never paired, raw probability vectors, and trajectory scores. The step-label file has no source item ID, generator/version field, completion outputs, or success count. The PRM checkpoint, calibration, exact second-stage sample count and decoding settings, DPO beta, software environment, and immutable lineage manifests are unavailable. No decontamination or benchmark-overlap analysis is reported, and compatibility between the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 derivative cards and upstream FOLIO/FOLIOv2/LogicAsker terms is not established.

The checked repository is also not an executable record of the reported experiment: two generation scripts use undefined `args.data_path` and a misspelled LogicAsker prompt filename, while the DPO entry point uses undefined `args.model_name`. Script defaults also differ from the reported PRM-labeling setup. Finally, evaluation concentrates on logic and three claim-verification datasets; reported benchmark performance is not evidence that the verifier generalizes to other reasoning domains or that the released data are uncontaminated.
