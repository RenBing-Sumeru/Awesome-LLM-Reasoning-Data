The official EMNLP 2025 paper studies a specific construction problem: how to turn sampled symbolic reasoning into process supervision without requiring human step labels or a scalable formal solver. Natural-language chains may exploit surface content, while formally checking every generated first-order-logic operation is expensive and brittle. The proposed compromise is to write each attempt as a Symbolic ReAct trajectory—variable-length Thought, Action, and Observation steps followed by a final label—and infer step labels from whether sampled continuations can still reach the known answer (Paper §§1, 3.1–3.2).

This belongs in `rollout_search_test_time_trace_data` because the important object is not one final answer. The construction samples 10 continuations from every intermediate prefix, learns a Process Reward Model (PRM) from the resulting labels, and uses PRM scores to keep or pair later rollouts. Three released record types expose different slices of that chain: 1,505 trajectories with 12,448 step labels (`input`, step list, and `+`/`-` labels), 15,412 accepted SFT rows (`question_id`, `question`, `response`), and 21,472 DPO rows (`question_id`, `question`, `positive`, `negative`).

The work does not supply executable symbolic proof states, an online search policy, or evidence that every retained step is formally valid. Its L4 boundary is therefore citation- and artifact-verified but reuse-partial: the raw second-stage candidate pool, rejected yield, per-step scores, PRM checkpoint and calibration, decontamination, and item-level lineage are unavailable. The Card is useful because it records both the observable data transformation and the missing evidence needed to attribute the reported gains.
