The contribution is a three-layer offline transformation: **answer-checked Monte Carlo continuations → stepwise pseudo-labels and a PRM → selected SFT traces and score-ranked DPO pairs**. A one-shot Symbolic ReAct prompt asks a model to formalize facts and rules and to expose Thought/Action/Observation operations. For each prefix, 10 completions from Llama3.3-70B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct are sampled; at least one ground-truth-reaching completion produces a positive label, and zero successes produces a negative label. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is then trained as a binary PRM.

The feedback contract is mixed. The terminal comparison is programmatic at the answer-label level, but the generated symbolic operations are not executed by a formal verifier. The PRM observes text prefixes and learned pseudo-labels; it cannot directly observe proof validity. In the second stage, it accepts an SFT trajectory only when every predicted step is positive and the final answer is correct. For DPO, the product of step-positive probabilities is a trajectory score, and two candidates form a pair only when their score gap exceeds 0.25. “Negative” in a released pair therefore means lower PRM score, not demonstrated logical invalidity.

Relative to prior offline Monte Carlo process supervision over natural-language traces, the change is the Symbolic ReAct representation and its use as the object being labeled and filtered. Relative to nearby rollout/search work such as TreeRL, the search is offline prefix completion rather than an on-policy tree supplying RL advantages; relative to A*-Thought, the verifier selects newly generated symbolic trajectories rather than compressed paths. Monte Carlo estimation, PRMs, SFT, and DPO are existing components; the distinctive object is their linked symbolic-data pipeline and its released SFT/DPO derivatives.
