The closest baseline is offline Monte Carlo process supervision: sample continuations from a reasoning prefix, use terminal correctness as a proxy for prefix quality, and train a PRM. The paper does not originate that recipe, ReAct, PRMs, SFT, or DPO. Its concrete change is to make the trace itself symbolically structured—explicit formula construction and rule-oriented Thought/Action/Observation steps—and then carry the learned prefix signal through two downstream data products.

At the object level, the work links three supervision interfaces: hard `+`/`-` labels on steps, PRM probabilities aggregated into a trajectory score, and chosen/rejected full-trajectory pairs. At the selection level, it exposes two thresholds with different meanings: every step above 0.5 plus a correct final answer for SFT, and a trajectory score gap above 0.25 for DPO. At the release level, it publishes the labeled seed file and exact SFT/DPO derivatives, but not the raw candidate pool or score vectors. The novelty is therefore a symbolic representation plus an integrated data-transformation recipe, not a new formal verifier.

For rollout/search data research, this distinction is useful because it lets curators ask whether gains come from the representation, the 10-completion budget, the 70B/72B completers, the learned PRM, the filter, or the optimizer. The current release cannot fully separate those factors. Before reusing the approach, a study should retain every candidate and completion, normalize or calibrate trajectory scores, log per-source lineage, and compare against equal-budget natural-language and formal-execution baselines.
