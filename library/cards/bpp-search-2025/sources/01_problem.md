Operations-research language models must convert a natural-language problem into a mathematical program whose sets, parameters, variables, objective, and constraints are mutually consistent. Earlier public resources used by this work often expose only the problem and objective value, leaving the intermediate modeling decisions unavailable for supervision or diagnosis. The paper addresses this annotation gap with StructuredOR and separately studies how to search over intermediate modeling decisions with BPP-Search.

For the rollout/search track, the key problem is also a release-boundary problem. BPP-Search reasons over a branching Q -> SP -> V -> OC tree, scores intermediate nodes, prunes candidates, and ranks a final queue. A final answer alone cannot reveal which alternatives were explored, why a branch was rejected, whether a verifier failed, or how much inference budget produced the selected model. The official release therefore has to be read as final structured examples, not as a public corpus of BPP-Search trajectories.
