The paper reports 124 StructuredOR questions across ten domains: Agriculture 24, Logistics 20, Education 15, Sports 12, Military 12, Energy 12, Telecommunications 11, Manufacturing 9, Health Services 5, and Finance 4. At the pinned official Hugging Face revision, all 124 files parse as JSON, with 86 train and 38 test records. Their exact `question` strings are all unique and no exact string appears in both directories. That audit does not test semantic near-duplicates, shared templates, or parameter variants.

Search comparisons are conditional. The evaluation retains only problems for which fully traversed GPT-4o ToT produced at least one correct answer: 30 of 38 StructuredOR, 72 of 211 MAMO-ComplexLP, and 143 of 289 NL4OPT problems. Within these policy-solvable subsets, the paper reports that PRM-guided search and pairwise final selection improve accuracy and reduce traversal relative to the compared strategies. The reported wider-beam ablation is not monotonic: similar correct and incorrect candidates can receive similar PRM scores, and increasing beam width can reduce accuracy.

These experiments support claims about the proposed search stack under the reported conditional protocol. They do not establish the quality, coverage, or leakage resistance of the 124-row public dataset, and they do not independently validate the learned selectors. Because raw trees, selected and rejected nodes, checker outputs, scores, and per-example budgets are absent, the release cannot reproduce the causal path from additional search compute to the reported final result.
