The distinctive contribution is the coupling of a structured mathematical-modeling representation with two learned selection stages. Intermediate beam retention uses a process classifier, whereas final choice uses pairwise comparisons across completed candidates. This separates local process ranking from terminal candidate ranking instead of asking one scalar signal to serve both roles.

For data curation, the more important distinction is between the method object and the released object. StructuredOR makes the final sets, parameters, variables, objective, constraints, and objective value explicit, but the BPP-Search tree that produces a candidate is not preserved. The paper is therefore useful both as a search recipe and as an example of why a final-example release is not equivalent to a rollout or search-trace release.
