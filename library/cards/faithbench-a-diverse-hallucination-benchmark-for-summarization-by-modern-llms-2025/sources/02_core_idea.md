FaithBench evaluates faithfulness to a supplied passage, not whether a summary agrees with outside world knowledge: a true statement can still be a hallucination if the source does not support it. It starts from Vectara’s summary leaderboard and holds the source passage fixed while comparing summaries from 10 modern models in 8 families. Instead of spending expert effort on obvious errors, it selects passage-model groups where five popular detectors, including GPT-as-a-judge, disagree most. Eleven annotators then mark suspect summary spans, link them to supporting or contradicting passage spans when possible, and record an explanation. The taxonomy preserves ambiguity rather than forcing a binary label: summaries can be consistent, clearly unsupported but benign, questionable, or unwanted; unwanted errors are intrinsic when contradicted by the passage and extrinsic when neither supported nor factually defensible. Thus the benchmark evaluates both how models hallucinate and whether detectors can recognize difficult, nuanced cases.
