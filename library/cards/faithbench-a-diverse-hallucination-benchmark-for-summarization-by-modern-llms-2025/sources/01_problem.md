Summary hallucination detectors are often tested on a narrow set of older models or easy examples, so good offline scores can conceal failures on modern, abstractive summaries. Evaluation also needs to distinguish unsupported content from borderline inferences that readers may find acceptable.

FaithBench collects difficult passage-summary pairs from ten current models across eight families, then has experts label unsupported spans and their evidence. It evaluates both which models hallucinate on these cases and whether automatic detectors recognize the errors.
