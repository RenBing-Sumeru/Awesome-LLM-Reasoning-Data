Existing multi-agent systems often deploy separate planner and worker models and optimize only the final answer or freeze one role while training the other. MATPO places both roles in one model instance and computes advantages from their respective rollouts, giving distinguishable gradients to planning decomposition and worker execution. The novelty is role-level policy optimization and credit assignment under shared weights rather than the multi-agent architecture itself. This makes multi-agent specialization a learned behavior rather than two roles temporarily simulated by prompts at inference. Local advantages also separately diagnose planning decomposition and tool-execution failures. Planner-worker decomposition is not new. The change is role-specific advantage estimation under shared weights, giving planning and tool execution distinct gradients so specialization is learned rather than role-played.
