The first-pit label is a finite-search estimate. Four failures at temperature 0.7 mean that this RFT checkpoint did not sample a correct recovery within that budget. A low-probability recovery may exist, a stronger explorer may succeed, or a different segmentation may move the boundary. The width ablation itself shows that labels and downstream effects change with search size, so “first wrong step” is an interpretive shorthand rather than a proved property.

Outcome verification is imperfect. A rationale can reach the correct numeric answer through cancellation, guessing, or invalid reasoning. The GSM8K code mitigates this for retained positives by checking embedded equations, but free-text logic is still unchecked; MATH depends on answer extraction and symbolic/format equivalence code. Valid alternative formats can be rejected, while parser artifacts can be accepted. The paper explicitly notes that its correct-answer assumption is weaker for small answer spaces such as true/false or multiple choice.

Pair coverage is endogenous to model skill. Easy GSM8K questions often produce too few incorrect candidates; hard MATH questions often produce too few correct ones. A problem needs both sides to form an outcome pair and recoveries to form a granular pair, so the final distribution over source questions differs from the original training distribution. Maximum-edit-distance heuristics further favor particular negative styles, and removing conclusions changes the object optimized by DPO.

Self-generated preference learning can narrow the policy. The paper reports improved top-1 accuracy alongside reduced answer diversity, weaker majority-vote behavior than SFT/RFT in some analyses, and reward win rates that rapidly reach one. These signals are consistent with overconfidence, memorization of limited training questions, or reward exploitation. Experiments are limited to two math datasets and 7B base models; continual rounds, larger models, broader domains, and strongly instruction-tuned reasoners are untested.

The release is incomplete for data audit. The source task files and code exist, but raw 100-way candidates, per-row deduplication results, correct/incorrect labels, outcome pairs, step prompts, four continuations, extracted answers, first-pit decisions, discarded examples, and final gpair files do not. There is no immutable release manifest, and the code-paper difference in chosen selection means regenerating with current code need not reproduce the reported corpus.

Rights and contamination are unresolved. The paper states licenses for datasets and model assets, and the author checkpoints advertise Apache-2.0, but the GitHub repository has no detected license and generated-data terms are unstated. No explicit decontamination audit covers train/test overlap, paraphrases, generated rationales, or base-model pretraining exposure. The README also swaps or misspells checkpoint links, leaving task-to-checkpoint identity ambiguous without independent inspection.
