Self-Explore's central move is to reinterpret a rejected rationale as a collection of search states. Instead of assigning one negative label to the whole sequence, it asks from which prefix the current model ceases to recover the answer and moves the preceding context into the preference prompt. This makes rejected data productive without requesting dense human judgments.

The resulting supervision is finer than outcome preference but cheaper than an independently labeled process-reward corpus. One target-model family plays generator, explorer, reference policy, and final policy roles at different stages. Programmatic final-answer checks provide the only external signal. The construction therefore shows how an answer verifier plus step-conditioned sampling can synthesize process-shaped pairwise data.

The paper is also valuable for separating three meanings often conflated under “first error.” A semantic first error is the earliest mathematically invalid step; a process-reward label is an externally assigned judgment of a step; Self-Explore's first pit is the earliest prefix with empirical recovery rate zero in four trials. Only the third is produced. Naming that distinction prevents a useful model-relative label from being overstated as ground truth.

The method does not introduce a learned verifier or release a new static dataset. Its novelty lies in recycling failures, localizing the negative span, and showing through ablations that the chosen/rejected span geometry matters. The absent generated artifacts remain an important gap between an open recipe and an auditable open data release.
