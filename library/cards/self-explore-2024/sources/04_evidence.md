The official ACL paper evaluates SFT, RFT, outcome-supervised DPO, and Self-Explore across all three 7B families on GSM8K and MATH. It reports average improvements over SFT of 11.57% on GSM8K and 2.89% on MATH, with gains for every model family. These are downstream accuracy results under the authors' training and checkpoint-selection protocol; they support the usefulness of the generated supervision but do not validate every first-pit label.

DeepSeek-Math ablations isolate the span design. Table 1 reports 74.83/34.92 accuracy for whole pairwise data and 78.47/37.68 for granular pairwise data on GSM8K/MATH. Using only the first chosen step or rejecting the pit plus all later steps performs between those conditions, supporting the specific choice to retain a full recovery completion while rejecting only the pit. A separate ablation varies exploration width from four to 32 and finds no improvement from more samples, showing that the operational label's usefulness does not monotonically track a larger search budget.

Appendix Table 5 reports the actual training-set sizes. Granular-pair counts are 56,283, 36,812, and 38,618 for GSM8K and 31,248, 33,960, and 39,496 for MATH across Mistral, Llemma, and DeepSeek. Tables 6–7 show selective question coverage: in MATH, only 48.19%–62.92% of source questions have at least four granular instances; in GSM8K, the corresponding range is 57.00%–61.95%. These statistics show that the paired corpus is shaped by which problems yield both correct and incorrect self-generations.

The paper also records evidence against a simple “better reward means better data” story. Preference-trained models produce less diverse sampled answers than SFT/RFT models, and Appendix E shows the chosen-over-rejected win rate quickly converging to one. The authors connect this to overconfidence or reward exploitation. Their GPT-4 FLASK evaluation of 100 random GSM8K test questions gives Self-Explore the highest reported robustness, correctness, and efficiency scores, but it is a model-judged sample, not an independent corpus audit.

The official repository at commit `54b8ba9f4cee1ef52f72de916e100e0da2ae6863` substantiates that the pipeline is executable: it contains raw-generation, answer-checking, RFT/pair construction, exploration, gpair formation, DPO, and evaluation code plus source task JSONL files. It does not contain any paper-run generated intermediate or final pair files and has no GitHub release or repository license. Two author Hugging Face checkpoints exist, but the README's task labels and links are inconsistent, so the checkpoint mapping is not a clean reproduction manifest.
