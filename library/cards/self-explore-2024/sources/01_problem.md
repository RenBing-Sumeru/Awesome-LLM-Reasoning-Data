Rejection-sampling fine-tuning keeps self-generated solutions that reach the reference answer, while outcome-level preference learning contrasts complete correct and incorrect solutions. Both discard structure inside failed traces. If a rationale follows several useful steps and then makes one mistake, whole-sequence rejection penalizes the correct prefix; it may also penalize coherent downstream reasoning whose only problem is that it inherited an earlier wrong value.

Self-Explore asks whether the same model family can turn those rejected traces into finer supervision without human step labels or a separately trained process reward model. Its operational target is not “the first mathematically false statement” in an absolute sense. It is the first tested step after which the RFT explorer fails to sample any continuation that reaches the gold final answer under a fixed budget.

This distinction defines the data problem. The construction pipeline must preserve the source question and answer, the model-specific candidate rationale, outcome verification, step segmentation, four continuation attempts from each prefix, and the first zero-recovery decision. Without that lineage, a final `prompt/chosen/rejected` row hides whether the rejected step was truly wrong, merely hard for this checkpoint to recover from, or mislabeled by answer extraction.

The paper studies this recipe on GSM8K and MATH with Mistral-7B, Llemma-7B, and DeepSeek-Math-7B-Base. Its relevance to open data construction is therefore less about a downloadable corpus—the generated corpora are not released—and more about how failed rollouts can be converted into auditable pairwise process supervision.
