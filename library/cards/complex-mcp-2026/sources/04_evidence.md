Under the paper's main ReAct evaluation, every model is run independently three times on all 47 tasks. The best reported row in Table 2 is Gemini-3-Flash: `55.31 ± 0.00%` success, `85.79 ± 0.50%` completion, and `4.39 ± 0.19%` misbehavior. The reported human success rate is `93.61 ± 1.74%`. These numbers show a gap on the benchmark's own state-difference contract; they do not prove that the released reference trajectories are suitable training data or that the evaluator captures every semantic requirement.

The paper reports about 29,964 tokens for the full-context tool descriptions and an average of 11 tool rounds. In Table 3, retrieval-based variants underperform full-context prompting; iterative RAG reaches `36.88 ± 2.01%` success for Gemini-3-Flash. The authors associate errors with retrieval saturation, clean-slate assumptions or omitted state checks, and premature defeat after transient errors. These are benchmark-conditioned observations, not evidence that one retrieval design universally dominates.

The strongest artifact evidence is structural. Direct inspection of pinned `data.parquet` finds exactly 47 rows and seven columns, no null cells, one accepted reference `output` per task, and a nested target `gt_env`. The official config enumerates 15 MCP servers and 315 tools, and the runner, client, construction script, and evaluator expose episode execution and scoring logic. Appendix C provides three successful and two failed qualitative model trajectories.

The release boundary is equally important. The 47 parquet rows are successful ground-truth/reference demonstrations accepted during task authoring. They are not the paper's complete model-by-task-by-trial rollout set. The repository tree does not supply all successful and failed evaluated histories, and Appendix C's five examples do not close that gap. Aggregate scores therefore cannot be reverse-engineered into a complete trajectory corpus.

The current artifact also exposes a version discrepancy: the paper says the hardest task requires more than 60 tool invocations, while the inspected parquet's reference call counts do not exceed 59. This may reflect a paper-era/current-main difference or a counting-definition difference; without a paper-linked release, it should remain unresolved rather than being treated as a paper error.
