- The official repository has no paper-linked tag or release. All code/data audit claims here concern current-main commit `617e963bd838bee5793a39e6b34165b79535828f`, not a proven ICML experiment snapshot. The paper's “more than 60” hardest-task invocation statement and the current table's maximum reference count below that threshold remain an unresolved version or counting discrepancy.
- Exact replay is not available. The runner does not persist complete evaluated rollouts, trial IDs, model-sampling seeds, environment hashes, result manifests, or exported fault schedules; provider revision and default temperature/top-p are also unknown. A seeded process-local session does not freeze external model behavior or all transient failures.
- The release contains 47 accepted successful reference trajectories only. It does not contain the complete successful and failed rollouts for every evaluated model and each of three runs. Failed authoring attempts, rejection counts, and a detailed acceptance rubric beyond the visible yes/no prompt are also missing.
- The current evaluator's `content` branch accepts exact matches, substring matches, or Levenshtein similarity over 0.7. This may accept a semantically wrong near-match or reject an acceptable reformulation, but no independent calibration set or measured false-positive/false-negative rate is released. This is an implementation risk, not an observed error-rate claim.
- In the inspected current evaluator, the exclusion-key literal appears to omit a comma between `rid` and `oid`, which would concatenate the two strings and may leave those generated IDs unexcluded. This is a code-review finding and potential collateral-change accounting risk; the available evidence does not quantify how often it affects benchmark outcomes.
- Seeded network failures can return status `internel error`, while current runner aggregation recognizes only `ok`, `error`, and `failed`. Such events may be omitted from failure-status counts. This is an implementation-level accounting risk, not evidence of a measured benchmark error rate or necessarily of an incorrect final-state score.
- Synthetic LightTalk histories can sample an `attacks` corpus containing prompt-injection instructions with 10% probability per contact, yet the paper reports no dedicated prompt-injection metric or robustness calibration. The final-state verifier may not reveal whether an agent followed malicious text when the resulting state still satisfies encoded checks.
- The synthetic knowledge-base generator model, prompts, upstream source provenance, annotator identities/count, agreement process, recruitment, compensation, and consent are undisclosed. No train/dev/test split or systematic model-pretraining overlap/decontamination audit is supplied; public tasks, targets, traces, and evaluator code create future exposure risk.
- The repository root is MIT and the arXiv paper is CC BY 4.0, but no separate statement establishes rights for benchmark rows, synthetic entities, or model-generated text/tool outputs. The absence of that rights chain blocks treating the trajectories as generally reusable training data.
- The paper evaluates agents only. It does not establish SFT, preference optimization, process-supervision, reward-model, or agent-RL use, and its benchmark scores do not prove trajectory quality or generalization beyond the 47 synthetic tasks.
