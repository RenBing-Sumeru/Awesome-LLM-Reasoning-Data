The main limitation is the release boundary. No public task manifest, training episode corpus, SFT corrections, CT routing records, reward labels, accepted/rejected ledger, UI-TARS-2 checkpoint, or domain-specialized checkpoints were verified. The public research repository and desktop client are useful official artifacts, but they do not make the reported training pipeline inspectable at item level.

The episodes are not independently replayable. Windows, Ubuntu, Android, websites, browsers, applications, tools, and games are version-sensitive; shared-filesystem side effects and stateful sessions further increase dependence on exact initialization. The report does not provide VM or container images, dependency versions, website snapshots, game code and checksums as a bundle, reset scripts, action timing, timeout rules, or deterministic seeds.

Reward uncertainty is material. The GUI-General outcome reward model sees only the last five screenshots, and the 300-trace internal study reports a relatively high false-positive rate. Judge prompts, reference construction, scalar thresholds, reward weights, and human calibration are unavailable. A rising training reward can therefore reflect learning under the authors' evaluator without proving that terminal states are consistently correct.

Data governance is under-specified. In-situ demonstrations are collected on annotators' personal computers, yet consent, capture boundaries, personal-data redaction, audio and screen retention, security review, and compensation are not disclosed. Internet tutorials, videos, websites, open-source traces, games, and prior post-training data are named only by class, without an item-level license or provenance manifest.

Split and contamination controls are also unknown. The paper reports filtering and deduplication during task construction but not benchmark overlap checks, source-level holdouts, or separation across CT, SFT, RL, and evaluation. Website drift and repeated flywheel recycling can introduce near-duplicates across cycles. Benchmark performance must not be used as a substitute for this missing audit evidence.

The report itself has no comprehensive safety or failure section for UI-TARS-2. GUI and SDK actions can modify files, invoke terminals, browse authenticated contexts, or operate external services. Authorization boundaries, sandbox escape testing, dangerous-action review, and recovery from unintended side effects remain unknown.
