A GUI-agent training example is not just an instruction paired with a final answer. It is an episode unfolding inside mutable software: the policy receives a task, a recent interaction context and compressed memory, observes pixels or tool output, emits a GUI or SDK action, observes the next state, and continues until success, failure, or a budget boundary. A useful disclosure must therefore cover the task source, state and observation representation, action space, memory, environment version, termination rule, reward, and record lineage.

UI-TARS-2 addresses how to produce and optimize such long-horizon episodes at scale. The report spans desktop, mobile, browser, terminal, software-development, MCP-style tool, and game settings, where actions can change a shared filesystem and later observations depend on earlier actions. This makes a trajectory meaningful only together with its stateful environment and reset conditions.

The report supplies substantial method-level detail but not an open training-data artifact. It names source families, annotation procedures, filtering stages, three feedback paths, and a large sandbox architecture, yet does not publish the task inventory, episode corpus, accepted/rejected routing ledger, reward records, environment images, or the UI-TARS-2 checkpoint. The audit problem is therefore to distinguish a described data factory from a released, replayable dataset.
