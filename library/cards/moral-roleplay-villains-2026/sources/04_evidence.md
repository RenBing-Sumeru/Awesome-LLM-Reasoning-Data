Large-scale evaluation finds a stable monotonic decline in fidelity as character morality decreases across major models. Traits directly opposed to helpfulness and honesty—such as deceit, manipulation, and selfishness—are portrayed least faithfully, with models often replacing nuanced malevolence with crude threats or refusal. General chatbot competence is a poor predictor of villain role-play, and some strongly safety-aligned models perform particularly poorly. This supports the safety–creativity conflict hypothesis, although the rubric must avoid equating more harmful content with greater character fidelity.
