The primary sources are the NeurIPS 2025 Spotlight paper and appendix (arXiv:2505.24760; OpenReview `GqYSunGmp7`) and the official `open-thought/reasoning-gym` repository. The paper addresses a construction bottleneck in RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR): fixed human-written or scraped question-answer collections do not naturally provide an expandable task stream, controlled difficulty, or an executable reward for every newly generated instance (Paper §1–2; Appendix A.1–A.3).

Reasoning Gym packages more than 100 task-specific Python generators and verifiers across algebra, algorithms, arithmetic, cognition, code, games, geometry, graphs, induction, and logic. The framework is not a canonical fixed corpus. One generated item has a `question`, an oracle `answer`, and task-specific `metadata`; an RL or evaluation system adds a model response and receives an answer-level scalar score from that task's verifier. A reproducible identity must also include the code/package version, generator configuration, seed, item index, virtual size or reseeding state, mixture weights, curriculum state, and scorer dependencies.

The decision boundary is narrow. These are predominantly single-turn, text-based environments with automatic outcome feedback, not multi-turn agent worlds, multimodal tasks, human preference data, or process labels. Passing the verifier does not label the reasoning steps or establish that a rationale is faithful. The paper also does not release a fixed accepted/rejected rollout corpus: the reusable public artifact is the executable construction and reward substrate.

This object belongs in `data_construction_open_release_recipes` because it exposes how prompts are generated, mixed, scored, and adapted during training. Its L4 value comes from the explicit record shape, task registry, appendix configurations, RL scaffold, experimental evidence, and release audit. Downstream scores show that the environment can be used for RLVR and evaluation; they do not certify every generator, verifier, or generated item.
