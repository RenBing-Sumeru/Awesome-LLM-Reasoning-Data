The prior baseline is formal theorem proving benchmarks that expose standalone statements or mathlib-like theorem tasks and score candidate proofs by compiler acceptance. TheoremBench changes the object by grouping theorem developments around classical results and by evaluating dependency-aware proving through main theorem and subtheorem coverage.

The direction signal is the move from single-statement proof success toward theorem-development progress. The quality signal is mechanical Lean4 verification plus metrics that separate parent theorem success, partial coverage, and proof length/cost. What is not new is the use of Lean as a verifier or pass@k as a metric. Before reuse, inspect the theorem extraction pipeline, license, environment pinning, contamination risk from public Lean code, and whether premised tasks leak too much of the proof structure.
