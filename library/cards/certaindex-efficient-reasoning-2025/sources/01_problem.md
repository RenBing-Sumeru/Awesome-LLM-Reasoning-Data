Chain-of-thought sampling, self-consistency, search, and other reasoning programs often spend a fixed number of tokens or rollouts on every request. Yet an answer may settle early for an easy question, whereas a difficult question may still change after several apparently plausible intermediate answers. A uniform budget therefore wastes inference computation on already stable cases, while a simple length cap risks stopping precisely the cases that still need exploration.

The paper asks whether a serving system can recognize, without being tied to one decoding or search algorithm, when an additional interval of test-time computation is unlikely to alter the final answer. The target is not a direct estimate of correctness: it is a decision about the expected value of more reasoning. Intermediate answer evolution is consequently made into a signal for early exit, token redistribution, and scheduling across concurrent requests.
