Certaindex measures how reliably a reasoning program's intermediate answers have stabilized. When successive probes converge on the same answer, the score is high and another segment of inference is less likely to change the output; when the answer is still moving, the score remains low and the request keeps its opportunity to consume computation. This separates the operational question of whether to continue from a claim that the model has proved its answer correct.

The central design choice is to expose answer stability as an algorithm-agnostic systems interface. Rather than building a prompt-specific stopping rule for one sampler, the same score can govern early exit, allocate tokens among requests with different uncertainty, and coordinate stages of a larger reasoning program. Test-time scaling then becomes selective: additional computation is directed toward cases whose answer trajectory has not yet settled.
