The NeurIPS paper evaluates the stability signal across chain-of-thought, self-consistency, Monte Carlo tree search, and related reasoning programs. Its comparisons test whether stabilized intermediate answers expose inference intervals that no longer alter the final result, rather than merely measuring whether a shorter completion is possible. The reported evidence indicates that the signal can identify surplus token use while preserving the accuracy reported for the corresponding reasoning workloads.

In the Dynasor integration, the authors report up to 50% lower computation and up to 3.3 times higher throughput on real workloads without an accuracy reduction. These are system-level results: their meaning depends on the selected workloads, model behavior, probe cadence, thresholds, batching policy, and service constraints. A reproduction should therefore report accuracy, consumed tokens, latency, and throughput together instead of treating one headline efficiency number as a task-independent guarantee.
