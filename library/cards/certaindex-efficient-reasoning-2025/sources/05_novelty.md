Earlier efficiency controls commonly specialize to one decoding strategy, one search procedure, or a separately trained verifier. Certaindex instead takes a directly observable property of the running program—whether its intermediate answers are settling—and makes it a shared control signal across different reasoning algorithms. This gives the stopping decision an explicit empirical object: answer changes over the computation already performed.

The paper further connects that signal to serving decisions rather than limiting it to a benchmark early-exit curve. In Dynasor, answer stability affects which request receives more tokens, which request exits, and how stages are coordinated. The novelty is thus the joint framing of a model-side behavioral metric and a systems scheduler: test-time scaling is treated as allocation of finite computation to requests whose output may still change.
