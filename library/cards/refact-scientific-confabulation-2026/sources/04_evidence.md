Evaluation of nine models from 1B to 70B reveals a salient-distractor failure: 61% of incorrect span predictions are semantically unrelated to the true error, and scaling does not remove the pattern. Comparative judgment is paradoxically harder than independent detection, with GPT-4o F1 falling from 0.67 independently to 0.53 pairwise. The results show that LLM judges are distracted by surface differences and that pairwise evaluation cannot be assumed more reliable. Bucketed analysis by error position and response length shows that salience bias is not driven by one model size or a narrow data subset. The pattern persists from 1B to 70B models, showing that scaling alone is insufficient. The main experiment supports systematic positional salience and pairwise interference, persisting from 1B to 70B models. It does not show that single-error settings represent real multi-error answers.
