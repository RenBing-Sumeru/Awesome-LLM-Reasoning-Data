Scientific answers often contain one local error while remaining fluent and mostly correct. Existing factuality judges typically assign one binary score to the whole response and may focus on salient but irrelevant sentences, failing to localize the actual error or remain stable when comparing similar answers. Positional labels also reveal whether judges exhibit order and salience bias when errors occur at the beginning, middle, or end. A local scientific error may occupy one span of a long, otherwise fluent and mostly correct answer. Whole-response judges are distracted by salient sentences or surface pair differences and cannot reliably localize or minimally repair the true error.

ReFACT builds expert positional annotations from real scientific QA and constructs variants containing one controlled error, separately evaluating independent detection, span localization, comparative judgment, and correction.
