CodeScout's novelty for this atlas is not that terminal search, patch parsing, F1, or reinforcement learning is individually new. It is the combination of a lightweight repository-localization environment, a multi-granularity programmatic terminal verifier, repeated current-policy attempts, and a public full-chat release that preserves both successful and zero-reward siblings.

Relative to repair-oriented agent data, the task deliberately stops before code editing and test execution. This reduces environment setup cost and isolates search behavior, but also narrows what the reward certifies: matching locations modified by the gold patch is not the same as producing a correct repair. The `LocalizationFinish` schema makes terminal predictions machine-checkable at file, class/module, and function/entity levels without claiming step-level reasoning validity.

Relative to success-filtered rejection-sampling corpora, the direct 4B/14B artifact keeps negative outcomes. The **8,281 zero-reward rows**, 2,317 mixed groups across the two configurations, and 745 all-zero groups expose within-task variation and repeated failure. The separate 1.7B RFT stage is conventional success filtering and should be analyzed separately; the novelty of the public training-rollout artifact lies in not collapsing its direct-RL evidence to the perfect subset.

Relative to papers that describe online rollouts but publish only prompts or aggregate curves, CodeScout releases the full terminal conversation plus decomposed terminal reward. This permits command-level behavior analysis, reward-component studies, and attempt-group comparisons. However, it stops short of a replay-grade ledger because the chat is not joined to exact repository state, gold target, policy checkpoint, seed, stop reason, or loss mask.

The auxiliary exact-four-turn term also makes the feedback contract inspectable as a composition rather than a single opaque score. Researchers can separate localization evidence from interaction-length shaping in the released fields. At the same time, because a turn bonus can be positive independently of localization correctness, it should not be treated as stronger verification or as proof that a four-turn search is efficient.

For **Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data**, the most useful conceptual contribution is that failed and mixed attempt groups are releaseable research objects. They allow audit questions about selection, gradient exposure, verifier failure, and rollout budget that cannot be answered from final checkpoints or best-only traces. The paper's benchmark performance does not establish this novelty; the published schema and verified row/group distributions do.
