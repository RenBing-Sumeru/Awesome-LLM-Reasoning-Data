**Release evidence.** The strongest evidence in this Card is not a benchmark score but the pinned public data itself. Both Parquet footers and the Hugging Face dataset endpoints agree on **54,845 rows**: **39,040 CodeScout-14B** and **15,805 CodeScout-4B**. Every released row has the documented five-field top-level schema, and the reward dictionary exposes localization totals and components. These counts and scalar columns were audited over the complete release, not extrapolated from viewer samples.

**Attempt structure.** The 14B configuration contains 9,760 unique `instance_id`/step groups, each with exactly four rows and `rollout_number` 0–3. The 4B configuration contains 1,979 groups over 1,640 unique task IDs. Of those groups, 1,973 contain eight attempts; the remaining six contain **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6** rows. This verifies that the release normally preserves the paper's repeated-attempt structure, while also demonstrating incomplete group logging that is not explained by a public manifest.

**Failure retention and group diversity.** Across both configurations, **8,281 rows have zero localization reward** and 46,564 have a positive reward. The 14B release has 5,047 zero rows and 33,993 positive rows; 4B has 3,234 zero and 12,571 positive. At group level, 14B has 1,448 mixed zero/positive groups, 639 all-zero groups, and 7,673 all-positive groups. The corresponding 4B counts are 869 mixed, 106 all-zero, and 1,004 all-positive. Thus the artifact is demonstrably not a best-of-\(n\) or success-only dataset.

**Reward distribution.** For CodeScout-14B, mean localization reward is 2.04919, with 17,608 rows at the perfect value 3.0. Component means are 0.82059 for file, 0.68350 for module, and 0.54511 for entity. Its separate turn term is 1 for 34,818 rows and 0 for 4,222. For CodeScout-4B, mean localization reward is 1.84886, with 6,185 perfect rows; component means are 0.75195, 0.60553, and 0.49139. Its `multiturn_reward` field is present but zero for all 15,805 rows, consistent with the 4B configuration not using the 14B exact-four-turn auxiliary reward.

**Budget reconciliation evidence.** Paper settings imply \(300\times32\times4=38{,}400\) nominal 14B rollouts and \(200\times8\times8=12{,}800\) nominal 4B rollouts, or **51,200 total**. The public release instead has 39,040 and 15,805 rows, respectively—**3,645 more** in total. At group level, this is 160 more 14B groups than the nominal 9,600 and 379 more 4B groups than the nominal 1,600, with the six incomplete groups included. The arithmetic is verified; the cause—retries, restarts, evaluations, duplicates, or some other logging path—is unknown.

**Paper and implementation evidence.** The paper and code align on the patch-derived three-level F1 verifier, structured finish action, asynchronous GSPO scaffold, and loss-masking of no-finish maximum-step rollouts. The pinned repository also fixes SkyRL and OpenHands SDK commits. However, current 14B and 4B script defaults do not match the paper's rollout/turn settings, and `README_Training.md` does not provide an end-to-end immutable replay procedure. This supports a construction recipe, not exact reproduction of the reported runs.

**Outcome evidence boundary.** The paper reports that CodeScout models improve code-localization performance on SWE-Bench Verified, Lite, and the Python subset of Pro. Those are author-reported model results and were not independently reproduced in this curation. They demonstrate that the recipe was used in training, but they do not prove that every released row is correctly attributed, legally reusable, uncontaminated, or sufficient for replay. The data-quality assessment therefore rests on schema, counts, reward semantics, lineage, and release completeness rather than benchmark gains.
