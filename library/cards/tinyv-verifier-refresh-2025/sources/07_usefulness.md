For the data-construction and open-release recipe track, TinyV is a worked example of verifier refresh as a lifecycle: audit a fixed verifier on current policy outputs, recover disagreement cases, construct positive and negative supervision around its routing boundary, train a compact judge, and place it behind the original programmatic gate. This pattern is useful when a stable checker has high precision but loses recall as output forms diversify.

A reusable record should preserve more than TinyV's public row. It should include immutable source and generator revisions, all four candidates, extraction and format outcomes, both judge prompts and outputs, disagreements, human adjudication, synthetic-parent links, rejected variants, balancing decision and seed, split assignment, verifier request/response, and final reward. The exact 5K RL manifest and paper-run trajectories are necessary to audit how refresh changes the online objective.

The public artifacts support verifier SFT, controlled reward comparisons, HardVerify evaluation, and inspection of a Prime-gated veRL integration. Reuse should begin by declaring dataset licenses, pinning revisions, reconciling paper and launcher settings, creating source-disjoint held-out verifier tests, measuring false-positive costs and calibration under online prevalence, and auditing semantic overlap.

The construction is a useful recipe, not a quality certificate. Reported benchmark and policy gains demonstrate utility in the tested settings; they do not establish that the missing 638K pool was sound, that the public balanced subset represents deployment prevalence, or that learned judgments are safe ground truth.
