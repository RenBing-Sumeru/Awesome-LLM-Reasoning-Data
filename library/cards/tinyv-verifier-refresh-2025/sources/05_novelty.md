TinyV's distinctive contribution is to turn verifier blind spots into an explicit refresh dataset and then deploy the learned correction behind the original rule system. It connects four objects that are often reported separately: an audit of false negatives, real and synthetic answer-equivalence supervision, a compact binary verifier, and a gated online RL reward.

The design targets the rule verifier's conditional error distribution rather than generic correctness judgments. Real cases arise from Prime-rejected generated answers accepted by two different large judges; synthetic cases deliberately vary language, format, and notation; the final SFT set balances recoverable and non-recoverable Prime rejections. The hierarchical gate limits learned-verifier calls and retains the programmatic acceptance path.

This novelty has a clear boundary. TinyV is not process supervision, formal proof checking, or a certified replacement for Prime. It learns an answer-level judgment policy from model-generated labels and inherits extraction assumptions, source-answer errors, judge bias, and distribution shift. Its value for the track lies in making verifier refresh a concrete construction and release recipe whose hidden objectives and missing ledgers can be audited.
