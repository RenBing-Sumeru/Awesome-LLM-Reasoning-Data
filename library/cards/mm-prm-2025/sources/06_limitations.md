The soft target has a narrower meaning than the paper's occasional language of step correctness suggests. It is a continuation-success estimate under MM-Policy, finite sampling, and one answer judge. A sound prefix can score low when future rollouts are weak; a flawed prefix can score high when later continuations repair it or the judge accepts a wrong equivalent. The paper itself notes that MC values also encode difficulty, criticality, and rollout uncertainty.

The terminal verifier is an LLM judgment, not a programmatic solver. In the released `omegaprm.py`, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct receives the question text, reference answer, and generated answer, but not the image, and is sampled at temperature 0.5. No calibration set, repeated-decision agreement, false-positive/false-negative analysis, or judge-version pin is reported. A separate `mllm_as_a_judge.py` uses Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct and the image for local-step scoring, but the documented traversal and formatting path does not consume its `llm_as_a_judge` field; it must not be conflated with the Monte Carlo training labels.

Selection changes the empirical data distribution. Roots with all-success or all-failure rollouts disappear, MC-zero branches terminate negative paths, short paths are removed, identical process strings are deduplicated, and positive leaves are downsampled. Without the paper-run trees and sampled records, the frequency of these decisions, step-depth distribution, class balance, failure coverage, and image/problem source mixture cannot be audited.

Reproduction remains partial. The released model result file supports a 747,779-sample run, but the examples themselves are absent. The paper and public PRM training shell disagree on epoch count and learning rate; the data-generation shell leaves the policy model at the unresolved literal `/path/to/model`; random seeds and immutable run revisions are missing. The appendix totals 5.14 million policy examples while the experiment section says approximately 4 million were used, with no row-level filtering ledger.

Best-of-N evidence is bounded by candidate quality. The PRM cannot recover a correct solution that was never sampled, and Min, Average, Max, log-probability, and odds aggregators impose different length and error-sensitivity assumptions. Benchmark gains therefore do not establish label validity, calibration, source cleanliness, or general suitability as an RL reward. MM-K12 has only one public 10,000-row split, while the paper's 500-item test mapping, original problem/image sources, decontamination, and upstream licenses remain unknown.
