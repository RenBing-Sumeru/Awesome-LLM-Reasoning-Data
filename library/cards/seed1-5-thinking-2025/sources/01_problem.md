Frontier reasoning reports can describe impressive training outcomes while leaving readers unable to determine what data was used, how traces were selected, which feedback signals governed optimization, or whether the recipe can be audited or reused. This matters especially when a report combines verifiable and non-verifiable reasoning tasks under one reinforcement-learning pipeline.

Seed1.5-Thinking provides enough operational detail to map parts of its data and feedback interfaces, but it does not release source-level mixtures, item lineage, licenses, public splits, or a training-data decontamination protocol. The relevant question for this Card is therefore what the report actually discloses about its post-training data pipeline, rather than whether its reported model result can be reproduced from public artifacts.
