The final ICML paper reports that state-of-the-art models solve only about 41% of MathConstruct, showing that construction search and long constraint reasoning remain difficult even when answers are automatically verifiable. An early 126-problem version reported roughly 54%; the difference reflects model sets and benchmark revisions and should not be compared directly across versions.

Parameterized variants reveal cases where a model succeeds on an original problem but fails after the size changes, supporting the benchmark’s ability to expose memorization or brittle templates. Programmatic verifiers provide deterministic judgments of constraint satisfaction, but they do not assess the faithfulness of explanations and can reject equivalent outputs when parsers are too narrow.
