Most process-supervised math pipelines begin with worked solutions or step labels written by people or stronger proprietary models. AlphaMath asks a narrower construction question: can a pretrained model turn questions and known final answers into its own training traces and intermediate value targets through search? The seed is therefore not “zero” supervision. It contains 7,473 GSM8K and 7,500 MATH training questions with their final answers, while the human-written solution analyses are withheld (Paper §4.1, Appendix Table 7).

The data problem is to convert a sparse terminal judgment into two coupled objects: complete solution paths for next-token training and step-level targets for a value head. That conversion must cope with a practically unbounded action space, optional Python execution, incorrect but partially useful paths, and a checker that can only compare the emitted final answer with a reference. A useful audit must therefore distinguish reference answers, generated reasoning, executed outputs, terminal labels, MCTS estimates, and the selected release rows rather than calling all of them “process supervision.”
