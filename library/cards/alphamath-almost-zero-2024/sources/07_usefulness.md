For the assigned Data Construction and Open Release Recipes track, AlphaMath is a concrete blueprint for turning answer-labeled prompts into a multi-object training corpus: positive SFT paths, negative paths, execution observations, and trajectory-conditioned step-value targets. A controlled replication can vary tree count, simulations, depth, temperature, terminal checker, and positive/negative cap while measuring how those choices change coverage, path diversity, target calibration, and downstream policy/value performance.

The released round-3 rows are suitable for guarded studies of joint language-model/value-head training, positive-versus-negative path mixtures, value-guided decoding, and false-positive Q values. They should not be treated as independently verified process labels. Reuse for training requires a dataset-license decision, pinned source/model/evaluator revisions, parsing of the serialized `output` field, and a policy for malformed code, reference-answer errors, and near-duplicate paths.

The stronger research object would preserve every raw expansion, execution result, terminal decision, visit count, edge value, tree linkage, filter decision, and selection probability across rounds. It would pair the original answer checker with a second symbolic or human audit and report target error by source, depth, terminal class, and construction round. That augmentation would let the track compare search-derived process supervision with true step validation instead of inferring data quality from benchmark accuracy.

Reuse class: useful now as an audit and replication reference; conditionally usable for math SFT and value modeling after license, snapshot, and checker review. It is not sufficient on its own for claims of process correctness or contamination-safe evaluation.
