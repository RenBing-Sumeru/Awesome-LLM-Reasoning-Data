Table 2 reports a controlled sequence for the main DeepSeekMath-Base-7B system. With the authors' two-shot prompt, scores are 59.7 on GSM8K, 33.2 on MATH, 21.9 on GaoKao2023, and 9.2 on OCWCourses. After three AlphaMath rounds with greedy decoding, the reported scores are 73.5, 53.6, 40.5, and 26.1. Value-guided SBS with `B1=3` reports 84.1, 66.3, 51.4, and 33.1; MCTS with 40 simulations reports 83.2, 64.0, 48.4, and 33.8. These are author-reported model results, not an independent reproduction and not direct evidence that each released step target is valid.

The inference comparison narrows the compute tradeoff. On MATH, Table 3 reports greedy at 53.62% and 1.6 seconds per problem, SBS `B1=3` at 66.30% and 2.3 seconds, and 40-simulation MCTS at 64.02% and 10.1 seconds. The learned value signal is associated with better search under these settings, but the comparison changes candidate generation, latency, and selection behavior together.

The appendix provides evidence against a simple monotonic self-improvement story. Figure 10 shows that round 3 does not improve every MATH test difficulty or subject category and degrades some easier categories, which the authors cite as the reason for stopping after round 3. A 100-error sample in Appendix E attributes 53% to numerical errors, 45% to logical errors, and 2% to other causes including ambiguous questions or bad reference answers. The paper also visualizes a value-model failure mode: some incorrect solutions receive values near +1 (Figure 5).
