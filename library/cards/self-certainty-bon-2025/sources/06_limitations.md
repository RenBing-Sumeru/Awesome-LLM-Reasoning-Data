Self-certainty measures distributional concentration, not truth. Artificially high-confidence errors can win, and averaging across tokens may hide a localized decisive mistake. Pure self-certainty can underperform majority voting when answers converge; Borda then depends on extraction rules, answer frequency, and the exponent p. The recommended p changes with N, model, and task and is tuned on validation queries, so selection results need a tuning-provenance and leakage audit. Computing the score requires the full vocabulary distribution at every generated token, which is unavailable from many closed APIs and makes raw trace storage much larger than response text. The paper's empirical-reference experiment shows distribution-shift sensitivity, and USC comparisons are incomplete under context overflow. Exact benchmark revisions, generation-length caps, candidate manifests, seeds for every run, and contamination controls beyond LiveBench-Math timing are not fully disclosed. The repository has implementation code but no formal release, raw rollout/logit data, pinned environment, or explicit license. Benchmark gains do not establish score calibration or data quality.
