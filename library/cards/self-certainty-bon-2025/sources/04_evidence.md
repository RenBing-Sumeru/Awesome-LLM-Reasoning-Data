Across the reported benchmarks, self-certainty often improves over greedy, first-extractable, perplexity, or sampling baselines as N grows, while Borda voting usually improves fixed-answer aggregation. These are selector evaluations, not proof that the score labels process correctness. The paper reports several counterexamples and boundaries: pure self-certainty is below self-consistency on fixed-answer MATH/GSM8K settings; one illustrated highest-confidence wrong response answers 12.5 while the correct answer is 64; the oracle remains far above self-certainty on LiveCodeBench; and an empirical rather than uniform reference distribution degrades GSM8K under distribution shift. Some USC cells are omitted because more than 20 percent of examples exceed the context limit. The official NeurIPS record and author repository verify the paper and implementation, but no raw candidates, full probability arrays, per-item scores, or selection logs were found.
