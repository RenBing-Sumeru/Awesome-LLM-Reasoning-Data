VerifierQ treats a multistep solution as an utterance-level Markov decision process. A partial solution is the state, the next utterance is the action, and the verifier predicts a bounded Q-value expressing the downstream promise of that action. Rather than sample many possible next utterances to compute a maximum, it uses an implicit Q-learning value approximation that is compatible with offline data.

The method also adds a two-expectile conservative Q-learning term to control overestimation. The resulting verifier is not merely a classifier of the current step; it is trained to propagate future reward information through the solution. At test time, this Q-value signal is used to evaluate candidate solutions, changing the quality of selection as the number of generated solutions grows.
