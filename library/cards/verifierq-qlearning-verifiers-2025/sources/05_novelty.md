Earlier verifier work commonly trains a process reward model by supervised step labels, while Q-learning approaches often rely on online action sampling. VerifierQ changes the verifier-training objective: it uses an utterance-level offline Q-learning formulation with a bounded Bellman update, IQL-based maximum approximation, and adjustable conservative Q-learning. This makes the verifier's score an estimate of continuation value rather than only local label likelihood.

The paper does not introduce a new mathematical dataset or a new generator. Its distinct contribution is a critic-side scaling mechanism that can be compared directly with PRM and voting as candidate count rises. The important audit question is whether its process labels, offline trajectories, and hyperparameters support the assumed MDP and whether the Q-value remains calibrated enough to rank novel paths.
