Tables 2 and 3 compare accuracy, total tokens, and sequential tokens. FFS often reduces token use and is competitive on the studied reasoning models; for example, DeepSeek-R1 uses 31.1K average total tokens versus 42.2K for majority voting in the paper's aggregate, while results vary by dataset. The evidence is not uniform: FFS trails majority voting on several Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus and QwQ-32B settings, and on the non-reasoning DeepSeek-V3 it is worse than majority voting across the reported tasks. Welch tests on sampled length distributions support a separation between correct and incorrect trace lengths in the analyzed data, but this is model- and task-dependent.

The experiments support the conditional claim that first completion can be a useful low-cost selector when correct traces have a favorable length distribution. They do not show that an early trace is intrinsically correct, that asynchronous wall-clock order equals semantic trace length, or that discarded traces are low-quality training negatives. Benchmark accuracy and token savings evaluate the policy; they do not validate a trace corpus. The official paper is https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18149, and its linked repository was checked but did not provide a usable release at review time.
