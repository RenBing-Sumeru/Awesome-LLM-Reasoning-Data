First Finish Search: Efficient Test-Time Scaling in Large Language Models (arXiv:2505.18149) targets the token and latency cost of reasoning-time sampling. Majority voting and other Best-of-N selectors wait for several complete traces, while budget forcing lengthens one trace. The paper observes that, for the studied reasoning models and tasks, correct traces often finish earlier than incorrect traces and asks whether completion time itself can be the selector.

The decision boundary is important: FFS is a training-free decoding policy, not a correctness verifier and not a released reasoning dataset. Its auditable data object is a set of concurrent partial traces for one prompt, with token-by-token progress, EOS or length-limit events, completion order, cancellation of unfinished jobs, trace lengths, parsed final answers, and ground-truth evaluation performed only afterward. The accepted artifacts do not establish a released prompt manifest or rollout corpus. The method therefore demonstrates a selection rule over transient traces, not the quality of reusable data.
