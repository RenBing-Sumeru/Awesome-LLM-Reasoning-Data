For the **Data Construction & Open Release Recipes** track, LeanNavigator is useful in two different ways: as an executable recipe for producing formal supervision, and as a case study in information lost between search and release.

- **SFT for tactic or proof prediction.** Reusers can train a model from serialized Lean states to tactic text, but should first pin the Zenodo file, verify its checksum and row count, decide whether multiline targets are full proofs or step targets, and revalidate records in a fixed Lean environment.
- **Graph-aware dataset reconstruction.** A stronger release should retain seed theorem/file IDs, graph IDs, node and edge IDs, tactic attempts, Lean outcomes, terminal distances, chosen-path rationale, and environment hashes. This would permit ancestor-grouped splits and replay.
- **Search-policy research.** The 100-template FAISS retrieval, 200-action cap, breadth-first queue, 30-minute limit, 200,000-transition ceiling, and eight-step terminal-distance filter define a concrete search budget that can be compared with generative tactics, best-first search, or learned policies.
- **Negative and preference data.** Error transitions and valid-but-unselected paths could form programmatic negative examples or ranked alternatives, but they must be released with Lean outcomes and common-state grouping; they cannot be recovered from the current payload.
- **Verifier audit.** Bulk replay under the reconstructed original environment and later Mathlib4 versions could separate serialization errors, environment drift, invalidated proofs, and genuine theorem-state incompatibility.
- **Contamination and split audit.** Derive train/validation/test partitions by source theorem or connected graph, not by flat row. Report exact state/proof duplicates and structural/semantic overlap with MIL, MiniF2F, and other formal corpora.
- **Release engineering.** Publish one manifest binding the Zenodo checksum, exact 4,702,639-row claim, token count script, code commit, Mathlib4/Lean/LeanDojo versions, dependencies, license, and split files.

Current reuse class: conditionally suitable for supervised proof/tactic prediction after integrity, environment, grouping, rights, and overlap checks. It is not sufficient for direct graph-search replay, process supervision, preference learning, reward modeling, or RLVR because the public archive omits the required transitions, failures, outcomes, and groups.
