Construction begins with participant journey labels, not released browser histories. An LLM screens label accuracy, feasibility, login requirements, and quality; authors manually review candidates, leaving 696 of 2,380 journeys (29.2%). Labels are embedded with `text-embedding-3-small`, reduced with UMAP, clustered with HDBSCAN, and connected in a theme graph. For composed tasks, a seed cluster is sampled, one or two breadth-first hops collect 3–6 related clusters, and 3–4 representative journeys per cluster are supplied to GPT-5.4 to select and order a subset.

Final QA removes login-gated or low-quality tasks, requires at least two sites, complete rubrics, coherence of at least 2/5, observable evidence, natural computer-use wording, no PII, and author adjudication. A source journey may be reused at most three times. Exact seeds, clustering parameters, cluster assignments, source-to-task mappings, and rejection manifests are absent.

At evaluation time, a computer-use agent operates Chrome and other capabilities in an Ubuntu OSWorld2 VM with live Internet. Default experiments allow up to 100 environment steps; selected scaling runs use 200. Runs are expected to contain `steps.jsonl` or `traj.jsonl`, referenced screenshots, and normally a numeric `result.txt`. The scorer extracts chronological actions and screenshots, calls Gemini separately for all rubrics, and writes rubric results and aggregates.

Public configs launch Chrome and `socat`, set `proxy=false`, `fixed_ip=false`, and mark `possibility_of_env_change=high`. The exact OSWorld commit, VM image, browser build, dependency set, API snapshot, network locale, credentials, reset semantics, and site state are not pinned. Although construction appendices mention rubric weights, public tasks omit them and the scorer uses an unweighted mean.
