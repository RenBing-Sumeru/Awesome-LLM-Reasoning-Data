Odysseys decomposes long-horizon completion into task-specific observable checkpoints. During evaluation, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite Preview receives one rubric at a time together with the full action history and chronological screenshots. It returns a textual rationale and binary success/failure for that checkpoint. The unweighted mean of rubric outcomes gives partial credit; `perfect` is 1 only when every rubric passes, and trajectory efficiency is the mean rubric score divided by environment steps.

The task source attempts to preserve realistic intent. Authors report collecting 2,380 labeled Chrome journeys from 248 consenting Prolific participants in the US and UK, filtering them to 696 retained journey labels, and composing 90 tasks. Another 30 tasks come from authors' real personal queries, while GPT-5.4 generates 80 hard tasks from journey-inspired seeds under human review.

This feedback contract is judgment-required rather than executable verification. Released OSWorld configs deliberately set the native evaluator to `infeasible`; the external multimodal Gemini judge supplies the score. Rubric-level judgment reveals partial progress, but it remains sensitive to screenshot coverage, judge drift, and omitted state.
