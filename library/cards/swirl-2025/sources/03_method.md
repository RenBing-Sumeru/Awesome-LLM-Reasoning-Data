**Inputs and generation.** The QA pool contains 10,000 non-Easy HotPotQA training questions; five Gemma 2 rollouts per question produce 50,000 search trajectories. The math pool contains 7,500 GSM8K training questions and 37,500 calculator trajectories. The generator is the same Gemma 2 policy later finetuned; Gemma 2 27B is the main setting, with 2B and 9B ablations. Exact model revisions, temperature, top-p, seeds, and retry rules are unknown. (Paper §2.1, pp.3–4; Appendix B)

**Interaction substrate.** At each step the policy emits reasoning plus a tagged tool call or a final answer. QA search queries are embedded by Gecko-1B into 768-dimensional English vectors and matched by nearest-neighbor lookup against articles from the relevant split. Tagged mathematical expressions are executed by SymPy and the result is appended to context. Generation stops at the tagged final-answer marker or the cap of 5 steps for HotPotQA and 10 for GSM8K; malformed-tag and tool-exception behavior is not disclosed. (Paper §2.1, pp.3–4; §2.3, p.5; Appendix A; Appendix E)

**Selection.** Four trajectory pools are compared: unfiltered; process-only; outcome-only; and the intersection. Process filtering asks Gemini 1.5 Pro Thinking for a GOOD/BAD decision at every action and keeps the full trajectory only if every action is GOOD. Outcome filtering uses a separate search- or calculator-specific prompt to compare the final answer with the golden answer and require YES. Exact retained counts, judge retries, ties, and failure logs are unknown. (Paper §2.1, p.4; Figure 1; Appendix A, pp.21–22)

**Action-prefix transformation and reward.** For each retained `tau=(s1,a1,...,sK,aK)`, SWiRL emits K overlapping subtrajectories ending at `a1` through `aK`. Optimization samples prefix state `s`, produces current action `a`, and maximizes `J(theta)=E[R(a|s)]`, with rewards summed step-wise. Gemini 1.5 Pro supplies this generative action reward without golden answers. The paper says offline finetuning uses the same policy-gradient algorithm as Gemma 2's human-feedback reward optimization, but does not name the algorithm or report optimizer, learning rate, batch sizes, epochs, KL/reference policy, sequence lengths, hardware, compute, seeds, or checkpoint selection. (Paper §2.2, pp.4–5; Figure 2)

**Outputs and uses.** The principal output is a finetuned Gemma 2 tool-use policy learned from fixed action-prefix records. Appendix C uses the same synthetic trajectory pools for an SFT comparison; this supports SFT as a studied use, not as SWiRL's main objective. Agent/tool training and evaluation are also demonstrated, but the paper does not release a benchmark. (Paper §4; Appendix C)

**Split and replay boundary.** HotPotQA-train and GSM8K-train seed training, while HotPotQA and GSM8K also appear as evaluation tasks and CofCA, MuSiQue, and BeerQA test transfer. Appendix F publishes the 300-item HotPotQA, CofCA, and MuSiQue evaluation IDs, not GSM8K/BeerQA IDs or a training manifest. A reproduction must pin source revisions, question IDs, Gemma/Gemini snapshots, prompts, retrieval corpus and index, Gecko-1B, SymPy/Python, tool outputs, filters, action-prefix weighting, reward mapping, optimizer, and evaluation judges; most of these are not publicly pinned.
