The closest baseline is not a benchmark but a family of training recipes. STaR, RFT, and ReST-style methods generate or reuse model outputs and commonly select successful outcomes for supervised improvement. DQO and OREO address offline multi-step RL, including value-oriented machinery. The paper positions SWiRL against both families. (Paper §3, pp.5–7)

What changes is the interface between trajectory data and optimization: a complete tool-use episode is selected as a unit, then serialized into one overlapping prefix record per action; a generative reward model scores the action conditioned on that prefix, and the fixed records enter offline policy-gradient training without a separately trained value network. Stage-1 process selection may retain an incorrect final answer if every action is judged reasonable, while stage-2 reward uses no golden answer. This separates process quality, outcome correctness, and optimization feedback instead of collapsing them into one label. (Paper §2; Figures 1–2)

The directional value for reasoning-data research is this explicit change of supervision boundary. It permits objective-specific questions about prefix duplication, long-trajectory weighting, stored environment observations, process false positives, and whether incorrect outcomes help or harm step-wise RL. The comparison with Appendix C also shows that the preferred filter depends on whether trajectories enter SFT or RL. (Paper §4.2; Appendix C)

What is not new includes synthetic self-generation, search/calculator tool calls, LLM judging, outcome filtering against golden answers, SFT, and policy-gradient optimization. The reported scale and benchmark accuracy do not make the hidden trajectories a data release. Reuse requires official records, filter and reward outputs, environment versions, objective configuration, lineage, and licenses before the recipe can become a training artifact.
