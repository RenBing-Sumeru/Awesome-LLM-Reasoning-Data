SWiRL's central move is to turn a fixed, full search/calculator trajectory into one training state per action: prefix `s` supplies all earlier actions and tool observations, the final action `a` is the optimized response, and a generative reward model supplies `R(a|s)`. The same Gemma 2 policy first generates the trajectories and is then finetuned on this offline decomposition. (Paper §2 opening; §2.2, p.5; Figures 1–2)

There are two distinct feedback stages. During synthetic-data selection, Gemini 1.5 Pro Thinking labels the current action GOOD or BAD given its preceding context; process-only selection retains a full trajectory only if every action is GOOD. A separate outcome filter asks a model judge whether the final answer matches the golden answer and returns YES/NO. During stage-2 SWiRL optimization, Gemini 1.5 Pro instead acts as a generative reward model for each action prefix without receiving golden answers. The paper does not disclose that reward prompt, scalar mapping, normalization, or calibration, so the stage-2 reward must not be represented as the Appendix A binary process filter. (Paper §2.1, p.4; §2.2, p.5; Appendix A, pp.21–22)

The feedback can observe the serialized context, current response, and stored tool result. It cannot by itself establish that retrieved evidence is true or uncontaminated, that the search corpus is correctly versioned, or that a plausible intermediate action will produce a correct terminal answer. The environment contributes observations—Gecko-1B vector retrieval for QA and SymPy execution for math—but success selection and RL reward remain model judgments, hence the `mixed` verification contract. (Paper §2.3, p.5; Appendix A)

Relative to DQO and OREO, the paper emphasizes offline tool-use training with action-level/subtrajectory reward and no separately trained value network. Relative to STaR, RFT, and ReST-style synthetic training, it tests process-based retention—including trajectories with incorrect final outcomes—and step-wise RL rather than relying only on correct-outcome SFT. These ingredients have prior art individually; the contribution is their combined data/feedback interface for multi-step tool use. (Paper §3, pp.5–7; §4.2, pp.8–9)
