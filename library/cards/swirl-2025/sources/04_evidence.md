Table 1 evaluates process-filtered HotPotQA SWiRL with Gemma 2 27B, retrieval access, and GPT-4o judging on 300 randomly subsampled examples per dataset. It reports HotPotQA accuracy 67.8 versus 58.6 for base Gemma 2 27B, CofCA 39.3 versus 31.7, and MuSiQue 43.6 versus 35.4. The named comparison rows from prior work are imported from Wu et al. (2024); these are author-reported results, not an independent reproduction. (Paper Table 1 and caption, pp.7–8)

The cross-task study uses a different 300-item subsample and reports base `[.65, .65, .54, .59, .45]`, SWiRL trained on GSM8K `[.79, .71, .56, .68, .49]`, and SWiRL trained on HotPotQA `[.76, .73, .62, .68, .50]` over GSM8K, HotPotQA, CofCA, BeerQA, and MuSiQue respectively. This supports cross-task transfer under the paper's judge and sampling setup; it does not identify whether gains come from unique data, repeated prefixes, the reward model, or optimizer details that were not disclosed. (Paper Table 2, p.9)

The filter ablation is especially relevant to the data claim. For SWiRL, process-only trajectories perform best and intentionally include both correct and incorrect terminal outcomes; outcome-only filtering is generally worse than even no filtering, except on MuSiQue. Appendix C reverses the local preference for SFT: process-plus-outcome filtering is better than process-only, while SFT remains below SWiRL across the filtering strategies. This is evidence that selection interacts with the objective, not proof that process labels are intrinsically high quality. (Paper §4.2 and Figure 4, pp.8–9; Appendix C, Figure 8, p.24)

Scaling evidence is bounded. Figure 6 reports that 100 HotPotQA trajectories do not generalize robustly, 1,000 show gains, and 10,000 improve further. Appendix B reports that Gemma 2 2B and 9B improve in-domain but have mixed out-of-domain behavior, whereas 27B improves consistently across the tested QA datasets. These are qualitative figure trends over one recipe, not a general scaling law. (Paper §4.2, pp.9–10; Appendix B, Figure 7, p.23)

For a process-label analysis of 500 trajectories from 100 questions, Table 3 reports HotPotQA 82.5% to 91.0% and GSM8K 87.5% to 91.6%, macro-averaged within and across trajectories. Appendix D's separate 100-example judge audit finds HotPotQA false-positive/false-negative rates of 4%/1% for Gemma 2 27B; on GSM8K it reports 15%/0% for Gemma 2 27B, 0%/10% for GPT-4o, and 4%/0% for Gemini 1.5 Pro, with cited errors involving units. Small calibration samples and judge reuse across filtering, reward, and evaluation leave correlated-error risk. (Paper Table 3, p.10; Appendix D, Tables 4–5, p.25)

All numbers above establish only the reported experimental behavior. They do not certify record-level trajectory correctness, reward calibration, contamination control, license compatibility, or the quality of an unreleased corpus.
