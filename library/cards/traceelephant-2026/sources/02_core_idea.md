The contribution is a fully observable failed-episode record that links terminal task outcome to two human causal-attribution targets: the responsible functional agent and the earliest decisive failure step. Rather than treating every later symptom as an independent error, TraceElephant applies a recoverability-aware rule: a downstream verifier may be responsible when it fails to catch a recoverable upstream mistake, and a decisive step is the earliest point after which no feasible continuation is judged able to recover.

The feedback contract is mixed. GAIA and AssistantBench official answers, or SWE-Bench Verified tests, supply the binary task outcome. Three annotators with at least one year of multi-agent-system development or debugging experience independently label the agent and step, jointly resolve uncertain cases, and then cross-review or re-annotate; final labels require unanimous agreement. The environment predicate can observe final answer or test success, but cannot programmatically establish causal responsibility. Conversely, experts can inspect full inputs, outputs, inter-agent content, and observable tool logs, but their counterfactual judgment is not an executable proof and no scalar reward is released.

The closest comparison in the Atlas is AgentErrorBench/AgentDebug, which also turns failed agent rollouts into localized root causes and feedback. TraceElephant differs by centering a benchmark for responsible-agent and earliest-step prediction across multiple multi-agent architectures and by evaluating static diagnosis against dynamic replay-oriented attribution. Its direction signal is the co-location of episode observability, terminal outcomes, and causal labels; the label uncertainty and replay substrate must remain separate from the task verifier.
