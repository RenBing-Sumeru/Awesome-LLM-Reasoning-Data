For `environment_agent_trajectory_data`, TraceElephant is suitable for evaluation and audit only under the present evidence. Researchers can benchmark responsible-agent and decisive-step localization, compare trace-only diagnosis with replay-assisted attribution, test sensitivity to removing metadata or inputs, and audit whether errors cluster by system, benchmark, tool, or episode length. It is also a useful schema-design reference for joining task metadata, full state/action/observation records, terminal outcomes, expert labels, and annotation provenance.

It is not currently established as safe training data. Training an attribution model, critic, process reward model, or recovery policy would require a declared split, task-level decontamination, successful controls, strict evaluator semantics, licence review, and record-level privacy and lineage metadata. Any derivative should retain the pinned HF revision and LFS SHA, source task ID, system and environment version, ordered input/output/tool events, terminal predicate, all annotator and consensus fields, and replay configuration. Evaluation on GAIA, AssistantBench, or SWE-Bench Verified task IDs used in training must be reported as contaminated.

The practical audit sequence is to validate JSON against a published schema, recount 220 metadata and 220 step files, diff the repaired archive against prior revisions, replace substring scoring with normalized exact matching, and replay a stratified subset under pinned containers and services. Until those checks are available, use the release as a failure-attribution benchmark and audit reference, not as an asserted RL or reward corpus.
