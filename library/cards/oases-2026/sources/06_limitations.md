The process reward inherits every error of the outcome scorer. Exact match can penalize equivalent wording, token F1 can reward partial overlap, and noisy references can contaminate all state deltas. A higher state score is correlational: new evidence may be irrelevant while the evaluator guesses better, and useful retrieval may initially lower the score. Consecutive deltas can be negative or unstable, while the shared model may learn shortcuts in its evaluator role.

Co-training adds several evaluation rollouts for every main search trajectory and therefore raises training cost. Results are limited to multi-hop QA with answerable reference strings and a particular retrieval stack; applicability to open-ended research, coding, or judgment-based tasks remains unknown. Reproducibility is constrained by unpinned Wikipedia/retrieval state and absent official code/data, rollout buffers, prompts, checkpoints, seeds, format failures, licenses, and contamination records.
