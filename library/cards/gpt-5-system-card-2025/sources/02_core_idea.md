The disclosure ledger has four separate layers. First, OpenAI names three broad training-source classes: publicly available internet information, information accessed through third-party partnerships, and information provided or generated by users, human trainers, and researchers. It also reports rigorous quality and risk filtering, reduction of personal information, and use of the Moderation API and safety classifiers. No source manifest, mixture, count, date range, record, or rights map is released.

Second, the report identifies post-training mechanisms. GPT-5 reasoning variants are trained to reason through reinforcement learning. The real-time router is continuously trained on real signals: user model switches, preference rates for responses, and measured correctness. Safe-completions center safety training on the assistant output and seek to maximize helpfulness subject to safety-policy constraints. A sycophancy score derived from production-representative conversations is also described as a reward signal. These are useful contract-level clues, but the examples, graders, reward functions, aggregation, calibration, and optimization artifacts are unknown.

Third, the report presents safety, factuality, deception, red-team, chain-of-thought, and Preparedness evaluations. Fourth, it describes deployment mitigations such as routing, prompt-injection defenses, system-level biological protections, account-level enforcement, API controls, and a Trusted Access Program. Evaluation items and deployment controls must not be relabeled as training data unless the report explicitly connects them to training.
