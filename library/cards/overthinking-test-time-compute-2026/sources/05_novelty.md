The contribution is not another way to make a model reason longer. It supplies a diagnostic vocabulary for the failure of that strategy: marginal utility, positive and negative answer flips, and a difficulty-specific overthinking threshold. These quantities make it possible to say that a longer chain was harmful to a particular previously solved example, rather than inferring efficiency only from a mean accuracy curve.

The paper also changes the comparison target for test-time methods. Instead of treating the highest observed accuracy as the whole objective, it asks for a cost-aware frontier and a selectable stopping point. This is a useful complement to search and verifier proposals because it evaluates whether their added tokens should be spent at all, and it highlights that more test-time computation can be a quality risk as well as a latency cost.
