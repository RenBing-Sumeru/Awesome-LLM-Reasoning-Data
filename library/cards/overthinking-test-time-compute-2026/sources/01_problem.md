Test-time scaling is often evaluated as though more reasoning tokens should monotonically improve an answer. That assumption encourages fixed, long budgets even for questions that have already been solved, and it hides the deployment cost of generating thousands of extra tokens. A longer trace can also invite a model to second-guess a valid conclusion, so the maximum budget may be neither the most accurate nor the most efficient choice.

The paper asks how the marginal return of extra reasoning changes with budget and problem difficulty. It needs an analysis that can distinguish useful reconsideration from harmful revision at the level of individual questions, rather than only reporting a final accuracy average. The resulting decision problem is when a serving system should stop allocating thought tokens once the expected gain no longer justifies their cost.
