The evidence supports the following pipeline, with disclosed settings separated from final-run unknowns.

1. **Inputs and source selection.** The final math prompts come from OpenMath-2-Math; code prompts come from StackExchange CodeGolf and OpenCodeReasoning; science prompts come from StackExchange Physics and organic-chemistry PDFs. Source and mixture choices are first compared through matched student-training experiments (paper Sections 4.1-4.2).
2. **Prompt-quality selection.** Code questions are selected using GPT-4o-mini difficulty labels. Math and science use GPT-4.1-mini response length as a difficulty proxy. The release recipe reports filtering to 180k math, 60k code, and 60k science prompts before later downsampling; immutable selection manifests are unavailable (Section 4.3; Appendix R.2).
3. **Deduplication and evaluation decontamination.** Math and science questions are exact-deduplicated; code is not. A training prompt is rejected when normalized Indel similarity to an evaluation prompt is at least 75% **or** the pair shares a Qwen-tokenizer 13-gram. The detector was tested on 3,092 constructed contaminated and 3,000 clean examples, but its match/removal ledger is not released (Section 4.4; Appendix F).
4. **Downsampling and teacher generation.** The selected pool is randomly reduced to about 75k questions—Figure 4 shows roughly 53k math, 16k code, and 6k science—and each question receives 16 QwQ-32B responses. All successfully produced answers are retained; the final recipe has no correctness verifier, unit-test gate, model-judge acceptance threshold, or scalar reward (Sections 4.4-4.6; Appendix R.3).
5. **Released object.** The result is 1.2M Parquet rows in one train split, each exposing `difficulty`, `source`, `domain`, and `conversations`. The response combines reasoning and final answer in one assistant message; there are no step labels or environment observations.
6. **Training.** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is fully fine-tuned with LLaMA-Factory and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3, packing, Qwen2.5 template, cutoff length 16,384, global batch 512, learning rate 8e-5, five epochs, cosine schedule, warmup ratio 0.1, and BF16 (Appendix D, Table 9). This is answer-level SFT/teacher distillation, not RL.
7. **Evaluation.** The study repeatedly trains students and compares average behavior on eight main benchmarks; AIME 2025, HMMT 02/25, HLE MCQ, and LiveCodeBench 06/24-01/25 are reserved for final held-out evaluation. The official Evalchemy repository is the linked evaluation harness.

The paper reports 22,000 H100 GPU-hours for annotation on 16 one-GH200 nodes, 25,000 A100 GPU-hours for one OpenThinker3-7B training run on 512 A100 GPUs, and 32 GPU-hours for one evaluation (Appendix O). These are reported resource figures, not a fully replayable job manifest.
