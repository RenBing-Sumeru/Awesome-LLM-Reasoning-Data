The official ICLR 2025 paper asks whether a verifier must be a discriminative classifier with a separate scalar head. In Best-of-N reasoning, the solution generator can already produce many candidates, but selection depends on a reward model that may compress its decision into one opaque score. GenRM instead expresses correctness through the language model's next-token distribution: given a problem and candidate solution, predict `Yes` or `No`; GenRM-CoT first generates a verification rationale and then predicts the verdict. This turns the verifier target, rationale teacher, filtering rule, and inference budget into inspectable reasoning-data choices rather than treating the reward as an unexplained number. (Paper Sections 1 and 3)

The paper fits Data Construction and Open Release Recipes because it specifies how queries and candidate solutions are generated, labeled, balanced, paired with verification rationales, filtered, and mixed with correct-solution SFT, and because the authors release GSM8K feedback records. The checked release serializes four physical fields—`inputs`, `targets`, `question_id`, and `model_output_id`. The input string contains the task instruction, question, candidate solution, and verification prompt; the target is either a direct `Yes`/`No` token or a critique ending in a verdict. The paper also studies programmatically generated algorithmic data, but the official repository inspection only confirmed the GSM8K release.

The boundary is outcome verification, not independently supervised process rewards: a rationale can discuss and label individual steps, yet the retained training example and deployment score are anchored to the candidate answer's final correctness. The Card reaches L4 through the full ICLR paper and appendix, the project page, and inspection of official release files. It does not establish safe training reuse because generated-rationale license scope, decontamination, an immutable release manifest, code, and trained verifier checkpoints remain unresolved.
