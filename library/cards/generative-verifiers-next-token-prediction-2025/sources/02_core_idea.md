The central change is to make verification an ordinary language-generation task. Direct GenRM receives a problem, candidate solution, and correctness question and is trained by SFT to emit `Yes` or `No`; its scalar score is the conditional probability assigned to `Yes`. GenRM-CoT receives an added “Let's verify step by step” instruction, generates a critique, and then assigns the same verdict probability conditioned on that critique. With K sampled rationales, the paper averages K conditional `Yes` probabilities; K=32 is the default unless stated otherwise. The same model is also trained to generate correct solutions through an auxiliary SFT mixture, with lambda controlling generation relative to verification. (Paper Section 3, Eqs. 3-6)

The feedback contract is mixed. Training labels come from answer correctness: programmatic checks for Last Letter and Word Sorting, and known GSM8K answers for math candidates. Algorithmic critique targets are generated by deterministic task procedures, whereas Gemini 1.0 Pro creates GSM8K critique targets using a privileged correct reference solution; rationales whose final decision disagrees with the label are removed. At deployment the reference is absent, and the learned model's `Yes` probability—not the teacher or an executor—scores each candidate. The verifier can observe the problem, the candidate text, its own generated rationale, and the prompt. It cannot observe an independent proof of every step, causal faithfulness of the rationale, or whether multiple sampled rationales make independent errors.

The closest baseline is a discriminative outcome reward model trained on the same balanced correct/incorrect pairs. LLM-as-a-Judge already generates critiques but is prompted rather than task-trained; DPO ties generation and preference learning through a different objective. GenRM's concrete distinction is a single next-token interface that jointly supports direct verdicts, generated verification rationales, solution SFT, and inference-time rationale aggregation. CoT, majority voting, reference-guided grading, and SFT are prior components; the contribution is their verifier-specific data and objective composition, not a claim that each component is new.
