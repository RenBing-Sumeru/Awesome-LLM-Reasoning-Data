For pre-training, the paper names six multimodal categories: caption, interleaving, OCR, knowledge, video, and agent. It says the corpus mixes open-source and in-house data and undergoes filtering, synthesis, and deduplication, but it does not publish source manifests, item counts, mixture ratios, or licenses.

For non-reasoning instruction data, human annotators create a seed set, a seed model produces multiple candidate responses, and annotators rank and refine the highest-ranked response. For visual coding, visual reasoning, and math/science tasks, the paper says rejection sampling expands SFT data when rule-based or model-based verification is more suitable than human judgment.

For long-CoT data, the authors assemble multi-step QA with ground truth, sample multiple trajectories with Kimi k1.5 under curated reasoning prompts, then filter wrong chains using an off-the-shelf reward model and rule-based rewards. Long-CoT warmup is followed by an online policy mirror descent RL variant: a reward model gives binary correctness relative to ground truth and a length reward penalizes excessive output. Curriculum and prioritized sampling use difficulty labels and per-instance success rates.

For agent data, an internal platform runs virtual machines in bulk across Desktop, Mobile, and Web action spaces. Heuristics collect screenshot-action records that are processed into dense grounding and continuous trajectories; human annotators provide computer-use trajectories with synthesized CoT. The environment, task instances, action schema, trajectories, and logs are not released.
