WebChoreArena asks whether browser agents that perform ordinary navigation can also complete tedious tasks that require retaining many observations, calculating over collected values, or carrying information across pages. The paper first appeared as arXiv:2506.01952v1 on 2025-06-02 and is listed by the official COLM site as an accepted COLM 2026 paper. Its decision boundary is benchmark construction and evaluation: it does not introduce a post-training algorithm or claim that the benchmark tasks were used to train the evaluated agents.

The released object is 532 human-curated task configurations over self-hosted WebArena-compatible sites: 117 Shopping, 132 Shopping Admin, 91 Reddit, 127 GitLab, and 65 cross-site tasks. 10 annotators selected from the authors created 117 templates—25, 29, 20, 28, and 15 respectively—and expanded them to about 4.5 instances per template through more than 300 hours of refinement. A task row specifies its sites and start state, intent template and instantiated intent, required observation modality, task types, execution description, instantiation values, and terminal evaluator configuration.

This belongs in Environment and Agent Trajectory Data because every evaluation instance induces a state-action-observation episode whose success is decided against live simulated-site state. The release boundary is equally important: the repository exposes tasks, wrappers, evaluator code, and a 102-task analysis subset, but not the paper-run successful and failed trajectories, observations, full result bundles, or a fully pinned WebArena image. The supported reuse class is evaluation only; task release must not be represented as a public trajectory-training corpus.
