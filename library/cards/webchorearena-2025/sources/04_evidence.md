Table 1 reports a large gap between WebArena and WebChoreArena under the same two agent scaffolds. With AgentOccam, GPT-4o falls from 42.8% to 6.8%, Claude 3.7 Sonnet from 52.0% to 23.5%, and Gemini 2.5 Pro from 54.8% to 37.8%. With BrowserGym, the corresponding changes are 36.4% to 2.6%, 51.5% to 23.1%, and 59.2% to 44.9%. These are author-reported accuracies, not an independent reproduction; cross-benchmark deltas also combine changed tasks with possible environment and evaluator effects.

Table 2 evaluates one task from each of the 102 non-cross-site templates. Adding screenshots to accessibility trees leaves GPT-4o at 2.9%, reduces Claude from 24.5% to 11.8%, and reduces Gemini from 46.1% to 39.2%. Table 3 further separates tasks whose answer depends on text inaccessible from the screenshot. The evidence supports an observation-modality sensitivity finding, not a general claim that images always harm web agents.

Table 4 tests a calculator on 215 calculation-specific tasks. Accuracy changes from 3.7% to 2.8% for GPT-4o, 19.5% to 18.6% for Claude, and 40.0% to 42.8% for Gemini; the agents use the tool on 35, 59, and 41 tasks respectively, each below 28% of the subset. The paper concludes that merely providing the calculator is insufficient because agents often do not invoke it.

Section 6 and Appendix B document counting across pages, arithmetic over more than 15 numbers, forgotten constraints and output formats, lost page state, hallucinated products, early termination, and abandoned searches. These failures substantiate the benchmark's intended difficulty. They do not establish that every task is annotation-error-free, that the fuzzy judge is calibrated, or that the unreleased runs can be replayed exactly.
