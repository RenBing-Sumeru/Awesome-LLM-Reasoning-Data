The prior baseline was mostly closed-form benchmark scoring or expensive standalone human evaluation, with some earlier LLM-as-judge uses treated as convenient automation rather than an object of measurement. This work changes the evaluation unit: the judge itself becomes auditable through human agreement, bias stress tests, and two complementary preference surfaces.

What is new is the combination of a compact multi-turn stress test, a live anonymous preference arena, released human judgments, and explicit analysis of judge biases. The quality signal is not only that GPT-4 can grade; it is that the paper measures when GPT-4 agrees with humans, when order or verbosity can distort the result, and which mitigation rules reduce known failures.

What is not new: pairwise human preference, model win rates, prompt-based judging, and aggregate leaderboard metrics existed before. Reuse checks should inspect whether the benchmark has leaked into training, whether public MT-Bench questions still discriminate current models, whether Arena votes come from the intended date range, whether judge prompts match the published implementation, and whether the target use needs row-level labels rather than aggregate agreement.
