Zheng et al. published "Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena" as arXiv:2306.05685 in June 2023, with the arXiv record marking it for the NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks Track. The problem is not generic chatbot benchmarking; it asks whether a strong model such as GPT-4 can serve as an auditable judge for open-ended, multi-turn assistant answers where exact-answer metrics are weak and human preference collection is expensive.

The evaluation surface has two parts. MT-Bench is a controlled set of 80 manually designed two-turn questions across writing, roleplay, extraction, reasoning, math, coding, STEM knowledge, and humanities/social-science knowledge. Chatbot Arena is a crowdsourced anonymous battle platform where users compare two model responses to the same prompt and vote after interacting with both. The feedback contract is therefore preference or score judgment: expert human pairwise labels, crowd arena votes, GPT-4 pairwise judgments, and GPT-4 single-answer grades converted into comparable preferences or aggregate scores.

The work evaluates judge reliability rather than proposing a training recipe or a formal verifier. Its evidence is bounded by the released questions, sampled human preferences, judge prompts, model set, and mitigation rules for position bias, verbosity bias, self-enhancement bias, and math reasoning failures.
