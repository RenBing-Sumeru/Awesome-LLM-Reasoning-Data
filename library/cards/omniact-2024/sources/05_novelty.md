The prior-work baseline is agent-environment, tool-use, and benchmark-harness papers in the same atlas category. The change introduced by OmniACT: A Dataset and Benchmark for Enabling Multimodal Generalist Autonomous Agents for Desktop and Web is the particular task object and feedback contract: desktop/web screenshots, natural-language tasks, action scripts, UI states, and benchmark records scored by script/action execution checks and benchmark scoring over desktop/web tasks. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17553; data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Writer/omniact; huggingface: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Writer/omniact; doi: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2402.17553), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
