
The closest baseline is not single-turn mathematical RLVR but web-agent training through fixed demonstrations or off-policy experience. Behavior cloning learns only from expert actions. DigiRL and AWR reuse older trajectories, and WebRL adds GPT-4-generated data plus a separately trained outcome reward model. Concurrent multi-turn on-policy systems focus on games or coding, while search-agent RL often restricts interaction to simple API calls. WebAgent-R1 changes the web-data loop by collecting full browser episodes from the current policy and immediately optimizing them with the environment's own terminal rules.

The individual components are not wholly new: WebArena and its rule-based evaluators predate this work; behavior cloning, GRPO, parallel rollout, context compression, and interaction-budget scaling are established ideas. The differentiating contribution is their composition for text-only, long-horizon web interaction: BC supplies a reward-reachable starting policy, asynchronous browser instances produce grouped current-policy episodes, dynamic compression makes those episodes trainable, and M-GRPO uses built-in terminal checks without a replay buffer or learned outcome reward model. For this atlas, the direction signal is that the rollout group, browser state, outcome rule, and interaction limit become part of the reasoning-data contract. It is not evidence that binary-reward trajectories are intrinsically high quality, and the absence of released online episodes limits direct comparison of trajectory diversity or failure coverage.
