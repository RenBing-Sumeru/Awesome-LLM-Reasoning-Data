
On the 165-task WebArena-Lite evaluation split, Table 2 reports that Qwen2.5-3B rises from 6.1% under prompting to 20.0% after behavior cloning and 33.9% after WebAgent-R1; Llama3.1-8B rises from 8.5% to 20.6% and then 44.8%. These numbers show an improvement for the evaluated policies under the paper's environment and rubric. They do not establish that each BC trace is correct, that every online trajectory has useful intermediate actions, or that the unreleased rollouts are reusable.

The ablations make the data dependency more concrete. WebAgent-R1-Zero begins from the 6.1% prompted Qwen policy, rarely receives positive rewards, and slightly deteriorates under RL, supporting the claim that BC initialization is required in this sparse-reward setup. For WebAgent-R1-CoT, QwQ-32B augmentation improves the SFT starting point from 20.0% to 24.5%, but subsequent RL reaches 30.3%, below the standard WebAgent-R1's 33.9%; the authors hypothesize that more deterministic long-CoT patterns restrict exploration. Effects are also uneven across sites: for Qwen, Reddit falls from 42.1% after BC to 26.3% after RL and Map stays at 26.9%, even though the five-site average rises.

Table 3 associates the thinking-format prompt with more interaction rounds and higher prompting success, and Figure 5 reports higher success as the maximum interaction count increases for prompting, SFT, and RL agents. This supports interaction count as a relevant test-time budget, but the paper does not publish the exact sweep values in text or equal-budget controls against other test-time selectors. Artifact evidence is narrower than performance evidence: ACL Anthology and arXiv verify the paper, and the official Apache-2.0 repository contains environment, training, and evaluation code; no separate online-rollout dataset, trained checkpoint, tagged release, or frozen environment image was verified.
