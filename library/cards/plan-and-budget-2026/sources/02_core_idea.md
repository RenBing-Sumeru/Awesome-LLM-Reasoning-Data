Plan-and-Budget separates deciding what to solve from deciding how long to think about it. It creates a plan, estimates the difficulty or uncertainty of the resulting subquestions, and assigns a nonuniform reasoning budget instead of extending every trajectory by the same amount.

The framework is valuable only if its planning overhead is repaid by better allocation. The paper therefore frames efficiency as a joint correctness–token question: spending less is not a gain if the allocation merely moves error from easy questions to hard ones.
