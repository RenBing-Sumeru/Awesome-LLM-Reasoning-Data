The artifact evidence is concrete but partial. The official final dataset at Hub revision `8229771661afc5ffb4b1306fbdd7ea76bc03b7b8` reports 31,990 train rows, fields `gt` and `conversations`, and three messages per row. The official repository links the final set, a 16,333-row raw example, a 40,000-row SFT-prompt set, and turn-level intermediates. This verifies that selected data and pipeline examples exist; it does not provide a complete candidate-to-decision lineage or show that the paper's partitions can be reconstructed. A syntax check of the official code snapshot also fails on the extra quote in `infer_math/process_prompt_turn1.py`, so the published scripts are evidence of the intended stages rather than an unmodified executable build.

For Qwen2.5-Math-7B-base, Table 3 reports MATH500 final accuracy of 77.2 for self-rewarding IFT, 78.6 after correctness-ranked DPO, and 80.2 after correctness-reward PPO. On OlympiadBench the corresponding values are 39.4, 40.1, and 43.4; on Minerva Math they are 28.7, 34.6, and 38.4. The intrinsic self-correction baselines are 51.4, 18.1, and 8.4 final accuracy on those three benchmarks. These comparisons support the trained control policy under the paper's setup, not the correctness or reuse safety of every public row.

Self-evaluation remains asymmetric. Table 4 reports correct/wrong recognition of 93.0/47.7 on MATH500, 89.6/45.9 on OlympiadBench, and 91.7/36.1 on Minerva Math for self-rewarding IFT. Selected PPO and DPO checkpoints change these class-specific errors rather than removing them. A model may therefore terminate too readily on wrong answers even when aggregate final accuracy improves.

The paper also separates data-efficiency and reward effects. DPO can build pairs for only 40% to 60% of prompts, whereas PPO can use absolute correctness on all sampled trajectories. Under a fixed test-time budget in the separate Llama study, the IFT plus M-DPO system reports 42.8% MATH accuracy with 26.4 sampled responses versus 40.4% for independent sampling with 64 responses. Llama experiments and a Llama-3.1-70B-it distillation ablation broaden the model evidence, but they use different formats and data scales from the public 31,990-row Qwen example.

The reward-design ablation supplies a negative result important for auditing: a modified PPO reward is exploited by deliberately producing an incorrect first answer and then correcting it. This is direct evidence that a seemingly reasonable multi-turn reward can create a shortcut. It does not imply that the released SFT rows have been audited for all verifier or policy shortcuts.

No reported benchmark establishes data quality by itself. The experiments do not resolve exact/near/semantic contamination, symbolic-parser false positives, upstream licenses, per-record provenance, rejected-candidate coverage, or checkpoint-to-table mapping.
