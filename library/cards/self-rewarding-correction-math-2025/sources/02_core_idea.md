The contribution is a two-stage recipe that first converts self-correction control flow into supervised sequence data and then optimizes the resulting policy with rule-based correctness. The same language model is trained to generate a solution, emit a generative label such as `[VERIFY] correct` or `[VERIFY] wrong`, revise after a wrong label, and stop after a correct label.

Sequential rejection sampling operates component by component because the complete behavior is sparse in a base model. The collector samples an initial solution, checks its final answer with ToRA/SymPy, samples an evaluation, and retains an evaluation compatible with the desired branch. It then requests a revision for incorrect-initial examples and also constructs correct-to-correct revisions under misleading wrong feedback. The paper retains three correct-ending patterns: incorrect initial/wrong label/correct revision, correct initial/wrong feedback/correct revision, and correct initial/correct label/stop. Horizon is limited to two iterations, and at most one trajectory is kept per base sample.

The feedback contract is mixed. During construction, an external programmatic answer checker decides which components survive. During SFT, the selected full episode is next-token supervision. During PPO, terminal answer correctness is a scalar reward. During iterative M-DPO, correctness differences define pairwise preferences; prompts whose sampled trajectories all receive the same correctness score are skipped. During inference, the sampled `[VERIFY]` label rather than the external checker controls continuation.

The 31,990-row Hugging Face release is the final packed SFT example, not a release of all raw candidates, verifier decisions, PPO rollouts, or DPO pairs. Benchmark accuracy therefore measures downstream policies under this recipe; it is not a per-record quality certificate for the released data.
