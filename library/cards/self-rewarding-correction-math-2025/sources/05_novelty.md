The prior-work baseline includes intrinsic self-correction by prompting, self-correction controlled by an external gold or learned outcome reward, STaR/RAFT-style rejection-sampling fine-tuning, generative reward models, and rule-based RL for single-turn mathematical reasoning. These lines separately provide correction, evaluation, or verifier-guided optimization.

The paper's specific change is to serialize answer generation, generative self-evaluation, conditional revision, and termination into one model's training trajectory. Sequential component generation addresses the sparsity of complete self-correction paths, and correct-to-correct cases teach the policy not to damage an answer under misleading negative feedback. A second stage then applies either scalar correctness through PPO or correctness-derived preferences through multi-turn DPO.

This is an operational data contribution rather than a new source of mathematical truth. ToRA/SymPy remains the training oracle, NuminaMath-CoT remains the prompt source, SFT and DPO/PPO are existing objectives, and `[VERIFY]` labels are a chosen control representation. The paper itself says natural-language alternatives could replace the special labels.

The direction signal for `data_construction_open_release_recipes` is the explicit separation of generator, checker, accepted control path, final packed example, and downstream optimizer. The public intermediates make parts of that path inspectable, while the absence of a deterministic selection ledger shows why open files do not automatically create an auditable release.

Reuse should compare the exact recipe against matched STaR/RAFT, single-turn SFT/RL, external-reward, and intrinsic-correction controls. It should also measure verifier errors, correct-to-wrong transitions, stage-wise acceptance, and rejected candidates. Better benchmark results alone are not evidence that sequentially selected rows are uncontaminated, licensed, or individually correct.
