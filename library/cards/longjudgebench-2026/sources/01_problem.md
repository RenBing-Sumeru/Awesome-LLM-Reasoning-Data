Long-form outputs require document-level judgements of organization, coverage, consistency, and scenario-specific criteria, but existing judge benchmarks mostly test short answers. LongJudgeBench measures whether LLM judges remain reliable under these demands.
Short-output benchmarks average under 500 tokens and do not cover report structure, cross-section consistency, depth, or document elements. Long-form judging also expands context with references and rubrics, creating order bias, overflow, and safety refusals that ordinary accuracy can conceal.
The authors do not define long form as a length threshold alone: a judge must handle instruction, one or more documents, reference judgment, and optional auxiliary fields. Gold labels can be scores, preferences, rankings, or answer verification, so original protocols must survive normalization.
