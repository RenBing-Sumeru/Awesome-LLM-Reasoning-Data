The survey treats a reward model as a learned proxy for preference, not as the preference record itself. It organizes the lifecycle into collection, modeling, usage, applications, and benchmarks, exposing the interfaces where annotation, aggregation, model fitting, and evaluation can fail. A useful audit boundary therefore preserves the prompt, candidates, feedback source, disagreement or aggregation rule, predicted reward, and the optimizer or selector that consumes it. This separation is the Card’s key routing lesson: a high reward-model benchmark score is neither a direct measurement of human preference nor a guarantee of safe downstream optimization.
