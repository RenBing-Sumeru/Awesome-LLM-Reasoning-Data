The public artifacts substantiate the data contract independently of model scores: all three dataset cards document the same eight logical fields, a single hosted `train` split, 16 predictions and correctness flags per row, and pass rate as their mean. Their row counts sum to 32,736. Stage-specific image archives are present for perception and visual reasoning, while text reasoning explicitly has no images.

In the paper's Qwen3-VL-8B comparison, staged training reports a visual-math average of 51.10 versus 49.64 for merged training, with mean response length 445 versus 562 on the validation analysis. In the curriculum comparison, capability plus difficulty reports 62.99, versus 58.56 for merged training, 60.53 for capability-only ordering, and 60.36 for difficulty-only ordering. These experiments support the tested scheduling recipe; they do not prove that the released difficulty labels are intrinsic, that individual traces are valid, or that the source mixture is contamination-free.

The paper's motivating diagnosis also uses a different feedback system from training: Claude-4.5-Haiku labels perception errors, and manual review agrees on 33 of 40 sampled judgments. The resulting 86.9% perception-error estimate is therefore a judge-dependent diagnostic, not the programmatic RLVR reward and not ground truth for the released rows.

Evidence for exact reproduction remains partial. The repository provides the construction utilities, reward code, stage launchers, data bindings, models, and hosted datasets, but not the 16 raw sampled completions, sampling seeds, token-level logs, per-attempt latency, or reward/advantage logs.
