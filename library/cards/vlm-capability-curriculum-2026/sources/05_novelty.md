The contribution is the combination of a capability-ordering claim with a released answer-level difficulty object. Relative to merged post-training, the recipe treats visual perception, textual reasoning, and visual reasoning as distinct stages with an explicit dependency order. Relative to conventional easy-to-hard curricula, it makes capability order a separate axis and composes the two axes rather than reducing all examples to one scalar ranking.

For rollout-data research, the useful distinction is that repeated sampling is not only an evaluation procedure: its extracted outcomes become a persistent per-problem curriculum field. The release preserves both correct and incorrect answer-level candidates, unlike a winner-only selection artifact. It should still be described as an answer-level repeated-sampling release, not as a corpus of reasoning traces, because extraction discards the underlying completions.

Stage 1 also operationalizes a model-relative perception filter: retain questions that two VLMs fail from pixels but answer from captions. This constructs a targeted perception set, but the filter is coupled to the selected Qwen2.5-VL family and to caption quality rather than establishing a universal definition of perceptual difficulty.
