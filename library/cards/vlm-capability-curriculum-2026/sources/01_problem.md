Vision-language RL post-training commonly merges perception, text reasoning, and visual reasoning examples or orders all examples by a single notion of difficulty. That treatment can hide a dependency the paper isolates: if the model reads the image incorrectly, more reasoning can elaborate the wrong premise rather than repair it. The practical data question is therefore not only which examples are hard, but which capability should be trained first and what repeated-sampling evidence supports the ordering.

This work also exposes an audit gap in many curriculum claims. Aggregate benchmark gains do not reveal the record used to assign difficulty, the model and budget that produced it, or whether failed attempts remain available. VLM-CapCurriculum makes an answer-level version of that record public, while stopping short of a full rollout-trace release.
