The central contribution is the use of active learning for constructing step-level process-supervision data. ActPRM uses a shared language-model backbone with multiple classification heads to estimate both prediction confidence and disagreement among heads for every reasoning step. A trajectory is selected for annotation whenever any step before the predicted first error is sufficiently uncertain. The authors apply this procedure to more than one million NuminaMath reasoning trajectories, retain roughly 60%, label their step correctness with QwQ-32B, and release them as ActPRMData. The official dataset currently contains about 663,000 records with a question, reasoning steps, final answer, per-step standard deviations, and binary hard labels.
