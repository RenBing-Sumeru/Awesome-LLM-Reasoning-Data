Process reward models must determine whether each step in a mathematical reasoning trajectory is correct, but step-level labels usually require human experts or expensive reasoning models. Annotation cost grows rapidly with trajectory volume and problem difficulty. Monte Carlo supervision requires many rollouts and is weak at identifying the first erroneous step, while labeling every trajectory with an LLM judge also wastes computation.

ActPRM lets the current process reward model first identify the most uncertain examples in an unlabeled trajectory pool and sends only those examples to an expensive reasoning model for annotation. Its goal is not to reduce the number of source problems or generated trajectories, but to reduce the subset that requires step-level labeling while preserving or improving first-error detection.
