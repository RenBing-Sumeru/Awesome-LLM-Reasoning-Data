The first limitation is verifier weakness. Safety accuracy peaks at 97% on 100 balanced curated sites, feasibility ranges from 75% to 89% on small human studies, and success-judge accuracy ranges from 78.0% to 82.6% on 100 labeled trajectories. These samples do not establish corpus-level safety or success. False accepts add unsafe, impossible, or failed records; false rejects remove valid behavior. Because training selects exact judge-success endpoints, judge bias, reward hacking, and verifier gaming can be inherited by the SFT set.

The second limitation is environmental brittleness and real-world effect. Websites change content, DOM structure, locale, prices, availability, consent dialogs, and navigation flows. Public tasks lack capture and replay timestamps or archived site states, so a valid task can become impossible and evaluation is not deterministic. Agents receive broad Playwright access. Prompt prohibitions against purchases, posts, accounts, personal data, downloads, and state changes are not an action-level transaction sandbox; visits and actions may affect analytics, product prices, company decisions, accounts, or site state.

The current v3 viewer contains a registration task that asks for placeholder name and email details, conflicting with the stated no-account and no-personal-information policy. This is direct evidence of guardrail leakage and dataset-version drift, not proof that every row is unsafe. A robust release would need machine-enforced action restrictions, policy tests, a violation ledger, and human review of high-impact actions.

Privacy controls are opt-in and incomplete. Current `BrowserConfig` defaults `remove_pii` to false. When enabled, scrubadub applies to processed text, while raw HTML and screenshots returned by the pipeline remain unchanged. URLs, DOM metadata, action arguments, judge text, contact information, credentials, and images can therefore retain sensitive content. The paper-run setting, consent basis, credential scanning, image redaction, retention, access control, deletion, and takedown procedures are unknown.

Release completeness is a blocking limitation for trajectory reuse. V2 and v3 expose tasks but not observations, screenshots, raw HTML/DOM, reasoning, actions, success/failure labels, judge scores or rationales, parse errors, unsafe-site rejections, training subset IDs, or checkpoints. Failed rollouts and accepted/rejected ledgers are absent. The official HF organization exposes no public model, despite paper and project release language.

Copyright and licensing remain unresolved. MIT covers the checked code and is declared on task repositories, but it does not by itself grant rights to third-party site text, images, HTML, screenshots, personal/contact data, or derived trajectories. There is no per-row source license, terms-of-use analysis, attribution, capture timestamp, consent, or takedown status. The unreleased multimodal corpus cannot be audited for these obligations.

Reproducibility is version-fragile. V2 has 146,746 rows, v3 has 146,441, and there is no mapping for the 305-row reduction. ArXiv versions, code head, data schemas, models, results, and counts changed; code has no release tag. No immutable manifest binds the Common Crawl input, accepted hosts, prompts, model endpoints, browser/container, PII setting, seeds, retries, trajectories, judge decisions, checkpoints, and reported tables. Decontamination is also incomplete beyond website-disjoint splits and the stated exclusion of WebVoyager sites from the 20K pool.

Finally, reported training and judge results are author-reported. There are no independent end-to-end reruns, seed-level variances, complete calibration records, or fixed live-site environments. Secondary learned judges reduce single-judge dependence but do not create programmatic ground truth. These limitations are partly explicit paper disclosures and partly curator inferences from the data and feedback contract.
