The exact task scale is versioned. Paper Tables 3–4 and HF v2 agree on 146,746 tasks: 143,811 train and 2,935 test, with `domain` and `task` fields. Current v3 contains 146,441 rows: 143,508 train and 2,933 test, with `website`, `instruction`, `steps`, and `criteria`. The 150K label is rounded, and v3 has 305 fewer rows than v2 without a published row-level migration explanation.

The safety result is a small controlled audit, not corpus-wide validation. On 100 curated domains balanced between 50 safe and 50 unsafe, the paper reports accuracy, precision, and recall of 85%, 0.77, and 1.00 for Llama-3.1-70B; 95%, 0.91, and 1.00 for GPT-4o; and 97%, 0.96, and 0.98 for Gemini-1.5-Pro. Thus the abstract's 97% figure is the best result on this balanced set, not evidence that 97% of released rows are safe.

Feasibility is evaluated separately by humans. Workers attempt tasks on 100 sampled safe websites per proposer model; reported completion or verifiability rates are 75% for Llama-3.1-70B, 85% for GPT-4o, and 89% for Gemini-1.5-Pro. These are sample-level proposer comparisons, not feasibility labels for all 146K public rows. Depending on the proposer, 11–25% of audited tasks are not human-verifiable.

The success judge is also imperfect. On 100 trajectories with human binary success labels, thresholding judge success above 0.5 gives reported accuracy of 78.0% for Gemini-1.5-Pro, 81.7% for Llama-3.1-70B, and 82.6% for GPT-4o. The scaled v2 run instead uses Qwen3-235B as zero-shot judge, and the SFT pool keeps only success equal to 1. Full confusion matrices, calibration, agreement, site/category slices, and the underlying validation records are not released.

In the reported Qwen3 experiment, Qwen3-1.7B starts at 11.5% success under a Qwen3-235B judge and reaches 56.9% after training, an absolute increase of 45.3 percentage points. The authors report that the top checkpoint exceeds the Qwen3-235B collection policy and Llama-4-Maverick and reaches 94.7% of Gemini-2.5-Flash performance; secondary learned judges show the same direction. These are author-reported live-web and judge-mediated results, not independently reproduced ground-truth success rates.

The 20K training ablation retains 10.5K Qwen3-235B trajectories with endpoint judge success 1. The scaled corpus is reported as approximately 150K trajectories with about 15 steps each, 2.2M screenshots, 2.2M action/reasoning traces, and 150K judge traces, at 1,200 V100 GPU-hours. The official release does not expose those records: the HF organization reports zero public models and only the v1/v2/v3 task datasets.

Current code supports task proposal, live collection, Playwright action, observation serialization, judging, SFT, evaluation, and visualization under an MIT license. The checked head has no tag or GitHub Release and postdates paper v2. This demonstrates that a related recipe can run; it does not bind current code to the exact paper task rows, trajectories, selection ledger, checkpoint, or tables.
