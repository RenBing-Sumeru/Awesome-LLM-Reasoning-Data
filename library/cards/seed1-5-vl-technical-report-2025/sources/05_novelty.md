For reasoning-data curation, the strongest contribution is the unified feedback ledger across modalities. General visual prompts are scored by a preference model, while STEM expressions, boxes, points, constrained instructions, puzzles, and games use task-specific verification. Both reward streams enter one PPO run, yet the reasoning trace is deliberately excluded from direct reward-model supervision. This makes the exact supervision boundary unusually clear.

The second contribution is cyclic data construction. Hard prompts drive RL; verifier-approved responses become the next LongCoT SFT data; and successive RL checkpoints become better trace generators. Pre-training also mixes human annotation, public records, web crawling, model annotation, rendering, simulation, and diffusion-based edits. The report therefore illustrates how a frontier multimodal dataset is a changing system of records, not a single static corpus.
