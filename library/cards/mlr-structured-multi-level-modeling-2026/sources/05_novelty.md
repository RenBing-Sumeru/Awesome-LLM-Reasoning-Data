Existing chain-of-thought supervision treats reasoning as a token sequence or imposes a fixed external planner–executor architecture. MLR derives cognitive modes from trajectories and represents every step with why it is performed, what subgoal it serves, and what it produces. The change is the hierarchical data representation and decision interface rather than longer chains or a new final-answer loss.

It also allows one failure to be separated into high-level planning versus low-level execution errors.
