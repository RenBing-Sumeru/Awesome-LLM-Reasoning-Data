Prior desktop-agent benchmarks already provide virtual machines, screenshot/action loops, application tasks, and terminal checking. WeaveBench does not newly introduce GUI control, shell tools, containerized desktops, expert task writing, LLM judges, or success-rate reporting in isolation.

The first concrete change is the task admission object. P1 rules out tasks in which GUI or CLI can be substituted away, P2 requires multiple interleaved phases, and P3 requires linked cross-application state. C1-C4 then connects public-workflow sourcing to self-contained packaging, blind review, and three-agent pilot revision. This makes hybrid dependency and long-horizon structure auditable at task-construction time instead of treating them as post hoc labels.

The second change is the feedback interface. The judge does not inspect only the final file: it actively retrieves evidence across the full trace, deliverables, screenshots, files, logs, shell checks, and hidden anchors; it preserves clause, artifact, dimension, and shortcut evidence before reducing the result to a scalar. The outcome-only ablation provides a quality signal for trajectory access, while the 35.2% E5 reward-hacking share in the authors' failure taxonomy shows why shortcut auditing is operationally relevant. Neither result validates the judge itself.

For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is the joined episode contract: source provenance, task bundle, frozen state, hybrid actions and observations, full trace, artifact evidence, hack decision, scalar reward, and terminal threshold can be inspected as one lineage. This supports study of where supervision attaches and what is lost when full episodes are collapsed to outcomes. The 114-task scale, 8 domains, 23 subcategories, and multiple runtimes add breadth, but scale and benchmark scores are not novelty or data-quality evidence.

Before reuse, researchers still need the exact machine-readable provenance index, a GitHub commit for the paper tables, reconciled judge identity and threshold, pinned prompt/runtime versions, full successful and failed trajectory manifests, judge calibration, split/decontamination policy, and component-level rights. Without those checks, the concrete novelty remains an evaluation design and audit surface, not a verified post-training corpus.
