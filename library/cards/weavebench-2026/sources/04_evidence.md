All figures below are author-reported under the paper's model, runtime, budget, and judge settings and have not been independently reproduced.

- On the fixed OpenClaw runtime, the highest reported PassRate is 35.1% for Claude Opus 4.7. Across model-runtime pairings, the highest is 41.2% for Claude Opus 4.7 with Claude Code (Paper Tables 2-3, page 8). These establish difficulty and runtime sensitivity under the reported contract, not record-level task correctness.
- Interface ablations report at most 0.8% PassRate for GUI-only and at most 3.5% for CLI-only, versus 35.1% for Claude Opus 4.7 in the hybrid setting (Paper Tables 4-5, page 8). The comparison supports P1-P3's hybrid-interface motivation but remains coupled to the evaluated agents and budgets.
- For GPT-5.5, outcome-only grading raises PassRate from 33.3% under the audited judge to 53.5% (Paper section 4.4 and Figure 4, page 9). This is evidence that full-trajectory inspection rejects some artifact-only successes; it is not an error-rate calibration for the judge.
- Across 2,209 OpenClaw trials on three frontier backbones, the authors count 1,735 failures. Their taxonomy assigns 35.2% to E5 reward hacking and 30.4% to E4 long-horizon discipline (Paper section 4.6, Figure 6, and Appendix F). The labels come from judge/manual trajectory inspection rather than an external gold standard.
- Best live rollouts have median 76 and mean 88 tool calls, range 14-271; 113 of 114 exceed 20 calls. They also have median 16 GUI-to-CLI switches and median 15 applications or business states, with every task showing at least one switch and at least three states (Appendix A.2 and Figure A1). These are observed best-rollout statistics, not guarantees for every run.

The paper says task anchors and scoring rules are human-reviewed and sampled verdicts are manually audited; Appendix B.1 additionally says every verdict was co-author spot-checked. Because those descriptions differ and no independent calibration set, false-positive/false-negative rate, or inter-rater reliability is reported, judge fidelity remains an audit question. Benchmark performance therefore must not be used as proof of data quality, release completeness, or safe training reuse.
