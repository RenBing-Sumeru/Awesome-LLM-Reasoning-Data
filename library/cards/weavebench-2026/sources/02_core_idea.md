WeaveBench's contribution is to bind long-horizon hybrid-interface task design to a trajectory-aware terminal judge: the task must make GUI and CLI/code mutually necessary, and success is decided from the full interaction plus resulting artifacts rather than from a final file alone.

The construction surface and feedback surface are explicit. Each task is a self-contained environment bundle admitted by P1-P3 and installed in a rollback-capable Ubuntu VM. During evaluation, the agent receives screenshots and runtime tool responses and emits GUI actions together with CLI, file, code, browser, or other tool calls. After termination, an isolated host-side OpenClaw judge can re-fetch the task specification, full trace, deliverables, images, files, logs, shell checks, and hidden ground-truth anchors. It returns per-clause `satisfied`/`partial`/`false` evidence, per-artifact correctness, eight dimension scores with reasons, a hack flag with quoted trajectory evidence, and one scalar in [0,1] (Paper section 3.4; Appendix B.2-B.5; official `AGENT_JUDGE.md` and judge prompt).

This is a mixed verification contract, not a programmatic oracle. The judge combines environmental inspection and shell/file checks with learned model judgment over semantic evidence. It can observe archived actions, text, screenshots, deliverables, and anchor-backed checks available to the isolated evaluation side. It cannot certify intent, eliminate learned-judge bias, or guarantee stable decisions across prompt, provider, model alias, or environment versions. If a hack is detected, the score is zero; otherwise the final score is the minimum of mean eight-dimension score and deliverable correctness. Supervision therefore attaches to the full episode and ends in a scalar reward, with clause, artifact, dimension, and hack details retained as process evidence.

The closest benchmark line includes OSWorld and related desktop-agent suites, which already provide VM-backed interaction and terminal task checking. WeaveBench's concrete change is the P1-P3 admission rule plus an active judge that inspects the trace for shortcut behavior and re-fetches heterogeneous evidence. The paper's outcome-only ablation is the key reason this matters: removing trajectory auditing raises the reported GPT-5.5 PassRate from 33.3% to 53.5%, showing that final artifacts alone accept behaviors rejected by the full contract. This result supports trajectory access under the reported setup; it does not prove that the learned judge is calibrated or that the benchmark data are high quality.
