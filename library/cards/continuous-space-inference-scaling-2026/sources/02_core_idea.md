The authors first expose a gap between latent-path availability and latent-path selection. Dropout-based sampling gives COCONUT multiple distinct answers and a growing Pass@N curve, so additional test-time samples contain useful potential. Yet confidence, self-consistency, and reward-model reranking recover only a small portion of it because correct and incorrect latent trajectories occupy insufficiently separable representation regions.

Their response is both diagnostic and constructive. They measure reward-model discrimination, geometric distribution, and trajectory dynamics to locate the bottleneck, then add a lightweight post-training objective that gives correct and incorrect answer-verified rollouts more distinguishable geometric behavior. The goal is not to claim that a standard text-space PRM works in latent space, but to show what representation property must improve before verifier-guided scaling can work reliably.
