The paper contributes a negative transfer result that is unusually useful for test-time scaling: diverse continuous trajectories can create the same kind of candidate-set opportunity as textual chains, but standard text-derived verifier supervision does not make those trajectories rankable. It separates “can generate a correct candidate” from “can score a correct candidate,” preventing Pass@N alone from being mistaken for an end-to-end scaling result.

It also ties this failure to representation geometry rather than leaving it as a generic reward-model weakness. By analyzing correct and incorrect latent trajectories and then making a small answer-verified geometric intervention, the paper turns a transfer failure into a concrete research target. This reframes test-time compute in continuous reasoning as a joint sampling-and-discrimination problem rather than a direct reuse of textual Best-of-N recipes.
