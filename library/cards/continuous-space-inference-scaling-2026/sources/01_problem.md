Multiple sampled text chains can be reranked by process or outcome reward models, making test-time scaling effective in ordinary language-space reasoning. Continuous reasoning models instead perform intermediate computation in hidden representations. They can sample diverse latent paths, but it is not established whether the same verification and reranking recipes can recognize good paths well enough to turn that diversity into accuracy.

The paper asks whether latent-space reasoning has a usable inference-time scaling curve and, if not, where the failure arises. A high Pass@N would show that correct solutions occur among samples, while weak reranking would show that the system cannot identify them. The challenge is to separate correct from incorrect continuous thought trajectories without explicit textual reasoning steps, so the search budget can be converted into a better final answer rather than merely more latent samples.
