The paper's main contribution is to locate contamination inside the agent trajectory rather than treat the whole question as simply contaminated or clean. BML labels a suspicious search direction before a page is visited; QCL labels source-question exposure without an answer; EAL labels direct answer-key retrieval. This separation shows that a repository/URL hit alone does not establish performance inflation, while explicit answer exposure strongly changes predictions.

It also connects evidence provenance to temporal behavior. Intermediate predictions reveal whether a page changes the answer immediately or after further reasoning. Survival and escalation analyses ask when correctness appears and whether weak exposure leads to stronger leakage later. The resulting audit object joins question provenance, search/visit observations, contamination state, prediction state, and terminal correctness.

For Track 6, this reframes web observations as potentially adversarial environment state. A citation is not automatically legitimate evidence: it may be a mirror of the test item. The work does not contribute a verified public training/replay dataset, a perfect detector, or a controlled causal intervention, and those boundaries are part of the result.
