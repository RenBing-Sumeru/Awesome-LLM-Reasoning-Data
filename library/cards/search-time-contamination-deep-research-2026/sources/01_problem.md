Deep-research agents are evaluated by giving them a question, open-web Search and Visit tools, and permission to synthesize evidence over multiple turns. Public benchmarks make this protocol fragile: the same web can contain the benchmark name, exact test question, or question paired with its answer. Retrieval can then replace the intended reasoning task with benchmark-artifact lookup while still producing a correct, cited response.

The paper calls this Search-Time Contamination (STC) and distinguishes three step-level events. Benchmark Metadata Leakage (BML) occurs when Search returns suspicious benchmark/data-hosting or exam-related URLs. Question-Context Leakage (QCL) occurs when visited content reproduces exact or highly specific question context without an answer. Explicit Answer Leakage (EAL) requires retrieved content to pair the exact question fingerprint with its ground-truth answer. One trajectory can contain several types and can escalate from a weak URL signal to direct answer exposure.

The data object is therefore a provenance-bearing search episode: benchmark question and label; reasoning; Search query; ranked URLs/snippets; Visit URL and page content/summary; intermediate prediction; per-turn leakage labels; and final answer/correctness. The audit covers 6,803 questions from six medical/clinical benchmarks. It is an evaluation audit, not a training corpus or controlled contamination-injection study.
