Search-enabled Tongyi outperforms its search-disabled version on all six full datasets, but post-hoc leakage splits show why this gain cannot be assigned uniformly to retrieval. BML-only subsets do not consistently improve: BML groups are better on four benchmarks and worse on two. Direct answer exposure is different. Within BML, EAL-containing groups often show much higher final accuracy; all-three-type groups reach 100% on five datasets and 90.16% on MedMCQA, though several groups are small.

Turn-aligned changes are stark. After EAL, accuracy moves from 7.69% to 89.74% on MedQA, 17.86% to 82.14% on MMLU, 19.25% to 79.45% on MedMCQA, 8% to 48% on MedXpertQA, 20% to 100% on HLE-149, and 0% to 80% on Medbullets5op. EAL's Cox hazard ratios for reaching a correct prediction range from 2.20 to 8.92; BML and QCL effects are mixed. QCL raises the subsequent EAL hazard on most datasets, with ratios from 2.50 to 6.74.

Generalization checks show corpus overlap, not simply open versus curated search, drives risk. On the first 100 MedQA items, reported leakage is 60% for Gemini, 9% for Step, and 0% for Valyu; when Valyu is tested on PubMedQA—derived from a corpus its search system supports—65 of 100 questions leak. The abstract reports up to 4% performance inflation on HLE biology/chemistry subsets. These are associations under live, non-random retrieval, not a causal estimate from controlled answer injection.
