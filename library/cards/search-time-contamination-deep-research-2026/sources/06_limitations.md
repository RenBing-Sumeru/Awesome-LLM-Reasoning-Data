**Detector error.** BML patterns omit unknown hosts and include broad terms such as question, test, and quiz; suspicious URLs can be false positives and unmatched mirrors false negatives. QCL lexical overlap misses paraphrases and can flag legitimate source recovery. EAL depends on DeepSeek V4 Pro: Medbullets5op recall is 83.33%, and MedQA recall is not measured. Detector versions and thresholds must be pinned.

**Experimental boundary.** Contamination groups arise after live trajectories, not random assignment. Question difficulty, searchability, age, and source type can confound accuracy gaps. Disabling all Search changes far more than answer leakage; it is not a precise EAL intervention. Intermediate missing predictions are scored wrong, and small EAL/QCL groups can yield extreme percentages. The study is concentrated in clinical/medical QA.

**Replay and release.** Web rankings, snippets, pages, and paywalls drift. Proprietary agents hide most steps. The anonymous result repository is currently inaccessible, so complete success/failure retention, tool failures, detector code, timestamps, and snapshots cannot be checked. A frozen evidence archive is necessary for replay, subject to webpage rights.

**License, privacy, and safety.** The paper is CC BY 4.0, but benchmarks and retrieved pages have separate licenses/terms. Search traces can retain copied third-party text, URLs, possibly identifiers, and model reasoning; no record-level PII/robots/consent/redaction/storage audit is reported. Exact leakage patterns and leaked Q&A pairs are dual use: they can improve defenses or be deliberately published to game evaluations. Medical correctness metrics must not be read as clinical safety validation.
