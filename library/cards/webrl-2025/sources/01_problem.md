Published at ICLR 2025, WebRL addresses a specific online-training bottleneck for open web agents: useful browser tasks are scarce, success feedback is sparse, and a policy can forget earlier skills as its own rollout distribution changes. The work is not a general open-web crawler or a static instruction-answer dataset. Its decision surface is a self-hosted WebArena-Lite episode in which a model observes simplified page HTML plus action history, emits browser actions, receives terminal feedback, and supplies future curriculum or replay data.

The seed side contains 1,186 WebArena-Lite training samples with an instruction, an oracle trajectory, and a reward function. Evaluation uses 165 held-out tasks over 5 sites: Reddit, GitLab, CMS, OpenStreetMap, and OneStopShop. This makes WebRL relevant to both environment-agent trajectory data and training-usage optimization: the trajectory is generated inside an evolving policy-environment loop, while selection, reward assignment, and replay determine which parts become supervision.

The evidence boundary is important. The paper, appendix, official code, model checkpoints, ORM checkpoint, external environment stack, and one SFT artifact are verifiable. The complete generated-task stream, raw success and failure trajectories, ORM labels, replay decisions, and historical environment snapshots are not released as an immutable corpus. The Card therefore documents a reconstructable method and a partial artifact release, not a fully reusable trajectory dataset.
