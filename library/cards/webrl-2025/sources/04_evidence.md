On the 165-task WebArena-Lite evaluation set, the paper reports that Llama-3.1-8B rises from 4.8% to 42.4% average success rate and GLM-4-9B rises from 6.1% to 43.0%. WebRL-Llama-3.1-70B reaches 49.1%. Under the reported setup, these exceed GPT-4-Turbo at 17.6%, GPT-4o at 13.9%, and AutoWebGLM at 18.2%. These are author-reported results for one self-hosted benchmark configuration, not independent reproduction or evidence of open-web deployment reliability. (Table 1)

The learned ORM is evaluated on WebArena-Lite test records and 100 manually labeled rollouts. The paper reports approximately 80% accuracy, while compared baselines are slightly above 70%. This supports using the ORM as a nontrivial outcome proxy, but it leaves roughly one-fifth error at aggregate level and provides no complete per-site calibration, adversarial evaluation, or public prediction log. (Section 3.8)

Ablations attribute the final result to all three coupled components: curriculum learning, KL-constrained update, and replay. Removing replay causes performance to worsen across phases; too-low or too-high replay confidence is inferior to the middle band; adding failed histories to replay yields slower and more volatile learning because terminal failure can penalize locally correct actions. Across 4 random-seed runs, the overall trend rises but fluctuates. The Map site declines late and GitLab dips slightly, so average improvement hides cross-site interference over the 8 phases. These negative results are especially relevant to trajectory selection and reward assignment.
