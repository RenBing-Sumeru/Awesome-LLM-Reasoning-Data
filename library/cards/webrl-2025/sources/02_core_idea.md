WebRL's central contribution is to make data construction part of online policy optimization. Starting from 1,186 demonstrations, it alternates browser rollout collection, failure-conditioned task generation, learned outcome labeling, actor-critic updates, and successful-experience replay. Across 8 phases, each phase admits 500 filtered instructions whose difficulty is intended to match the current policy rather than a fixed task bank.

The feedback contract is mixed. WebArena-Lite reward functions label the original executable tasks and supervise construction of a 12,200-sample outcome-reward-model dataset. Newly generated tasks do not come with such executable checks; a learned ORM instead receives the instruction, full action history, and final HTML, then compares YES and NO probabilities to emit binary 1/0 reward. The verifier can observe the visible final page and recorded actions, but not hidden state, omitted earlier HTML, unintended side effects, or conditions absent from the final page.

Supervision attaches at several levels: terminal reward labels the episode, the critic estimates trajectory value for state-action updates, and actor confidence selects replay records. This differs from SFT or Filtered BC, which learn from fixed demonstrations, and from DigiRL, which performs online learning on a fixed task set. WebRL's direction signal is the coupling of failure-derived curriculum, learned terminal judgment, KL-constrained optimization, and selective replay; it is not evidence that the learned ORM is equivalent to an executable environment verifier.
