The contribution is a coupled recipe: first synthesize SFT reasoning chains that expose trial, error, and self-verification; then make RL exploration deliberately diverse with high-temperature multi-rollout sampling, answer-level reward, RLOO normalization, entropy regularization, and a normalized-KL/EMA anchor. The authors also study a separate inference-scaling procedure that truncates a long completion at successive 10% prefixes and has the base model summarize/finalize each prefix. (Paper §§3.1–3.3.)

The observable feedback is narrow but concrete. Candidate final answers can be compared with known math labels, so the reported RL reward is 1 for correct and 0 for incorrect; detected pathological responses receive -1. The contract cannot observe whether every intermediate step is valid, whether a correct-looking answer used sound reasoning, or whether the parser accepts every equivalent mathematical form. The SFT chain's critiques are synthetic LLM text rather than separately released human or verifier annotations.

Closest comparisons are ordinary outcome-reward RL on math and test-time sampling/scaling. T1's stated difference is not a new ground-truth verifier: it combines an error-aware trace-synthesis pattern with high-temperature, 64-way rollout exploration and analyzes long-chain inference budgets. For the Atlas, the direction signal is the coupling of trace form, outcome reward, and sampling diversity; users should still audit the trace generator and release before interpreting it as a process-supervision resource.
