The prior-work baseline is not “no reasoning data”: outcome-reward math RL, multi-sample search, entropy/KL regularization, and test-time sampling already exist as general ingredients. T1 changes the composition and the object that is presented to SFT. Rather than keeping only successful solution traces, it asks LLMs to turn multiple attempts plus correctness-grounded critiques or verifications into one coherent, error-aware chain. It then connects that chain form to a high-temperature 64-rollout RL protocol and a long-response inference analysis.

The direction signal for reasoning-data work is therefore an interface choice: a trace may explicitly represent an attempted route and a correction while ultimate supervision remains an answer-level label. This can expose recoverable errors to a learner, but it does not establish that individual corrections are true or that the trace is faithful to an underlying model process. RLOO, normalized KL, entropy bonus, and answer checking should be treated as reported integrations, not as claims of wholly new primitives.

Before reuse, check whether the desired task has reliable final-label verification, whether malformed-response penalties will transfer, whether teacher-generated critiques are acceptable, and whether the training/evaluation corpora are separated. A comparison to process-reward or step-verifier resources must not silently equate synthetic self-verification prose with independently checked intermediate labels.
