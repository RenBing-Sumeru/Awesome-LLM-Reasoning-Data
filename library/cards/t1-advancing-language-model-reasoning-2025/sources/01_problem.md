T1 is an ICML 2025 paper in PMLR 267 about how to obtain and exploit longer mathematical-reasoning behavior without treating a benchmark score as evidence that a training dataset is sound. Its primary record is the PMLR proceedings paper; OpenReview records it as an ICML 2025 poster. The paper is relevant to `rollout_search_test_time_trace_data` because its central training/evaluation surface is a long textual trace sampled many times under an answer-level verifier, followed by an analysis of more inference tokens—not a step-labeled process-reward dataset.

The construction starts with public MATH-train and NuminaMath problems. An intended SFT example is a question plus an LLM-synthesized chain containing several solution attempts, answer-grounded feedback, and a merged final solution. An RL item is a filtered math prompt whose completions receive a final-answer correctness signal. This gives the Atlas a concrete data object and feedback contract: public questions, synthetic process text, and outcome verification. It does not provide a tool-using environment, a human annotation protocol, or released per-step correctness labels.

The decision boundary is important. This card covers the recipe and its public artifact, not a claim that the available files are ready for direct training reuse. The paper reports roughly 12,000 SFT questions and 30,000 RL questions, whereas the current dataset repository exposes two heterogeneous JSONL files without a documented mapping back to those counts, rows, or experiments. That gap is why this is an L4 reading/audit card with `partial` status rather than an endorsement of the release. (Paper Appendix A; official Hugging Face dataset, checked 2026-07-23.)
