Before AgentRewardBench, web-agent evaluations commonly relied on benchmark-specific functional rules, while human review served as a costly fallback and LLM judging was a plausible but insufficiently audited alternative. The concrete change is to make the evaluators themselves the benchmark target. The same 1,302 full episodes are paired with expert references, rule outputs, and multiple LLM-judge outputs so precision, recall, disagreement, and failure modes can be compared on a shared record set.

The release adds unusual audit depth for this type of benchmark. It exposes cleaned trajectories, step-linked screenshot files, raw expert annotation rows, task-level splits, evaluator inputs and outputs, completion arguments, provider responses, parsed labels, costs, and source-trajectory metadata. Both successful and unsuccessful completed episodes remain available. The automatic-evaluator layer is therefore inspectable rather than represented only by aggregate scores.

This work does not introduce the five source benchmarks, AgentLab, BrowserGym, the four source model families, human trajectory review, environment rewards, or LLM-as-a-judge as general ideas. It also does not produce a trained reward model, an agent policy, a deterministic replay package, or a fully licensed training corpus. The four-level optimality field appears in prompts, release data, and scoring code, but its weak reconciliation with the paper's “3,906 binary annotations” is a release-version complication rather than a separate demonstrated contribution.

The direction signal for `environment_agent_trajectory_data` is that feedback provenance should travel with an episode. A terminal reward without its predicate, a judge label without its representation and model configuration, or a human label without an annotation protocol is insufficient for audit. AgentRewardBench demonstrates a useful multi-signal record, while also showing that disagreement and missing provenance are part of the data object.

Reuse should verify the pinned trajectory and annotation hashes, join the task split explicitly, preserve duplicate annotation rows and `Unsure`, distinguish expert from LLM-generated reasoning, pin all evaluator configurations, and retain both success and failure. It should also resolve licensing, upstream rights, environment versions, replay fixtures, decontamination, privacy, and annotation reconciliation before any training-oriented conversion. Scale and benchmark scores are not substitutes for these checks.
