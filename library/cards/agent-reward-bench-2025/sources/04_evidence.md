The release-scale evidence agrees at the unique-trajectory level. The paper reports 1,302 trajectories across five benchmarks and four source LLMs. The pinned Hugging Face inventory contains 132 AssistantBench, 300 VisualWebArena, 398 WebArena, and 472 combined WorkArena/WorkArena++ cleaned JSONs, totaling 1,302. It also contains 1,302 screenshot directories and 15 judgment files per trajectory. These counts establish release shape, not correctness or training-data quality.

Against the expert success reference, no LLM judge in the main overall comparison exceeds 70% precision; the best reported overall value is 69.8% for GPT-4o(A). This is material for trajectory filtering because a high false-positive rate can admit failed episodes. The functional rule evaluator reaches 83.8% precision but only 55.9% recall, supporting the authors' claim that task-specific rules underreport semantically successful trajectories. High precision does not make the rule contract complete.

Input representation changes the simplified judge. With GPT-4o-mini, Table 2 reports success precision of 64.5% for screenshot only, 61.5% for accessibility tree only, 62.1% for both, and 60.7% for neither. The non-monotonic result is evidence that judge behavior is representation-sensitive; it does not prove that screenshots are generally superior or that any representation is sufficient.

The paper reports 89.3% inter-annotator success agreement on a second annotation of GPT-4o WebArena trajectories. Direct exact-match calculations from the pinned CSV produce nearby but not identical values depending on subset and `Unsure` handling, and no frozen script invocation or agreement output reproduces 89.3% exactly. No paper-level exact agreement is reported for the four-level optimality label. Consensus discussion may improve consistency, but the release does not preserve a complete independent-disagreement and adjudication history.

The qualitative error analysis identifies grounding mismatch, misleading agent reasoning, missed instruction details, and misunderstood action intent as sources of false-success judgments. Side-effect positives are rare, making precision low and conclusions unstable for that field. These are direct limitations of the feedback signals. They should not be collapsed into a single leaderboard number.

The strongest evidence-based conclusion is that AgentRewardBench enables record-level comparison among expert labels, environment rules, and LLM judgments while preserving both successful and failed completed episodes. The paper demonstrates evaluator evaluation. It does not demonstrate that these trajectories improve a reward model or policy, that the expert labels are error-free, that environment replay is deterministic, or that benchmark performance certifies data quality.
