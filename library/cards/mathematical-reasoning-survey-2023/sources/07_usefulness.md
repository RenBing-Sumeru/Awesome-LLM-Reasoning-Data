Use this paper when framing a mathematical-reasoning project or judging whether a benchmark matches your question. It gives a reader concrete handles—task, dataset, method, output form, and benchmark—for comparing papers that might otherwise all appear to address “math reasoning.” It is also a helpful bridge between problem solving and theorem proving in language. Before selecting a primary study, use the survey's organization to write down the mathematical object, the expected solution or proof, and the success rule that matter for your question. That small record prevents a convenient benchmark from being mistaken for a complete measure of the ability you need.
