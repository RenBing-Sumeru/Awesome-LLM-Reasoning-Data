The survey's evidence comes from its review and evaluation of existing tasks, datasets, benchmarks, and methods rather than from one new universal score. Results should therefore be interpreted within the original problem type and benchmark. A useful comparison asks whether two systems solve the same kind of mathematics, receive comparable information, produce the same form of output, and are judged under the same rule. An answer-correctness result and a theorem-proving result can both concern mathematical reasoning without measuring the same behavior. This matters when interpreting apparently similar evaluation results. The paper provides a structured account of these distinctions, while the cited benchmark papers remain the source for exact splits, formats, and checking procedures.
