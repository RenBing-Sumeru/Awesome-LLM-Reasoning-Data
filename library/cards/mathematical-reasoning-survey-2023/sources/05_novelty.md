The paper's contribution is a broad task–data–method view of mathematical reasoning across a decade of deep-learning research. Rather than introducing a new solver, it connects recent advances in large-scale neural language models to established mathematical reasoning benchmarks and methods. Its distinctive value is to make the relationship between problem solving, theorem proving, data, and evaluation visible in one account. Individual primary papers supply the new systems and scores; this survey supplies the context needed to judge what kind of change those results represent. It is especially helpful when a new result sounds general but has only been shown on one mathematical object or output format.
