The paper organizes the area around key tasks, datasets, and methods, then evaluates existing benchmarks and methods and discusses future directions. This structure helps readers connect a mathematical objective—solving a problem or proving a theorem—to the data and evaluation used to study it. It also places recent language-model work in a longer deep-learning history rather than treating it as detached from prior mathematical reasoning research. The key idea is to read a mathematical result through its full setting: what object must be handled, what form a solution or proof takes, and what criterion determines success. This also exposes where a result is silent: a final answer may establish correctness for one item without showing how a system reached it. That makes broad claims about “math reasoning” easier to examine precisely.
