SCS augments terminal outcome reward with a consistency signal computed from controlled continuations of the same partial trajectory. It first samples an initial rationale, keeps a prefix according to truncation ratio k, and resamples the suffix m times. Each continuation sees a slightly perturbed version of the image, with Gaussian noise strength drawn independently from a bounded range. The option answers from the continuations form a set A; the paper's normalized formulation assigns consistency reward c times (N minus the number of distinct answers) divided by N. Fewer distinct options therefore produce a larger reward.

The full feedback contract mixes three signals: answer accuracy from the known option, output-format compliance, and answer consistency after truncation and perturbation. The consistency term is differentiable through the policy-gradient objective as a scalar trajectory reward, but it remains a heuristic: a shared wrong continuation can be consistent, and several valid rationales can be diverse. SCS is inserted into RLOO, GRPO, REINFORCE++-baseline, and REINFORCE++ rather than defining a new optimizer.

Compared with plain outcome-reward RL, SCS inspects counterfactual continuations before rewarding the initial trajectory. Compared with a process reward model, it does not learn dense step labels from a separate model. Its track relevance comes from the resampling record itself: truncation, perturbation, option diversity, and component rewards must be retained to attribute any gain to data/feedback construction rather than only to the optimizer.
