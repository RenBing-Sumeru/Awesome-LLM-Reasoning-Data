Across the six reported evaluation benchmarks, adding SCS improves the corresponding RL baselines in Table 2. The largest reported average change is RLOO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, from 57.8 to 65.5, or 7.7 percentage points; smaller gains are reported for GRPO, the REINFORCE++ variants, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and InternVL3-8B. Component ablations attribute gains to both truncation-resampling and visual perturbation. Three-run intervals are reported, although the table contains an apparent mean/row inconsistency for one REINFORCE++-baseline entry that should be checked against code or logs.

The paper also manually judges 100 correctly answered cases per benchmark and asks o3-mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash to assess the same traces against public solutions. It reports fewer unfaithful rationales after SCS, with aggregate reductions around 13.6-15.2% depending on judge. This is direct evidence about a sampled evaluation set, but it is not a released dense process annotation standard and is conditioned on answers already being correct.

The official NeurIPS page, paper, code repository, and HF dataset support the existence of the method and artifacts. The HF Qwen file has 16,180 prompt rows, but no verified per-rollout reward logs were found. Accuracy gains and judged examples evaluate training behavior; they do not prove that released prompt data, image assets, or latent rollout records are complete, decontaminated, or uniformly correct.
