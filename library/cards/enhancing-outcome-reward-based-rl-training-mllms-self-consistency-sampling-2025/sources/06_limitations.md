SCS assumes that a correct reasoning trajectory should lead consistently to one correct option and models the reasoning tree as having a unique correct trajectory. Real questions can admit multiple valid rationales, and consistent continuations can share the same misconception. The reward may therefore penalize legitimate diversity or reinforce correlated errors. Visual Gaussian perturbation can also change task-relevant evidence rather than only test robustness.

The demonstrated scope is image-based multiple-choice reasoning with three MLLM families; the paper itself notes that generality to LLMs and more MLLMs is not established. SCS adds resampling compute: the appendix reports 17.2 hours versus 12.5 hours in one comparison, about 38% more. Exact source manifests, splits, decontamination, image licenses, perturbation seeds, rollout/reward logs, checkpoint-selection effects, and hardware/run-scope reconciliation remain incomplete. The released prompt files do not permit independent replay of every reward assignment.
