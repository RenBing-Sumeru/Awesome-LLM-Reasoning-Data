The paper evaluates first-turn accuracy, second-turn accuracy, the net change between turns, wrong-to-right transitions, and right-to-wrong regressions. On MATH, the reported SCoRe model reaches 60.0% on the first attempt and 64.4% on the second, a 4.4-point within-model delta. On HumanEval, it reports 52.4% and 64.6%, a 12.2-point delta. These are results for the reported Gemini models and task splits, not estimates of the intrinsic quality of an exported dataset.

The ablation table supports the role of both stages in the tested MATH setup: removing Stage I reduces the reported second-minus-first delta from 4.4 to 2.2 points, and removing reward shaping reduces it to 2.6 points. The paper's analysis of offline SFT also shows that correction performance can improve on fixed base-model first attempts while degrading on first attempts sampled from the learner, which supports the collection-policy mismatch diagnosis.

For inference scaling, the paper compares equal total sample budgets and reports that K parallel samples followed by one correction each can outperform 2K independent parallel samples in its setting. This supports sequential use of the learned policy under the evaluated budget; it does not establish a universal compute advantage, verifier reliability, or trace-data quality.
