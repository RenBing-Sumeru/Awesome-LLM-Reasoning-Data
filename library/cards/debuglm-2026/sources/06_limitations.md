DebugLM requires control of training and prior knowledge of the source mixture; it cannot be retrofitted to an already frozen service. Dataset-level labels are coarse, while overlapping or derivative datasets may not admit one unambiguous true source. Emitting one tag per sequence also makes multi-source tracing depend on repeated sampling, temperature, and a permissive aggregate success rule.

The reported record is not a full debugging episode. It preserves no action-observation trajectory, executor output, exception or test trace, patch candidate, retry history, or runtime terminal state. A refusal is a trained target, not evidence that an underlying defect was repaired. The paper likewise releases no structured failure record linking an observed bad output to its tag, selected quarantine, post-intervention response, and replay result.

Replayability is weak. Official code, checkpoints, transformed records, source revision hashes, stage manifests, full split counts, filtering rules, loss weight, optimizer configuration, batch sizes, epochs, and the Stage 2 GRPO reward implementation were not confirmed. Decontamination and compatibility among upstream dataset licenses are also unknown.

Security and validity remain open. A later fine-tuner could erase or spoof tag behavior; a developer trigger creates an access-control surface; and the model's self-report is not authenticated against external lineage logs. Scaling to many overlapping tags may introduce capacity or interference effects. Benchmark scores, including strong tracing results, do not resolve these audit risks or establish record-level causality.
