On the five-source held-out evaluation, DebugLM reports macro Tracing Success Rate of 95.89% for Llama-3-8B and 95.68% for Qwen-3-8B. The dedicated 1B post-hoc classifiers reach 97.95% and 98.57%, respectively, so the result supports a compact in-model interface but does not show that self-reporting is universally more accurate than an external classifier. The intervention matrices show near-perfect targeted refusal and near-zero off-target refusal in the studied source domains.

The paper probes several boundaries: format conversion to two-choice questions, 199-author and 14-subcategory lineage, semantic perturbation on 576 held-out TOFU instances, and two-source QuoteSum synthesis. Under the ten-sample success rule, multi-source tracing reaches 99.60% for the Llama-based model and 99.76% for the Qwen-based model. Because this criterion aggregates stochastic outputs, it is not equivalent to recovering all sources in one deterministic response.

Utility evidence is mixed. Table 5 reports the Llama-based model falling from 58.57% to 52.11% on MMLU and from 79.18% to 73.55% on ARC-Challenge; the Qwen-based drops are smaller. Free-form utility additionally depends on one model judge, with no reported human calibration or inter-judge analysis.

These benchmark results evaluate trained behavior, not the quality, coverage, licensing, or replayability of the constructed records. No official code, transformed dataset, checkpoint, stage manifest, or failure log was confirmed, so independent reproduction of the reported construction pipeline remains unsupported by released artifacts.
