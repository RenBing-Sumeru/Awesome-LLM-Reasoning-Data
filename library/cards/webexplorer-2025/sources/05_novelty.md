The prior-work baseline described by the paper has two families. Graph-based systems explicitly expand website/entity nodes and edges, which requires hand-designed expansion and node-selection heuristics. Earlier evolution systems usually inject information and make questions longer, which can produce unnatural formulations. WebExplorer does not originate difficult web QA, web-agent trajectories, ReAct, or reinforcement learning.

The first concrete change is model-based exploration as a construction substrate: an LLM navigates from a Wikipedia seed and internally accumulates an information space rather than materializing a rule-built graph. The second is long-to-short evolution: five prompted rounds remove salient clues, obscure dates/locations/names, or replace explicit entities with alternative terms while retaining the answer. Table 1 provides a model-relative difficulty signal for this change, not a record-level validity certificate (Paper §§2.2–2.4).

The third contribution is the linkage from task construction to two training objects. An undisclosed commercial model supplies correct ReAct trajectories for approximately 13K-example SFT, while approximately 12K QA items support online GRPO with format and DeepSeek-V3 correctness rewards. The progressive 64K/50→96K/75→128K/100 budget makes trajectory horizon part of the optimization recipe rather than merely an evaluation setting (Paper §§3–4).

What is not new includes the search/browse action space, ReAct serialization, rejection sampling, Qwen3-8B, SFT, GRPO, format rewards, and LLM-as-judge. The contribution is an engineering composition and data-construction direction, not evidence that every evolved question or longer trajectory is intrinsically better. Before reuse, inspect source-page lineage, answer uniqueness, rejected examples, judge calibration, BrowseComp overlap, the exact teacher and base snapshots, and the missing training implementation. The 100-row QA release cannot validate the paper-scale recipe by itself.
