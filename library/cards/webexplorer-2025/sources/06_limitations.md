The paper has no dedicated limitations section. The following boundaries combine explicit nondisclosure with curator inference grounded in the data and feedback contracts.

**Construction and lineage.** The exploration/evolution LLM and commercial SFT teacher are unnamed; their snapshots, prompts beyond the two appendix templates, temperature, candidate counts, retries, and acceptance yields are unknown. The public rows omit seed entity, source URLs, page snapshots, exploration/evolution traces, SFT trajectory, split, and training assignment. The claim that answers remain unique and verifiable is encoded in prompts, but no independent checker or human audit is disclosed.

**Selection and retention.** SFT exclusively uses correct rejection-sampled trajectories. Failed attempts, incorrect final answers, timeouts, tool errors, and rejected evolutions are not released, so false-negative behavior and the keep/drop boundary cannot be audited. Online GRPO rollouts and per-rollout rewards are also absent. Only 100 QA rows are public versus approximately 40K reported QA, approximately 13K SFT trajectories, and approximately 12K RL items.

**Reward risk.** DeepSeek-V3 judges free-form final answers against references. Its exact version, prompt, score scale, normalization, calibration, disagreement handling, and attack robustness are unknown. It may reward semantically wrong but reference-like answers or reject equivalent expressions. The 0.2 format term can favor protocol-conforming output without establishing correct reasoning, grounded citations, or faithful intermediate steps.

**Environment and replay.** Search depends on live Google/Serper results; browse depends on Jina, changing webpages, and Gemini 2.5 Flash. No immutable observation cache, API/model snapshot, reset contract, or deterministic replay fixture is released. A trajectory that succeeded during collection may therefore yield different observations later.

**Evaluation and contamination.** Three BrowseComp-en exemplars are inserted into synthesis prompts, the evolution style is inspired by BrowseComp, and BrowseComp-en/zh are evaluation surfaces. No question, answer, source-page, or near-duplicate overlap audit is reported. Figure 5 is correlational: longer trajectories and higher scores rise together, but the paper does not isolate data, reward, optimization, sampling, or budget effects.

**Rights and versioning.** The HF dataset/model cards declare Apache-2.0 and OpenReview uses CC BY 4.0, but the pinned GitHub tree has no LICENSE file despite its badge, and rights for browsed third-party content are not documented. The 15-author arXiv/GitHub/HF citation conflicts with the 14-author OpenReview metadata, whose revised PDF also changes “web agents” to “deep research agents.” These issues do not invalidate the paper, but they require version-aware citation and reuse.
