The most defensible immediate use is as a construction and audit reference, not as a paper-scale training release. A data builder can adapt the disclosed two-stage recipe—Wikipedia seed exploration followed by five rounds of clue removal/obfuscation—provided every generated QA retains source URLs, page snapshots, answer verification, generator version, prompt revision, and rejected candidates. Difficulty should be measured separately from factuality and uniqueness.

For agent post-training, the paper supplies a useful experimental decomposition: compare QA-only online RL with correct-trajectory SFT, then test the incremental effect of GRPO and progressive horizon. A faithful baseline should preserve the approximately 13K SFT versus approximately 12K RL distinction, eight rollouts per GRPO group, `0.2 * R_format + R_correct`, and the 64K/50→96K/75→128K/100 schedule. It should also ablate format and correctness rewards, a programmatic or citation-grounded verifier, failed-trajectory retention, and fixed versus live observations.

For verifier design, this work is a concrete warning against calling final-answer LLM judgment an environmental success predicate. Auditors can stratify DeepSeek-V3 decisions by exact match, equivalent paraphrase, unsupported but correct-looking answer, conflicting sources, and malformed tool use; compare judge versions; and report false-positive/false-negative rates before training. BrowseComp exemplars and evaluation variants should receive record-level overlap checks.

The released 100 `id/query/answer` rows are suitable for schema inspection, qualitative task analysis, and small controlled tests under the HF access and license terms. The released model and inference/evaluation code provide a starting point for evaluation if external APIs and model revisions are pinned. They are not sufficient for reproducing data generation, SFT, GRPO, or trajectory statistics.

Reuse classification: **reading/audit reference now; limited QA inspection only; full training reuse blocked pending verification**. Promotion to a training-ready artifact would require the complete QA/SFT/RL manifests, explicit rights, teacher/judge metadata, synthesis and training code, splits and decontamination, rejected and failed examples, reward logs, and deterministic replay fixtures.
