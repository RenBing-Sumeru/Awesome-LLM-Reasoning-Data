The one-sentence contribution is: WebExplorer creates harder web-search QA by letting an LLM explore from a seed and then evolving the question from long to short, and it connects those QA pairs to correct-trajectory SFT followed by online GRPO for an 8B web agent.

In the construction stage, an undisclosed LLM receives a Wikipedia seed and three BrowseComp-en exemplars, searches and browses to assemble an internal information space, and writes an initial QA pair. Five evolution rounds may remove redundant clues, replace dates, locations, or names with vague descriptions, and search for alternative terminology while preserving the exact answer. The resulting object is an answer-bearing task, not yet a trajectory (Paper §§2.2–2.4; Appendix B).

Trajectory supervision then has two contracts. SFT attaches token-level imitation to an entire correct ReAct episode: thoughts, structured search/browse calls, tool responses, and the final answer. RL attaches a scalar reward to an online episode: `R = 0.2 * R_format + R_correct`, where the first component checks protocol structure and DeepSeek-V3 judges the final response against the reference answer. The judge sees the final response and ground truth, but the paper does not establish that it checks every cited webpage, every intermediate inference, evidence sufficiency, or trajectory faithfulness. There is no executable environmental terminal predicate.

The closest paper-described baselines use explicit graph expansion or short-to-long evolution that injects information. WebExplorer's specific change is to let the model construct the information space implicitly and then make questions harder by deleting or obfuscating clues. ReAct, search and browse tools, SFT, GRPO, rejection sampling, and LLM-as-judge are reused components rather than new inventions. Compared with WebDancer, WebSailor, WebShaper, and Explorer, the direction signal is the coupling of query difficulty construction with long-horizon agent post-training, not the mere presence of web trajectories.

The release boundary changes the interpretation: 100 QA rows and model/inference artifacts can support task inspection and limited evaluation, but they do not expose the paper's approximately 13K SFT trajectories, approximately 12K RL set, per-rollout rewards, or construction history. Consequently, the public object cannot directly reproduce either supervision contract.
