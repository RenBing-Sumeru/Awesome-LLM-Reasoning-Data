- **Terminal-verification boundary:** the checker validates the final output, not program-to-CoT faithfulness or the truth of intermediate reasoning. Appendix G contains accepted, correct-answer programs with flawed or incomplete logic; the manual pass rates are 96/100 for two-stage outputs and 90/100 for GRPO outputs.
- **Predicate and reward boundary:** Stage 1 syntax validation only establishes that definitions compile. The paper's 0.5 partial reward intentionally favors predicate alignment even when the answer is wrong, while the pinned implementation tests overlap with any suggested predicate rather than use of all suggested predicates.
- **Checker risk:** the acceptance path depends on Prolog-term conversion, LaTeX parsing, normalization, numerical tolerance, prefix fallbacks, and hand-written special cases. The paper reports no calibrated false-accept or false-reject rate across answer types.
- **Release mismatch:** the paper reports 6,100 initial programs and a 97.4% union; pinned Hub revision `0aa6c9783d698ea3fbe5e71a76cfb01d2cd0d5af` has one 6,093-row train split, while its prose says the dataset is disabled and lists 7,923 training examples. No official row manifest maps these counts.
- **Lineage loss:** public rows omit MATH IDs, source CoTs, buffer version, generator, attempt number, execution output, verifier verdict, reward components, failed candidates, and recovery model. The released file cannot reconstruct the search or distinguish initial from recovered examples.
- **Reproducibility and rights:** public scripts require user-supplied configuration and the standalone pipeline checker differs from the paper method. The dataset is tagged MIT and the paper calls MATH MIT, but the pinned code tree has no license file. Exact paper-run temperatures, seeds, and total generation budget remain unknown.
- **Evaluation scope:** MATH train supplies the corpus and MATH-500 supplies the main ablation. No broader duplicate, overlap, or pretraining-contamination audit is reported, and benchmark improvement is not proof of record-level quality.
