The construction evidence is internally consistent at the paper level: 6,100/7,500 items pass the initial pipeline; the four recovery models add 9.7, 14.9, 15.2, and 15.7 points individually; their union adds 16.1 points to reach 97.4%. At 2,000 GRPO steps on the DeepSeek-V3 failure set, predicate-aware reward improves coverage by 9.7 points versus 7.8 for binary reward. These figures describe the reported generation runs, not the size or quality of the current release.

The ablation establishes that the tested pipeline uses both staged predicates and answer-type prompts: its MATH-500 verified accuracy is 68.2%, versus 41.6% without staged predicate generation and 26.3% without the answer-type list. A same-configuration SFT comparison reports 36.8% after training on Prolog-MATH and 33.2% after training on the original MATH CoTs. Neither benchmark result verifies every released program or attributes all gains to data quality.

The strongest negative evidence is the manual audit. Of 100 two-stage programs, 96 are judged mathematically correct or reasonable; of 100 Qwen2.5-3B GRPO programs, 90 have correct logic. Appendix G shows a two-stage solution that makes a false digit assumption but reaches the right answer, and a GRPO program that considers only the factor 167 of 2004 while coincidentally returning the correct exponent 12. Terminal equivalence therefore has a measurable semantic gap.

Official artifacts expose pipeline, SFT, GRPO, reward, and verifier code, plus a live 6,093-row Hugging Face split. They also reveal reproducibility drift: the standalone generation script uses substring output matching rather than the paper's adapted symbolic checker, and several scripts still require user-supplied paths, endpoints, or checkpoints.
