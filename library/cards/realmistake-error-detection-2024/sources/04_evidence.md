The central result is low evaluator recall on genuine errors: models frequently label expert-confirmed flawed responses as correct. Outcomes change substantially with prompts, and seemingly specific explanations do not reliably correspond to correct verdicts. Human detection is clearly stronger, while self-consistency and majority voting provide no stable remedy. Because errors arise naturally from strong model outputs rather than synthetic perturbations, the evidence directly challenges self-correction assumptions, although 900 examples cannot represent all open-ended tasks.
