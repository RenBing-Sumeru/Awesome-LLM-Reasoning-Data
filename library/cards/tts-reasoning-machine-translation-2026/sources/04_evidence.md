Across general-purpose Qwen-3, Cogito, and Grok-3-Mini settings, the paper finds limited and dataset-dependent direct-translation gains from larger reasoning budgets. Models often stop near their own reasoning ceiling even when allocated far more tokens, and one forced “wait” beyond the natural stop consistently degrades reported quality. These observations make a negative result valuable: additional test-time compute is not automatically productive outside tasks with easily verifiable answers.

The paper reports a different pattern for translation-specialized DRT models on their in-domain task, where quality rises with budget until natural thinking length saturates. It also finds post-editing reliably beneficial despite costing a second stage. The evidence supports a conditional recommendation, not a universal scaling law; performance depends on domain training, the translation workflow, and the evaluation metric used.
