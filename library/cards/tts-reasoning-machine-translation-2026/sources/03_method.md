The authors evaluate twelve reasoning models over eight translation benchmarks spanning literary, biomedical, cultural, commonsense, terminology, and retrieval-augmented settings. They regulate a model’s reasoning inside its thinking span with a token budget, record actual reasoning length, and optionally insert one “wait” token when the model would stop. Translation quality is measured with reference-based and reference-free COMET variants plus an LLM judge and literary-specific metrics.

They compare direct translation with post-editing, where a second stage receives the source and the model’s own draft; one variant also includes a quality score for the draft. The experiments separate general-purpose models from DRT models fine-tuned for translation. This exposes a concrete budget decision: spend inference on a first-pass chain of thought, force an additional continuation, or use another pass to inspect and revise a completed translation.
