Reasoning models often improve on mathematics and code when given extra inference-time computation, but translation has different failure modes. It requires linguistic fidelity, terminology, cultural context, and long-range coherence; a longer chain of thought can introduce noise rather than repair a draft. Existing claims about test-time scaling therefore do not establish that simply assigning more thinking tokens yields better translation.

The paper asks when additional reasoning budget helps translation, when it merely plateaus, and when it makes quality worse. It contrasts direct one-pass translation, forced extrapolation beyond a model’s natural stopping point, and two-stage post-editing. The central decision is not whether a model can think longer, but which workflow should consume that extra computation.
