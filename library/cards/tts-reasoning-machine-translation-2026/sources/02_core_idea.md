The study treats test-time scaling as task- and workflow-dependent rather than a universal quality knob. For general-purpose reasoning models, direct translation receives only small, inconsistent gains before performance and actual thinking length plateau. Forcing another thought with a “wait” intervention is not neutral: it overrides the model’s own stopping decision and consistently harms translation quality in the reported experiments.

In contrast, domain-specific fine-tuning aligns a model’s reasoning behavior with a translation task, allowing quality to rise up to a self-determined reasoning depth. A post-editing workflow also turns extra computation into a useful second pass: the model reviews an existing translation rather than trying to lengthen the first one indefinitely. The paper thus locates value in targeted self-correction, not unrestricted thought extension.
