The contribution is a boundary study of test-time scaling, not a claim that longer thought universally improves generation. By testing direct translation, forced extrapolation, fine-tuned models, and post-editing in one protocol, the work makes workflow choice part of the inference-time compute decision. It identifies that an apparently simple intervention—forcing a model past its stopping point—can be systematically counterproductive.

It also distinguishes two ways of using extra budget that are often conflated: lengthening the same reasoning episode and initiating a revision episode over a concrete draft. The latter gives the model an object to criticize and repair, whereas the former can amplify unresolved uncertainty. This distinction is useful for designing test-time systems in non-verifiable domains where a final answer cannot be selected by an exact checker.
