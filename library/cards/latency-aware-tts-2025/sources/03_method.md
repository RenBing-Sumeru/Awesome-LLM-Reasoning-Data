The authors evaluate sequential and parallel scaling under both token and wall-clock budgets on MATH-500, AIME24, AIME25, and GPQA-Diamond, across models from 3B to 32B parameters. Parallel scaling uses multiple reasoning branches and majority voting. Sequence-wise acceleration uses speculative decoding, whose draft length controls how many successive tokens can be proposed and verified concurrently.

They search over branch count and speculative draft length with a greedy procedure. At each step the procedure evaluates whether expanding branches or increasing draft length gives a larger accuracy benefit under the same time constraint, then continues only while the result improves. The record linking the method to deployment is explicit: branch configuration, draft configuration, latency, throughput, tokens, and benchmark correctness are jointly observed rather than reported in separate experiments.
