Test-time scaling is usually compared by accuracy gained per generated token. That view is incomplete for an interactive system: sequential reasoning can use fewer tokens but decode them slowly, while multiple branches can exploit otherwise idle hardware and reach the same answer faster. A compute-optimal strategy is therefore not necessarily latency-optimal when a user or service has a wall-clock deadline.

The paper asks how to allocate inference computation when time, rather than tokens alone, is the binding budget. It separates two sources of concurrency: generating several candidate branches in parallel and accelerating successive tokens through speculative decoding. The practical target is an answer policy that chooses their balance for maximum accuracy before the latency deadline expires.
