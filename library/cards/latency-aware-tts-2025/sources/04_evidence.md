The paper reports that a strategy with superior accuracy per token can be slower under small-batch serving. Its illustrative s1.1-32B experiment reaches similar accuracy with sequential scaling using nine times fewer tokens, yet parallel scaling reaches it with 1.6 times lower latency. This demonstrates why token count alone can misstate a real-time test-time scaling trade-off.

Across reported settings, the joint allocation can improve accuracy by up to 7.3 percent over single-parallelism baselines under a one-minute constraint; the paper reports a 32B model reaching 82.3 percent on MATH-500 within one minute and a 3B model reaching 72.4 percent within ten seconds. These are hardware- and configuration-sensitive results, but they provide direct evidence that concurrency configuration is part of inference-time scaling, not merely an implementation detail.
