The main novelty is to replace the usual token-only budget with a serving-time budget. This exposes a distinction hidden by compute-optimal comparisons: a long sequential trajectory may be economical in tokens but underuse a memory-bound accelerator, while several shorter branches can use concurrency to increase accuracy before the same deadline. The relevant frontier is therefore accuracy versus latency, not accuracy versus length alone.

The work also unifies branch generation and speculative decoding as two control variables in one allocation problem. Parallel branches move the accuracy curve upward by exploring alternatives; speculative decoding moves it left by reducing the time per generated sequence. Treating them jointly gives a systems-level selection policy for test-time reasoning rather than proposing either as an isolated acceleration trick.
