For **Data Construction & Open Release Recipes**, SeRL is most useful as an online self-play blueprint, a self-consensus reward audit target, and a release-completeness case.

1. **Reproduce the data object before training.** Store each generated question with its eight-shot context, seed/generated context labels, generation settings, all filters, 16 complete responses, extracted answers, equivalence matrix, selected majority, binary rewards, step, policy hash, and iteration. A prompt-only file is insufficient.
2. **Separate agreement from truth.** Evaluate the majority cluster against held-out ground truth where available, stratify by majority ratio, and report wrong-consensus rates. Compare majority agreement with independent verifiers or expert review instead of treating reward 1 as correctness.
3. **Audit tie and exception behavior.** Measure Math-Verify extraction and grading exceptions, retain their inputs, and compare first-maximum, shortest-answer, deterministic canonicalization, and ambiguity-aware tie policies.
4. **Reconcile the executable recipe.** Publish one immutable config per backbone that resolves the 2-seed/6-generated versus 6-seed/2-generated context, shortest-answer tie-break, micro-batches, initial KL, 7,500 instructions, dependencies, seed IDs, RNG state, and checkpoint selection.
5. **Deduplicate and version generated questions.** Explain the 2,802 duplicate prompts in the 7,532-row snapshot, publish unique-count and near-duplicate statistics for every iteration, and bind every file to its generating checkpoint and filter state.
6. **Add a release ledger.** Publish accepted and rejected generations, failures, expired groups, responses, rewards, logs, checkpoints, manifests, dataset card, component licenses, and semantic contamination results.

The reusable portion is the **recipe and audit scaffold**, not a paper-exact trajectory corpus. The code can support controlled reimplementation of online question generation, Math-Verify clustering, and Reinforce++ updates. The bundled 500-row seed and static prompt files can test parsers and deduplication. They should not be presented as the complete training data used for reported results.

The main directly evidenced training use is RLVR-style response optimization with answer-level/full-response scalar rewards. The dataset is not process supervision: chain-of-thought is present in responses, but individual reasoning steps have no correctness labels. It is also not ground-truth-verifiable RL for generated questions. Using the release for reward-model training, preference learning, or clinical deployment would require additional labeling and review.

A well-audited derivative could make SeRL especially valuable to this track by preserving the evolving curriculum and negative evidence. Wrong consensuses, verifier exceptions, lexical rejects, expired groups, and duplicate clusters are not incidental debris; they are the records needed to understand why a self-play data loop succeeds or fails.
