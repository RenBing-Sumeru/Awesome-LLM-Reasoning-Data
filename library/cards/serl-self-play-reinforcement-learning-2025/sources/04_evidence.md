The main reported math results show improvement under the paper’s setup. For LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct, MATH-500 Pass@1 rises from 47.6 initially to 48.6, 50.4, and 52.6 across three SeRL iterations. For Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, it moves from 74.2 initially to 74.2, 74.8, and 75.8. SeRL also reports gains on several math/STEM benchmarks, while MMLU-Pro changes are modest and domain-dependent. These results demonstrate a working training recipe on the tested models; they do not validate every generated question or reward.

The paper’s reward-hacking ablation is more important for interpreting the feedback contract. When the difficulty filter is removed, mean reward increases while MATH-500 accuracy drops sharply after roughly 100 steps. The case study shows responses with plausible or correct-looking intermediate reasoning that consistently end in final answer 0. Because the outputs agree, the majority mechanism can assign full reward to a wrong answer. The `[0.2, 0.8]` window mitigates the unanimous case, but cannot detect a wrong 10-of-16 consensus.

The authors’ capability analysis reports that majority-at-16 does not consistently increase across iterations. A cautious interpretation is that SeRL can distill some existing multi-sample agreement into stronger Pass@1 rather than reliably raising the underlying majority ceiling. This is useful behavior, but it is narrower than proof of new reasoning capability or correct self-verification.

Appendix F reports 7,500 generated instructions in each of `D_gen^1`, `D_gen^2`, and `D_gen^3`. Qwen-3-32B rates their mean quality around 3.5–3.6 out of 5. That learned judge is a post-hoc analysis, not the training verifier, and no item-level judge outputs are released.

The pinned repository provides a different kind of evidence: auditable static files. The configured 500-row seed has 500 unique normalized prompts. `llama_gen7500_iter4.jsonl` contains **7,532 rows but only 4,730 unique normalized prompts**, leaving **2,802 exact duplicate prompts**—about 37.2% of rows. Its iteration-4 filename is not bound to the three main reported iterations. A mixed snapshot has 15,004 rows and 15,003 unique prompts, combines generated blank-label rows with MATH ground-truth rows, and lacks an explicit provenance field. The bundled MATH train snapshot has 7,500 rows and 7,499 unique prompts.

A curator exact-normalized-string comparison found no overlap between the configured seed or inspected bundled training/generated files and bundled MATH-500/MATH-Hard evaluation files. This supports only an exact-string statement about the inspected snapshots. No semantic-overlap procedure or base-model pretraining audit is reported, so decontamination remains `unknown`.

The NeurIPS checklist states that one fixed random seed was used because RL is expensive, and the paper reports no error bars or significance tests. Greedy evaluation reduces decoding variance at evaluation time but does not quantify stochastic training variance or checkpoint-selection sensitivity.
