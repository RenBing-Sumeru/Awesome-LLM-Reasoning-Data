Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is often evaluated on established mathematical benchmarks. When those benchmarks or closely related solutions may have appeared in pretraining, a post-training gain can mix several effects: learning from the reward, retrieving memorized content, adapting to a response template, or exploiting regularities in the evaluator. The paper asks whether reported gains still appear on a task constructed after the evaluated model releases.

RandomCalculation is the paper's lower-contamination control. Its released data object is a prompt containing a procedurally generated LaTeX arithmetic expression paired with a SymPy-derived numeric answer. It does not contain a gold reasoning trace. The public generator builds 20 subsets indexed by 1-20 recursive binary operations, with 1,000 examples per subset; the RLVR experiments use the 5- and 10-operation subsets.

This design supports a narrower question than "is the model reasoning?" It tests whether a reward signal produces reliable improvement on newly generated answer-verifiable arithmetic under the reported setup. Generation after model release lowers the risk of verbatim item leakage, but does not prove the absence of structurally equivalent expressions, familiar algorithms, or other pretraining influence.
