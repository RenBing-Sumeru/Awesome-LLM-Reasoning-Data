For each prompt, TrajFusion samples 16 candidate chains of thought and assigns a binary label from the extracted final answer. It estimates problem-level error frequency from the fraction of rejected samples and error diversity from the number of distinct rejected final answers. Incorrect trajectories are grouped by that final answer, frequent groups are prioritized, and the shortest trajectory in a selected group represents that failure outcome.

The adaptive selection count is reported as `k(x) = min(k_max, floor(alpha * r(x) * u(x)))`, where `r(x)` is error frequency and `u(x)` is distinct-wrong-answer diversity. The count grows when errors are both frequent and diverse, while `k_max` caps target length; the ablation reports the strongest tested result at `k_max = 4`. The operational value of `alpha` is not disclosed.

The feedback contract is deliberately narrow. Pattern matching extracts a boxed or inline answer, and the OpenCompass math verifier applies symbolic evaluation/exact-match criteria against the reference. That terminal decision partitions candidates into accepted and rejected sets and determines whether a prompt survives at all: a prompt with no correct candidate among the 16 is excluded. The verifier does not locate the first faulty step, score intermediate reasoning, or establish that a selected wrong trajectory contains a pedagogically useful error.

The text placed between attempts is not generated diagnosis. A transition is randomly drawn from a fixed, curator-written repository of error-recognition, success-confirmation, and optional certainty phrases. Thus the supervision teaches a textual pattern of reconsideration around verified outcomes; it does not provide grounded natural-language feedback explaining why an earlier solution failed.
