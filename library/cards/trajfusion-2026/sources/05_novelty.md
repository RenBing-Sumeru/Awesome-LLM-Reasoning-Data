TrajFusion's specific contribution is a drop-in replacement for the data-retention step of RFT: instead of retaining only one accepted trajectory, it reuses sampled rejected trajectories, selects a bounded set by problem-level error frequency and wrong-answer diversity, and serializes them with fixed reflection markers before a correct solution. The resulting record can be trained with the same next-token objective and requires neither a preference pair nor an auxiliary critic.

The ingredients are not individually new. RFT already uses repeated sampling and terminal verification; self-correction data already juxtapose mistakes and revisions; preference and reinforcement methods already use negative outcomes; other math-data work already filters or diversifies generated solutions. The novelty claimed here is the combination of adaptive failure selection and single-sequence fusion inside an otherwise standard RFT/SFT pipeline, including a demonstrated long-context variant.

The method should not be described as process supervision. Its verifier observes the final answer only, and its reflection phrases are generic templates rather than step-grounded critiques. Nor is it an online reinforcement-learning environment: construction is offline, the selected sequence is static, and training uses ordinary negative log likelihood.

As an atlas entry, its reuse status is limited. The official ACL paper and arXiv version document the recipe and templates, but this review did not confirm an official code repository, constructed 15K/100K dataset, checkpoint, project page, or Hugging Face release. Reimplementation therefore requires reconstructing undisclosed choices such as `alpha`, seeds, correct-trace selection, exact source snapshots, and split controls.
