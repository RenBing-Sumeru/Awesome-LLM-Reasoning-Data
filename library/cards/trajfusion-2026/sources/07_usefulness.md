Within `data_construction_open_release_recipes`, TrajFusion is most useful as a recipe for converting a rejection-sampling log into static SFT records while retaining some failed attempts. It gives curators concrete knobs to study: candidate count, malformed-output filters, terminal verifier, error-frequency statistic, wrong-answer grouping, diversity rule, per-group representative, maximum fused errors, reflection vocabulary, and correct-only fallback.

A reproducible implementation should preserve a row-level ledger rather than only the final fused text. At minimum, record an immutable prompt/source identifier and source revision; all 16 raw candidates; extraction output; verifier version and result; malformed-output reason; `r(x)` and `u(x)`; wrong-answer group identifier and frequency; selected/unselected flag; selection rank and trajectory length; sampled reflection-template identifier; chosen correct-candidate identifier; fusion order; random seed; and final record checksum. This separates what the generator wrote, what the verifier decided, and what the selector retained.

The recipe is a useful controlled baseline for comparing correct-only RFT against error-aware SFT under equal prompts, correct traces, teacher sampling budget, and base model. Audits should add manual step-level review to a stratified sample, including verifier-accepted trajectories, selected wrong trajectories, extraction failures, and high-frequency wrong-answer groups. Report false accepts, false rejects, the first-error location, whether the reflection matches that error, and performance by source and difficulty.

For open release, publish the final fused 15K/100K records together with the complete raw candidate pool or an auditable manifest of it, configuration files, source snapshot hashes, split/decontamination report, verifier and extraction versions, template repository, selection code, seeds, model/checkpoint identifiers, checksums, and explicit data/code licenses. Until those artifacts exist, this entry is safe for methodological comparison and reimplementation planning; plug-and-play training reuse is blocked.

The pattern may extend to other domains only when a trustworthy terminal predicate exists and a failed trajectory is safe and meaningful to expose. In open-ended or tool-using settings, replace answer-only verification with domain-appropriate execution tests or expert review, and do not assume that a generic reflection marker turns an arbitrary failure into sound supervision.
