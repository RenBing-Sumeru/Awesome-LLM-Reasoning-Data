The cleanest causal comparison is against vanilla RFT built from the same sampled queries and the same correct trajectories, differing in whether selected wrong paths are included. Across six benchmarks, TrajFusion raises the average Pass@1 for LLaMA3-8B from 27.9 to 34.0 at 15K and from 38.8 to 41.3 at 100K. For DeepSeekMath-7B, the corresponding averages rise from 45.8 to 48.8 and from 50.2 to 52.3. This controlled contrast supports utility of the fused supervision under the reported setup; it does not certify that every retained error is valid or that the underlying dataset is clean.

The fusion-count ablation on LLaMA3-8B shows diminishing returns. Increasing the cap through the reported settings raises the average number of fused errors and improves MATH from 39.6 at 0.62 errors per sample to 44.2 at 1.49 errors per sample, while the next setting reaches 44.1 at 1.74 errors. GSM8K similarly peaks at 74.8 before falling to 74.0. The authors therefore use `k_max = 4` as the strongest tested balance rather than treating more errors as automatically better.

A diversity-controlled ablation holds the mean number of fused errors near 1.13. Selecting errors from different wrong-answer outcomes reaches 74.0 on GSM8K and 43.2 on MATH, versus 72.3 and 40.7 when selecting from the most frequent wrong-answer bucket. The high-diversity variant also generates shorter outputs (120 versus 291 tokens on GSM8K; 1,056 versus 1,603 on MATH). This evidence supports outcome diversity as a useful selection heuristic, although distinct final answers are only a proxy for distinct reasoning failures.

The long-form study reports gains over matched RFT at both context lengths: at 4K, Pass@1 moves from 16.7 to 23.3 on AIME24, 10.0 to 16.7 on AIME25, and 79.4 to 82.6 on MATH-500; at 32K, it moves from 40.0 to 43.3, 26.7 to 30.0, and 88.4 to 90.4. AIME uses only 30 questions and sampled metrics are also reported, so these numbers should be read as model-utility evidence, not as a direct audit of trajectory correctness, contamination, or release quality.

The paper additionally observes an early training advantage and changing output length/reflection frequency, but it does not report human error annotation, step-level verifier precision, extraction false-positive/false-negative rates, seed variance, source-wise retention, or an audit of whether fused reflections actually correspond to the preceding mistake.
