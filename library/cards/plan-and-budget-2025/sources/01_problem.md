Plan-and-Budget studies a test-time-compute allocation problem, not a new post-training dataset: fixed token limits can reduce long reasoning but can also truncate hard problems. The ICLR 2026 paper models a query as sub-questions of unequal uncertainty, then evaluates whether planning plus local allocation changes task score and billed completion tokens (paper §§3–5).

For this atlas category, one potential episode is concrete: an upstream task item, an LLM-produced decomposition, difficulty/credit annotations, a scheduled budget, a target-model prediction, token-use fields, and a task score. The author repository distributes pre-decomposed evaluation inputs and code that can generate such results, but it does not supply an author-produced prompt-to-output result manifest. This is therefore an inference/evaluation recipe with partial trace availability, not evidence of a ready-to-train trace corpus.

The paper covers MATH-500, sampled NaturalInstructions, and TravelPlanner; it does not establish that these public benchmark inputs are safe training data or that its gains generalize outside those task/evaluator combinations. L4 evidence here is the final arXiv/OpenReview paper and linked author code, including preprocessing, inference, and evaluation files.
