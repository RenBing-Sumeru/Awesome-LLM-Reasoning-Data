Prior fixed-budget work makes one global length decision, while planning-only baselines expose a decomposition without allocating local shares. Plan-and-Budget combines those components: it turns model-generated sub-question credits into a schedule-conditioned allocation and evaluates the resulting episode with task score and total completion-token cost.

The practical novelty is therefore an allocation interface—`steps`, `credits`, level budget, schedule, and token accounting—not a new verifier, a process-supervision dataset, or an RL objective. The repository makes the interface inspectable enough to replay the algorithm, including its floor-and-remainder allocation rule. What remains non-novel or inherited is the upstream benchmark data, task metrics, LLM prompting, and external-model service.

For reasoning-data research, this is a direction signal because it forces attribution questions: changes in E3 can arise from planning, credit estimates, scheduling, model/provider behavior, task scorer, or total compute. Reuse should first check the distributed decomposition provenance, split/version alignment, and whether a proposed deployment needs hard rather than prompt-level budget enforcement.
