The primary modeling limitation is the gap between likelihood and correctness. The foresight score measures how probable a same-policy continuation is after a candidate step. A confidently repeated misconception may therefore receive a high score, while a valid but unfamiliar or branching argument may receive a low score. This creates both false positives and false negatives. The paper reports downstream ablations, but no labeled study of step-ranking accuracy, calibration, abstention, or adversarially constructed branches. (Curator inference from the feedback contract)

The continuations are correlated evidence. They share the same model, prefix, tokenizer, decoding code, and learned biases; four rollouts per beam are not independent votes. Local score-based decisions can discard a globally useful path, and training can shift the policy toward prefixes that make its own future generations more probable rather than more semantically correct. ACO relaxes the influence of inconsistent estimated advantages, but it cannot manufacture correctness information that is absent from the proxy. Cross-policy and cross-checkpoint transfer of the ranking remains untested. (Curator inference; Paper §2.2–2.3)

The released implementation has unresolved reproducibility risks. On the inspected unpinned `main`, the exploration script creates three process pools with five slots when `num_foresight=4` but creates `step_prob` with four; the preference constructor’s equality guard would skip such a record. The constructor also keeps `prompt` outside the `chosen`/`rejected` message lists, while the shown training encoder consumes those message lists and not the separate prompt field. These observations may reflect code drift or a missing preprocessing step, so they should be reproduced before being labeled definitive defects. The training shell retains blank paths, and no tagged release binds it to the paper runs.

Data and release lineage are incomplete. The HF collection exposes 150K Magpie and 48K OpenHermes source repositories rather than the exact selected 25K/32K manifests, and it does not expose a verified 100K/128K preference snapshot. Source records include responses, which the sampler copies to `ground_truth`; although the visible selection code does not use that field, a pinned end-to-end audit is needed to rule out label leakage. Rejected candidates, paper-run process pools, pair-level splits, seeds, logs, code/model hashes, and retention policies are unknown. The model and source-data repositories declare Apache-2.0, but code and generated-preference licensing remain unknown.

The contamination and experimental boundaries are also narrow. Appendix C uses embedding visualization and mean distances, not exact overlap detection. Main evidence is concentrated on LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct, two source mixtures, and named public benchmarks; Qwen transfer is summarized without the full per-task table in the HTML surface. General-domain results include small declines, and the scaling study is limited by compute. Benchmark gains cannot identify which preferences are correct or whether the method remains stable with a new sampler, policy family, language, domain, or larger rollout budget.
