The official ACL Anthology record identifies this work as an ACL 2025 long paper (July 2025, pages 13153–13167; DOI `10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.644`). It asks a narrow construction question: can a language model turn general natural-language queries into useful reasoning-training records when no answer label, execution environment, human preference, stronger teacher, or auxiliary reward model is available? Existing self-training can cheaply generate responses, but selection becomes underdetermined when no outside system can say which complete answer—or which intermediate step—is correct. (Paper §1)

Genius addresses this gap with a policy-internal value proxy. At each response prefix, the current policy samples candidate next steps and future continuations, converts continuation log probabilities into foresight scores, keeps paths by score-based sampling, and constructs positive/negative trajectory pairs with advantage weights. The resulting training object is not merely “a chain of thought”: it links a source query and policy checkpoint to a prefix, candidate set, future-rollout pool, normalized log probabilities, selected beam, chosen and rejected trajectories, and their advantage values. The official constructor serializes the final preference view as `prompt`, `chosen`, `rejected`, `chosen_weights`, `rejected_weights`, and `chosen_average_weights`. (Paper §2.2; official repository: `foresight-sampling/`)

This makes the paper directly relevant to the Data Construction and Open Release Recipes category: it specifies a query-to-preference-to-checkpoint pipeline and publishes sampling, preference-construction, and ACO-training code. The adjacent problem it does not solve is semantic verification. Its score observes how probable a continuation is under the same policy; it does not observe a reference answer, test result, proof state, human judgment, or external correctness signal. “Purely unsupervised” therefore describes the absence of external labels in the Genius loop, not proof that the selected branch is correct.

The Card reaches L4 because the ACL paper, full appendices, official repository, two model repositories, and two source-query repositories expose enough detail to reconstruct the intended modules and audit the feedback contract. The evidence boundary remains material: the exact 25K/32K query manifests, the reported 100K/128K preference snapshots, rejected-sample manifests, immutable experiment revision, item-level decontamination, and a complete license chain were not verified as released.
