Under the paper’s LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct setup, the seven-benchmark reasoning average rises from 49.65 for the base model to 57.08 after Genius training on Magpie-25K and 56.90 after training on OpenHermes-2.5-32K. The strongest unsupervised baseline in the corresponding Table 1 blocks reaches 54.45 and 54.26, respectively. Individual scores are not uniformly large gains: for example, Magpie Genius reports 34.64 on MATH and 30.35 on GPQA, while OpenHermes Genius reports 34.42 and 34.82. These are author-reported model results, not measurements of per-pair correctness. (Paper Table 1)

The component evidence is more diagnostic. Removing foresight lowers the average from 57.08 to 53.91 for Magpie and from 56.90 to 53.65 for OpenHermes (−3.17 and −3.25 points). Replacing score-distribution sampling with the paper’s greedy variant gives 52.98 and 53.80 (−4.10 and −3.10). With foresight data held fixed, ACO scores 57.08/56.90 versus DPO 55.51/55.73 and ROPO 55.30/55.25. These ablations support the tested search and weighting choices jointly with the rest of the implementation; they do not calibrate foresight as correctness probability or establish universal ACO superiority. (Tables 3–4)

General-domain and code results are mixed enough to constrain interpretation. Table 2 reports Arena-Hard increasing from 30.31 to 50.00 for both Genius variants, but Magpie MMLU-Pro changes from 48.62 to 48.44 and OpenHermes WikiBench from 27.65 to 27.00. Appendix D reports MBPP 69.65→71.60/71.98 and LiveCodeBench 19.50→19.75/21.25 for Magpie/OpenHermes. Figure 5 reports a 6.67-point AIME 2024 improvement for the tested checkpoints, while Figure 6 contains only 10K-level down-sampled training curves and the authors explicitly cite compute limits. (Table 2, Figure 5, §4.2, Appendix D Table 6)

Artifact evidence supports partial reproducibility. The official repository exposes the sampling, preference-construction, ACO-training, and evaluation code; the official HF collection exposes two LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct checkpoints and two source-query datasets. Both model pages and both source-dataset pages declare Apache-2.0. However, the collection does not expose a verified 100K/128K generated preference snapshot, and GitHub shows no tagged release. The visible quick-start code also has process-array length and prompt-conditioning handoff questions documented in this Card.

No independent reproduction was identified in the confirmed artifacts. Appendix C’s embedding-distance analysis shows that the sampled source-query embeddings differ on average from the benchmark embeddings, but it is not an item-level contamination test. Consequently, the strongest supported conclusion is that the authors report gains and informative ablations under their setup and publish substantial implementation surfaces—not that every constructed preference is correct, calibrated, uncontaminated, or directly reusable.
