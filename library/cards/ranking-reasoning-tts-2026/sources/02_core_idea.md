The core idea is to treat benchmark ranking as a statistical problem over repeated trials, not as a deterministic post-processing of one score. The paper formalizes dense evaluation with a model–question–trial response tensor and implements multiple ranking families in Scorio, including paired comparisons, item-response models, voting rules, and graph or spectral methods. A ranking method is judged by its agreement with a high-budget reference and its stability under trial subsampling.

The paper also distinguishes two budget regimes. With many trials, several reasonable procedures converge to nearly the same order; with one or few trials, uncertainty and the choice of prior matter far more. Greedy decoding can reduce variance when it agrees with stochastic sampling, but it can bias rankings when the two behaviors diverge. Thus the extra inference samples are not only a way to solve questions; they change what it means to measure a model fairly.
