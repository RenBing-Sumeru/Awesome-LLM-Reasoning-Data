The ACL paper reports that, across 20 models and four Olympiad-style benchmarks with up to 80 trials, most full-trial rankings closely agree with the Bayesian high-budget reference, with mean Kendall’s tau-b from 0.93 to 0.95. It further reports that 19 to 34 methods recover exactly the same ordering in that regime. This supports the claim that many ranking families are effectively interchangeable when enough repeated evidence is available.

The low-budget results are the more operational finding. In the single-trial setting, the best methods reach approximately 0.86 tau-b, while a greedy-decoding prior reduces variance by 16–52 percent at one trial only when greedy and stochastic rankings align. The evidence therefore warns against reporting one ranking without its trial budget and sampling protocol. It is grounded in binary mathematical correctness, so readers should not assume the same calibration for subjective or partial-credit evaluation.
