The prior baseline is prompt-only or heuristic RAG evaluation, especially RAGAS and few-shot proprietary LLM judges, plus more annotation-heavy sampled evaluation. ARES changes the evaluation loop by making judges domain-adaptive through synthetic training and by wrapping model predictions in PPI confidence intervals. The direction signal is the explicit separation of component criteria and statistical uncertainty, which makes configuration comparisons auditable. The quality signal is stronger ranking and scoring evidence across KILT, SuperGLUE, AIS, and existing RAG systems. What is not new: binary relevance/faithfulness criteria, synthetic QA generation, and classifier fine-tuning are existing pieces. Reuse requires checking validation-label quality, domain shift, language, generator choice, judge checkpoints, unlabeled sample construction, and license terms for datasets and code.
