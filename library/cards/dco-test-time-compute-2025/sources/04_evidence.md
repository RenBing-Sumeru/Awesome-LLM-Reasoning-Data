The paper first exposes the problem with a four-epoch Llama-3-8B MATH experiment. Cross-entropy raises pass@1 from 4.4% to 7.4%, but the reported pass@256 falls from 65.2% to 44.5% and pass@4k falls from 82.5% to 63.0%. This is direct evidence that better one-sample accuracy can coexist with worse large-budget coverage. The authors report that DCO variants form a stronger performance frontier when their training coverage parameter is close to the test-time sampling count.

For proof search, the reported pass@4k cross-entropy baseline obtains 55.6% on Mathlib and 37.4% on MiniF2F. An ensemble of step-wise DCO models reaches 62.2% and 43.6%, compared with a five-times-compute cross-entropy baseline at 57.0% and 39.5%. These findings support matching training confidence to a search regime, but remain evidence for the tested models, data, verifiers, and sampling budgets; the paper itself shows that choosing a DCO setting too large or too small for the deployment N can move performance below the frontier.
