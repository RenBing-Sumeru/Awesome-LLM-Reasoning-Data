The closest test-time methods increase the number of sampled answers, apply a verifier, or change the search tree after a conventional fine-tuning stage. This paper changes the training objective itself so that the model distribution is prepared for the intended number of test-time attempts. It identifies an explicit failure mode of treating one-sample likelihood as the sole training target: the policy can become confidently wrong and lose the answer diversity that pass@N needs.

The reusable novelty is the parameterized bridge between a training coverage objective and an inference coverage objective. In proof search, the step-wise formulation further makes the training parameter a way to shape depth-versus-width exploration. The work does not claim one universal confidence level or one loss that dominates every test-time strategy. Rather, it establishes that the deployed sampling budget is part of the training specification and must be recorded when comparing post-training procedures.
