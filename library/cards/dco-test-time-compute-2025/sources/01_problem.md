Fine-tuning is commonly optimized for the probability of one reference completion, while a deployed reasoning system may draw many independent completions and accept success when any one is correct. These objectives agree for pass@1 but need not agree at pass@N. Continued cross-entropy training can concentrate probability on a small number of answers; if a confident answer is wrong, repeated sampling may reproduce the same failure rather than provide the diverse attempts that make additional inference compute useful.

The paper asks how the training objective should depend on the intended test-time sampling budget. Its decision surface contains a prompt, a completion distribution, a training coverage target, N sampled completions at deployment, and a correctness predicate. The study tests whether standard cross-entropy is aligned with large-N coverage, identifies confidence as the mechanism of misalignment, and asks whether a loss designed for pass@N can preserve exploration without discarding the ability to exploit a high-confidence correct answer.
