For direct mathematical answers, the authors fine-tune Llama-3-8B-base on MATH and evaluate a verifier-backed pass@N protocol. They derive DCO from the probability of emitting the correct completion at least once in the chosen number of draws. DCO reduces to cross-entropy at one draw, but increasingly attenuates updates to already high-confidence examples as its coverage parameter grows. The paper trains multiple variants because no single setting is expected to be Pareto-optimal for every deployment N.

The method is also adapted to formal proof search. In LeanDojo, the model proposes a tactic from a proof state, Lean validates it and returns a next state, and search ends at an invalid tactic, a depth limit, or a completed proof. A step-wise DCO variant controls confidence at every proof step and thereby the effective branching width of the search tree. The paper evaluates direct answers, chain-of-thought answers using approximate confidence estimates, and proof search; reproduction must log loss parameter, sampling count, verifier, tree width and depth, model confidence, wall-clock cost, and outcome.
