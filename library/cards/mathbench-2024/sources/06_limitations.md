Correctness is answer-level correctness under the benchmark key and scoring protocol. It does not certify a model's reasoning trace, mathematical proof validity, or conceptual understanding beyond the selected item format.

The public release can enter training corpora, so later leaderboard claims need contamination and version checks. The multiple-choice reformulation improves scoring stability but can introduce option artifacts and allows guessing or option-order sensitivity.

Reuse risk is highest when CE scores are treated as reward labels for training without auditing prompt format, shuffled choices, answer normalization, language distribution, and topic coverage. License and redistribution should be checked at the artifact level.
