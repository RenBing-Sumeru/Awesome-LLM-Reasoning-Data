MathBench was released on arXiv on 2024-05-20 and cited by the official repository as Findings of ACL 2024. It addresses a gap in math evaluation: many LLM math benchmarks collapse difficulty, language, theory knowledge, and application problem solving into one aggregate score.

The evaluation surface is a bilingual hierarchical benchmark of 3,709 questions across arithmetic, primary, middle-school, high-school, and college stages. The final set is split into MathBench-T for 2,209 theoretical knowledge questions and MathBench-A for 1,500 application questions.

The decision boundary is benchmark/evaluation surface, not a training recipe and not a proof verifier. It is useful for the atlas because it exposes answer-level math competence through staged topic metadata and a reproducible scoring contract rather than only reporting broad "math ability."
