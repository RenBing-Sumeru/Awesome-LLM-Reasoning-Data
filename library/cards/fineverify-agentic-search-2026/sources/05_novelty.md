Existing test-time scaling methods often generate more paths and apply frequency voting, a global reward, or a single confidence estimate. FineVerify changes the verifier surface from one answer-level judgment to a matrix of candidate-by-sub-question checks. The same decomposition is used across candidates, making the score comparable and leaving an audit trail for why one trajectory outranked another.

The novel component is not web browsing by itself, nor simply self-verification. It is the use of an explicit fine-grained verification record as the consumer of a growing trajectory pool. Before reuse, readers should test whether the sub-questions are complete, whether they can be answered from retrieved evidence rather than model priors, and whether aggregation rewards superficial coverage over logically sufficient evidence.
