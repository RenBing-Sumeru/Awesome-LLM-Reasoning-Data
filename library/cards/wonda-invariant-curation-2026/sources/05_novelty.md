InvBench already framed one-shot invariant generation as program plus loop marker to invariant, used UAutomizer-generated training targets, and evaluated whether a proposed invariant helps formal verification. Earlier generate-and-verify systems also used symbolic tools to filter or repair LLM proposals. AST cleanup, LLM rewriting, SFT, and verifier portfolios are therefore not individually new.

WONDA's concrete change is to make the training target itself a verifier-measured object. It separates four properties that raw solver output had conflated: non-degeneracy, induction, sufficiency for the target assertion, and lower solver time. Its semantic simplifier is allowed to leave the equivalence class of the source invariant; soundness is recovered by rechecking the actual rewritten candidate rather than trusting the LLM. This converts a proof artifact into multiple formally labeled alternatives and preserves why a row was retained.

The grade contract also exposes a distinction important for reasoning-data research: `correct` is not the same as `sufficient`, and neither guarantees `faster`. The Qwen3-0.6B result demonstrates that speed-oriented curation can improve portfolio time without improving correctness rate. That is more informative than treating every verifier-passing record as one homogeneous positive class.

Compared with Loopy, LaM4Inv, and LEMUR, the method spends extra verifier/teacher budget during dataset construction and retains a one-shot inference interface. Compared with raw InvBench training, it changes target syntax, semantic diversity, and acceptance. Its directional value is the open candidate-level contract and release, not a first use of formal verification or a proof that semantic simplification always dominates raw data. Before reuse, a study should equalize candidate/verification budgets, compare with non-LLM simplifiers and iterative inference, audit program-level splits and licenses, and pin the paper-matching implementation.
