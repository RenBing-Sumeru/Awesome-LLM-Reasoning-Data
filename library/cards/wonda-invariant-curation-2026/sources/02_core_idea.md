WONDA makes “high-quality invariant” an executable data contract rather than a readability judgment. A candidate must be non-degenerate; induction is checked as `V(A, P, I)`; sufficiency is checked as `V(A ∪ {I}, P, q)`; and practical utility compares the parallel verification time `max(t1, t2)` with direct baseline time `tb`. This produces grades 0–3: non-inductive; inductive but insufficient; inductive and sufficient without speedup; and inductive, sufficient, and faster. The reported V2 SFT set accepts grades 2 and 3.

The construction has two transformations. Deterministic AST normalization removes tautologies and contradictions, minimizes parentheses, and strips casts. The logical rewrite rules are semantics-preserving, but cast stripping is explicitly not guaranteed to be; the downstream verifier is therefore essential. For normalized invariants of at least 20 characters, Kimi K2 Thinking sees the program, invariant, and loop marker and samples four compact alternatives. Candidates may be weaker, stronger, equivalent, or incomparable to the raw invariant; only the two formal checks decide whether they become training targets.

The feedback system can observe validity of the encoded C verification queries and verifier runtime under one pinned environment. It cannot certify semantic equivalence to the source invariant, readability, uniqueness, portability to another verifier or architecture, absence of source leakage, or ownership of the program. A grade-3 label is consequently environment-relative evidence, not a universal quality label.

The closest baseline is InvBench's one-shot training on raw UAutomizer invariants. WONDA retains that source/evaluation setting but changes the target distribution and exposes induction, sufficiency, and speedup as separate fields. Iterative systems such as Loopy, LaM4Inv, and LEMUR instead use repeated generate-and-verify or repair at inference; WONDA improves a single proposal and is complementary to those loops rather than replacing them.
