Removing unmet constraints shifts the task distribution and does not directly teach satisfaction of the full original request. Repeated replay may overrepresent easier subsets or encourage policies whose responses are easy to relabel. The Card does not identify a released audit of how often constraints are removed, which constraint types survive, or whether the pseudo-instruction distribution stays representative.

Entropy measures policy uncertainty, not semantic diversity, difficulty, or data quality. Its interaction with response length and decoding settings requires calibration. The learned judge may introduce systematic bias for soft constraints, while deterministic rules can be brittle to formatting or incomplete criterion specifications. Per-constraint human validation and checker error rates are unknown.

Exact source revisions, source-wise counts, deduplication, decontamination, train/evaluation overlap, full rollout groups, rejected replay candidates, judge/checker versions, rollout budgets, seeds, and checkpoint linkage are not released. Apache-2.0 declarations cover the official artifacts, but compatibility with every upstream prompt source and rights for generated outputs remain to be established.
