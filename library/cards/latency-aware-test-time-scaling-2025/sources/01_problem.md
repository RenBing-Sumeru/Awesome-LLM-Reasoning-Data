The official record is a Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2025 paper (pp. 17124–17137); arXiv v4 was posted on 12 September 2025. The paper asks why a token-optimal test-time scaling policy need not be latency-optimal. In low-request inference, autoregressive decoding can be memory-bound: a longer single chain may use tokens efficiently yet underuse accelerator compute, while several concurrent branches can raise throughput with little initial latency cost. The decision boundary is explicit: the argument targets small- and medium-scale device/workstation workloads, not large servers handling hundreds or thousands of requests, which the authors describe as compute-bound. (Paper §§1, 3.1, 7; Appendix A.4)

For this Card, the relevant object is an evaluation record rather than a released training set: benchmark prompt and answer; target/draft model pair; output-length budget; branch count; speculative-decoding draft length; candidate answers; aggregation rule; measured wall-clock latency; and final accuracy. The reported surfaces are MATH-500 (500 items), AIME24 (30), AIME25 (30), and GPQA-Diamond (198). The work belongs in `rollout_search_test_time_trace_data` because branch sets, selection, and test-time budgets determine the evaluated behavior. It does not provide process labels, a learned reward, or a public candidate-trace corpus. (Paper §4.1)

The Card reaches the bilingual content bar from the official venue paper, tables, and appendix, but not the replayability bar: hardware, runtime versions, raw candidates, prompt-level configuration mappings, and timing logs remain unavailable.
