- **Latency/accuracy operating point.** On s1.1-32B and MATH-500 at the 1,024-token setting, the baseline is `96.2 ± 0.2 s` and `75.0 ± 0.6%`; latency-optimal TTS is `57.2 ± 0.4 s` and `82.3 ± 1.8%`. The table summarizes this as a 1.7× speedup and +7.3 percentage points. At 8,192 tokens, the baseline is `419.5 ± 0.7 s`, `85.4 ± 0.9%`, versus `283.1 ± 0.6 s`, `92.7 ± 0.3%` for the joint configuration. Results are means and standard deviations over three runs. (Table 1)
- **Small-model headline.** The official abstract reports s1.1-3B reaching 72.4% on MATH-500 within 10 seconds; s1.1-32B reaches 82.3% within one minute. These are model-and-system operating points, not evidence that a released trace dataset is high quality. (Abstract; §5.1)
- **Search-cost ablation.** Grid and greedy search reach the same MATH-500 accuracies for s1.1-32B (82.3%), s1.1-3B (72.4%), DeepSeek-Distill-32B (62.2%), and QwQ-32B (94.5%). Grid search uses 56 evaluations for each; Table 2 reports 10, 11, 9, and 8 greedy steps, respectively. The prose says “8–10,” so the table and prose disagree for s1.1-3B. (Table 2; §5.3)
- **Selector ablation.** On s1.1-32B/MATH-500, confidence average-vote scores `83.0 ± 1.2`, `86.3 ± 1.3`, `92.3 ± 1.2`, and `93.5 ± 0.8%` at 1,024/2,048/4,096/8,192 tokens, compared with majority voting's `82.3 ± 1.8`, `85.9 ± 0.7`, `91.7 ± 1.3`, and `92.7 ± 0.3%`. The gain is small and does not validate intermediate candidates. (Table 3)
- **Boundary evidence.** Appendix A.4 shows that multi-request load can erase the memory-bound advantage: for one s1.1-32B configuration at 85.6% accuracy, latency rises from 91.3 s (one request) to 93.1 s (four) and 156.8 s (sixteen). Another 83.0%-accuracy configuration rises from 159.8 s to 602.8 s and 2,621.8 s. (Table 4)

These are author-reported results from the official paper; no independent reproduction or raw timing/candidate release was located.
