The contribution is a latency-constrained allocation rule over two forms of concurrency. Branch-wise parallelism generates `B` candidate solutions concurrently and aggregates their final answers. Sequence-wise parallelism uses speculative decoding, where a smaller draft model proposes several tokens and the target model verifies them in parallel. The authors treat `B` and draft length as competing consumers of underused compute, then use greedy search to decide which dimension to expand for a target latency. (Paper §§3.2–3.3, Algorithm 1)

The feedback contract is final-answer and system-level. Majority voting is the default selector; four confidence-weighted variants are an ablation. Benchmark scoring measures the selected final answer, while wall-clock latency constrains the configuration. This contract observes answer agreement or model confidence and aggregate correctness/latency. It does not label reasoning steps, establish that a majority answer is correct, or reveal whether gains come from a particular candidate, answer extractor, tie rule, or draft-token acceptance event.

Prior test-time scaling work optimizes token budget through longer chains, repeated sampling, or verifier-based selection; self-consistency already supplies majority voting, and speculative decoding already supplies lossless acceleration. The paper's narrower change is to put branch count and speculative draft length in one latency-aware resource-allocation problem and search their joint configuration. It is therefore a systems-and-evaluation direction signal, not a new verifier or open reasoning dataset. (Paper §2)
