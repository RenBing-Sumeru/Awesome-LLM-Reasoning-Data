Closest weak-supervision RLVR methods replace labels with majority voting, formatting, or one-shot signals, but a gain there can still be read as evidence that the surrogate carries useful task information. This paper deliberately extends the control to Bernoulli noise and selected wrong labels, while holding GRPO and data fixed and testing multiple model families.

Its novelty is therefore the counterfactual reward audit plus a clipping ablation that ties the anomaly to amplification of a pretrained behavior. It does not introduce a new reward for deployment; the released data and code support diagnosis and reproduction.

The changed experimental decision is to use deliberately uninformative or anti-correctness controls, not to introduce a new base model, benchmark, or final-answer verifier.

This changes the evidentiary bar for future weak-reward claims.
