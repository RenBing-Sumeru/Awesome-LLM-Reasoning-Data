Table 1 evaluates Average@8 on Math500, Minerva Math, Olympiad Bench, AIME24, and AMC23. For Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, GVM-GRPO reports a five-benchmark average of 40.42 versus 38.11 for GRPO, and GVM-RAFT++ reports 39.64 versus 36.42 for RAFT++. For Qwen2.5-Math-7B, final differences are much smaller: 49.86 versus 49.07 for GRPO and 49.13 versus 49.06 for RAFT++. These are author-reported sampled accuracies; evaluation temperature is 1.0 for the 1.5B model and 0.7 for the 7B model.

Figure 2 reports about 2× faster convergence for `N'=8,N=8n` and 4× for `N'=32,N=32n`, plus final gains of about 1.25 and 5 percentage points on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B. The paper explicitly measures speedup in plotted update steps. Appendix C says a typical `N'=8,N=8n` GVM iteration takes 90 minutes on 4×H100, but does not give a matched wall-clock accounting for the uniform baseline, pilot generation, verifier calls, and gradient-statistic computation.

The appendix supplies useful negative evidence. Figure 9 finds similar convergence trends as pilot/total sample settings grow. Figures 7–8 show that Qwen2.5-Math-7B large-`n` pass@n deteriorates during training; the authors state that this behavior already appears in vanilla RAFT++/GRPO and is not caused by GVM allocation. They also report that larger `N'` can shift budget toward harder prompts and lower observed training reward.

The evidence supports prompt-dependent allocation as an optimization intervention in the tested mathematics runs. It does not demonstrate process-correct rationales, intrinsic data quality, semantic decontamination, or lower total cost. No released paper-run ledger permits independent reconstruction of the exact prompts, responses, checker outputs, gradient statistics, allocations, and checkpoints behind these curves.
