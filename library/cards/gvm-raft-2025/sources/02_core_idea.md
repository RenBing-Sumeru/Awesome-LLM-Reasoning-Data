GVM-RAFT turns the per-prompt rollout count into an adaptive acquisition decision: use a pilot to estimate answer acceptance and accepted-response gradient magnitude, then allocate a fixed global budget to reduce the variance of the training-gradient estimator.

For each prompt, the current policy first produces `N'` responses. Math-Verify yields binary outcomes, from which the method estimates acceptance probability `p_i`; gradients of accepted responses estimate `G_i`. Algorithm 2 assigns `n_i` proportional to `G_i / sqrt(p_i + alpha / p_i^(beta-1))`, normalizes counts to the global budget `N`, and adjusts integers to preserve that budget. Appendix C reports `alpha=0.001` and `beta=2`.

The feedback contract has three distinct layers. Math-Verify observes the extracted final answer and oracle, not the validity of intermediate reasoning. `p_i` summarizes current-policy success; `G_i` summarizes checkpoint-dependent gradient magnitude among accepted pilots. RAFT++ trains on accepted responses with importance weighting and clipping, whereas GRPO forms group-relative advantages from final rewards. GVM observes neither semantic rationale faithfulness nor intrinsic prompt quality; it only schedules further acquisition using these empirical signals.

Compared with ordinary RAFT++ or GRPO, the key difference is non-uniform prompt allocation under a matched total generation budget. Compared with difficulty-aware rejection sampling such as DART-Math, GVM combines pass behavior with a gradient term and recomputes the schedule online as the policy changes. The direction signal is therefore an auditable sampling-policy layer between prompt sourcing and optimization, not a new verifier or a reusable quality label.
