Release completeness is unresolved. The paper claims approximately 40 million responses and says all data and methods are public, while the official dataset card explicitly calls the release a subset. The exact released-versus-complete record mapping, rejected examples, generation prompts, decoding parameters, and category-specific CV thresholds are unknown. The dataset-level CC-BY-NC-4.0 label does not by itself establish compatibility with every upstream corpus. The paper says PPL comes from a prior 32B model, whereas the dataset card says a 7B model.

Verifier reliability is uneven. A learned fallback can overturn Math-Verify failures; science and open-ended records rely on learned judges; instruction constraints are partly model-generated; and code uses no more than ten available public tests. The dataset card itself warns of inaccurate ground truths. Decontamination is documented for AIME2024 only, not GPQA-Diamond or LiveCodeBench. Repeated responses are also correlated through common prompts, teachers, and validators, so pass rate and CV are pipeline-dependent measurements. Finally, the experiments lack an ablation that isolates corpus scale, verification, CV selection, and the unusually high learning rate, and Stage II reduces LiveCodeBench performance.
