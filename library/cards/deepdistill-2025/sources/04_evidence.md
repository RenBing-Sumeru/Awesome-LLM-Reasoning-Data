The paper reports pass@1 on AIME2024, GPQA-Diamond, and LiveCodeBench. Stage-I Qwen2.5-72B reaches 79.2, 65.7, and 63.8, while the Stage-I 32B model reaches 75.8, 66.3, and 64.2. Annealing the 32B model changes these to 77.9, 66.9, and 61.7: math and science rise, but code falls. A learning-rate ablation on the 72B model reports that reducing 8e-5 to 8e-6 lowers AIME2024 from 79.2 to 72.5 and LiveCodeBench from 63.8 to 60.2. These are recipe-level results, not proof that every record or difficulty label is correct.

The official Hugging Face repository makes the schema and 60 category-by-teacher-by-pass JSONL files inspectable and labels the dataset CC-BY-NC-4.0. Its card also warns that every unique question is repeated 12 times, some `ground_truth` labels may be inaccurate, and only a subset of the complete dataset could be open-sourced. The paper's broad statement that all datasets and methods were released should therefore not be used as a completeness guarantee. The linked GitHub project tree contains paper PDFs and assets but no end-to-end DeepDistill implementation.
