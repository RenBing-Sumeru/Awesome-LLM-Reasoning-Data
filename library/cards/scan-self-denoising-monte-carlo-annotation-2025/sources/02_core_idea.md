SCAN-Pro contains roughly 197K step–score supervision records. Its central feedback consists of soft process labels estimated from lightweight-model continuations and then corrected through self-denoising. The paper first analyzes how Monte Carlo noise is distributed around earliest-error boundaries and then uses neighboring-step structure and a robust objective rather than treating every estimate as ground truth. The data targets low-cost PRM training while reducing generated tokens and inference cost relative to large human-supervised pipelines.
