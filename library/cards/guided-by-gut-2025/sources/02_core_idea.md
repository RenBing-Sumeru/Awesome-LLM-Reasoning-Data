GG uses two distinct feedback layers. During inference, step confidence is the geometric mean of the generated tokens' probabilities, while novelty is the proportion of new tokens relative to the explored context; candidates are scored by C(step) + lambda_N*N(step). Modified DVTS splits a total budget N into N/M independent subtrees, samples M next steps at each depth, greedily retains the highest-scoring step, and finally confidence-votes among completed answers. This score is intrinsic and cannot certify correctness. Before search, GRPO calibrates the model with a ground-truth-dependent reward: a correct answer receives 1 + C(R)^4 and an incorrect answer receives 1 - 10*C(R)^4, where chain confidence emphasizes the last k steps. Thus programmatic answer checking supervises calibration, but test-time branch selection uses a learned policy's own scalar signals.
