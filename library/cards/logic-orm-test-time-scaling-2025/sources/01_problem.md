Outcome reward models can rank many test-time reasoning attempts, but their behavior depends on the candidate data used to train them. For three-way deductive-logic tasks, ordinary chain-of-thought sampling often produces many correct traces and a narrower set of mistakes. That imbalance leaves a practical gap: an ORM may learn easy answer-format or surface cues without seeing enough plausible, answer-aligned failures to distinguish a valid derivation from a confident wrong one.

LogicORM studies that gap through FOLIO, ProverQA, and JustLogic. The target object is not a single final answer. It is a collection of independently sampled reasoning traces, each paired with a gold True/False/Uncertain label, a parsed A/B/C prediction, and a binary outcome reward, followed by a learned scalar score used to select one candidate from a Best-of-N pool. The Echo construction deliberately asserts a candidate answer before requesting a rationale, creating wrong traces that are internally shaped around the asserted conclusion; an LLM judge then removes errors it can readily recognize.

The work belongs in Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data because the operative contract is “sample N candidates, score every complete trace with an ORM, and retain the maximum-scoring candidate.” The public release makes part of the training side inspectable: 10,009 FOLIO CoT rows and a 19,105-row Echo-augmented file. It does not publish the corresponding scored test-time candidate pools, rejected Best-of-N traces, selection indices, or historical ORM scores. The Card therefore treats the release as an auditable training-data and verifier recipe, not as a complete test-time trace log.
