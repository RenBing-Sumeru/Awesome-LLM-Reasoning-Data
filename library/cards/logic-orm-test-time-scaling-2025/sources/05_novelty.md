LogicORM's narrow novelty is to use answer-conditioned Echo prompts as a source of difficult negative reasoning traces for outcome reward modeling. Ordinary multi-sample CoT captures mistakes a reasoner makes spontaneously. Echo generation instead selects a target conclusion first and asks the model to rationalize it, increasing the chance of fluent but invalid paths organized around a wrong answer. A second model-mediated filter then removes errors that are easy for the judge to identify and retains the judge-blind remainder.

The construction differs from random negative sampling and from merely increasing the number of ordinary rollouts. It changes the negative distribution by conditioning generation on all three task labels, then trains the same binary full-trace ORM interface on the augmented data. The released FOLIO pair makes the effect on the serialized corpus unusually legible: 9,096 all-negative Echo additions transform the 10,009-row CoT file into a 19,105-row combined file without changing the 7,383 positive rows.

Several elements are inherited rather than new. FOLIO, ProverQA, and JustLogic provide the logic tasks and gold labels; exact final-answer agreement supplies standard outcome supervision; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GPT-4o, LoRA, multi-sample generation, and Best-of-N selection are established components. The paper does not introduce step labels, a process reward model, a new environment, or a proof checker. Its contribution is the data-construction and selector linkage: targeted negatives are used to train a scalar ORM that controls test-time candidate choice.

For the Atlas, the important novelty is also an audit lesson. “Hard negative” here means a wrong trace that survives one GPT-4o judgment prompt, not a formally verified subtle error. The construction can therefore concentrate the judge's blind spots and the generator's answer-conditioning artifacts. A faithful reusable record would preserve both retained and discarded Echo candidates, their asserted label, the judge prompt and response, the keep/drop decision, and the later ORM score; the public release preserves none of those fields natively.
