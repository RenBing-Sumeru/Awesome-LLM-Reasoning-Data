The strongest evidence is the released FOLIO data itself. The ordinary file has 10,009 rows over 1,001 unique inputs: 7,383 reward-positive and 2,626 reward-negative. The combined Echo-CoT file has 19,105 rows over the same number of unique inputs: 7,383 positive and 11,722 negative. Direct multiset comparison shows that every CoT row is present in the combined file and that the 9,096 additional rows are all reward 0. Each file also contains one exact record twice. These curator calculations agree with the paper's FOLIO totals while revealing the release's duplicate and the exact size of the retained Echo augmentation.

Appendix Table 2 describes a larger pre-filter construction boundary. The three FOLIO forced-answer pools contain 10,010 True-prompted candidates (6,552 correct, 3,458 incorrect), 10,010 False-prompted candidates (5,459/4,551), and 10,004 Uncertain-prompted candidates (4,918/5,086). Only the final combined file is released. The difference between those raw counts and the 9,096 added negatives demonstrates that filtering and selection occurred, but the absence of raw candidate IDs, judge responses, and keep/drop decisions prevents reconstruction of which negative was removed for which reason.

The model side is also concrete but incomplete. The official Hugging Face collection exposes six ungated full-weight ORMs: CoT and Echo variants for FOLIO, ProverQA, and JustLogic, all based on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. The public repository contains the generator, trainer, and inference components. This supports the stated data-to-verifier lineage. It does not establish that the released weights were trained from a reproducible row manifest, because ProverQA and JustLogic traces are absent, the FOLIO files have no split IDs, checkpoint data hashes are unavailable, and the paper-versus-code epoch setting conflicts.

The paper reports that CoT-plus-Echo ORMs improve deductive-logic selection across FOLIO, ProverQA, and JustLogic and four reasoners. Those are author-reported task results for specific models and candidate budgets; they are evidence about the proposed selector under the reported evaluation, not proof that every released trace is valid, diverse, licensed for reuse, or free of leakage. No historical scored candidate pools were released for independently checking whether gains arise from better negative coverage, score calibration, a larger candidate pool, or benchmark-specific artifacts.

Two internal inconsistencies materially affect reproduction. Appendix Table 4 prints a JustLogic Echo-CoT total of 49,197, while its correct and incorrect counts are 27,918 and 41,279, which sum to 69,197; Appendix B's sampling description does not resolve the difference. Separately, the paper reports three ORM epochs and the shell passes three, but the checked-in trainer hard-codes two. These are not reasons to discard the work, but they must remain visible beside the reported results.
