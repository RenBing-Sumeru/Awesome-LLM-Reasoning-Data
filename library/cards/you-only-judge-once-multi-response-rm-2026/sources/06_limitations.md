First, labels in MR²Bench still depend on humans, environments, executors, or model judges, so missed cases and bias become reward blind spots. Second, although the release contains roughly 94K crowdsourced preferences for image response ranking and video QA, coverage of languages, environments, robots, tasks, and model sources remains limited and public release creates contamination risk. Third, offline scores do not automatically become real RL or deployment gains. Reuse should audit deduplication, agreement, false positives, unseen-environment generalization, licensing, and safety boundaries.
