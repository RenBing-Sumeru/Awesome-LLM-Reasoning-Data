The paper supports the following reconstruction, with disclosed values separated from unresolved implementation details.

1. **Source prompts.** Math prompts are drawn from 890 pre-2024 AIME problems, 7,500 MATH problems, and 28,505 Numina-MATH problems. Code prompts are drawn from 7,505 Codeforces problems, 2,011 Code Contests problems, and 558 LiveCodeBench problems before July 2024 (Appendix A, Table 9). These six printed components total 46,969, not the reported 49,969. The three math components total 36,895, while Section 3.1 reports 40K math problems and Section 2.2 describes `D_START` as 40K math reasoning samples.
2. **Hint library.** The authors define six hint functions. Qwen-2.5-72B creates multiple alternative expressions for each function. The paraphrasing prompt, candidate count, decoding settings, retained variants, and mixture weights are unknown.
3. **Initial behavior and insertion.** QwQ-32B-Preview generates long reasoning. Hint-infer intercepts the termination point or last `wait`; Hint-RFT additionally uses `Wait`, `Alternatively`, and the end of reasoning as insertion locations. Exact parsing, capitalization, punctuation, and fallback behavior are not disclosed.
4. **Tool interaction.** The model emits Python, receives interpreter output or an error, then continues reasoning, verifies, or debugs. Appendix J.1 reports library-use counts including sympy 14,469, math 11,655, numpy 5,790, itertools 3,131, scipy 2,942, cmath 104, and networkx 90. No sandbox image, package versions, timeout, filesystem or network policy, resource limits, state-reset rule, or observation schema is released.
5. **Selection.** Active learning retains cases in which the initial answer is wrong and the post-hint answer is successful. Each hint is checked through multiple sampling iterations, but the count and aggregation rule are unknown. Repetitive responses and incorrect code executions are removed. The final-answer extractor and comparator, coding tests, repetition rule, exception handling, filter thresholds, rejected counts, and Figure 1 `modify` operation remain undisclosed.
6. **Seed training.** The paper reports 10,000 accepted mathematical reasoning samples in `D_seed`. Full-parameter fine-tuning of QwQ-32B-Preview produces START-0. Unique prompt count, prompt-to-trajectory multiplicity, source allocation, and acceptance yield are unknown.
7. **Expansion.** START-0 generates `D_START`, reported as 40,000 mathematical reasoning samples, and the original base model is fine-tuned on it to obtain START. The expansion-stage rollout and acceptance protocol is not separately specified. The framework is also applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B.
8. **Training.** Appendix H reports three epochs, global batch 128, cosine scheduling, 3% warmup, maximum training context 16,384, full-parameter DeepSpeed ZeRO-3, and learning rates of `7e-6` for QwQ-32B-Preview and `2e-6` for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B. The optimizer, weight decay, gradient clipping, seeds, and dependency versions are unknown. Training uses 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, but GPU memory, wall time, GPU-hours, generation compute, and failed runs are absent.
9. **Evaluation settings.** Appendix H says greedy decoding with at most 32,768 generated tokens and six tool uses per reasoning process. Section 3.4 instead reports top-p 0.95 and temperature 0.6. The paper does not identify which setting governs `D_seed`, `D_START`, or each evaluation table, so construction temperature and top-p remain `unknown`.
10. **Split and contamination.** The paper says it applies the Qwen2.5-Math decontamination method. No local implementation, normalization, threshold, removal count, or audit ledger is supplied. Coding uses pre-July-2024 LiveCodeBench for construction and 112 August–November 2024 problems for evaluation, but exact item IDs and snapshot hashes are missing.

The outputs used for training are selected reasoning-plus-tool trajectories supervised through conventional full-parameter SFT. Reproduction requires implementing and pinning four unpublished modules—the answer checker, Python environment, candidate sampler, and filter/modification pipeline—and resolving the count and decoding inconsistencies before reported results can be compared on equal terms.
