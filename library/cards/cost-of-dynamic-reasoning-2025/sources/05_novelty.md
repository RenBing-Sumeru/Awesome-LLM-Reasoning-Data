Prior agent papers mainly compare task success, while LLM-serving studies commonly assume one static inference request. This work's specific change is to expose the alternating LLM/tool control flow, history growth, branch concurrency, tail latency, KV-cache pressure, and GPU energy of dynamic reasoning in one systems analysis, then relate these quantities to sequential and parallel test-time budgets. It does not introduce CoT, ReAct, Reflexion, LATS, LLMCompiler, prefix caching, or the component benchmarks, and its power projections are not a new measured dataset. The direction signal for Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data is the audit schema implied by the study: a useful trace must retain workflow and budget parameters, tool and LLM timing, model and serving versions, hardware telemetry, and the derivation from per-request records to aggregates; accuracy alone is insufficient.
