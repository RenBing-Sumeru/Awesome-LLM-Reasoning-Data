
The findings are configuration-specific: two Llama-3.1 model sizes, A100 hardware, one vLLM/PyTorch/CUDA stack, selected agent-workload pairs, and tool latencies that may change. The paper uses 50 questions per design point in the scaling study but does not release the selected IDs, seeds, exact benchmark revisions, parser versions, or full replay configurations. AgentBench's optional trace writer and small example trace do not substitute for the experiment's complete trajectories or raw latency and energy logs. DCGM is named for GPU utilization, but the device-power sampling and energy-integration procedure is not disclosed at audit-ready depth. Table III assumes 71.4 million or 13.7 billion queries per day and extrapolates GPU-only energy, omitting CPU, memory, network, storage, cooling, and request batching; it must not be read as observed facility demand. Finally, concurrent modifications to LATS improve latency relative to the original sequential implementation but complicate direct cross-implementation comparison.
