Prior SFT selection work often reports quality or diversity gains on smaller, more homogeneous pools. This paper's distinctive move is not a new score; it reimplements six known self-scoring selectors on two much larger heterogeneous pools, reports five random runs, documents expensive or infeasible branches, and preserves a negative result when gains fail to survive scale.

The individual ingredients are established: gradient influence, instruction-following difficulty, rating-token uncertainty, cross-entropy, K-center diversity, compression, K-means, and token length. Several are materially adapted for feasibility, especially the batched DiverseEvol procedure. The paper therefore measures “method under a scaled implementation and resource budget,” not an untouched algorithm transported to a larger pool.

The direction-level contribution is twofold. First, diversity and implementation cost become part of the selection contract rather than an afterthought. Second, the paper proposes a cheap K-means-plus-length recipe that is strong for Llama3-8B in the reported setting. This makes negative scaling evidence actionable for builders—provided the terminal selection rule is actually released.

The most important novelty boundary is the public-code mismatch. Counting tokens and finding cluster centers are not equivalent to allocating a quota by cluster size and taking the longest records in each cluster. Before reuse, a team must implement and test the paper rule, disclose cluster allocation and tie-breaking, and publish selected/rejected IDs; otherwise it is reproducing the repository utility, not Table 4.
