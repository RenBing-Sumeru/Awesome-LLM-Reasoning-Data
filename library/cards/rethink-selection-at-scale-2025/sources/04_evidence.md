Across the main 10K experiments, the average scores of the tested selectors generally lie inside the range of five random runs. Table 1 reports one-sided Mann–Whitney U tests for the baseline-versus-random comparisons, and every listed p-value is greater than 0.05. This supports the narrow author claim that the replicated self-scoring methods do not significantly outperform random selection in these two pools and two model families; it does not establish that random sampling dominates every data-selection method.

The diversity ablation supports a second, conditional result. Adding K-means to the four quality methods usually raises the reported average, and the cross-entropy-plus-diversity gains exceed three points for Qwen2 and five points for Llama3 (Paper §5.2, Table 5). Not every method/model improves, so this is evidence that diversity can repair some self-scoring choices under the tested setting, not a universal ordering of diversity over quality.

For token length, Table 4 reports Llama3-8B averages of 56.23 on OpenHermes and 55.51 on WildChat. The latter exceeds the full-WildChat SFT average of 54.58 in Table 3. These are author-reported run aggregates, not independently reproduced record-quality measurements. The paper cautions that length selection is not optimal for every model and may weaken as base models improve.

The comparison set is shaped by resource limits. The authors exclude external-model scoring for financial cost, NUGGETS for a stated requirement exceeding 2,000 hours on 40 A100-80G GPUs, and DQ for over 1 TB memory. DiverseEvol uses eight A100-80G GPUs for 5–7 days, and ZIP takes about seven days to select 50K (Paper §5.3). Experiments beyond 50K selected records are not run because of baseline runtime. These exclusions make “almost all you need” a resource-conditioned finding.

The public artifact evidence is weaker than the paper-level result. The repository exposes method adaptations and training utilities at commit `5a635e42f84640f3c6b23a526fcfb1f251765f2b`, but no selected 10K/50K IDs, five random subsets, per-record scores, checkpoints, logs, evaluation outputs, or end-to-end length recipe. The filtered WildChat release is one 2,547,377,673-byte JSONL with a minimal card and no exact row count. Thus the main tables cannot be rebound to immutable records or independently reproduced from the release.
