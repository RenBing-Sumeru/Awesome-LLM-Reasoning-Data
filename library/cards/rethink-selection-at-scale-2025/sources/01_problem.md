The official ACL Anthology record places this paper in Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2025, published in November 2025. The paper asks whether self-scoring SFT-data selectors that look effective on 10K–300K single-source pools still outperform random sampling when the candidate pool grows to the scale of real instruction mixtures. Its target is subset construction for supervised fine-tuning, not response generation, preference learning, RL, or a new correctness verifier.

One input record is an existing instruction-response or multi-turn conversation from OpenHermes2.5 or an English-filtered WildChat pool. Selection can attach a model-derived quality score, an embedding/cluster or compression state, and token counts, then retain 10K or 50K records for answer-level SFT. The data object needed for audit is therefore larger than the selected text: it also includes upstream revision and ID, selector implementation, score or feature, selected/rejected decision, random seed, SFT run, and downstream metrics.

This work belongs in Data Construction and Open Release Recipes because it tests selection rules and their scaling/release behavior. It is valuable as a negative result: under the tested conditions, sophisticated selectors rarely show statistically significant gains over five random controls, and implementation/compute constraints materially alter several baselines. L4 here means the paper, appendix, venue record, code tree, and public source-pool surfaces support a complete bilingual audit. It does not mean the paper-specific selected subsets, checkpoints, contamination audit, licenses, or preferred end-to-end selector have been released.
