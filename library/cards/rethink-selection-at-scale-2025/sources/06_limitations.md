The authors explicitly limit the conclusion to the tested self-scoring methods and note that no selector is best for every model; token length may become less useful as the base model improves (Paper §7). The following release and audit risks are curator inferences grounded in the paper and inspected artifacts:

- The experimental boundary is six self-scoring selectors, two source pools, Qwen2-7B and Llama3-8B, and chiefly 10K/50K subsets. External scoring, NUGGETS, DQ, and 100K+ selected subsets are absent, so the title is stronger than the demonstrated domain.
- LESS validation/warm-up sizes, IFD clustering, SelectIT aggregation, and DiverseEvol are changed for scale. Performance differences can reflect both pool size and implementation changes; DiverseEvol's adaptation is explicitly identified as a possible degradation source.
- Selector scores are not correctness verifiers. High gradient influence, uncertainty, loss, distance, compression value, or length can favor wrong, unsafe, duplicated, or low-rights responses. No correctness-label audit or selected/rejected spot check is released.
- The paper's preferred token-length selector is not reproducible from the public code. `kmeans_sample.py` uses only the first 100 embeddings and selects cluster-center records, whereas the paper allocates proportionally and keeps longer items within clusters.
- Exact 10K/50K and random IDs, per-record scores/clusters, SFT files, seeds, checkpoints, logs, and metrics are missing. There is no immutable manifest binding source revisions, code, model/tokenizer, LLaMA-Factory, Open-Instruct, benchmark hashes, and table rows.
- No exact, n-gram, semantic, or provenance contamination audit is reported against BBH, GSM8K, HumanEval, MMLU, or IFEval. OpenHermes is a broad multi-source synthetic mixture, so unmeasured overlap can change both selector and benchmark conclusions.
- Rights are incomplete. The code has no root license, current OpenHermes exposes no detected license metadata, and the filtered WildChat card labels its payload Apache-2.0 without reconciling current upstream WildChat ODC-BY terms.
- WildChat privacy/toxicity claims depend on an unpinned upstream revision. The paper calls the extracted dialogues non-toxic, but the minimal filtered release supplies no transformation, moderation, PII, consent, or safety ledger for the exact payload.
- Five random controls are reported without selected IDs or a run-to-seed map. Single public seed-0 examples do not bind every selection and training run, and the paper reports no confidence intervals or multiple-comparison correction.
