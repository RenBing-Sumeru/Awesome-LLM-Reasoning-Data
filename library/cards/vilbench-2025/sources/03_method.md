For ViLBench construction, the authors evaluate six open-weight VLLMs as output and process reward models on 16 response candidates, then rank source examples by a PRM-preference indicator and sample the final per-benchmark quotas. This makes the benchmark intentionally sensitive to differences between process- and outcome-based selection; it is not an iid sample of the five source benchmarks.

For ViLReward-73K, selected prompts are searched with a visual-input adaptation of ReST-MCTS. The described search follows root selection, node expansion, route simulation, and value backpropagation. A prompt requests granular intermediate steps so that the model does not immediately emit the final answer. The paper reports ten MCTS iterations, but does not disclose the exact rollout policy checkpoint, decoding settings, seeds, or a per-prompt budget for the released corpus.

The authors describe three prompt-selection principles: distinct image content, questions intended to elicit reasoning, and source diversity. They choose the harder two MAVIS-Geometry levels as metadata candidates, one GeoQA170K question per unique image, multi-hop CLEVR-Math questions with distinct images, 1,544 non-direct plus 500 direct A-OKVQA questions, and ScienceQA examples above grade 7. The final source counts differ from some of these candidate descriptions after collection; the published Table 4 counts are the authoritative instance mixture cited here.

The feedback contract is mixed. GPT-4o judges a completed tree-search answer against the task answer; MCTS combines simulated continuation outcomes with a process-reward term to assign a partial solution a continuous value in [0,1]. The released record preserves only that final scalar, not the judge input/output or simulated children. It should therefore be read as a model- and search-conditional estimate of promising continuation, not proof that the visible local step is valid. The exact judge prompt, model version, answer-extraction rule, calibration, and adjudication agreement are unknown.

ViLPRM is a discriminative scalar model: Qwen2.5-VL-3B is fitted with a linear score head after the language head and trained by MSE against the derived continuous target. The paper does not use a generative score model for this component.
