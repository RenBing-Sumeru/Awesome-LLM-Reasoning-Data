DOTS makes the **choice of reasoning operations** the target of data construction. Instead of collecting only a solver's final CoT or choosing the best answer from several unconstrained samples, it searches a small, interpretable space of composed actions and produces a solver-conditioned trajectory label. The planner is trained to route a question through analysis, solution, and optional verification modules before the solver completes the answer.

The useful distinction from fixed prompting and generic test-time scaling is structural. Search decides whether to rewrite or decompose the query, whether to use CoT or PoT, and whether to invoke Self-Verification. Repeated ground-truth scoring estimates a trajectory value, while the shorter-path tie-break discourages selecting extra modules when success rates are equal. The resulting supervision is answer-level and trajectory-level; DOTS does not supply stepwise correctness labels, a learned process reward model, or an independently trained verifier.

The release also has an uncommon negative-evidence property: the inspected raw JSON retains failed dialogues, predicted answers, action-path identifiers, and binary scores. That makes it possible to study rejected reasoning behavior and evaluator sensitivity beyond a final-only SFT corpus. The contribution should nevertheless not be described as a complete search log: explicit accumulated scores, retained/pruned-set snapshots, prune reasons, and final chosen-SFT records are absent or unverified.

The novelty is bounded by the fixed action grammar and outcome evaluator. The 12 candidates are hand-defined combinations, not learned open-ended tools; the optimal label is specific to a task-solving LLM and answer checker; and GPT-4o supplies post-selection explanations rather than the selection reward itself. The paper's advance is therefore a concrete solver-conditioned search-and-distillation recipe, not a general proof that dynamic reasoning paths are semantically optimal.
