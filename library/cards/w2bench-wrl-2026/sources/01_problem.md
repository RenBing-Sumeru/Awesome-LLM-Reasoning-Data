Writing tasks contain concrete content, style, format, and length requirements, but reward benchmarks and reward-model training commonly score broad qualities such as fluency or helpfulness. This can reward an elegant answer that misses the actual instruction.

The paper introduces W²Bench/WEval for requirement-level reward-model evaluation and WRL for training a requirement-aware reward model and policy. Its central question is whether dropped requirements can supply a controlled ranking signal for writing RL.
