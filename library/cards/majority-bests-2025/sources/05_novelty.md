MoB does not generate a new reasoning path, train a verifier, or ask the reward model for a different kind of score. Its contribution is a selection transformation over the same N outputs and scalar rewards used by BoN: estimate the Best-of-m answer distribution and return its mode. Relative to self-consistency, it preserves reward ranking within each hypothetical subset; relative to BoN, it aggregates the distribution of maximum-reward outcomes instead of trusting one maximum; relative to Weighted BoN, it does not simply sum raw rewards by answer. The closed-form O(N log N) implementation and adaptive m rule make this transformation practical. For the atlas, the novelty lies in the reusable selector and released scored-pool schema, not in claiming that reward-model scores are ground truth or that a modal answer is intrinsically better reasoning.
