At N=128 with Qwen2.5-3B and GRM, Table 1 reports that adaptive MoB exceeds BoN on four of five benchmark slices: MATH500 69.95 versus 63.95, MMLU-Pro-Math 69.30 versus 66.10, MMLU-Pro-Chem 56.45 versus 49.00, and GSM8K 82.85 versus 80.95; on CommonsenseQA it is lower, 77.40 versus 77.70. Across the paper's 30 generator/reward/benchmark settings, the authors report improvement over BoN in 25. These results support the narrower claim that modal aggregation can improve final-answer selection under the tested pools and reward models; they do not show that every completion, reward, or extracted answer is high quality. The theory proves consistency of the m-out-of-N bootstrap estimate under finite answer support, reward-tail assumptions, m tending to infinity, and m/N tending to zero. The code and scored pools are released, making offline selector comparisons auditable, but benchmark gains are not evidence of data rights, decontamination, or reward-label validity.
