The disclosed recipe is:

1. **Pretraining corpus and mixture.** The 405B model trains on 15.6T text tokens, about 3.8×10^25 FLOPs, and more than 16,000 H100 GPUs. Meta describes publicly available online data, no Meta user data, a December 2023 cutoff, and a final mix of about 50% general knowledge, 25% math/reasoning, 17% code, and 8% multilingual. Source manifests and rights are not released.
2. **Filtering and scale.** URL, MinHash document, and repeated-line deduplication combine with PII/adult/unsafe-domain filters, repetition/outlier heuristics, Llama-2-derived quality/domain classifiers, and language identification across 176 languages. AdamW uses peak LR `8e-5`, 8,000 warmup steps, decay to `8e-7` over 1.2M steps, and token batches growing 4M→8M→16M.
3. **Long context and annealing.** Six progressive stages extend 8K to 128K over about 800B tokens. The final 40M tokens at 128K upsample high-quality sources, linearly anneal LR to zero, and end with checkpoint averaging.
4. **Human preference and RM.** For each prompt, annotators compare two responses from two current models, label four preference strengths, and may edit the chosen answer, yielding `edited > chosen > rejected`. The RM uses qualifying comparisons; absolute counts, checkpoint, rubric, and calibration are closed.
5. **Rejection sampling, SFT, DPO.** Usually 10–30 candidates are sampled from a recent or capability-best chat model for human-annotation prompts; the general RM keeps the favorite. Largest-model SFT runs 8.5K–9K steps at LR `1e-5`; DPO uses LR `1e-5`, beta `0.1`, format-token masking, and a `0.2` chosen-sequence NLL term. Six rounds recollect preferences/SFT data. PPO was tested but not adopted.
6. **SFT mixture.** More than 25M synthetic fine-tuning examples are reported overall. The final SFT mix is 52.66% general English, 14.89% code, 3.01% multilingual, 8.14% exam-like, 21.19% reasoning/tools, and 0.11% long context.
7. **Capability data.** Code uses a 1T-token, >85%-code expert and more than 2.7M synthetic SFT examples, including about 1M execution-feedback and 1.2M backtranslation dialogs, filtered by parsing, linting, compilation, tests, execution, self-verification, and judges. Math uses QA-converted pretraining records, human skill prompts, final-answer checks, self-verification, outcome/stepwise RMs, MCTS, Python, and mistake correction. Tool records cover Brave Search, Python, Wolfram Alpha, file processing, The Stack functions, multi-agent API synthesis, executable calls, and human message-level feedback.
8. **Safety.** Human/vendor and synthetic adversarial data, guided mutation, Rainbow Teaming, safety DPO, and refusal rewriting train the policy. Released components include Llama Guard 3—an 8B classifier for 13 MLCommons hazards plus Code Interpreter Abuse—Prompt Guard, and Code Shield. Their training records and internal benchmarks remain closed.
