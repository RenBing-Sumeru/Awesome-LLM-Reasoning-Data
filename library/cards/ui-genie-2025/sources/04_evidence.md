On the authors' custom RM benchmark, UI-Genie-RM reports overall F1 of 79.6 for step evaluation and 82.1 for outcome evaluation. That benchmark samples positives from AndroidControl, AMEX, and AndroidLab and constructs negatives with agent errors or trajectory corruption, so it tests the proposed contract but is close to the training sources and negative-generation mechanisms. It is not an independent audit of all released labels.

For agents, the paper reports AndroidControl success rates of 93.8/72.9 (low/high) for 3B, 94.3/74.2 for 7B, and 94.8/77.0 for 72B. Dynamic AndroidLab success is 28.8, 38.7, and 41.2 respectively. On Android Arena, the 7B model reports 20.4 functional success, 24.4 LLM-judged success, and 51.4 ESAR; both functional and LLM-judged success are 0.0 for the hard and multi-frame-query subsets. These results show that the training pipeline changes agent behavior, not that every synthetic example is correct.

The ablation supports the history/unification choices: five-image unified RM input reaches 79.6 step and 82.1 outcome F1, versus 61.3/60.5 without history. The gain is not uniform—hard step F1 is 68.7 with five images versus 71.5 with three. Best-of-10 inference also gives modest or absent hard-task improvements, including 7B hard task success staying at 1.4 and hard step score dropping from 61.1 to 58.9.

Release evidence is partial but concrete. The RM Hub repository has one 2.16 GB JSONL and 472,769 viewer rows with fields `messages`, `images`, and `positive`. The Agent repository has a 74.35 MB UI-Genie JSONL, 16,693 screenshots in 2,208 trajectory directories, and an additional 155.55 MB `AMEX_Agent_34K.jsonl` whose relationship to the named release is undocumented. The NeurIPS supplemental archive contains only three demonstration trajectories with screenshots, XML states, and history JSON; it is illustrative, not a full construction package.
