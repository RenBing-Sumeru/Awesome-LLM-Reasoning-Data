The paper evaluates Med-REFL on MedQA-USMLE and additional medical QA benchmarks, reporting average gains up to 4.11 percentage points and a further 4.13-point improvement over strong 7B/8B results. Comparisons include models trained without fine-grained reflection and tests on datasets outside the main construction source, providing both mechanism and transfer evidence. The released data and code improve reproducibility. These results support better benchmark reasoning under the tested models, but they do not verify clinical decision quality or patient safety.
