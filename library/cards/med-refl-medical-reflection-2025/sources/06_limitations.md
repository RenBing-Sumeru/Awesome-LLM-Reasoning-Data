The preference labels rely on model-based scoring and answer-level medical benchmarks, so the verifier may reward persuasive but clinically incomplete reasoning. Multiple-choice questions provide clearer correctness signals than open-ended diagnosis or treatment planning, limiting transfer. Synthetic reflections can inherit the generator’s medical misconceptions, and the paper does not replace specialist review. Reuse should audit intermediate claims against trusted medical sources, report verifier disagreement, and avoid treating benchmark gains as permission for autonomous clinical use.
