Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ) asks how far reasoning-oriented reinforcement learning can scale when it starts directly from a pretrained base model rather than an SFT- or distillation-initialized policy. The canonical source is the NeurIPS 2025 Main Conference paper and its appended supplement; the official repository, prompt data, policy checkpoints, and critic checkpoints define the public artifact surface.

“Zero” has a narrow meaning. The main Qwen2.5 Base runs begin PPO without a preliminary SFT or distillation stage. They do not begin from an untrained model and do not use zero data: base-model pretraining remains upstream, while RL consumes tens of thousands of curated prompt/reference-answer pairs from public and synthesized sources. Human choices also define prompts, filters, answer extraction, reward rules, PPO, and evaluation.

Two data objects must be kept separate. The released object is a two-message prompt/reference-answer row. The material training object is an online full-response trajectory: prompt, generated tokens, stop state, one terminal correctness reward, token-level critic values, token-level advantages, policy probabilities, and PPO ratios. ORZ samples 64 responses per prompt, yet the paper-run trajectories are not published.

The public files contain 56,878 original rows, 72,444 extended rows, and 13,451 hard rows. Exact-string auditing after whitespace normalization found 2,503, 24,025, and 611 duplicate rows respectively, leaving 54,375, 48,419, and 12,840 unique prompt strings. Rows omit source record IDs, per-record licenses, split membership, curation decisions, difficulty scores, rollout data, and checkpoint/run binding.

This belongs in **Data Construction & Open Release Recipes** because ORZ exposes a concrete base-model RLVR stack and reusable prompt/checkpoint artifacts, while demonstrating that open code and weights do not substitute for the exact online data, feedback, failure, and lineage ledger behind a reported RL run.
