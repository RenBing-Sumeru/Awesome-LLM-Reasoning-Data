The paper's novelty is to combine an internal trace-quality signal with a serving-system control point. Prior pruning often treats every token as the relevant cost or treats trace similarity as redundancy. STEP instead asks whether a partial trace is worth retaining when its KV cache is about to displace other work, making memory saturation a first-class part of the test-time scaling policy.

Its pseudo-label design is deliberately lightweight: no human step annotation and no large external reward model are required. That does not make the score a proof of correctness, but it is sufficient for relative pruning. The contribution is therefore not a new final-answer verifier; it is a runtime allocation mechanism that uses early hidden evidence to decide which parallel reasoning futures remain worth paying for.
