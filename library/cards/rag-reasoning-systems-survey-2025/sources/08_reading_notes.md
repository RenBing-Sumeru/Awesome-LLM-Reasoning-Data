Read the three-category taxonomy before looking at individual methods. For each system, write down where information enters, what component chooses the next search, what material is retained, and whether the final answer can be traced back to evidence. Then compare the system only with work that shares the relevant information flow; a one-shot retrieval design and an iterative search process should not be assumed equivalent merely because both use documents. The open-challenge section is most valuable after this exercise, because questions about trustworthiness and multimodal adaptation become concrete design choices. Keep the distinction between answer accuracy and evidence use visible throughout. An answer can be correct for the wrong reasons, while a grounded process can still fail if the retrieval collection omits the needed material.
