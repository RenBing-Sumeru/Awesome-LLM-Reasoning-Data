The survey's evidence is the taxonomy and its review of published methods and benchmarks. The official abstract specifically says it categorizes methods and datasets, then identifies challenges and future directions. That evidence supports a comparative account of the design space, not a single best design. A reported result must still be read with its retrieval collection, retrieval policy, task, and answer evaluation. A larger or better-matched collection can change the difficulty of evidence selection; a different search policy can alter both accuracy and cost; and an answer-only metric may not show whether the conclusion was supported by the retrieved material. Use the survey to find comparable families of systems, then examine the primary result under its own corpus, search procedure, and grounding criterion before treating it as evidence for a design choice.
