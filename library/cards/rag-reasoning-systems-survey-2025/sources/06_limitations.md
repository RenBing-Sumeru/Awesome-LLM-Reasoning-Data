The survey spans systems with different corpora, retrievers, models, search budgets, and task definitions, so headline scores are not automatically comparable. Retrieval may return irrelevant, incomplete, or misleading evidence, while a fluent reasoning trace may still ignore or misuse the evidence it received. More searches can create more opportunities to find useful information, but they also change cost and can compound distraction. Because the paper itself releases no common evaluation harness, readers should inspect each primary work's corpus, search procedure, stopping behavior, and grounding test before transferring a conclusion. Check whether a system is evaluated only on answer correctness or also on the relationship between its conclusion and retrieved material. The survey can identify the relevant comparison, but it cannot replace the local evidence needed to establish reliability for a specific application.
