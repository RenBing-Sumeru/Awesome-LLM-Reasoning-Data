The method is a survey synthesis over methods, datasets, and open challenges. Its central process can be represented as a question, retrieval or search actions, knowledge items, intermediate reasoning, and an answer. This representation is deliberately simple, but it exposes the decisions that matter in a primary system: how a query is formed, what source is searched, what retrieved material is retained, how it is used in reasoning, and when the system stops. The paper emphasizes that agentic systems may iterate this process, so the number and order of searches are part of the behavior rather than incidental implementation detail. When reading a method, draw this sequence and mark where information can be corrected or lost. That makes it easier to distinguish a retrieval failure from a reasoning failure, instead of attributing every wrong answer to the language model.
