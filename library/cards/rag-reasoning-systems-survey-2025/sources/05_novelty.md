Its contribution is a unified reasoning-search perspective instead of treating RAG and reasoning as independent tools. The three categories clarify two opposite causal directions: reasoning can improve retrieval, while retrieval can improve reasoning. They also identify a third design in which search and thought are interleaved rather than completed in separate stages. This matters because the same final answer can be produced by very different information flows. The survey's scope includes emerging agentic systems, which makes it useful for comparing static one-shot RAG with iterative evidence gathering. Its novelty is therefore organizational and diagnostic: it gives readers a way to state what a system changes, rather than crediting every improvement to an undifferentiated combination of retrieval and reasoning.
