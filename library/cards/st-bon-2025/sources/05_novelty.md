Prior BoN systems usually pay for complete candidates and then rank them with answer agreement or a learned reward model; efficient variants often score partial text with an external verifier. ST-BoN changes the selection boundary: it performs white-box, reward-model-free comparison of partial trajectories in latent space, then truncates losing paths before completion. The buffer-window vote also makes the decision a short sequence of selector states rather than a single early score.

The paper does not introduce repeated sampling, self-consistency, latent representations, or CoE itself. Its specific contribution is combining first-divergence timing, CoE-distance ranking, and buffered early selection into a BoN decoding rule with an explicit cost analysis. For this track, the direction signal is that a rollout record may need non-textual selector evidence. Recording only prompt and final answer would erase the method's central mechanism; a reusable trace must retain hidden-state feature definitions, score direction, window decisions, stopped candidates, and realized compute. Reported benchmark gains are a quality signal for the selector under the tested setup, not proof that any public data release has complete provenance or reliable labels.
