ST-BoN's contribution is an early selector that replaces completed-answer voting or an external reward model with consistency among the model's own latent trajectories. Parallel samples are decoded until step c, the first point at which no two sampled token prefixes remain identical. For each prefix, CoE summarizes how sentence-level hidden representations change across layers; squared differences between CoE features define pairwise distance, and the candidate with the smallest mean distance to the others is the current estimate.

Because one estimate at c is noisy, decoding continues for a buffer of tau steps. The method recomputes the best candidate at each step and selects the candidate that wins most often over the window; all other paths can then be truncated while the selected path continues. The feedback contract is thus an internal, algorithmic trajectory-value signal: lower latent distance and more window wins indicate selection, but neither signal is a proof of correctness.

The closest baselines in the paper are Full-BoN with majority or semantic consistency and Full-BoN with a process or preference reward model. ST-BoN retains parallel sampling and selection, but moves selection before completion and removes the learned reward model. This places it in Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data because the selector state, sampling budget, stopped candidates, and compute record are as important as the final answer.
