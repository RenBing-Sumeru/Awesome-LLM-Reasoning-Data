The paper reports that ST-BoN reduces dynamic GPU-memory use by more than 80% and inference latency by about 50% in the studied settings. On the four objective tasks, it reports matching Full-BoN accuracy at roughly 70-80% lower computed cost after increasing N within the saved budget, and gains of about 3-4 accuracy points under matched cost. On subjective tasks, the reported advantage is smaller and depends on the comparator. Component ablations on MATH and MMLU show that CoE plus the buffer window selects correct continuations more often than random estimation, while text-string or semantic prefix distances are near the random baseline in those tests.

These are method and selector results, not evidence that a released trace dataset is complete or high quality. The official paper, OpenReview record, and repository support the algorithm and executable implementation. The repository also documents benchmark inputs and a structured result format, but its public documentation does not demonstrate a complete release of all N prefixes, per-step CoE features, candidate scores, truncation decisions, random seeds, or the full experimental manifest. Benchmark accuracy and cost curves therefore evaluate ST-BoN; they do not substitute for a trace-level audit.
