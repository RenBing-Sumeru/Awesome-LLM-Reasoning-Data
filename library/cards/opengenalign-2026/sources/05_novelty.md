Generic preference collections are designed around chat, safety, or short-form helpfulness, and reward benchmarks can therefore miss long-context failure modes. OpenGenAlign changes the feedback object by fixing four long-generation dimensions and collecting multi-judge preferences across QA, data-to-text, and summarization. Using a Bradley-Terry reward model or PPO is not new; the contribution is a domain-targeted preference and benchmark surface with a human-agreement audit and cross-domain mixture test.
