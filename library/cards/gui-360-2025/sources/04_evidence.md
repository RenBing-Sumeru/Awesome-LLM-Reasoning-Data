Release scale is supported by the paper and the official dataset tree. Train contains 13,750 successful trajectories and 105,368 steps; GUI-360-Bench/test contains 3,439 and 26,284. Together these are 17,189 successful trajectories and 131,652 steps. The paper reports another 62,170 failed trajectories and 1,093,525 steps, physically represented by a separate `fail/` tree. Adding the reported counts gives 79,359 trajectories and 1,225,177 steps, while Hugging Face reports approximately 574 GB. The complete corpus was not downloaded and independently recounted for this Card.

For grounding, Table 7 reports Qwen2.5-VL-7B improving from 35.78% zero-shot to 82.30% after SFT, and UI-TARS-1.5-7B from 62.27% to 82.49% after SFT. For action prediction, Tables 9–10 report Qwen2.5-VL-7B visual-only step success rising from 17.52% to 50.08%, while visual+A11y rises from 14.18% to 25.78%. The latter remains far below visual-only SFT, so accessibility context is not uniformly beneficial under this setup.

For screen parsing, Table 8 reports OmniParser-v2 at overall F1 0.408 and mean IoU 0.735, compared with the strongest listed general model, o3, at F1 0.128 and mean IoU 0.578. The released code uses greedy one-to-one IoU matching at IoU ≥ 0.5 plus text similarity. Its separate generic per-sample `success` flag at recall ≥ 0.1 is not the paper's macro F1/IoU result and is not task completion.

EvaAgent's only reported calibration is 86% agreement with human annotators on 100 randomly sampled trajectories. The paper does not provide the human count or qualifications, inter-annotator agreement, confusion matrix, false-positive/false-negative rates, or adversarial robustness. All benchmark numbers above are author-reported static results without uncertainty or independent reproduction. They show that the released SFT views and scorers produce measurable changes; they do not certify record-level data quality, environment replay, or an RL reward contract.
