The prior-work baseline is agent-environment, tool-use, and benchmark-harness papers in the same atlas category. The change introduced by MCP-Atlas: An Agentic Benchmark for Evaluating Tool Use in Large Language Models is the particular task object and feedback contract: natural-language user goal, available MCP servers and tools, model tool-selection or tool-execution trajectory, final answer, and benchmark score scored by official harness scoring; exact per-task mix of deterministic checks, execution checks, and judge logic should be pinned from the repository revision. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00933; code: https://github.com/lastmile-ai/mcp-atlas), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
