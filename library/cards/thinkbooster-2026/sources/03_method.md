**Inputs, backends, and step extraction.** The paper evaluates MATH-500, OlympiadBench, GaoKao23EN, AIME 2024, AIME 2025, GPQA-Diamond, HumanEval+, MBPP+, and KernelBench. The toolkit can use Hugging Face Transformers, local vLLM, a vLLM service, OpenRouter, OpenAI-compatible endpoints, and compatible providers. Structured chain of thought can be induced by a system-level instruction. Native unstructured thinking is split after generation by `ThinkingMarkerDetector`, using linguistic markers and normalized sentence boundaries; the paper identifies reliable native-step extraction as unresolved.

**Generation strategies.** Offline Best-of-N and self-consistency first produce complete trajectories, then rerank them or vote on extracted answers. DeepConf offline also scores finished samples. Beam search, extended thinking, MUR, DeepConf online, phi-decoding, and uncertainty CoT can intervene during generation through candidate expansion, continuation, pruning, or confidence signals. Access requirements differ: Best-of-N and majority voting are offline, black-box, and need no prefill; beam search and extended thinking are online and black-box but require prefill; MUR, DeepConf online, phi-decoding, and uncertainty CoT are online, white-box, and require prefill; DeepConf offline is offline and white-box without prefill.

**Scorers and aggregation.** The default PRM config uses `Qwen/Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B`, with a 4,000-token scorer input limit; paper runs place it on a separate GPU. Uncertainty scorers compute entropy, perplexity, sequence probability, or probability differential from token log-probabilities. The current LLM-critic config defaults to value prompting, three judgment samples, temperature 0.7, and at most 300 tokens, but requires the user to select a critic model. Aggregation function and scoring window are grid-searched independently for each scorer–strategy pair. Offline Best-of-N with PRM uses a product over all steps; other offline scorers use their best aggregation with window 5; beam-search reports include mean/min with window 5 and ReProbe mean over all steps; MUR uses defaults.

**Model and decoding budgets.** Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct uses temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20, and at most 4,096 tokens. Qwen3-8B uses 0.6, 0.95, 20, and at most 32,768 tokens. GPT-OSS-120B uses 0.6, 0.95, 20, and at most 65,536 tokens. Qwen2.5 beam search has width 5, 8 candidates, and 30 maximum steps; Qwen3 beam search has width 3, 5 candidates, and 250 steps. Offline Best-of-N and self-consistency use 8 samples. MUR uses 8 candidates per step, momentum 0.9, and 50 or 250 maximum steps for Qwen2.5 or Qwen3. Qwen3 extended thinking permits at most 3 continuations. Results are averaged over three seeds where applicable.

**Evaluation contracts.** Math and science primarily use exact match, with optional LLM-as-judge when answer parsing is imperfect. HumanEval+ and MBPP+ use EvalPlus base and expanded tests. KernelBench checks syntax, compilation, and numerical correctness. These terminal checks establish benchmark outcomes, not step-level truth. The paper uses the math-trained PRM as a proxy on coding tasks because no coding PRM is released.

**Accounting and persistence.** Generator and PRM forward cost is approximated with \(2NT\) FLOPs per pass and summed as theoretical TFLOPs; shared prompt context is counted once and uncertainty overhead is treated as negligible. Wall-clock is captured in request records, but the paper does not compare fixed-serving latency. Hydra composes model, generation, strategy, scorer, and dataset configs, writes local output runs, supports resume, and optionally logs to W&B. The public tree tracks many input configs but not the resolved paper-run outputs, W&B export, or an immutable config-hash manifest.
