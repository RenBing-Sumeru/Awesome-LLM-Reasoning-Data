ThinkBooster's novelty is system-level unification rather than a new training objective or dataset. Best-of-N, voting, beam/Tree-of-Thought search, continuation-based extended thinking, dynamic exploration, confidence-guided decoding, and online/offline scoring are placed behind a shared configuration and execution interface. Table 1 makes model-access assumptions first-class: offline versus online, black-box versus white-box, and prefill availability determine whether a method is deployable on a given backend.

The scorer abstraction is also important. PRM, uncertainty/confidence, LLM-critic, and ReProbe-style signals can occupy the same selection role while having very different epistemic meanings. A PRM is a learned process proxy, an uncertainty measure derives from model probabilities, an LLM critic is a judgment procedure, and a probe depends on internal representations. Keeping these families separate from final benchmark graders prevents a runtime preference signal from being silently promoted to correctness.

For reasoning-data curation, the strongest contribution is the common trace and debugger contract. A single run can connect prompts, complete and partial candidates, step boundaries, scorer direction, pruning or selection, extracted answers, token/TFLOP/latency accounting, and configuration. The timeline and tree views make the lineage of a selected answer and its discarded siblings inspectable. This is more useful for auditing search behavior than a final answer and aggregate accuracy alone.

The release also provides a sharp case study in the difference between **trace-generation capability** and **trace publication**. Two cached demonstrations show that rich records can be serialized, and local outputs plus optional W&B can persist user-generated experiments. However, the public artifacts do not contain the corresponding paper-run corpus. The toolkit defines how a strong release could be captured; it does not itself deliver that historical release.

Novelty should not be expanded beyond this boundary. The work does not introduce human step supervision, establish ReProbe as reproducibly implemented in the audited tree, release a scored candidate dataset, prove cross-domain selector superiority, or show that these traces improve SFT, preference learning, reward modeling, or RLVR.
