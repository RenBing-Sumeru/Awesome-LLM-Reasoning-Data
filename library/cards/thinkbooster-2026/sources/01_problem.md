Test-time scaling papers often report a final accuracy–compute curve while hiding the runtime records that produced it: sampled answers, partial continuations, scorer calls, pruning decisions, selected indices, and resolved configurations. That makes it difficult to determine whether a gain came from generating more candidates, changing the search rule, using a stronger selector, or spending a larger token budget. It also prevents a curator from distinguishing an executable trace generator from an actual reusable trace release.

ThinkBooster addresses the systems side of this problem with one interface for nine reasoning strategies: Best-of-N, majority voting/self-consistency, beam search/Tree-of-Thought, extended thinking, MUR/dynamic exploration, DeepConf online, DeepConf offline, phi-decoding, and uncertainty CoT. The strategies differ in whether they score finished trajectories or intervene during generation, whether they need black-box or white-box model access, and whether they require prefill continuation. A common toolkit makes those differences explicit enough to compare, configure, and debug.

The relevant data object is a runtime reasoning record rather than a new training dataset. Depending on the strategy, it can contain a prompt, full trajectories, post-hoc step boundaries, candidate texts or tree nodes, PRM/confidence/critic/probe scores, kept or pruned status, a selected trajectory and final answer, token and theoretical TFLOP counts, latency, and strategy/scorer configuration. Correctness is then evaluated by benchmark-specific exact match, optional LLM judgment, EvalPlus tests, or KernelBench syntax, compilation, and numerical checks.

This work therefore belongs in **Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data**: the analytical unit is the candidate pool or search process under a stated inference budget. The paper performs inference-time generation, selection, debugging, and evaluation. It does not train a policy, release SFT or preference examples, fit a reward model, or run RLVR.

The Card reaches L4 because the final ACL paper, appendix, official repository at the audited commit, MIT license, PyPI package, Hugging Face surfaces, configs, strategy/scorer/evaluator code, API schema, and debugger cache support a detailed bilingual account. L4 does not imply a complete trace release. The public repository persists only two generated debugger demonstrations; the historical paper-run prompts, candidates, scores, selections, resolved configs, and result exports are absent.
