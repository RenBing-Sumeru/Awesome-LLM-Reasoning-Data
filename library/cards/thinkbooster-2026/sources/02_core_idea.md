The central contribution is a common execution and observation layer over heterogeneous test-time reasoning methods. ThinkBooster separates a reasoning generator, a strategy, a scorer when required, a benchmark evaluator, and an artifact/debug layer. This allows the same prompt and model backend to be routed through offline sampling, voting, stepwise search, continuation, or confidence-guided decoding while retaining a comparable record of candidates, decisions, cost, and outcome.

The nine strategies cover distinct control patterns. Best-of-N generates complete candidates and reranks them. Self-consistency votes over extracted answers. Beam search/Tree-of-Thought repeatedly expands and prunes partial paths. Extended thinking asks for bounded continuations. MUR/dynamic exploration adjusts stepwise exploration. DeepConf has online and offline variants. Phi-decoding and uncertainty CoT use internal confidence information while generation proceeds. Table 1 further distinguishes offline versus online operation, black-box versus white-box access, and whether prefill is required; these are deployment contracts, not cosmetic labels.

Four scorer families supply selection evidence. PRMs attach process rewards to steps or trajectories. Uncertainty/confidence scorers derive entropy, perplexity, sequence probability, or probability differential from token probabilities. An LLM critic uses value or vote prompting and therefore supplies judgment rather than a correctness proof. ReProbes are reported as white-box internal-state scorers. Final benchmark graders remain separate from these search-time selectors: a path can receive a high selector score and still fail exact-answer or executable tests.

The trace contract is unusually inspectable. Strategy results can expose the selected trajectory, extracted answer, step entries, candidate pools, scores and signal directions, selected index, token statistics, latency, completion status, and strategy/scorer metadata. The visual debugger converts these into timeline events and a trajectory tree showing generation, pruning, reranking, selected paths, and pruned siblings. Run records can include provider/model identity, budget and used budget, token totals, latency, config snapshots, confidence, and a selection reason.

That inspectability exists at runtime, but release completeness is much narrower. The tracked `cached_examples.json` contains two generated math examples, each at budget 8 with five runs: baseline, self-consistency, online Best-of-N plus PRM, beam search plus PRM, and offline Best-of-N plus PRM. The examples were generated with `anthropic/claude-sonnet-4` through OpenRouter and include prompts, trajectories, candidates, scores, configs, token counts, and latencies. They are a product demo cache, not the Qwen/GPT-OSS paper experiment corpus.
