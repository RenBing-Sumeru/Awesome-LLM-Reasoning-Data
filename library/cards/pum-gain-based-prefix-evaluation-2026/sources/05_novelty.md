Prior methods rely on final answers, generic step labels, or a single LLM judge, leaving a mismatch between feedback and the target. This work centers supervision on mathematical reasoning prefixes, uses estimating the solve-rate gain of lightweight student models with and without each prefix to generate and filter records, and releases PUM-MATH. The novelty lies in the data object and acceptance rule rather than standard training algorithms. Its value depends on whether prefix gain transfers reliably.
