Real-world numerical claims often combine amounts, dates, units, and several pieces of evidence. A language model can begin on a sound path yet drift when it overinterprets a different currency, an auxiliary detail, or apparently conflicting evidence. One-shot fact checking therefore fails not only because retrieval is noisy, but because a single reasoning trajectory may backtrack away from a correct interpretation.

The paper asks whether multiple inference-time reasoning paths can reduce this drift, and whether every claim deserves the same expensive expansion. It seeks a policy that samples and verifies alternatives for difficult claims while avoiding waste on straightforward ones. The target is evidence-grounded verdict selection, where extra computation is guided by both path quality and predicted claim complexity.
