The official paper reports that verifier-guided multi-path scaling mitigates the identified reasoning-drift failure on complex numerical claims. Its adaptive policy improves performance by 18.8 percent on QuanTemp and 21.27 percent on ClaimDecomp relative to single-shot methods, while reporting 1.8 times the computational efficiency of standard uniform test-time scaling.

The evidence is specific to evidence-based claim verification rather than general open-ended reasoning. It demonstrates that difficulty-sensitive allocation matters when claims vary in numerical and compositional complexity. A reviewer should inspect the retrieval quality, the verifier’s calibration, the complexity threshold, and the number of paths, because an apparent budget gain can move between these components rather than arise from one mechanism alone.
