Inference begins with the utilizer answering the question once without a sticker. At each following iteration, the extractor receives the preceding trace and question and produces a structured sticker; the modifier checks it and emits a corrected version; the utilizer then receives the corrected sticker, original question, and preceding answer to produce a new trace and answer. After the fixed number of iterations, the system aggregates the collected answers by majority vote. The record passed across rounds is therefore trace → sticker → revised sticker → new trace, rather than a growing raw transcript alone.

The authors initialize all three roles with distillation. They use solvable OpenThoughts mathematics problems, DeepSeek-R1 traces, and o3-mini-produced stickers to construct role-specific examples; for modifier and utilizer training, they simulate correction cases and retain only trajectories whose final reasoning is completely correct. A subsequent self-improvement stage lets the initialized framework generate iterative trajectories, then filters them for solution validity and correction significance before further fine-tuning. The paper reports roughly one thousand distilled examples for the initial stage and controls the retained error-to-correct versus correct-to-correct transitions at a one-to-two ratio in the later data.
