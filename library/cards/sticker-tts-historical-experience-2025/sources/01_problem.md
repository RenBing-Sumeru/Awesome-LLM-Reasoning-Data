Independent sampling is a simple way to spend more inference computation on difficult reasoning problems, but it often produces many similar chains of thought. Earlier attempts can contain a correct sub-result, a useful constraint, or the precise place where an approach failed, yet ordinary sampling discards that experience and asks the model to begin again. Passing an entire long history into the next prompt is not a clean solution either: salient facts can be buried in verbose traces, while a bare final answer gives too little material for revision.

The paper asks how a reasoning system can make accumulated attempts useful without making every later round read an uncontrolled transcript. Its target is a repeatable inference loop in which a compact record of conditions, strategies, and detected mistakes transfers information from one attempt to the next, so additional computation is directed toward correcting or extending prior work rather than generating unrelated repetitions.
