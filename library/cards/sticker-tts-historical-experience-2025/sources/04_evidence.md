The official paper evaluates the framework on AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and OlymMATH, comparing it with self-consistency, parallel and early-exit variants, and reported reinforcement-learning reasoning systems under comparable inference budgets. With a 7B model, the introduction reports a 12.42% relative improvement over self-consistency on AIME 2025; it also reports a 9.79% relative improvement over Skywork-OR1 on OlymMATH and an 18.75% relative improvement over Light-R1 on AIME 2025 in the stated multi-round comparison.

The paper makes the cost claim concrete by counting generated long chain-of-thought solutions: its N iterations cost about as much as 2N long solutions because the extractor output is short, while the modifier and utilizer are the main long-generation components. At N=10, it reports a cost comparable to Cons@20 and says this setting outperforms most reported baseline Cons@64 results. Ablations remove the extractor, modifier, or both and reduce the reported AIME Cons@20 scores, which supports the claim that compression and correction each contribute rather than the gains coming only from repeated sampling.
