Many performance benchmarks reveal target functions or evaluate micro-kernels, omitting profiling, call-chain understanding, bottleneck localization, and regression-test selection required of real performance engineers. Simply checking whether code becomes faster can also reward tiny satisficing improvements without measuring proximity to expert optimizations.

SWE-fficiency defines pass-to-pass repository performance tasks. Agents receive a complete codebase and a slow workload without a repair location and must investigate, edit, and preserve relevant unit tests. Its 498 tasks come from nine major Python scientific-computing and HPC repositories. Expert pull-request speedups provide baselines, and Speedup Ratio measures how much of the expert-achievable gain an agent recovers.
