Existing Lean mathematics benchmarks are often small, concentrated on high-school competition problems, and expensive to expand because faithful formalization of natural-language questions requires expert labor. Filtering autoformalized statements only by compilability is insufficient: an incorrect statement may still compile and even be easy for a prover, introducing missing semantics, reduced difficulty, or unsatisfiable instances into evaluation.

FormalMATH asks how a large Lean 4 benchmark spanning high-school through undergraduate mathematics can be built at lower annotation cost and used to diagnose theorem provers. It combines specialized formalization models, multi-model semantic judgment, negation-based disproof, and final human verification in a human-in-the-loop data-production pipeline.
