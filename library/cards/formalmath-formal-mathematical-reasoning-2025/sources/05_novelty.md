Earlier datasets such as miniF2F rely primarily on small collections of manually formalized competition problems, yielding quality but scaling slowly. Pure LLM autoformalization can scale faster but often produces compilable yet unfaithful statements. FormalMATH changes the workflow by placing multi-model semantic voting and attempts to prove negated statements before expert review, so experts focus on candidates that pass automated gates.

Its novelty is neither the first Lean mathematics benchmark nor the first use of LLM autoformalization. It combines cross-domain scale with executable semantic-disproof filtering and systematically reports domain bias and the counterproductive effect of informal guidance. The principal object of innovation is statement fidelity in benchmark construction, not a new prover architecture.
