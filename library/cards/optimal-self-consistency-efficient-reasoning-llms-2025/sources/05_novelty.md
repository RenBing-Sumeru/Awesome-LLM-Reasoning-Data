Vanilla SC applies an equal sample count to every question; ASC and ESC stop individual questions with threshold or window heuristics; fixed allocation assumes prior difficulty information. Blend-ASC instead treats a batch of questions as a joint budget-allocation problem and interpolates between two rank signals during the run. This makes the output budget exact and removes the user-set stopping threshold while retaining a transition from strong low-sample behavior to an asymptotically motivated confidence rule.

The broader direction signal is the explicit elevation of allocation history and answer-count state into first-class test-time reasoning data. The theoretical analysis also separates per-question convergence from dataset-level scaling and aligned from misaligned items. The paper does not invent self-consistency, plurality voting, ASC, PPR-1v1, or beta confidence; it combines and analyzes them. Its benchmark improvements are evidence about allocation efficiency, not a quality certificate for the underlying reasoning traces. Reuse should compare against ESC/ASC under identical answer normalization and should audit whether the aligned-only evaluation changes the operational decision boundary.
