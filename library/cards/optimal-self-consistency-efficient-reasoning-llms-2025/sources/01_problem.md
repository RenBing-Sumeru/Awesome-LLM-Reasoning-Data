Self-consistency (SC) samples several chain-of-thought responses for one question and returns the most frequent normalized answer. Its decision boundary is the empirical mode, not an external correctness check: if the model's true answer is not the mode of its response distribution, additional samples converge toward the wrong answer. Uniform SC also spends the same number of calls on questions whose answer margins differ substantially.

The ICML 2026 paper studies how SC error scales per question and across datasets, then asks how a fixed global sample budget should be distributed. The relevant data object for this track is therefore not a new training corpus but a test-time collection of question-indexed response traces, final-answer counts, top-two margins, confidence scores, and allocation decisions. The official arXiv v2 paper is the primary source; no official code, cached generations, or allocation log was confirmed, so the card is L4-ready as a documented recipe and scaling study rather than as a reusable trace release.
