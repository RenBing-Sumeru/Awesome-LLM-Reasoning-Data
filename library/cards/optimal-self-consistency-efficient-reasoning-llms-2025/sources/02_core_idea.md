The paper recasts SC as empirical mode estimation and plurality voting. For each question, answer counts estimate the response distribution; the gap between the two most probable answers controls how quickly the empirical mode stabilizes. Across questions with a heavy concentration of small margins, the paper derives power-law dataset behavior and contrasts uniform, fixed-allocation, and dynamic-allocation sampling.

Blend-ASC is the proposed dynamic allocator. At step t of a total budget T, it ranks every question using a linear blend of Adaptive Self-Consistency (ASC) confidence and PPR-1v1 confidence, with ASC dominating early and the asymptotically motivated PPR score dominating later. The next sample goes to the lowest-confidence question, except that questions already receiving more than 16 times the current average are excluded. After the budget is exhausted, each question's empirical mode is returned. Feedback is entirely count based; no reward model or answer-correctness verifier enters online selection. Compared with vanilla SC and heuristic ASC/ESC, the distinguishing contribution is a budget-exact, hyperparameter-free allocation rule linked to mode-estimation theory.
