The main experiments simulate SC from 100 cached generations per question, so they are not a direct replay of adaptive online inference and cannot capture distribution drift, serving latency, or batching effects beyond the studied approximations. Restricting analysis to aligned questions removes exactly the cases where majority voting converges to a wrong answer. Multiple-choice benchmarks show weaker and less predictable scaling.

Answer normalization, benchmark revisions, prompt templates, random seeds, cache contents, and the exact aligned-question manifest are not released. PPR confidence depends on an estimated unique-answer count, and the 16-times-average cap is a practical heuristic. The global allocator also assumes questions can wait and share a budget, which may conflict with per-request latency. No code, raw generations, allocation logs, license, split audit, or contamination analysis was confirmed. Therefore the recipe is auditable from the paper but not yet reproducible as a released trace dataset.
