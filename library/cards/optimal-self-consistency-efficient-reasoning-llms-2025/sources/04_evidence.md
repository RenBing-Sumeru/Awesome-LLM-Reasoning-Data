The paper reports experiments across three model configurations and four benchmarks, with the main Blend-ASC comparison covering six model-dataset pairs on GSM8K, MATH, and GPQA-Diamond. In arXiv v2, Blend-ASC requires 4.8 times fewer samples than vanilla SC on average under the reported SC@64/128 matching protocol; Table 1 also shows that the factor varies materially by model, benchmark, temperature, and target. The allocator's overhead is reported as 1.8 seconds for GPQA-Diamond at a 128-sample budget, excluding generation cost.

These results support a sample-allocation claim under the paper's empirical-distribution simulation and aligned-question filter. They do not demonstrate that consensus answers are correct, that the 100 generations represent a stable production distribution, or that the stored traces are high-quality training data. The paper itself shows weaker scaling on misaligned questions and warns that multiple-choice behavior is not reliably predictable. No released run manifest was available for independent replay.
