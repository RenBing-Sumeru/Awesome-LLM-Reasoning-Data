Many agent tasks are pipelines rather than a single answer: a system may profile data, make charts, extract insights, and write a report. Their final quality can be valuable but not mechanically verifiable. Repeatedly refining the final report with an LLM judge risks propagating an early mistaken judgment through every later revision, while spending the same branch budget at all stages lets weak intermediate artifacts consume downstream computation.

The paper asks how to scale an open-ended multi-agent workflow when a reliable terminal reward is unavailable. It needs to decide where in the process to spend a fixed inference budget and how to use imperfect judges without allowing them to steer the entire pipeline repeatedly. The desired policy should remove poor local candidates early, preserve promising alternatives, and improve the final outcome under matched compute rather than by simply adding more agent calls.
