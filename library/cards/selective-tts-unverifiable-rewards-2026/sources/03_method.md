The implemented pipeline has four contracts: data profiling turns raw tables into compact metadata and analysis directions; visualization proposes, executes, and repairs plotting code; insight generation writes chart-grounded interpretations; and verification scores the artifacts. Candidate branches are generated at these stages, then stage-specific LLM evaluators rank them. A configurable pruning ratio removes low-scoring branches before the next stage, while total LLM calls are held comparable to the baseline.

The authors first compare judges against human experts and select a harsh judge with Kendall’s tau of 0.55 for the main scaling experiments. They then test the full process-level policy on five public tabular datasets, alongside random pruning and a final-stage-only variant. Human evaluation assesses report quality, and robustness analysis varies pruning ratios, branching conditions, compute budgets, and an alternative dataset to distinguish early local selection from merely choosing the best final report.
