The distinctive move is to treat test-time scaling as process refinement rather than temporal self-refinement. Earlier approaches often spend more computation by revising one output or sampling final answers. This work distributes the same budget across different pipeline stages and uses a different judge where the relevant evidence is available, so the system can prevent a poor chart or profile from becoming an expensive foundation for later reasoning.

It also addresses a boundary case for verifier-guided scaling: the final outcome is intentionally not programmatically verifiable. Rather than claiming the judge is perfect, the paper measures human alignment and constrains each judge to local pruning. That design connects reward uncertainty to a concrete compute policy and is useful for agent tasks whose final objectives are subjective, multi-part, or only partially observable.
