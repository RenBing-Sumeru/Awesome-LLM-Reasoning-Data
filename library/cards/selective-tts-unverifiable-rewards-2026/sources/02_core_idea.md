Selective TTS moves the unit of scaling from time to process stage. Instead of asking one agent to revise the same final answer over and over, it branches a data-analysis workflow and uses a dedicated local judge after each important stage. Weak profiles, chart choices, or insights are pruned before they become inputs to later agents, so later computation is concentrated on candidates that have survived the appropriate local quality test.

The paper deliberately treats a judge as a local allocation device rather than a globally trusted reward. A human-aligned harsh judge is selected for the visual-insight setting, but its influence is confined to the stage whose artifact it can inspect. This limits accumulated judge drift and makes the policy interpretable: the pruning ratio specifies how aggressively the fixed call budget moves from wide early exploration toward fewer, stronger downstream branches.
