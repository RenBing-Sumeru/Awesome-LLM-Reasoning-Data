The authors state two primary limitations: LLM extraction can hallucinate and therefore needs human verification for low-confidence cases, and the graph is bounded by documentary transparency, so omitted or concealed sources cannot be recovered. The public materials leave several additional reuse risks.

**Graph and feedback limits.** There is no independently labeled edge set, precision/recall result, confidence calibration, expert-routing threshold, reviewer count, adjudication rule, or agreement statistic. Appendix A.3 says vague descriptions can be interpreted contextually or assigned the default Direct Inclusion/Subset relation. A false-positive edge can create a spurious contamination path; a false-negative edge can make a derived dataset appear to be a leaf. These are curator inferences from the feedback contract, not author-reported error rates.

**Record and release limits.** Dataset-level edges do not preserve the origin of each normalized instruction. The reported 570K corpus, per-row source IDs, transformation logs, accepted/rejected manifests, split, checksums, and corpus license were not found in the repository. The repository itself has no LICENSE file, tag, or release. Consequently, the code recipe can be inspected, but direct training reuse of the reported corpus is blocked pending an immutable artifact and rights review.

**Audit limits.** Exact triplet and exact question matching have high interpretability but miss paraphrases, translations, reordered fields, synthetic rewrites, and answer-only contamination. MinHash reduces near-duplicate instructions under one configuration but does not prove benchmark decontamination. Conversely, a documented ancestor does not prove that a particular benchmark row survived every downstream transformation.

**Evaluation limits.** Vendi Score and Centroid Distance depend on Qwen3-Embedding-8B and capture dispersion rather than correctness, difficulty, safety, or training utility. The comparison does not isolate source selection from normalization and deduplication, and no SFT experiment tests whether the 570K corpus improves a model. Benchmark performance therefore cannot be used here as proof of data quality; none is reported for the constructed corpus.

**Version limits.** The inspected repository postdates the paper experiment and defaults to `gpt-5.4`, whereas Appendix A uses GPT-5.1, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and Gemini-2.5-Pro by role. Its checked-in JSONL row counts also differ from 430/971. Without a tagged paper snapshot, API/model behavior, prompts, web evidence, and graph outputs can drift.
