The official source is the ACL 2026 long-paper record (July 2026, pages 9606-9625), cross-checked against arXiv v1, the accepted PDF, and the author repository. The paper addresses a specific blind spot in post-training data construction: datasets are repeatedly copied, filtered, distilled, reformulated, or merged, but their ancestry is scattered across dataset cards, papers, repositories, and blogs. A pairwise content scan may find duplicate rows, yet it does not by itself explain which upstream source introduced an overlap or how it propagated through later mixtures.

This belongs in `data_construction_open_release_recipes` because it treats provenance as an operational input to source selection, deduplication, and contamination auditing. Its boundary is dataset-to-dataset lineage inferred from public documentation; it is not record-level training-data attribution and cannot recover a source that creators omit or conceal.

Two data objects must remain separate. The first is a directed graph in which a dataset node is connected to an upstream source by an edge carrying a derivation relation, confidence, and textual evidence. The second is a reported instruction corpus: 31 selected upstream leaf datasets are normalized to Alpaca-style instruction/input/output records and deduplicated from about 8.7M rows to 570K instructions. The official repository releases graph and dataset-metadata JSONL files, but the inspected revision does not release the 570K corpus or per-row paths back through the graph. That release gap is part of the Card's inclusion boundary, not a detail to infer from the diversity results. (Paper Sections 3-5; Appendix A and E; official repository at revision `681db3ab13eaa4391025d6c4b86ed5b58b73988e`.)
