The useful contribution for data curation is the end-to-end linkage between source mining, instruction selection, generated tasks, and reward routing. The report does not stop at saying that scientific data and RL were used: it describes page-level parser escalation, domain-level agent decisions, classifier distillation from strong-LLM labels, category/difficulty stratification, best-of-N response selection, pass-rate filtering, and the mapping of task classes to CompassVerifier, rules, environment feedback, or POLAR-7B.

At the optimizer layer, it also documents a concrete MoE failure boundary. The authors report that token-level importance-ratio clipping is unreliable when FP8 inference and training activate different experts, so their OREAL variant removes the token-level reward model and adds KL-Cov entropy control. This connects data filtering and reward granularity to the stability of the training system rather than treating them as interchangeable implementation details.

The ingredients are not all new: Intern-S1 starts from Qwen3 and InternViT, reuses InternVL3/SOPHIA data pipelines and several public RL datasets, and relies on existing CompassVerifier and POLAR-7B components. The direction signal is their integration across scientific CPT, offline instruction selection, and more-than-1,000-task online RL—not a claim that the training data itself is open or independently validated.
