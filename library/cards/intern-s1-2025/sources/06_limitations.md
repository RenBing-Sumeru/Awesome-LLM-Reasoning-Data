The CPT disclosure remains aggregate. The report does not publish a source manifest, per-source token counts, domain mixture weights, collection dates, page or document lineage, parser decisions, licenses, or retained/rejected records. It names private multimodal collections and anonymized user queries without disclosing governance, consent, or redistribution terms. Page-level deduplication is reported, but benchmark overlap and source-level contamination are not audited publicly.

Offline post-training omits the best-of-N value, response-generator identities, decoding parameters, score thresholds, exact record counts, and final mixture. Online RL reports more than 1,000 tasks, more than 100,000 generated candidates per task, and more than 20,000 retained mixed-RL samples, but supplies no task/sample inventory or mapping between tasks and verifiers. The apparent compression from candidates to retained records therefore cannot be independently reconstructed.

The reward layer is only partly auditable. CompassVerifier is intended to reduce rule-verifier false negatives, but no Intern-S1-specific calibration or false-positive/false-negative analysis is provided. Mixture-of-Rewards does not disclose per-task weights or scalar normalization. POLAR-7B, randomly selected reference-trajectory generators, and environmental feedback add learned or opaque dependencies. Hybrid filtering removes all-correct/all-wrong groups and problematic responses, but rejected samples are unavailable for bias analysis.

The released weights and Apache-2.0 repository make inference possible, not training reproduction. Complete Internbootcamp environments, training data, reward code, source rights, split manifests, and decontamination evidence remain unknown. Reported benchmark gains should not be read as proof of data quality.
