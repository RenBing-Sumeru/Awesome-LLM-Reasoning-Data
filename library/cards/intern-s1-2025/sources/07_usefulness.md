Within the frontier reports and data-disclosure ledger track, Intern-S1 is a detailed comparison case for scientific multimodal pipelines. It supports a structured audit: Which sources feed CPT? Which parsers or classifiers transform them? What is one offline instruction record? How is difficulty estimated? Which tasks are procedurally generated? Which reward component judges each task? Which records, environments, and verifier implementations are actually released?

The Card supports precise downstream labels. Intern-S1 uses demonstration-style SFT under the paper's "offline RL" terminology and mixed-verification online RLVR. Easy-to-verify tasks can receive CompassVerifier, rule, or environment rewards; open-ended dialogue receives POLAR-7B. The model weights are open, but the training records, complete task inventory, reward routing, and replayable environments are not. Builders can study the recipe and run the checkpoint, but cannot reconstruct the data pipeline from the checked artifacts.

The most reusable lesson is to separate recipe disclosure from artifact disclosure. Concrete counts, filters, and optimizer settings improve auditability, yet they do not make the source mixture, selected records, or verifier errors observable. That distinction is central to comparing frontier reports without turning benchmark results into claims about data quality.
