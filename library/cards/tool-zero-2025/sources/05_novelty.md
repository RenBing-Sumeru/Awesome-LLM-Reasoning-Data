The prior baseline is direct GRPO or SFT for function calling with a fixed rule reward. Tool Zero changes the feedback schedule: partial reference-token overlap supplies denser early exploration feedback, then a sigmoid shifts weight to exact AST equivalence as the policy becomes more competent. Its data-side construction combines parseability filtering, four xLAM multi-turn augmentations, and generic masking of function and parameter names.

The method's distinctive object is therefore not a new trajectory corpus but a coupling between offline function-calling completions, reference-based whole-completion verification, and a time-varying optimization objective. The reported BFCL ablation is a quality signal for that combined recipe under the authors' setup, not proof that every retained or augmented record is correct.

GRPO, critic-free group normalization, Qwen2.5 Base, ToolACE, xLAM, AST comparison, output-format rewards, tool-name masking, and MindSpeed-RL are inherited components. The paper does not introduce a live tool environment, learned verifier, process reward model, benchmark, or public checkpoint. MindSpeed-RL's generic GRPO code is supporting infrastructure rather than evidence of a released GG-GRPO implementation.

For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is that verifier strictness can be treated as a curriculum attached to a fixed completion-level data object. Reuse still requires the missing processed-record manifest, exact reward implementation, dataset revisions and splits, augmentation seeds, rejected/failure logs, benchmark pins, and resolution of version and score drift.
