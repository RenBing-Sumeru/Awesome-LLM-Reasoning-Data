The one-sentence contribution is: generate a constrained short CoT for each usable long s1K-1.1 trace, randomly mix the long and short demonstrations for SFT, and use an "appropriate" thinking instruction at inference. The rewriter prompt tells Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct to keep the original logical flow, steps, verification process, and final answer; remove redundant tokens; keep step-by-step form; retain alternative attempts; and add no new information. This is a generation constraint, not evidence that every rewrite is faithful. (Paper §2.2; Appendix A.1)

Its feedback contract is therefore weak and indirect. The rewriter observes the question and existing thought process and emits a new thought process. It has no reported answer checker, process verifier, human rater, reward, or accept/reject score. The only documented exclusion is context length. Training supervision is sequence-level imitation of the serialized response, while benchmark feedback is final-answer accuracy and output length after SFT. The contract can request preservation; it cannot observe whether a shortened step remains logically valid or whether the final answer changed.

The closest prior baseline is direct long-CoT SFT such as s1.1-32B, which uses the upstream long traces. The paper also compares free direct compression and a ThinkTwice-inspired rewrite to its structure-preserved rewrite, and compares long-only, short-only, and mixed training. What changes is neither the source question set nor a new verifier: it is the trace variant and its mixture with the original. This makes the work a construction recipe for rationale data rather than proof that short CoTs are intrinsically higher quality. (Paper §§3.1, 4.1-4.2)
