The authoritative source is arXiv:2505.03469v2, posted 21 May 2025. It studies a specific distillation problem: SFT on long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces can transfer reasoning capability but may also transfer verbose, redundant "overthinking" to the student. Rather than changing a reward or test-time search policy, the paper changes the training demonstrations: it derives shorter counterparts of existing long traces, mixes both forms, and prompts the trained student to use an intermediate "balanced" thinking mode at inference. (Paper §§1-2; Appendix A)

The data object is an instruction-to-reasoning-response record. The public `s1K-mix` viewer exposes `system`, `instruction`, `input`, and `output` strings; the response places a think-delimited reasoning trace before the answer. Long and short examples use detailed- and brief-thinking instructions. The paper describes the upstream `s1K-1.1` source as 1,000 DeepSeek-R1-distilled detailed trajectories with answers, and reports 984 rewritten short examples after 16 inputs exceed the context limit. The released mixture has one 1,984-row train split. (Paper §3.1, Appendix B; official dataset viewer)

This Card is in `instruction_demonstration_rationale_data`: the released evidence is a supervised rationale demonstration, not a multi-rollout record, a search tree, a learned reward, or a process-labeled verifier dataset. It is useful for auditing how trace length enters post-training, but its L4 content completeness must not be confused with release completeness: pair IDs, source manifest, rewrite logs, correctness checks, and decontamination remain unavailable.
