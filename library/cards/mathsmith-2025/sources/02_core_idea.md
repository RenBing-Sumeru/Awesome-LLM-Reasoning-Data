The central design choice is to train the data generator itself. GPT-4o first produces about 8,000 cold-start examples, each structured as a five-step construction rationale plus one focused problem. Qwen3-8B learns this format through LoRA SFT. GRPO then optimizes two variants: MathSmith-HC combines structural, reasoning-complexity, and answer-consistency signals; MathSmith-Hard removes the consistency term to emphasize the trace-length proxy.

Structural reward checks for the required rationale and problem XML-style spans and rewards exactly five rationale steps, with weights 0.7 and 0.3. Complexity is the average length of K teacher reasoning traces divided by a maximum-length constant. Consistency is 1 when a strict majority of the same teacher's extracted answers agree, otherwise 0; the reasoning reward weights complexity 0.7 and consistency 0.3. These are generator rewards, not proofs. Long traces may reflect difficulty, verbosity, or confusion, and same-teacher agreement can repeat the same error.

Concept provenance also supports a weakness-focused loop: generate a Practice Set, detect target-model errors, trace them back to concept sets, create aligned variants, fine-tune, and iterate until an accuracy threshold is reached.
