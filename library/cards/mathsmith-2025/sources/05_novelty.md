Earlier math synthesis commonly rewrites human questions, conditions on examples, or assigns prompt-level difficulty labels. MathSmith instead uses concept-explanation records as the seed object and trains a problem-generating policy with explicit scalar objectives. The combination of concept traceability, a construction rationale, generator-side GRPO, and released HC/Hard synthesizers is the distinctive contribution.

The HC/Hard split also makes a useful design contrast. HC trades some pressure for long teacher traces against a binary consistency check, while Hard removes that check. The weakness-focused pipeline then reuses sampled_concept as an operational provenance field for targeted generation. This is stronger than publishing only final questions because it exposes a route from source concepts to variants.

The novelty should not be overstated. Concept recombination, teacher solutions, self-consistency, and difficulty prompting predate this work. Nor does starting from PlanetMath guarantee contamination-free questions: semantic overlap, copied definitions, and shared benchmark concepts remain possible. The advance is a comparatively open, reinforced generator recipe and artifact family, not formal verification or a complete data lineage system.
