In the controlled 50K comparisons reported in Table 1, MathSmith-HC has the highest average score on the three hard benchmarks under the tested short- and long-CoT students. Under long-CoT training, the project page reports a hard-benchmark average of 71.8 for Qwen3-8B and 51.0 for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, corresponding to the authors' reported relative gains of 9.8% and 15.6%. These results support the tested training recipes; they do not by themselves validate every generated record.

The construction ablation in Table 3 is more directly informative. After generating 50K problems per variant, Available Ratio—defined as correctly formatted and teacher-solvable with a valid answer—is 71.50% for MathSmith-SFT, 84.92% for MathSmith-Hard, and 95.38% for MathSmith-HC. MathSmith-HC and MathSmith-Hard both report 36.6 average hard-benchmark accuracy in that table. The consistency term therefore improves this teacher-dependent availability measure without establishing independent correctness.

Figure 4 reports that MathSmith-HC remains ahead of NuminaMath-COT and OpenMathInstruct-2 as training volume grows from 50K to 200K on OlympiadBench. Figure 5 reports smaller or negative effects for 1.7B and 4B students but clearer gains for larger Qwen3 models. In the weakness-focused experiment, 1,000 Practice Set problems receive 32 Qwen3-30B-A3B completions, 923 remain after filtering, and concept-targeted variants outperform random variants in the authors' reported evaluation.

Release inspection also reveals a cautionary counterexample: the LongCoT viewer includes a solution trace that notices missing or inconsistent definitions, makes assumptions, and still guesses a boxed answer. Public availability makes such audits possible, but it also shows why teacher solvability and majority agreement are not equivalent to mathematical validity.
