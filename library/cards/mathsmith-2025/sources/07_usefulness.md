For the data-construction and open-release-recipes track, MathSmith is a useful end-to-end reference for separating source, generator, judge, and consumer objects. A reproduction can preserve PlanetMath page IDs and revisions; concept-explanation records; five-record sampling sets; policy candidates; rationale/problem spans; every structural, complexity, and consistency component; all K teacher solutions; extracted and normalized answers; rejection reasons; generator checkpoint; and the exact downstream subset.

The HC/Hard comparison is a ready-made verifier ablation. Curators can test whether consistency reduces malformed or ambiguous questions, whether trace length predicts independent human or symbolic difficulty, and where the two rewards select different records. Rejected candidates and non-majority teacher outputs are especially valuable audit data and should not be discarded.

The weakness-focused pipeline suggests a track-relevant use beyond generic scale-up: diagnose failures on a held-out Practice Set, trace each failure to its recorded concepts, generate controlled variants, and measure whether gains transfer without eroding easier capabilities. Before direct reuse, however, consumers should pin dataset revisions, create stable IDs and hashes, perform semantic decontamination and duplicate checks, run independent mathematical verification, and review upstream licenses. The open artifacts make those checks possible but do not replace them.
