Earlier test-time methods for agents generally allocate a larger budget to a single action: longer chain-of-thought, repeated decoding, or candidate selection. This work changes the unit of allocation to the environment interaction itself. A new step does not merely create another candidate answer; it can alter the agent’s evidence by navigating, querying, observing a result, or returning to a prior state. That makes the horizon a substantive compute-and-information choice.

The other contribution is to train this choice rather than treating it only as a prompt trick. The horizon curriculum confronts the optimization trade-off between insufficient exploration at short trajectories and noisy, delayed credit at long ones. The work does not establish that more interaction is always superior, nor does it replace per-step verification. Its reusable signal is a controlled comparison and training recipe for deciding when a system should buy more environment contact rather than more internal deliberation.
