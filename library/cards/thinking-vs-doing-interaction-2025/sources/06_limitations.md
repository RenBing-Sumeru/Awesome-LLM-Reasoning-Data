The results are tightly coupled to web environments whose observations and terminal evaluators make exploration useful. Longer horizons increase latency, browser or tool cost, and opportunities to drift; the project itself documents over-reliance on resets or search as a fallback and limited final-step self-verification. A check-again prompt only revisits a decision after completion, while the learned curriculum requires online collection, environment containers, and nontrivial compute. Claims about an optimal split between interaction and per-step thought should therefore be remeasured for each tool interface, task distribution, model, evaluator, and wall-clock budget.
