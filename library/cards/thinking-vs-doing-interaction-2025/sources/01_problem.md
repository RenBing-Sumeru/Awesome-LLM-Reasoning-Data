Most test-time scaling for language-model agents buys additional computation by asking the agent to think longer before it acts. A longer chain can reorganize information already in the context, but it cannot reveal a changed web page, inspect another listing, or recover from an action whose consequence is visible only after execution. For interactive tasks, treating more tokens at one decision point as the whole budget therefore misses the cost and value of acting again.

This paper asks how an agent should spend extra inference computation when the environment can provide new evidence. Its decision object is an interaction horizon: the permitted number of observation-action cycles in one rollout. The study compares that budget with per-action reasoning under web-task success evaluation, and asks whether a longer horizon lets a policy collect information, backtrack after mistakes, and revise a plan instead of merely extending the text produced before its next action.
