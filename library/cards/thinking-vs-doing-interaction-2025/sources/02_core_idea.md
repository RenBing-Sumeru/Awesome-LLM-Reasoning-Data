The central idea is that a web agent can scale along an interaction axis as well as a thinking axis. Extra environment steps expose fresh observations, so they can support exploration, revisiting an earlier page, checking a tentative answer, and re-planning after a failure. This is complementary to increasing reasoning tokens per action: one enlarges the information available to the policy, while the other mainly increases computation over the same information.

The authors turn that distinction into Test-Time Interaction, or TTI. Instead of training only at one fixed maximum trajectory length, TTI begins with short rollouts and gradually permits longer ones through a multiplicative horizon curriculum. The resulting execution record contains a task, successive observations and actions, the allowed horizon, terminal feedback, and compute cost. It makes interaction length a controllable deployment budget rather than an incidental property of an agent trace.
