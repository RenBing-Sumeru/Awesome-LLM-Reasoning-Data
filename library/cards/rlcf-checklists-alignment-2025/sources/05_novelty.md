Conventional RLHF, DPO, and reward modeling usually learn a task-agnostic preference function, and even rubric-based methods often rely on a small set of predefined shared dimensions. RLCF instead dynamically generates acceptance criteria for each instruction and decomposes reward computation into interpretable binary or local judgments. Programmatically checkable items can bypass language-model judges entirely. At the data level, WildChecklists preserves not only chosen–rejected pairs but also the intermediate criteria that produce those preferences. The novelty therefore lies in instance-level criterion generation, hybrid verification, and traceable feedback records rather than merely replacing one reinforcement-learning objective with another.
