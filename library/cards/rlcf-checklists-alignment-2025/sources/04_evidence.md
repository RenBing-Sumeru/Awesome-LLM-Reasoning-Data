The authors compare RLCF with generic reward models and other feedback methods across several instruction-following and alignment benchmarks. RLCF is the only evaluated method that improves performance on all five benchmarks, with gains of roughly 4 points on FollowBench-hard, 6 points on InFoBench, and 3 points on Arena-Hard. Ablations further show that instruction-specific checklists, item-level decisions, and heterogeneous verifiers each contribute to performance. The evidence supports the claim that decomposed requirements can supervise complex instructions more effectively than a single holistic score, although the gains still depend jointly on the base model, checklist generator, and judge reliability.
