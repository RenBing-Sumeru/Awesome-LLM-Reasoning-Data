General-purpose reward models usually compress response quality into a single scalar and apply fixed notions of helpfulness, correctness, or safety to every instruction. However, instructions differ substantially in constraints such as format, coverage, tone, calculation steps, and prohibited content. A single reward neither explains where a response fails nor reliably captures long-tail requirements. The paper therefore asks whether each instruction can be converted into a set of itemized, verifiable criteria and whether language-model judges and programmatic verifiers can score those criteria separately, producing finer-grained and more interpretable supervision than a generic reward model.
