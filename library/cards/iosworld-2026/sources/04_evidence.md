All numbers below are author-reported and were not independently replayed. On the 133-task suite with a 50-step cap, the strongest reported configuration is Opus 4.6 vision+XML: 51.9% overall strict pass, comprising 81.5% single-app, 36.7% multi-app, and 54.3% memory/personalization. These scores characterize model behavior under the published environment and judge; they do not prove task correctness, data quality, or real-device readiness (Paper Table 3).

The judge was compared with humans on 128 Opus 4.6 vision+XML trajectories. The paper reports 89% task-level agreement, Cohen's kappa 0.77, and F1 0.86; at rubric level it reports 86% accuracy, kappa 0.69, and F1 0.90. Across 1,094 judged criteria, 148 disagreements comprise 79 false positives and 69 false negatives. Semantic/report criteria show the largest error, roughly 13-16%, and task-level direction differs by category: the judge tends to over-accept single-app and over-reject multi-app cases (Appendix J, Tables 10 and 13-14).

The MCP ablation holds Qwen3.5 35B, all 133 tasks, the judge, and the 50-step budget fixed. Replacing GUI-only interaction with structured per-app MCP tools raises strict pass from 12.8% to 24.8% and mean rubric score from 0.33 to 0.683. This supports the narrower claim that tool interface changes outcomes under the same evaluation contract; it does not isolate training-data quality or establish that direct persistence-layer tools match deployable phone interaction (Section 4.3 and Appendix E).

Failure analysis includes all 422 failures from five frontier vision+XML configurations: 51% exhausted the budget, 26% gave up, and 23% stopped prematurely. Qwen vision+XML had 119 failures, 60 flagged as extreme action loops. This shows failures existed and were analyzed internally, but not that raw failures were publicly retained. The public project displays only 16 selected Opus 4.6 vision+XML trajectories, while repository `results/` and `artifacts/` are ignored; independent episode-level auditing therefore remains incomplete.
