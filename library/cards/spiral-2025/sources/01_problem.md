SPIRAL asks whether an LLM can acquire transferable reasoning without a fixed corpus of human-written problems and answers: the model instead learns from multi-turn competition against its current self, with rules and game outcomes supplying feedback. The camera-ready arXiv v3 is marked “Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2026”; the arXiv record was first submitted on 30 June 2025 and the official OpenReview entry is the venue record. (Paper p. 1; arXiv record; OpenReview forum `7Yayy5fNLg`.)

The object being constructed is not a static QA pair. A training episode begins from a TextArena game and initial state, alternates role-conditioned natural-language observations and complete reasoning/action responses from one shared policy, executes parsed actions, and ends with an environment outcome. The learner attaches a role return, a per-game/per-role EMA baseline, and a role advantage to the policy's turns. For Kuhn Poker and Simple Negotiation, earlier actions are concatenated into the current observation to preserve a Markovian context. (Paper §3, Algorithm 1; Appendix D.1.)

This belongs in Data Construction and Open Release Recipes because the changing policy, opponent, and environment jointly generate the training stream. It is adjacent to agent-trajectory and RLVR work, but it is not a frozen trajectory dataset, a step-level process-supervision release, or evidence that natural-language reasoning is faithful. The L4 boundary is therefore the open construction recipe plus verified paper, code, environments, configuration, models, and comparison-dataset URL; the exact online rollouts behind reported checkpoints are not released as immutable data.
