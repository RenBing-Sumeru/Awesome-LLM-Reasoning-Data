For every MATH training question, the respective backbone—DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (DS-7B) or DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (DS-32B)—collects 20 candidate self-rollouts, each capped at 4,096 tokens. The paper computes TLB as a mixture of the mean correct-response length and the maximum generation length, weighted by sampling accuracy. It then calibrates a rule-based correct/incorrect reward using the response's relative length deviation from TLB, ranks candidates, and writes winner/loser pairs.

The retained pair types are dual-correct pairs (DCP), which compare two correct responses and favor the more concise one, and dual-incorrect pairs (DICP), which compare two incorrect responses and favor the longer reasoning chain when it is within budget. For each question, the maximal-margin DCP and DICP are selected, the bottom margin fraction is removed, and at most one pair of each type is retained. The reported margin-drop fraction is 0.18 for DS-7B and 0.15 for DS-32B.

The resulting datasets contain 10,295 DS-7B pairs and 9,813 DS-32B pairs. The paper reports one epoch of SimPO with AdamW at learning rate 5e-6, beta 200, and gamma 1. Preference-data rollout construction is distinct from evaluation: the latter uses greedy decoding with a 32,768-token generation limit. Sampling temperature, seed, prompt template, exact answer extraction, and correctness-checker implementation are undisclosed.
