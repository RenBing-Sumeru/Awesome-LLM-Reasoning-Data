The evidence is author-reported model and budget evidence, not a validation of released-row correctness.

- **Main evaluation.** Table 1 reports Z1-7B at 76.4 on MATH500, 47.5 on GPQA Diamond, 35.3 on LiveCodeBench, and 22.3 on BigCodeBench-Hard, for a four-task average of 45.4. The authors describe performance as comparable to R1-Distill-Qwen-7B while using about 30% of its average thinking tokens. Evaluation uses one greedy sample per question at temperature 0; this does not disclose the teacher-generation temperature.
- **Budget curve.** Appendix Table 8 reports GPQA 47.5 at an observed average of 2,470 thinking tokens, LiveCodeBench 35.3 at 866, and MATH500 76.4 at 1,185 under varied maximum budgets. These points support a budget-performance analysis, but do not identify which released training rows caused the behavior.
- **Trajectory-length ablation.** Under a 74M-token training budget, the longest-greedy subset has 33K samples, mean trajectory length 2,216, and three-task average 50.8; the shortest-greedy subset has 90K samples, mean length 807, and average 49.1. The full 107K setting reports average 53.1 and average thinking tokens 1,507, versus 50.1/1,390 for random 64K and 48.5/1,236 for random 16K. This shows a measured length/quantity trade-off within the tested recipe, not universal superiority of long traces.
- **STW ablation and negative case.** Table 3 reports with-versus-without-STW scores of 76.4/74.0 on MATH500, 47.5/25.8 on GPQA, and 35.4/34.5 on LiveCodeBench, with cut-off rates of 11.4%, 41.4%, and 4.25%. Appendix D.3 shows that a 2,048-token thinking cap can truncate reasoning and produce 11 instead of the correct 29. Table 3's 35.4 differs slightly from the 35.3 reported in Tables 1 and 8.
