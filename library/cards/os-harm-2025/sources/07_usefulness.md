For the assigned `environment_agent_trajectory_data` track, OS-Harm is useful as an evaluation and audit reference: it specifies a task-to-VM-to-multimodal-episode-to-semantic-judgment pipeline and exposes feedback at both full-episode and first-unsafe-step granularity. A study can compare refusal, harmful execution, partial completion, and judge disagreement while retaining the observation/action history needed to diagnose where the safety decision arose.

As a benchmark surface, it supports controlled comparisons of computer-use agents across deliberate misuse, prompt injection, and ambiguous-task misbehavior. Report unsafe and completion results together, stratify by harm category and injection vector, fix the interaction budget, and include human spot checks; an unsafe rate alone confounds capability, task completion, and judge error.

As a judge-audit case, the 150 human-labeled o4-mini episodes can be used to reproduce precision, recall, F1, and conditional first-violation agreement, then test alternative trajectory representations or judges. Such work should preserve the original AER prompt and model versions and should not treat one author's category labels as an independent multi-annotator consensus.
