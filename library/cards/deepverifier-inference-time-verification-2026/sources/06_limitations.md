The system is expensive, dependent on its verifier backbone, and exposed to the same volatile web environment that the research agent must navigate. A flawed taxonomy can focus additional compute on the wrong issues, a verifier can hallucinate evidence or misjudge a valid answer, and repeated retries can compound cost or create new errors. The reported gains rely partly on capable closed-source models and selected agent benchmarks, so they do not establish a universal scaling law. The separate training resource is curated from a relatively small set of source trajectories and should not be treated as a comprehensive distribution of deep-research failures.
