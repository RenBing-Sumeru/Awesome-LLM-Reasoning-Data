The method has three modules. A decomposition agent condenses a long, step-indexed trajectory into the sources visited and the concrete facts retrieved, then matches suspicious behaviors to a taxonomy containing five major failure classes and thirteen subclasses. It produces localized potential errors and follow-up questions. A verification agent sequentially answers those questions with browsing, screenshots, and code execution. Finally, a judge sees the summary, suspected errors, questions, and gathered evidence, explains its decision, and assigns a score from one to four.

At test time, scores at most two mean the response is treated as incorrect, while scores at least three accept it. On rejection, the judge gives actionable retry guidance and may supply an answer when prior evidence already resolves the issue; the loop stops on acceptance or at a fixed retry limit. The taxonomy was built from 2,997 actions over 90 WebAggregatorQA tasks and 555 reconciled error points. For the optional DeepVerifier-4K training resource, the authors collect 400 agent trajectories, retain correctly accepting and correctly rejecting verification traces, balance them, and produce 4,646 prompt-response pairs.
