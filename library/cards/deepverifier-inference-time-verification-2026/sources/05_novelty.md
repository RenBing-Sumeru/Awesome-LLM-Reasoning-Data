The central novelty is to scale the act of verification, not just the number of agent rollouts. The paper combines an automatically constructed failure taxonomy with verification asymmetry: it converts a difficult whole-trajectory judgment into smaller claims that can be supported or refuted by targeted evidence. This creates a bridge between a verifier's judgment and an agent's next action, because each rejection identifies a failure type and asks for a concrete correction rather than treating the score as an opaque reranking signal.

It also integrates several levels of use around one verifier. The immediate use is a plug-in test-time module for diagnosis, evidence retrieval, feedback, and retry. A second use is creating filtered reflection supervision for an open model. That distinction matters: the paper does not claim that the data alone causes the primary gains; it shows that the non-training verification loop itself improves closed and open backbones, then offers the dataset as a way to transfer part of the capability into a smaller model.
