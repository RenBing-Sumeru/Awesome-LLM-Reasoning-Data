On GAIA-Web, DeepVerifier achieves 73.17 F1 for rejecting incorrect answers. The paper reports that it exceeds a vanilla agent-as-judge and an LLM-judge baseline by 12% to 48% in meta-evaluation F1. It evaluates generalization on full GAIA, XBench-DeepSearch, and BrowseComp after using WebAggregatorQA only for taxonomy construction, which is intended to separate the observed failure taxonomy from the final task distributions. The experimental setup includes Claude-3.7-Sonnet, GPT-4.1, and Qwen3-8B backbones.

For the test-time scaling claim, the full GAIA development curve reports increasing performance over zero through ten feedback rounds. The paper states 8% to 11% accuracy gains on challenging GAIA subsets when DeepVerifier is paired with capable closed-source models and 3% to 6% gains on XBench-DeepSearch. It also reports that a DeepVerifier-8B model fine-tuned with the released 4,646-pair resource improves open-model reflection after ten verification and feedback rounds. These results support the more limited conclusion that structured verification feedback can make additional retry compute productive in the evaluated deep-research settings.
