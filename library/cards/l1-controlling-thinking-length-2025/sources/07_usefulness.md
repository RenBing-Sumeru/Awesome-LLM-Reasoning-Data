For Track 5, L1 is a concrete example of turning test-time compute into a trainable trajectory condition rather than applying a post-hoc truncation rule. It exposes three objects that should remain separate in an atlas record: the source problem-answer pair, the on-policy reasoning rollout under a requested budget, and the correctness/length reward record.

The official scripts and checkpoints can support recipe-level reproduction, controlled generation at several token budgets, reward ablations, and comparison of exact versus ceiling semantics. A reusable dataset derived from this recipe should preserve the source revision and item ID, prompt template, exact/max mode, requested and realized tokens, full response, extracted answer, checker result, each reward component, termination cause, GRPO group, seed, and model/checkpoint revision.

Auditors can use those fields to test padding, repetition, verifier errors, difficulty-conditioned budget allocation, and training/evaluation overlap. Until paper-matched rollout and reward records are released or reconstructed under a pinned commit, the work is most reusable as an RLVR/test-time-compute recipe and model release, not as a ready-made trace dataset.
