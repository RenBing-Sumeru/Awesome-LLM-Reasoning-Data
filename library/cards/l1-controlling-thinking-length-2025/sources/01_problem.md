Reasoning models often spend more tokens on difficult problems, but ordinary long-CoT models do not let a caller choose that budget. Their outputs may run for tens of thousands of tokens or stop before a hard problem is solved. Test-time budget forcing can truncate a solution mid-step or insert continuation tokens without teaching the policy how to reorganize its reasoning.

L1 asks whether token budget can become an explicit conditioning variable learned through reinforcement learning. The LCPO training object starts from a mathematical problem and final answer, appends an exact-length or maximum-length request, and obtains an on-policy reasoning response. No gold intermediate rationale is required; programmatic final-answer correctness and realized token count provide the feedback.

For this atlas, the important object is the budget-conditioned rollout and its reward record, not only the released model. A reusable record would connect the source item, requested budget, full generated response, extracted answer, actual length, correctness signal, and length-adherence signal. The official release provides code and models, but a complete paper-matched training-rollout corpus was not verified.
