Terminal-agent benchmarks and execution-based grading predate TerminalWorld, and Terminal-Bench already provides a nearby comparison. The paper's concrete change is the construction object: public real terminal recordings are filtered, rewritten into goal-only instructions and cleaned reference solutions, reconstructed as Docker environments, and paired with state tests that must distinguish complete, no-op, and partial behavior.

The three-way validator makes the verifier-generation boundary unusually explicit. `AllPassing` checks that the intended reference reaches the tested state; `Nop` checks that tests are not vacuous; `Partial` checks that every chosen truncation/ablation loses at least one required condition. This is stronger than accepting a test merely because it passes the reference, but it remains a finite sensitivity check rather than a formal proof of soundness, completeness, or resistance to adversarial action sequences.

The data-direction signal is the separation of imitation evidence from outcome supervision. A public user's transcript motivates the task, a cleaned reference helps construct and validate it, and a newly evaluated agent can succeed with only 21.4% median command-set overlap. The reusable research object is therefore the environment plus terminal predicate, not a claim that the human or synthesized command sequence is the sole correct chain of thought.

The manually reviewed Verified-200 also changes the evaluation surface rather than creating a training subset. 4 experienced authors deliberately select and repair more diverse, complex, longer tasks and then use only this subset for model evaluation. That decision improves inspection effort on the evaluated surface but introduces selection bias and does not imply that the 200 reference solutions are authorized training examples.

What is not new should remain visible. TerminalWorld does not introduce a new optimizer, trained policy, preference objective, process reward model, or step-level label schema. Docker packaging, LLM synthesis, coding-agent environment construction, state tests, and Harbor are integrated components. Before reuse, researchers must verify the production settings, license, record-level lineage, test failure modes, and version-pinned replay; scale and pass rate do not settle those questions.
