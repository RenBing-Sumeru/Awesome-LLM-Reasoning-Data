- **Release evidence.** Dataset Viewer reports one `train` split with three string fields for MMathCoT-1M (1,019,059 rows), DualMath-1.1M (1,100,779), and URSA_Alignment_860K (863,534). The Hub cards tag the first two GPL-3.0 and the alignment set MIT. The official repository is Apache-2.0 and contains inference/scoring code, not verified data-construction or PS-GRPO training code.
- **Selector evidence.** In Table 2, URSA-8B-RM Best-of-32 reports 55.0 on MathVerse, 87.2 on MathVista-GPS, and 35.1 on MathVision; self-consistency reports 50.7, 85.4, and 33.1. This is selection accuracy under one candidate pool, not calibration of every process label.
- **RL evidence.** Table 1 reports URSA-8B average 54.7 and URSA-8B-PS-GRPO 58.2 across six benchmarks. With the same stated training data, hyperparameters, and rollout number, Figure 6 reports average improvement 6.8% for PS-GRPO versus 3.1% for vanilla GRPO. Table 4's best reported tested row uses gamma=0.5, rho=0.3, and average 58.2.
- **Failure evidence.** The paper reports its two scalar-process-reward GRPO variants have lower test accuracy than vanilla GRPO and shorter responses/fewer steps. This supports the need to separate PRM scores from their use as an optimization target; it does not prove the proposed penalty removes reward hacking.
