ReGenesis was published as an ICLR 2025 Oral paper. It asks a construction question: can a model create useful reasoning-path SFT data for itself from task instructions and final answers, without a stronger reasoning teacher or human-written task-specific demonstrations? The paper focuses on the poor out-of-domain transfer observed when prior self-improvement recipes directly sample task-specific chains of thought. It does not study online RL, learned process rewards, interactive environments, or human step annotation. (Paper §1–§3; ICLR proceedings record)

The source object is an answer-keyed training item `(q_i, a_i)`. Main experiments draw training items separately from GSM8K, NumGLUE, ReClor, ARC-Challenge, and StrategyQA; GSM8K human rationales are deliberately excluded. ReGenesis expands each item through 25 fixed general reasoning guidelines into task-adapted guidelines, non-solving reasoning structures, complete paths, and predicted answers. A final SFT row contains the instruction, one selected complete path, and the reference answer; the paper does not state that adapted guidelines or structures are retained in the final target. (Paper §3.1, §4.1.1)

This belongs in **Data Construction / Open Release Recipes** because its central contribution is a trace-generation and selection lineage, not a new benchmark or verifier. The L4 boundary is asymmetric: the official paper, appendix, venue status, affiliation, prompts, experimental settings, and results are inspectable, but no official code, generated corpus, trained checkpoint, or method project release was verified. The Card can therefore reconstruct the disclosed recipe and its audit questions, but it cannot certify a reusable release or reproduce item-level lineage.
