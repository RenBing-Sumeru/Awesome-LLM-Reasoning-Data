Prior self-improvement baselines already generated chains of thought, filtered candidates by reference answers or majority votes, and fine-tuned the same model. STaR also retried failed instructions with answer hints. ReGenesis does not introduce those components in isolation.

What changes is the representation before a complete path exists. Each task is crossed with 25 general reasoning guidelines; the model must adapt one guideline to the instruction and produce a non-solving structure before writing the path. This exposes two construction intermediates that direct CoT sampling leaves implicit. The method also places retry hints at those two upstream stages rather than in final path generation, creating a testable lineage distinction between freely generated and answer-conditioned examples.

The paper's comparisons support this distinction, with the full adaptation + structure + path pipeline outperforming either component-removal variant across the seven Table 4 tests, and upstream-only hinting outperforming all-stage hinting on six of seven Appendix Table 7 tests. These results make the abstraction ladder a plausible construction variable. They do not prove that the generated structures are faithful plans, because the same model creates and evaluates their downstream utility and no independent structure labels exist.

Self-Discover is a close representational antecedent: it composes task-specific reasoning structures from general modules. ReGenesis connects a related abstract-to-concrete idea to self-generated post-training records for the same target model. The claimed strategy-diversity evidence in Appendix A.8.2 is weaker than the accuracy ablations because Mistral itself labels and clusters 500 ReGenesis and 500 STaR paths. Before reuse, a curator should independently label strategy, validity, and redundancy while preserving guideline IDs, hint flags, and rejected paths.
