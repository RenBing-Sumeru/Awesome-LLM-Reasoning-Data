Many benchmark papers measure one agent domain or one capability. This survey's change is to connect those local evaluations to a common design vocabulary—curation, environment, interface, metric, and safety—and then place developer frameworks beside public benchmarks.

The contribution is a comparative taxonomy and review procedure, not a new environment, dataset, agent, or controlled leaderboard. Its distinctive value is making terminal verification and trajectory-evaluation choices visible when moving across web, SWE, tools, and generalist settings. It also connects public benchmark evaluation to development-time observability: final-response, stepwise, and trajectory assessment expose different failure information and different reference-based versus judge-based trade-offs, rather than merely adding another task list.
