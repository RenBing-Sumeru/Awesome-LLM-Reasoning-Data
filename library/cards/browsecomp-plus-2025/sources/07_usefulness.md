For the assigned `environment_agent_trajectory_data` track, BrowseComp-Plus is useful as a controlled evaluation substrate for search-agent episodes. The 830 query records can be joined to a fixed 100,195-document corpus, human evidence/gold qrels, hard negatives, released indexes, and episode outputs. This lets an evaluator represent query, environment state, search action, returned observation, retrieved IDs, terminal status, cited answer, and separate feedback outputs instead of retaining only a final accuracy bit.

The mixed feedback contract supports failure attribution. Researchers can distinguish a terminal or parsing failure from missing retrieval evidence, weak ranking, failure to cite retrieved evidence, and semantic answer failure. The paper's retriever, reranker, `get_document`, oracle-positive-document, inference-effort, corpus-expansion, and judge-comparison experiments provide concrete templates for controlled ablations. Any follow-up should report judge and artifact versions and should not interpret score gains as proof of dataset quality.

The construction process is also a benchmark-building recipe: start from hard factual questions, use a search-enabled model to propose clue/evidence URLs, scrape and parse pages, retain only recoverable cases, train and cross-check human annotators, label evidence and gold documents, mine hard negatives from decomposed subqueries, freeze a corpus and indexes, expose an agent tool, and evaluate retrieval, citations, terminal validity, and final answers separately. A stronger reuse would additionally preserve rejected items, deduplication logic, source-level rights, immutable versions, and all successful and failed episodes.

The four public GPT-5/o3 run archives are valuable for trajectory-schema and cost/behavior audits, but they are a partial release rather than a general training dataset. Before episode-level research, count and decrypt every file, measure completed/incomplete/error coverage, verify that all intended query IDs are present, and pin the producer, retriever, model, prompt, judge, dependency, and artifact revisions. Exact historical replay remains unsupported because proprietary API snapshots, seeds, retry state, and a unified manifest are absent.

The safe evidenced use is **evaluation and audit only**. The benchmark can compare retrievers and agents on a more stable substrate than the live web and can guide release standards for replayable web-agent data. The paper does not support treating its questions, qrels, documents, or trajectories as SFT examples, preference pairs, reward-model targets, process supervision, RLVR rewards, or agent-training rollouts. Those uses would require an explicit license, split/decontamination policy, full provenance, failure retention, and a separate study of training validity.
