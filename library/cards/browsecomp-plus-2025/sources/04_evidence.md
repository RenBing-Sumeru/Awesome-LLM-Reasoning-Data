The paper demonstrates that the fixed retriever materially changes end-to-end answer accuracy under the reported setup. With GPT-5, BM25 yields 55.90% answer accuracy and 61.70 retrieval recall, while Qwen3-Embedding-8B yields 70.12% accuracy and 78.98 recall. GPT-4.1 moves from 14.58% with BM25 to 35.42% with Qwen3-Embedding-8B; SearchR1-32B moves from 3.86% to 10.36%. These are paper-reported evaluation results, not independently rerun measurements and not proof that the released data are suitable for training.

Retrieval-only results expose the gap behind the final score. For evidence qrels, BM25 reports R@5/R@1000/nDCG@10 of 1.2/13.7/1.6, compared with 14.5/76.7/20.3 for Qwen3-Embedding-8B. For gold-document qrels, the corresponding values are 1.4/17.3/1.7 and 18.5/83.5/19.5. Because qrels are incomplete, especially when new documents are added, these metrics can count genuinely relevant results as false negatives.

Several controlled checks locate other bottlenecks. Supplying all labeled positive documents gives GPT-4.1 93.49% accuracy, and the authors manually check the remaining 6.51% as answerable. Enabling `get_document` raises GPT-4.1 from 35.42% to 43.61% and Qwen3-32B from 10.36% to 11.69% in the reported setting. Reranking Qwen3-Embedding-8B's top 20 raises evidence R@5 from 14.5 to 23.3 and GPT-4.1 end-to-end accuracy from 35.42% to 47.11%. Higher reasoning effort raises gpt-oss-20B from 13.37% to 34.58% and gpt-oss-120B from 24.94% to 42.89% with the Qwen retriever. These comparisons isolate retrieval, document access, reranking, and inference effort; they do not establish a training recipe.

The 9,771,311-document FineWeb-Edu expansion is an audit of scale and qrel coverage. Qwen3-32B answer accuracy drops from 10.36% to 7.11%, while measured neural retrieval recall also drops partly because newly found relevant documents are not judged. This supports the paper's warning that corpus expansion and incomplete qrels are confounded rather than proving the larger corpus is worse.

Judge choice also moves the score. For GPT-5 with the Qwen3 retriever, substring matching, GPT-4.1 judgment, and Qwen3 judgment produce 65.18%, 70.12%, and 71.69%, respectively. Citation recall is below overall retrieval recall, but citation scoring only checks whether cited numeric document IDs appear in evidence qrels; it does not verify that a quoted sentence entails the claim. Reported parametric-only accuracy is roughly 20% for o3 and 26% for GPT-5, which the authors discuss as possible BrowseComp exposure. The fixed corpus controls search-time drift, not knowledge already present in model weights.
