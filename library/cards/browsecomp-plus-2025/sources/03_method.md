Construction begins with BrowseComp's 1,266 question-answer pairs, but the BrowseComp-Plus benchmark release contains 830 retained items. OpenAI o3 with web search receives each original question and answer and is asked to produce clues, URLs, and evidence. The generator snapshot is unknown. It fails, abstains, or violates format on 124 cases, leaving 1,142. Selenium fetches cited pages and Trafilatura extracts text; removing 137 queries for which any cited URL cannot be scraped leaves 1,005 cases for human review.

Fourteen university students spend more than 400 hours verifying the candidates. They label evidence for every clue and require the combined evidence to support the complete answer; they label a document gold when it semantically contains the final answer. For an inadequate case, the protocol requires clue revision and at least 20 minutes of additional web search before rejection. Quality control includes about one hour of internal-development-set training, a 40-minute demonstration, group support, 10 cross-checked items per labeler, more than 80% agreement, and retrospective relabeling after detected mistakes. Compensation and consent are unknown. Human review retains 830 items; rejected item-level records are not released.

Hard-negative construction uses GPT-4o to decompose each retained question into approximately seven self-contained subqueries. SerpAPI returns up to 100 Google results for each subquery; the same Selenium/Trafilatura pipeline scrapes them before positives and negatives are deduplicated. The GPT-4o snapshot and exact deduplication method and threshold are unknown. The final fixed corpus has 100,195 documents, with means of 6.1 evidence documents, 2.9 gold documents, and 76.28 negatives per query; the mean document has 5,179.2 words and 32,296.2 characters.

At evaluation time, the agent searches a released fixed index. The main tool returns the top five documents, truncated to the first 512 tokens under the current code's `Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B` tokenizer. BM25 and Qwen3-Embedding indexes are released; ReasonIR and Jina-ColBERT are also evaluated. Some experiments expose `get_document` for full-document access. The action sequence is search, optional full-document retrieval, and final answer. The response template requests an explanation, numeric document citations, an exact answer, and confidence.

The producer schema can retain metadata, query ID, tool-call counts, usage, status, retrieved document IDs, and an ordered `result` stream containing tool calls, reasoning summaries, and output text. Returned responses with non-completed status may be serialized, but query-level exceptions can terminate before a record is persisted. Current evaluation first checks for `status == completed` and a final answer, then computes retrieval and citation metrics programmatically and answer accuracy with an LLM judge. The paper uses GPT-4.1; current code uses Qwen/Qwen3-32B with temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20, at most 4,096 output tokens, and thinking disabled.

Release components are distributed across separate official repositories: the 830-row query/label set, the 100,195-row corpus, indexes, code, and four encrypted trajectory JSONL files. The four combinations are BM25 or Qwen3-Embedding-8B paired with GPT-5 or o3. The code was inspected at commit `046949032b0328319cc9a02663a759ec601d9402`; query, index, and run revisions are recorded in `paper.yaml`. No unified immutable manifest binds those revisions, and the corpus revision, proprietary API snapshots, seeds, retry policy, per-condition rollout count, and exhaustive trajectory coverage remain unknown.
