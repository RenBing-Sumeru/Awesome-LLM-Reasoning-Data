The paper evaluates 15 model configurations on a fixed denominator of 196 checkpoints. Its best strict result is GPT 5.5 through Codex CLI 0.124.0 at 14.8%; the same configuration reaches 28.1% isolated and 66.8% core. The gap is diagnostic: models preserve core behavior substantially more often than they preserve current error/functionality requirements together with all prior regressions. No evaluated configuration fully solves all checkpoints of even 1 of the 36 problems.

The longitudinal quality results point in the same direction. Structural erosion increases in 77% of trajectories and verbosity in 75.5%. The mean count of high-complexity functions rises from 3.6 to 23.7. Mean cost per checkpoint grows 2.2 times from the first to the final checkpoint, and the reported accounting totals 13.18B tokens. These claims are paper aggregates, not recomputed values from a released run bundle.

The human calibration analyzes 473 public Python repositories and 13,667 sampled source-touching commits. At the reported aggregate, agent checkpoints are 2.3 times more verbose and 2.0 times more eroded than the human panel. This supports the claim that the metrics distinguish the produced agent code from the selected human-history baseline, but the repository/commit manifest and failed-metric exclusions are not released, so independent reconstruction is blocked.

Prompt interventions provide a useful negative result. Anti-slop and plan-first prompts can lower initial erosion or verbosity, yet they generally do not eliminate degradation; average checkpoint cost rises 12.1%, and strict correctness falls in the reported comparison. Thus cleaner-looking initial code is not evidence that the agent can preserve correctness and structure across the full sequence.

The released tests, reference implementations, runner, prompts, and metric code make the feedback implementation inspectable and permit new runs. They do not make the headline numbers independently reproducible: complete successful, failed, crashed, and timed-out paper trajectories, checkpoint workspaces, per-run tests, quality JSON, model endpoint snapshots, and an immutable result manifest are absent. Evidence is therefore strongest for the benchmark mechanism and aggregate failure pattern, and weaker for run-level causal attribution or exact numerical replay.
