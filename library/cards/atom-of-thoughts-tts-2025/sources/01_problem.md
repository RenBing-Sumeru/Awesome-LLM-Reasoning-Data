Long chain, tree, and graph reasoning methods commonly keep an expanding history in every later prompt. That history consumes context and can introduce irrelevant dependencies into a decision that only needs the current unresolved subproblem. Adding more test-time calls then partly pays to reread prior thoughts rather than to make progress, so a larger budget may be inefficient even when the underlying task needs multi-step reasoning.

Atom of Thoughts asks how to represent a reasoning process so that added computation acts on a compact, answer-equivalent state. The paper treats a complex problem as a sequence of self-contained subquestions. Its decision surface includes the current question state, a dependency graph, the contraction that creates the next state, optional search or reflection, prompt and completion cost, and the terminal answer. The aim is to reduce historical dependence without discarding the information needed to solve the original problem.
