The paper reports experiments on six benchmarks, evaluating AoT both as a standalone reasoning framework and as a plug-in enhancement for other test-time strategies. Its central evidence is a scaling comparison: the framework is reported to outperform baselines as computational budgets grow, consistent with the claim that less history-dependent prompting leaves more usable computation for the current reasoning state. The comparison spans reasoning and non-reasoning language models rather than depending on one specialized model.

The concrete result highlighted by the authors is 80.6% F1 on HotpotQA when AoT is applied to gpt-4o-mini. The paper states that this exceeds o3-mini by 3.4 points and DeepSeek-R1 by 10.6 points on that evaluation. This supports a strong task-specific result and a plausible budget-efficiency mechanism, but it does not prove that each DAG contraction is answer-preserving or that the same gains will hold for a task whose dependencies cannot be made self-contained.
