For an input problem, AoT asks the model to identify a dependency-based decomposition. The resulting directed acyclic graph specifies subquestions and their relations. A contraction step summarizes the solved or simplified dependencies into a new question state that preserves the answer target while reducing the amount of earlier context that must remain in the next prompt. The procedure iterates until the state is directly solvable, at which point the final answer is produced.

The paper also combines this Markovian transition structure with tree search and reflective refinement, so additional inference calls can explore or revise atomic states. The experiment suite covers six benchmarks and both reasoning and non-reasoning language models; the paper highlights HotpotQA with gpt-4o-mini. A faithful replay must store the original problem, every DAG, contraction prompt and output, state identifier, branch or reflection action, token or call cost, final response, and task-specific score, because the claimed efficiency depends on the state transformation rather than just the final answer.
