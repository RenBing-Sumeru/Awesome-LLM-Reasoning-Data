Joint verification may become costly as candidate sets grow and can be brittle when the pool is highly correlated or contains inconsistent formatting. A set-trained verifier may also be calibrated for one sampling temperature, model family, or parallel width but not another. Streaming cancellation risks cutting off a late correct solution, especially under distribution shift. Users should measure wall-clock latency, scorer cost, cancellation overhead, and accuracy separately for every parallel width rather than interpreting a reported speedup as architecture-independent.
