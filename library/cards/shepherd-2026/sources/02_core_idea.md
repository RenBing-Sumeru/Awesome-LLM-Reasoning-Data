Shepherd maps four agent-runtime concepts to functional-programming abstractions. A `Task` is a typed function and makes an agent substitutable. An `Effect` is a typed action record and separates intent from outcome. A `Scope` is a region-scoped handler that owns bindings and emitted effects. A `Trace` is a persistent Git-like graph in which effects become commits, forks become branches, accepted children can merge, and rejected children can be discarded.

The separation between intent and outcome is the feedback boundary. A supervisor can inspect an intent before the outside world responds and deny a destructive call before an outcome is materialized. Once executed, effects have one of three rollback contracts: reversible effects such as captured filesystem and sandbox state roll back natively; compensable effects such as some database writes require user-supplied compensation handlers; irreversible effects such as model calls and emails remain only as audit records. “Reversible execution” therefore does not mean that every external consequence can be undone.

Tree-GRPO uses the trace graph as a data-construction primitive. It keeps a root prefix, asks a stronger meta-agent to choose a fork turn and propose a bash action, restores the coupled agent-environment state just before that turn, and samples isolated sibling suffixes. Terminal outcome rewards are converted into two credit signals: inter-root GRPO advantage for shared-prefix actions and an intra-tree sibling advantage for post-fork actions. No learned value function or process-reward model is used.
