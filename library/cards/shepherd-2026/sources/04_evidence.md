The paper reports three proof-of-existence applications. On CooperBench, a supervisor over parallel coding workers raises pair-coding pass rate from 28.8% to 54.7%. Counterfactual Replay Optimization edits workflows and replays only the affected suffix; the abstract reports a 12.8% advantage over MetaHarness on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with 58% lower wall-clock. These results demonstrate runtime intervention and prefix reuse, but they are not evidence of a reusable trajectory release.

For the training application, Table 5 evaluates held-out Terminal-Bench 2.0 on 89 tasks with five seeds. Qwen3.5-35B-A3B scores 26.1%±4.21 at base, 34.2%±4.05 with Flat GRPO, and 39.4%±3.87 with Tree-GRPO. Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B scores 30.3%±3.62 at base, 33.8%±3.41 with Flat GRPO, and 37.2%±3.19 with Tree-GRPO. Thus Tree-GRPO adds 5.2 points over Flat GRPO for Qwen3.5 and 3.4 points for Nemotron-3 under the reported setup.

The substrate evaluation supports a narrower reset claim. Fork and revert operate on captured copy-on-write state; a mutation-size sweep reports 12/12 successful checks in which the post-revert workdir matched the pre-fork state. Fork latency stays around 134–143 ms in the main benchmark and roughly 340 ms in a Mac/Docker Desktop sweep. These measurements support captured filesystem/sandbox rollback, not rollback of arbitrary external services or irreversible calls.
