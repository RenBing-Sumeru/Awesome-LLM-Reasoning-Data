Correctness holds only relative to the released answer key and evaluator. A correct score does not prove that the model used the intended visual evidence, and an incorrect answer does not identify which reasoning step failed.

The benchmark remains vulnerable to public-data contamination, prompt sensitivity, answer-key errors, and differences between standard and vision-only settings. Post-release corrections mean that leaderboard comparisons need a dated dataset revision.

Reuse risk is mainly artifact risk: license and redistribution terms must be checked at the dataset level; hidden/public policy is not a substitute for contamination analysis; and aggregate accuracy should not be treated as a reusable reward without a separate feedback-contract audit.
