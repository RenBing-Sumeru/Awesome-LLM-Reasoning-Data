Retrieval similarity is only a heuristic: the paper observes that textually similar but logically different solutions can inject noise, particularly on simpler tasks. Public tests cover only part of program behavior, while the LLM judge can share errors with the generator; their weighted score is therefore neither a formal verifier nor a guarantee that retained prefixes are correct. Error localization and truncation may preserve an earlier hidden mistake, and similarity filtering may remove a useful alternative. The stepwise representation can also expand code that could be written in one line into multiple lines, the limitation explicitly noted by the authors.

Reproducibility and data audit are further limited by the lack of linked code, knowledge-base contents, retrieval neighbors, full trees, rejected candidates, reward logs, sandbox image/version, model prompts and revisions, seeds, or the 2.4K distilled dataset. Source-level licensing, split policy, contamination against evaluation benchmarks, and the effect of mixing 2.4K generated records with 170K foundational records are not disclosed at record level. Benchmark and fine-tuning improvements therefore should not be interpreted as proof of trace correctness, release completeness, or data cleanliness.
