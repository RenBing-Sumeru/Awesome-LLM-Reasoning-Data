Final-answer verification is a weak proxy for rational process. A derivation can contain invalid steps yet land on the correct answer, while regex extraction or SymPy comparison can reject an equivalent expression. The pipeline does not independently verify that a Helpful Critic found the first causal error or that a Misleading Critic's claim is genuinely plausible.

The exact phrase `\boxed{This critic is not critical.}` participates in selection, creating a format-sensitive shortcut. A model can learn the rejection token pattern without assessing the critique, and a semantically valid rejection in another form can fail selection. Because the three roles come from the same model family, they can share correlated misconceptions or co-adapt to private lexical conventions. Similar prompts reduce obvious role-style leakage but do not eliminate content leakage.

Evidence is mixed across rounds and models, and later self-play is not uniformly better. Separate GPT-4o-, self-correction-, and QwQ-assisted data pipelines complicate causal attribution for downstream tasks. The official training script's loop defaults and learning-rate ordering do not cleanly match the paper description, and tracked filenames containing vertical bars make ordinary repository checkout fail on Windows.
