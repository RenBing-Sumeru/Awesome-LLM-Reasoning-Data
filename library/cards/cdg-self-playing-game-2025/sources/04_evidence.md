On the paper's main pass@1 / maj@8 / maj@32 evaluation, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct moves from 85.3 / 91.2 / 93.0 to 86.8 / 92.0 / 93.1 on GSM8K and from 49.4 / 58.3 / 63.4 to 51.7 / 60.7 / 66.0 on MATH500 after CDG-2. The first round is mixed: GSM8K pass@1 rises to 85.5 while maj@8 and maj@32 fall, and all three MATH500 metrics fall to 49.0 / 56.8 / 62.2.

Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct also shows non-monotonic rounds. Its MATH500 metrics rise from 55.4 / 58.6 / 62.9 to 57.6 / 62.9 / 66.2 after CDG-1, then fall to 56.1 / 61.5 / 65.6 after CDG-2. For Llama-3.1-8B-Base with the extra 30,000-example imitation stage, MATH500 improves from 29.4 / 36.5 / 42.6 to 33.8 / 39.2 / 43.6 after CDG-2, while CDG-1 is again mixed.

The paper further evaluates stepwise error detection, self-correction, and long-chain reasoning. Those experiments do not isolate the main CDG episode corpus: stepwise-error data uses multiple Llama solutions and GPT-4o annotations, self-correction data selects final-correct two-pass traces and removes loops, and long-chain data uses QwQ-32B-Preview plus additional filtering. They support utility of the broader training design in the reported settings, not exclusive causality from the released CDG rollouts.

Official code corroborates the role prompts, sample multiplicities, answer extraction, threshold selection, exact rejection phrase, and conversion formats. Benchmark changes are not evidence that every critique identifies a real first error, every solution trace is faithful, the rollout corpus is diverse or uncontaminated, or the public release has complete lineage. Repository evaluation numbers use different decoding and harness settings from the paper table and should not be merged into one comparison.
