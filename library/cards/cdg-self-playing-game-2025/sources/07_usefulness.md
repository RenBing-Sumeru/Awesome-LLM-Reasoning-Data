For the data-construction and open-release-recipes track, CDG is most useful as a worked example of converting self-play outcomes into several supervision objects rather than as a single undifferentiated dataset. A reusable implementation can derive Prover SFT conversations, Helpful-Critic examples, Misleading-Critic examples, correction preferences, and resistance preferences from the same episode graph.

Curators should preserve the original question and reference answer; initial solution and correctness; critic role and full critique; all four revision candidates and verifier results; selection threshold and outcome; exact prompt template; loop, checkpoint, and decoding configuration; and the final training serialization. Both selected and rejected episodes matter, because a selected-only corpus hides threshold bias and critic failure modes.

The construction is also a useful stress test for feedback robustness: compare acceptance rates for valid and invalid critiques, measure whether role identity leaks through wording, replace the phrase-based rejection test with semantic adjudication, and stratify outcomes by parser confidence and problem source. Reuse claims should distinguish the raw 19,473-question pool, the Google Drive rollout artifact, the converted role datasets, and the Hugging Face checkpoint; they are related but not interchangeable objects.
