CDG assigns one policy family three roles. The Prover first solves a question. If the extracted answer is wrong, a Helpful Critic is asked to identify and explain the first mistake without supplying the complete solution. If the answer is correct, a Misleading Critic fabricates a plausible error. The Prover then sees the critique without being told its intent and must either revise the answer or reject the criticism.

Outcome verification is deliberately simple: a regex extractor obtains the final answer and a SymPy-based grader compares it with the reference. A Helpful Critic succeeds when an initially wrong solution becomes correct; a Misleading Critic succeeds when an initially correct solution becomes incorrect. The Prover succeeds by correcting after useful feedback or retaining correctness under misleading feedback. In the formal reward, resistance to misleading feedback is scaled by `eta`.

The main optimizer is ReST. Multiple revisions are sampled for each critique, then threshold rules convert successful behavior into Prover, Helpful-Critic, and Misleading-Critic SFT datasets. DPO and PPO appear as ablations, not as the main released recipe. This feedback contract is mixed: mathematical answer comparison is programmatic, while the critique and revision are language-model actions whose process validity is inferred only from the final answer and an exact rejection phrase.
