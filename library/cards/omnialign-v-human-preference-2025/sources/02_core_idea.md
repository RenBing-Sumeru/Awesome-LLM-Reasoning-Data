OmniAlign-V turns visual instruction answers into image-grounded preference pairs: the curated answer is chosen, while a LLaVA-NeXT response selected by an LLM judge as most inconsistent with the prompt intent and context is rejected. The released 150K pairs retain the image, prompt, and two responses, allowing DPO to learn from a visual comparison rather than a single answer. Unlike one-answer multimodal instruction corpora, its central contribution is this comparison; unlike text-only preference sets, the judgment remains tied to an image. Whether model-selected divergence reflects visual helpfulness rather than answer style remains an open question.
