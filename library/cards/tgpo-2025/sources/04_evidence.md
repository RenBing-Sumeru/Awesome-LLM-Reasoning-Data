The experiments use Browser-use with Qwen3-14B on 300 Online-Mind2Web tasks from 136 websites and SeeAct with Qwen2.5-VL-72B on 50 C-WebShop tasks. All compared optimization methods use 2 epochs, 8 H20 GPUs, and learning rate `1e-5`. The paper does not disclose seeds or uncertainty intervals, so the reported values are single-study author results rather than independently reproduced estimates.

On Online-Mind2Web, Table 1 reports TGPO at 38.4% success, 10.71 average steps, and 2.52 redundant steps. DPO reports 34.0%, 11.53, and 2.88 under the same table. On C-WebShop, TGPO reports 78.6% success, 8.66 average steps, and 0.97 redundant steps; DPO reports 72.1%, 9.85, and 1.41. These comparisons support the narrower claim that the reported TGPO system improves success and interaction efficiency relative to the paper's DPO implementation in those settings.

Table 2 reports raw trajectory label-conflict rates of 38.71% for Online-Mind2Web and 26.95% for C-WebShop. This measurement motivates node-level supervision: the same or merged state can be followed by actions inside trajectories with different terminal outcomes. The paper does not release the denominators, conflict records, or detection implementation, so the rates cannot be independently audited.

The KTO-Tree ablation improves over KTO on both datasets. As a curator interpretation, that comparison supplies some evidence that tree-derived step labels matter independently of the TGPO dynamic weighting. It does not fully isolate state merging from all other implementation choices, and the underlying trees and labels are unavailable.

The evidence remains bounded to two web-agent stacks and 350 reported tasks. There is no released code, trajectory set, tree, process-reward record, chosen/rejected pair file, checkpoint, environment snapshot, or independent rerun. The paper also does not report state-merge precision/recall, VLM-judge calibration, sensitivity to alpha, reward-weight robustness, or a train/evaluation overlap audit.

The benchmark results are not evidence that the derived data is high quality. They measure the final systems under the authors' undisclosed implementation. Data quality would additionally require record-level lineage, verifier error analysis, split and contamination controls, failed-example retention, release versioning, and rights information, all of which remain missing.
