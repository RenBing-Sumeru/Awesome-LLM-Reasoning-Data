Within `environment_agent_trajectory_data`, TGPO is useful as a design pattern for converting repeated episodes into decision-level supervision. A comparable implementation would retain task identity, state and action serialization, transitions, terminal labels, merge evidence, all four reward components, cumulative branch rewards, chosen/rejected actions, dynamic weights, and record-level lineage. This schema makes the hidden transformation from episode outcome to preference record auditable.

The uses actually demonstrated in the paper are limited to an SFT initialization, offline preference learning, web-agent training, and evaluation. The SFT checkpoint is the starting policy; tree-derived node pairs feed the preference objective; the resulting agents are evaluated on Online-Mind2Web and C-WebShop. The paper does not establish any broader training use, and the missing SFT recipe prevents reconstruction of the initialization.

For construction research, the merge-tree recipe can be tested against alternatives: exact environment-state IDs, DOM-diff matching, learned state equivalence, or no merging. Each study should preserve false merges and missed merges, then measure how they change cycles, subgoal distances, reward components, pair rankings, and downstream results. The four reward terms should also be ablated with fixed tasks and compute.

For verifier and selection research, the undisclosed VLM judge can be replaced by a versioned evaluator with a published prompt, expected-change schema, calibration set, and adjudication policy. Pair construction should log ties, zero-variance nodes, reward gaps, duplicates, and dropped branches. These records would permit false-positive/false-negative analysis and reveal whether high dynamic weights amplify judge or merge errors.

For evaluation, the reported success, average-step, redundant-step, and conflict-rate measurements offer comparison targets only after task and environment versions are pinned. A defensible follow-up must separate training, model selection, and evaluation tasks; replay terminal predicates; report multiple seeds; and compare under the disclosed 2-epoch, 8-H20, `1e-5` budget or a clearly documented alternative.

The present reuse class is **blocked pending verification**. The Card can support reading, implementation planning, and audit design, but no training or evaluation asset can be safely reused because code, data, models, environment snapshots, task/split manifests, licenses, and replay metadata are all absent.
