The contribution is to convert repeated linear web-agent episodes into a trajectory merge tree, attach mixed process feedback to its action branches, and optimize node-local action preferences instead of copying one terminal label onto every step.

TGPO aggregates trajectories for the same task into a tree whose nodes are unique web states and whose edges are actions. Two states are merged when their standardized URLs match—with essential parameters retained—and either their effective action sequences after the URL change agree or their image hashes are identical. This derived object exposes shared prefixes, divergent decisions, correction paths, and cycles across successful and failed executions.

Each branch receives four process signals. The subgoal term is `R_subgoal = L_min / (d(s0,st) + min_q d(st,q_goal))`; the redundancy term is `-1` when a state cycle is detected and `0` otherwise; action accuracy is `+1` when an undisclosed VLM judges that the expected interface modification occurred and `0` otherwise; format validity is `+1` for a syntactically valid action and `0` otherwise. They combine as `R = R_acc + R_format + R_red + alpha * R_subgoal`, with only an empirical alpha range of 2–5 disclosed.

At a tree node, divergent branches are ranked by cumulative reward to produce a node-level `(chosen action, rejected action)` pair. TGPO applies a DPO-style policy-versus-reference log-ratio margin and weights the pair by `|r_w-r_l| / sigma(R_s)`, emphasizing nodes whose competing actions have different cumulative outcomes. Tie handling, zero-variance safeguards, reward-gap thresholds, pair balancing, and record counts are unknown.

The feedback contract is mixed. Environment transitions and terminal success/failure provide episode evidence; URL/action/hash logic, shortest-path progress, cycle detection, and the action parser provide tree-derived or programmatic signals; the VLM supplies judgment-required action-effectiveness labels. These signals can observe collected transitions, syntactic validity, approximate progress, and a predicted UI change. They cannot establish semantic task correctness at every step, recover unvisited branches, or guarantee that merged states are behaviorally equivalent.

Relative to trajectory-level outcome labeling and ordinary DPO pairs over complete outputs, TGPO moves the preference object to divergent actions at a shared state and derives its ranking from cross-trajectory structure. The paper's KTO-Tree comparison indicates that the tree-derived labels contribute separately from the TGPO weighting, but it does not establish that the unreleased labels are calibrated or portable.
