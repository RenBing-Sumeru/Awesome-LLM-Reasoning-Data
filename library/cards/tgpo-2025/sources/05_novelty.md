The prior baseline is to supervise each web-agent action with the success or failure of its whole trajectory, or to form preferences at the level of complete outputs. That treatment loses the fact that a failed episode can contain useful actions and a successful episode can contain redundant or incorrect intermediate actions.

TGPO changes the data object before changing the optimizer. It merges repeated runs of the same task into a state-transition tree, then derives supervision at a shared decision node. The record consumed by preference learning is not merely a successful versus failed episode; it is a state, two divergent actions, their cumulative branch rewards, a chosen/rejected ordering, and a reward-gap/within-node-variance weight.

The feedback interface is also more explicit than a terminal bit. Four components represent approximate subgoal progress, cycle redundancy, VLM-judged action effectiveness, and action-format validity. This decomposition lets the method distinguish progress, syntax, interface effect, and repeated-state behavior, while keeping the terminal episode outcome as a separate signal.

The trajectory merge tree, shortest-path calculation, cycle detection, VLM judging, action parser, SFT initialization, and DPO-style reference-policy objective are not individually new components. The concrete change is their use to construct node-level preference records from cross-trajectory conflicts and to weight decision points by the separation between branch rewards. The work is therefore a construction-and-selection recipe, not an open trajectory dataset or a new environment.

The label-conflict measurements and KTO-Tree ablation are the paper's relevant quality signals, but neither proves that state merges, VLM decisions, or preference pairs are correct. The reported benchmark gains may come from the derived supervision, weighting, implementation details, or their interaction; absent data and code prevent finer attribution.

For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is that repeated agent episodes can be reorganized around shared states before preference construction. Reuse requires verifying the merge function, terminal checks, all four reward components, pair-selection edge cases, task splits, failed-trajectory retention, and immutable artifacts. Without those checks, the novelty can be studied as a recipe but not treated as a reusable release.
