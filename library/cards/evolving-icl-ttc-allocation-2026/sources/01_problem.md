Uniform best-of-N sampling repeats the same generation process for every test question, even after some questions have already produced a correct answer. Adaptive elimination saves samples by stopping solved questions, but the unresolved questions are still sampled from their original conditional distribution. When the correct solution is unlikely under that distribution, repeatedly spending more budget can yield near-duplicate failures rather than a new route to success.

This paper asks how a test-time allocation policy can decide both where computation should go and how later computation should change generation. Its setting is a collection of test questions evaluated together: early samples resolve easy questions and become a pool of candidate demonstrations, while later samples are reserved for unresolved questions and conditioned on semantically related successful examples from that evolving pool.
