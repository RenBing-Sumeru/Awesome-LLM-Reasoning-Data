Prior allocation methods primarily decide how many independent samples a question receives while leaving its sampling distribution unchanged. This paper changes the reusable inference record: a verified response to one test question becomes a retrieved demonstration that changes the conditional distribution of later, related questions. Allocation, verification, retrieval, and prompting are therefore joined in one round-based loop rather than evaluated as separate modules.

The novelty is not in a new verifier or a new language model. It is the transductive, test-set-level use of solved responses as an evolving control surface for additional inference. Before reuse, inspect whether examples from one evaluation item may legitimately condition another, whether the verifier makes false-positive demonstrations enter the pool, how prompts affect input-token cost, and whether the benchmark protocol allows test-set adaptation. Those checks are especially important because the main procedure uses ground-truth correctness during evaluation.
