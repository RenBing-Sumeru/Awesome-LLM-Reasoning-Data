The central modeling assumption is a single controlled error. Real agent failures can arise from several weak actions, delayed environment changes, interacting agents, hidden state, or successful recovery. The 18-mode taxonomy is explicitly non-exhaustive, and its applicability profiles come from sampled natural failures whose count and sampling procedure are unknown. Consequently, the reported benchmark characterizes failures that the injection pipeline can express and retain, not the full natural failure distribution.

“Golden by construction” is a useful but bounded claim. The 3-annotator majority review accepts 94% of step labels, 90% of agent labels on multi-agent traces, and 90% of error-family labels, while 2% have no clear decisive error. A substituted action may be the first controlled difference and still be semantically ambiguous, especially when its downstream symptoms resemble another family. Models frequently map planning, verification, and coordination causes to reasoning errors. Providing the task answer can worsen root-cause diagnosis by steering the judge toward the final mismatch.

Selection hides resilience. Only injections that make the source evaluator fail survive; post-injection successes, self-corrections, replay aborts, leakage-filtered traces, and cases with a salient answer are dropped. Because the task-evaluator inventory, versions, thresholds, outputs, and false-positive/false-negative audits are unavailable, this can select evaluator-sensitive failures and remove the counterexamples needed to learn when an apparent error is recoverable. This is a curator inference from the stated filter contract, not an author-reported training result.

Replay is only partly frozen. Static factual tool calls use a cache, but page-summary LLM outputs are deliberately not cached. Stateful browser and code sessions are re-executed on fresh instances; Bing DOM rotation causes 89% of reported `input_text` replay failures. No cache, snapshot, environment image, dependency lock, retry ledger, or fidelity output is released, so exact causal replay cannot be audited.

The experiment has no disclosed train/dev/test split. The 100 human-review traces and 1,444 ablation traces have no public IDs or overlap manifest. All tasks originate in 26 public benchmarks, but deduplication, task grouping, model contamination, and benchmark overlap are unknown. The paper says no PII is present and no human subjects are involved, yet reviewer recruitment, compensation, consent, raw corrections, and redaction evidence are not disclosed.

Release is the blocking limitation. Appendix A says all traces, labels, and evaluation code are released; the official repository says the opposite and currently publishes only a README and one image. Dataset/code licenses are unknown, and the manuscript's CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license must not be transferred to unreleased artifacts. No successful seed, natural failure, retained injected failure, rejected attempt, evaluator, prediction, or runnable environment is publicly available.
