Who&When Pro is currently safest as a reading/audit reference and a design specification for failure-attribution benchmarks. It gives a concrete target schema—task, full episode, responsible component, decisive step, error mode, and terminal outcome—and separates three signals that are often collapsed: environment success, intervention provenance, and a judge's diagnostic prediction.

For dataset construction, the warm-start recipe is a useful checklist. Preserve the successful seed and its task-evaluator output; log every prefix action and observation; record the chosen mode and injection slot; store the adaptive prompt and corrupted action; freeze static content and stateful environment versions; retain downstream continuations regardless of success; and attach every filter, retry, abort, human correction, and evaluator decision. A complete release should make success anchors and rejected failures queryable rather than publishing only failure-inducing interventions.

For evaluation research, the paper supports comparing all-at-once, step-by-step, and binary-search diagnosis under exact Step and relaxed Step@k; testing answer-visible versus answer-hidden prompts; stratifying by modality, topology, trace length, framework, and error family; and measuring both component localization and root-cause classification. Its results warn that a final reference answer can improve outcome checking while harming process diagnosis.

For post-training, the paper motivates—but does not demonstrate—SFT of diagnostic outputs, failure-conditioned retrieval, reward-model training, or self-evolution. Those uses are blocked because 0 benchmark rows are public, no dataset license exists, source-task contamination is unknown, successful and rejected traces are missing, and constructed labels have measurable human disagreement. Do not broaden `training_use` beyond evaluation and audit until a versioned release and rights review make the data object inspectable.

A future reuse gate should require row hashes and source IDs; complete successful/natural/injected/rejected counts; grouped splits; evaluator prompts/versions; human labels and corrections; API/model/framework commits; static caches and stateful snapshots; prediction files; code/data licenses; privacy/redaction rules; and a paper-to-release manifest resolving the present contradiction.
