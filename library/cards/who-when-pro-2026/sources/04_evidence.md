Table 4 evaluates ten frontier models with the all-at-once protocol. On text traces, Qwen3.5-122B obtains the highest responsible-agent accuracy at 57.5 and exact step accuracy at 73.9; GLM-5 obtains the highest error-mode macro-F1 at 22.2 and Joint accuracy at 25.3. On image traces, Qwen3.5-122B reaches 70.4 agent accuracy, GPT-5.4 reaches 65.2 step accuracy and 30.2 Joint, and Claude Sonnet 4.6 reaches 29.5 error F1. On video, GPT-5.4 reaches 85.1 agent accuracy, Claude Sonnet 4.6 reaches 64.4 step accuracy, Gemini 3 Flash reaches 40.0 error F1, and Llama-4 Maverick reaches 30.4 Joint. No model dominates every metric or modality.

The failure surface changes with modality. Figure 4 aggregates step accuracy at 69% for text, 62% for image, and 60% for video, whereas error-mode accuracy rises from 17% to 24% to 37%. Planning, verification, and coordination causes are frequently collapsed into reasoning symptoms. Trace length is another boundary: representative step accuracy falls from 94% below 3K tokens to 50% above 12K. These are author-reported results on unavailable rows and predictions, not independent reproductions.

Protocol ablations use a stratified subset of 1,444 traces. All-at-once gives the best Joint accuracy for 8 of 10 models and improves step accuracy by 7.6 percentage points over step-by-step. Relaxing exact localization to Step@1 produces average gains of 13.9 points on text, 16.9 on image, and 28.1 on video; the additional Step@2 gains are 4.4, 6.0, and 2.6. This indicates that many video misses are adjacent-step errors, while step-by-step tends to commit before the labeled error.

Ground-truth answers are not uniformly helpful. Section 4.5 and Appendix B show that giving the answer improves perception-error classification but degrades or leaves reasoning-error classification flat: the judge can shortcut to an outcome mismatch and skip the earlier root cause. Human validation independently exposes label uncertainty. Across 100 stratified traces and three annotators, majority approval is 94% for step, 90% for agent on multi-agent traces, and 90% for error family; 2% have no clear decisive error and Fleiss' kappa is 0.73.

The strongest artifact evidence is negative. Appendix A states that all traces, labels, and evaluation code are released, yet the official GitHub README says data and evaluation code are not public and the repository contains no implementation or rows. This discrepancy does not invalidate the paper's experiments, but it prevents verification of every result, split, label correction, evaluator decision, and scale claim beyond the manuscript.
