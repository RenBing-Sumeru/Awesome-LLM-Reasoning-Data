SCOP first teaches one 7B policy to emit successive reasoning, critique, and correction segments, then regenerates those segments on-policy before PPO updates. Pair-SFT is teacher-dependent: Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct rewrites reference solutions and annotates critiques/corrections for reasoning sampled from the initial policy. Post-validation retains an initialization item only if the annotator produces at least 6 correct corrections in 8 samples, producing the reported 60K examples. Thus “on-policy” describes the PPO refresh phase, not the full provenance chain. (Paper Section 4.1; Appendix B.2.)

The feedback contract attaches different signals to segment ends. A reasoning segment receives 1 when its extracted answer matches gold. For a critique, the policy samples `n` corrections and receives `m/n + h(c)`, where `m` corrections are correct; the main setting uses `n=8`. A correct correction receives `1 + alpha`, with `alpha=1`. Hallucination logic penalizes a critique that attacks correct reasoning or fails to flag wrong reasoning. The paper text defines `h(c)` as -1 or 0, whereas Appendix Table 8 and Table 10 select {-1, 1}; this unresolved specification difference matters for reproduction. (Paper Section 4.2; Appendix A.1 and B.3.)

The closest contrasts are Critic-CoT's iterative, earlier-policy critique synthesis and SCoRe/REFT's outcome-reward training. SCOP's specific change is to make reasoning, critique, and downstream repair samples come from the current policy and to score the critique through repair success. The Monte Carlo reward observes whether sampled corrections finish correctly, not whether the critique precisely identifies every faulty step, so it is process-positioned feedback rather than a process-correctness oracle.
