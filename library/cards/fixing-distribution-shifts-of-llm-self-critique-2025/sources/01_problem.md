The ACL 2025 paper targets a distribution mismatch in self-critique training. Static or iteratively synthesized critique data are generated from human-inserted errors, a teacher, or an earlier policy, but the trained model must critique errors from its current reasoning distribution. The paper's preliminary experiment shows models critique the previous round more effectively than their own current round, motivating a buffer refreshed by the policy being optimized. A second problem is reward hacking: final-answer supervision alone cannot distinguish a useful critique from hallucinated criticism followed by a lucky or failed correction. (Paper Sections 1 and 3, Figure 2.)

SCOP's data object is a branching mathematical self-play episode rather than an isolated critique. It joins a question and gold answer with current-policy reasoning, four critiques, eight corrections conditioned on each critique, extracted answers, a hallucination disposition, and stage-specific rewards. GSM8K and MATH plus Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct seed a 60K Pair-SFT initialization pool; cleaned Numina-CoT supplies later PPO prompts. The decision boundary is the construction of fresh feedback-bearing episodes, not a claim that self-critique is generally reliable or that outcome accuracy certifies process quality.

This belongs in the data-construction/open-release track because prompt filtering, teacher initialization, current-policy rollout refresh, reward attachment, and buffer lineage determine the training data. The official ACL paper and Appendices A-D provide L4 method evidence, but the paper-linked GitHub repository currently contains only MIT `LICENSE` and `.gitignore`. No dataset, trajectories, configs, checkpoints, or runnable implementation are released.
