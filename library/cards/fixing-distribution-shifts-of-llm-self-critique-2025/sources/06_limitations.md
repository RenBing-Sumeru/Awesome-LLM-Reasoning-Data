- **Teacher-dependent start:** Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct writes reference reasoning, critiques, and corrections and controls the 6-of-8 post-validation filter. On-policy refresh does not erase that initial style or selection bias.
- **Reward ambiguity:** Section 4.2 defines `h(c)=-1` for hallucination and `0` otherwise, while Appendix Table 8 and Table 10 use the selected set {-1, 1}. Without code, the implemented critique reward cannot be determined.
- **Conflated critique signal:** `m/n` measures whether the current correction policy can act after a critique. A precise diagnosis can score poorly when repair is weak; a vague critique can score well when the answer is easy to repair.
- **Coarse verification:** Final-answer matching does not prove intermediate reasoning or critique validity and may mishandle extraction or mathematical equivalence. The hallucination rule covers only obvious answer-correctness combinations, not subtle false criticism.
- **Model-relative filtering:** Removing policy-8/8-correct and annotator-0/8-correct prompts creates a middle band tied to two checkpoint revisions. It excludes mastered behavior and questions beyond the annotator, limiting coverage and comparability.
- **Compute burden:** Four critiques times eight corrections consumes about 1.92M generated tokens per optimization step, and a full run uses about 960 A100 GPU-hours. The authors also identify Monte Carlo scoring cost as a limitation.
- **Collapse and gaming risk:** Sparse/noisy reward can cause policy collapse on problems without labeled solution processes. PPO may exploit answer extraction, the hallucination rule, or correction bonuses; no adversarial reward audit is reported.
- **Scope and attribution:** Experiments cover two 7B models and mathematical/physics-style benchmarks. Larger models and other domains are untested, while gains combine teacher initialization, prompt filtering, on-policy refresh, reward shaping, and PPO.
- **Release failure:** At verified HEAD `4a24ea4`, the official repository contains only MIT `LICENSE` and `.gitignore`. Pair-SFT rows, Numina prompt manifests, rollouts, failed branches, rewards, prompts as files, configs, dependencies, and checkpoints are absent.
- **Audit gaps:** Generated-asset licensing, source-version lineage, decontamination, evaluation overlap, seeds, and exact answer-verifier behavior remain unknown. The MIT repository license does not cover artifacts that were never released.
- **Reporting discrepancy:** Table 1 yields a 3.66-point Qwen greedy advantage, whereas the prose reports 3.65. This is small but reinforces the need to cite table conditions rather than headline deltas.
