Prior self-critique recipes either synthesize critiques from injected or earlier-policy errors, train on a static teacher corpus, or apply outcome rewards to the final answer. Critic-CoT represents iterative critique-data synthesis; SCoRe and REFT represent outcome-supervised reinforcement learning. These baselines can improve correction but do not jointly ensure that the reasoning being criticized and the critic/corrector come from the current policy distribution.

SCOP's concrete change is a refreshed full-episode object plus stage-specific feedback. The same current policy produces reasoning, critique, and correction; multiple downstream repairs turn critique usefulness into a Monte Carlo reward; and a rule handles two obvious hallucination patterns. This makes data freshness, critique sampling, correction sampling, and reward placement independently auditable construction variables.

The work does not introduce a new mathematical truth source, remove teacher supervision, or validate critique steps. Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct still constructs and post-validates the Pair-SFT start, gold-answer matching remains the terminal oracle, PPO is existing machinery, and the RL prompt filter is model-relative. The paper's claim of avoiding synthetic data therefore applies at most to refreshed PPO rollouts, not to initialization.

For reasoning-data research, the useful direction signal is to compare policies trained on identical prompt and sampling budgets while varying buffer age and critique-feedback contract. Before reuse, builders should resolve `h(c)`, quantify verifier false decisions, preserve failed branches, and test whether the middle-difficulty concentration and high correction budget are necessary across other model sizes and domains.
