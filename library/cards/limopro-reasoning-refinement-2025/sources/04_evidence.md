For the LIMO source, the refined LIMO-P model improves AIME pass@1 from 56.7% to 63.3% while reducing average response tokens from 12,497 to 10,588. On AMC it moves from 91.9% to 93.8% with five percent fewer tokens, and on GPQA Diamond it moves from 67.2% to 71.2% with three percent fewer tokens. Across the reported datasets and source models, the paper summarizes accuracy gains of 0.9% to 6.6% and token reductions of 3% to 41%, with up to 71% improvement in its efficiency measure.

The results are not limited to one teacher source or one budget. The paper reports gains for data refined from DeepSeek-R1, Gemini Thinking, and QwQ, and presents scaling curves from 2k through 24k response-token limits. The LIMO-P curves maintain an advantage over the original LIMO at the shown budgets, supporting the claim that pruning removed low-value functional text instead of simply depriving the student of useful deliberation. The step-label validation is also relevant evidence, because the method's selective guarantee depends on separating progressive from functional content.
