LIMOPro separates four reasoning patterns. Progressive reasoning is the forward solution path and is always retained. Verification, multi-method validation, and error correction are functional patterns that may help, but can also be redundant. The key decision is therefore not to prune by surface length or a fixed position; it is to estimate how much a particular functional step changes the model's confidence in the answer, then remove only the least important eligible steps.

Perplexity-based Importance Refinement, or PIR, makes this decision quantitative. For a step, the method compares answer perplexity with the full reasoning chain and with that step removed. If the omission barely changes answer confidence, the step receives a low importance value and is a candidate for pruning. The resulting refined datasets are used to fine-tune models, so the claimed benefit appears at deployment time as shorter, more effective reasoning rather than as a one-off edit of a teacher response.
