PIR measures importance through the answer perplexity of a particular evaluator, not through a proof of causal necessity. Its step segmentation and functional labels can be wrong, and a low-PIR step can still matter for interpretability, robustness, or a future branch that the chosen answer does not reveal. Preserving all progressive steps may retain unnecessary text, while tuning a pruning ratio creates another trade-off. The method is evaluated on relatively structured reasoning traces and may transfer less cleanly to tool calls, interactive agents, or tasks where validation steps carry external safety value.
