The pipeline first segments a trace into coherent logical steps. It uses rule-based phrases to identify explicit verification and correction language, then uses Claude 3.7 Sonnet for contextual classification of ambiguous steps. Human evaluation of a random five percent sample reports unanimous agreement with the classification for 93.4% of steps. All progressive steps are kept. For each functional step, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct calculates answer perplexity with and without the step, and PIR ranks the eligible steps for selective removal.

The authors apply this to three sources: S1K with 1,000 Gemini Thinking traces, LIMO with 817 DeepSeek-R1 traces, and LIMO-V2 with 800 QwQ traces. They construct the corresponding refined sets and fine-tune models on them. Test-time evaluation measures pass@1 accuracy, average response tokens, and their efficiency ratio on AIME24, AMC23, and GPQA Diamond. It also varies response budgets from 2k to 24k tokens to check whether the refined models retain useful scaling behavior rather than merely performing well at one fixed length.
