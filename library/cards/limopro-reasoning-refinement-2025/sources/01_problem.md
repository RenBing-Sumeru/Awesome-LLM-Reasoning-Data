Reasoning models often inherit long chains of thought from powerful teachers. Those traces mix the progressive deductions that actually lead to an answer with functional behavior such as rechecking arithmetic, trying an alternative route, or correcting an earlier attempt. Functional behavior can be valuable for a human solver, but copying every instance into supervision makes a student verbose at inference time and raises latency, token cost, and the chance that a long response drifts away from the essential solution.

The paper asks how to shorten reasoning traces without treating all text as equally disposable. A generic compressor may delete the very deduction that establishes the answer, whereas simply training on the original traces makes a model carry redundant verification and repair loops into every response. The desired training record should preserve the logical backbone of the solution while removing only functional steps whose absence does not materially reduce confidence in the final answer.
