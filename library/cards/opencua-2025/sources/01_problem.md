Computer-use agents need more than isolated click labels: they must interpret a visual desktop, preserve task context over many steps, execute precise mouse and keyboard actions, recognize mistakes, and stop only when the goal is satisfied. Earlier resources often concentrate on one operating system, one application family, static GUI grounding, or synthetic environments, leaving limited open supervision for realistic cross-platform desktop work.

OpenCUA targets this data bottleneck with AgentNet, a corpus of 22,625 accepted human-demonstrated tasks on Windows, macOS, and Ubuntu. The paper reports more than 140 applications and 190 websites and summarizes the combined breadth as 200+ applications and websites. The construction problem is therefore not only collection at scale, but converting noisy low-level recordings into screenshot-action pairs, adding useful reflective supervision, and retaining enough provenance to audit privacy, rights, feedback quality, and replayability.

The central audit question is whether a released trace is valid for its intended use. A human action is not automatically optimal; a terminal task result does not validate every intermediate thought; an offline action match is not whole-task success; and a strong trained model is not proof that every data record is correct.
