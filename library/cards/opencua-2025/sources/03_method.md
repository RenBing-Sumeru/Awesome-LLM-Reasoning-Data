An episode begins with an English task instruction and an initial screenshot, followed by screenshots and PyAutoGUI-compatible actions such as clicking, moving, dragging, scrolling, typing, hotkeys, waiting, and success or failure termination. Annotators are drawn from student, crowdsourcing, and company pools; all reportedly sign consent. The paper prefers long tasks, rejects demonstrations shorter than five steps, separates Windows/macOS from Ubuntu collection, and states that Ubuntu tasks do not overlap OSWorld.

Raw recordings can contain thousands of input events. Rule-based Action Reduction merges scrolls, key sequences, hotkeys, drags, and double-clicks and inserts mouse movement preconditions where needed. State-Action Matching selects the frame immediately before pre-movement rather than the frame containing the final cursor location. The processed corpus contains 22,625 accepted tasks with a mean of 18.6 steps: about 12K Windows, 5K macOS, and 5K Ubuntu tasks. Table 2 separately reports 41,428 training trajectories for OpenCUA-7B/32B and 27,804 for Qwen2-7B/A3B, so task count must not be treated as the exact count of every training view.

The reflective pipeline labels each previous action as correct or incorrect and redundant or necessary. Steps judged incorrect or redundant are omitted from training. The released trajectory schema includes task identifiers and instructions; completion, alignment, efficiency, and difficulty fields; refined task descriptions; domain; and per-step screenshot, observation, thought, action description, action code, correctness, redundancy, reflection, step identifier, and marks. The official HF card documents multi-image chat formatting with loss only on the current assistant response.

Training mixes planning trajectories with GUI grounding and general SFT data.
