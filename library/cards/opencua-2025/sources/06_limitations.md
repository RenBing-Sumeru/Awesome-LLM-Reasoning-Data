The paper explicitly notes that human collection is costly and hard to scale, demonstrators may lack application expertise or optimal shortcuts, and consent introduces selection bias. A broad corpus can still underrepresent specialized enterprise workflows, accessibility modes, languages other than English, or tasks that annotators are unwilling to record.

Reflective CoT is post-hoc supervision. The generator sees the target action code, and the reflector sees both the before and after screenshots. The text may therefore encode the observed outcome or rationalize an inefficient action rather than recover the human's reasoning. No published calibration establishes step-level error rates for the Claude reflector or the faithfulness of generated thoughts.

Privacy controls are concrete but incomplete for audit. Recordings stay local until manual upload; annotators review video, actions, and accessibility trees; private tasks are rejected; internal humans and GPT-4o perform two-stage screening with human confirmation. Per-record consent and redaction logs, privacy prompts, detector error rates, and deletion procedures are not public. MIT declarations for model, dataset, tools, and code also do not settle third-party rights in website or application UI content, fonts, icons, or user material visible in screenshots.

Reproduction remains partial. The repository releases CoT generation and offline evaluation code, but lists the training pipeline as unfinished. AgentNet does not bundle immutable OS images, application versions, website states, credentials, locale, resolution, or reset scripts. The current HF viewer fails because trajectory and metadata JSONL files have different schemas, and several README paths have drifted from the live tree. Static Step SR can reject alternative valid actions, while online success varies with network, CAPTCHA, screen state, and application updates.
