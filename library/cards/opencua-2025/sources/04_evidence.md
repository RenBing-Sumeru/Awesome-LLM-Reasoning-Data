The release is substantial. The official repository provides AgentNet processing and visualization utilities, the reflective CoT generator, model inference support, and AgentNetBench evaluation. AgentNet is available as Windows/macOS and Ubuntu JSONL plus metadata and image archives, and OpenCUA model checkpoints are published. On the checked 2026-07-21 snapshots, GitHub was pinned to `dfc91ba89f700d10f26ec50362d308571482ab8b`, AgentNet to `d76ee50a63fad81cfdbe576416757d7c2091ed50`, and OpenCUA-7B to `a2efb7d2b104d477a4a2666a357e79550a28aafc`.

On 369 OSWorld-Verified tasks, OpenCUA-72B reports 39.0% success at 15 steps, 44.9% at 50 steps, and 45.0% at 100 steps; OpenCUA-32B reports 29.7%, 34.1%, and 34.8%. On the 100-trajectory AgentNetBench, OpenCUA-7B reports 79.0 coordinate, 62.0 content, 44.3 function, and 75.2 average Step SR; OpenCUA-32B reports 81.9, 66.1, 55.7, and 79.1. AgentNetBench includes manually refined alternatives, but it remains a static first-action metric rather than an executed terminal-state test.

A controlled Qwen2-VL-7B ablation trained on 14K Windows/macOS plus 3K Ubuntu examples improves OSWorld-Verified success from 11.5% to 15.3% when reflective CoT is added. This is evidence that the recipe can improve one model under one setup; it does not establish that each rationale is faithful or that benchmark performance measures record-level data quality. The 72B result is additionally confounded by mixed public and general data, a larger training budget, and 8K o3+Jedi rollouts.

Collection disclosure is also meaningful evidence: Appendix C.2 reports 634 annotators, 24,095 uploads before verification, roughly six months of work, about USD 20K in human annotation cost, USD 0.6 in CoT cost per task, and about USD 32K total. These numbers clarify scale and cost, but no independent recount or complete record-level provenance manifest is released.
