The prior-work baseline is iterative preference optimization that refreshes policy-generated data while treating the feedback source as fixed. Iterative DPO updates the policy and reference checkpoint; SPPO and related self-play methods compare self-generated responses; ReSTEM also uses an EM/self-training interpretation to improve a policy. Human-refresh RLHF collects new current-policy responses and pays for new preference labels. Self-Rewarding and Meta-Rewarding fold generation and LLM-as-a-judge capability into one model. ReST-MCTS* constructs feedback from search plus task ground truth. (Paper §2)

Mutual-Taught changes the identity of the feedback model during the same loop. Its E-step is recognizable on-policy DPO: five current-policy candidates are scored by the current RM and compressed into a length-controlled pair. Its M-step introduces a second dynamic data object: a comparison between the selected updated policy and its predecessor, filtered by an iteration-specific RM-score dispersion threshold and mixed with self-training pairs before RM retraining. This makes the preference dataset an interface between two checkpoints rather than a fixed input to one optimizer.

The new feedback interface has three operational parts. First, checkpoint selection attempts to prevent policy degradation before pseudo-pairs are constructed. Second, Low-Quality Data Filtering removes updated-policy responses that the existing RM regards as confidently worse while retaining mildly negative examples as regularization. Third, the RM is reinitialized from the base checkpoint for each refresh, so policy-derived data changes the learned RM without simply continuing its previous optimizer trajectory. These choices define the construction recipe and should be preserved in comparisons.

Several components are inherited rather than novel: UltraFeedback supplies prompts; Llama-3-8B-Instruct and FsfairX-Llama3-RM-v0.1 supply initialization; DPO supplies the policy objective; Bradley–Terry supplies reward modeling; Alignment Handbook and RLHF-Reward-Modeling are named as training frameworks; SimPO motivates sampling settings; and model-based judges supply evaluation. The paper's contribution is their coupled update and stabilization schedule, not a new preference semantics, human-label source, reward architecture, benchmark, or released dataset.

The EM label should be interpreted narrowly. Appendix D gives monotonic-improvement and stationary-point statements under exact E/M optimization and unbiased pseudo-label estimation. The neural implementation uses approximate DPO/RM optimization and RM-derived labels whose unbiasedness is not established. Consequently, the theorem explains the intended analogy under assumptions; it does not independently validate the practical pseudo-pairs or make the current RM a ground-truth preference estimator.

For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is that feedback-model refresh itself creates and selects data. A reusable lineage record must therefore include both policies, the RM checkpoint, all candidates and scores, the selection set, the filter threshold, accepted and rejected pairs, and iteration identity. Before reuse, a builder must inspect pseudo-label error against independent human judgments, RM calibration and gaming, split isolation, versioned configurations, and the missing implementation. Without those checks, Mutual-Taught is a mechanism comparison and audit case, not a ready-made dynamic preference release.
