Mutual-Taught's contribution is a two-way data loop: the current RM turns on-policy responses into DPO preferences for the policy, then the observed policy change is turned into pseudo-preference data for refreshing the RM. The authors describe the alternation as EM-inspired. The unknown latent object is an optimal human-preference response distribution; the implementation uses an evolving policy as its surrogate and a learned RM as its only operational preference signal. (Paper §4.1–4.2, Figure 2)

In the E-step, the previous policy samples responses and the current RM ranks them. DPO moves the policy relative to the previous checkpoint. Saved checkpoints are evaluated against that preceding policy on a fixed 2,000-prompt set, again using the current RM, and the highest RM-scored win-rate checkpoint is retained. In the M-step, the retained policy and its predecessor each generate a response for the same reward-update prompt. The method provisionally labels the updated response preferred, computes its RM margin over the previous response, and applies Low-Quality Data Filtering before Bradley–Terry reward training. (Paper Eq. 4–8; Appendix B–C)

The feedback system observes text responses and scalar RM scores. It can rank candidates under the current RM, estimate the standard deviation of that RM's scores, and detect when the updated response is strongly worse according to the same RM. It cannot directly observe human preference, factual correctness, safety, task success, or whether a higher score is caused by reward exploitation. It also does not independently validate the pseudo-label direction: the model being refreshed supplies the scores used to select and filter its own future training records.

The direction represented here is policy–reward-model co-adaptation under distribution shift. Iterative DPO, SPPO, and ReST-style methods also generate training data from an evolving policy, but commonly leave an external RM fixed or update only the policy. ReSTEM is also framed through self-training and EM, while Mutual-Taught's stated distinction is the explicit M-step that refreshes a separate RM. Self-Rewarding and Meta-Rewarding instead make the policy model act as an LLM judge and require strong judgment capability; Mutual-Taught retains a separate scalar RM. ReST-MCTS* uses ground-truth-checkable search data, unlike this judgment-only loop. (Paper §2)

The concrete difference is therefore not DPO, Bradley–Terry loss, checkpointing, or pseudo-labeling individually. It is the construction contract that couples them: on-policy RM labels train the policy; updated-versus-previous policy behavior trains the RM; model selection and variance-aware filtering attempt to stabilize both directions. The contract remains circular rather than ground-truth anchored, which is the most important boundary when comparing it with rule-verified or newly human-labeled refresh pipelines.
