The authors explicitly note two modeling limits: complex logical reasoning and long dependencies may converge slowly, and unrestricted iteration may over-optimize. Appendix H confirms an observed boundary rather than only a hypothetical one: iteration 4 lowers AlpacaEval-2 LC from 44.9 to 40.3 before the 60% model-selection threshold returns the prior policy. The threshold is tuned to stay above chance while allowing continued updates; its transfer to other policies, judges, or domains is not established. (Paper Limitations; Appendix H)

The central label assumption is self-referential. The updated response is marked preferred because the policy was optimized against the current RM, not because a human or task oracle compared the two responses. That same RM ranks the five E-step candidates, chooses the policy checkpoint on `D_MS`, computes the M-step margin, and supplies the uncertainty scale used for filtering. A blind spot or exploitable style preference can therefore affect every gate. This creates false-positive pseudo-pairs when high RM score does not reflect human preference and false negatives when useful policy changes score below the threshold. Reward hacking, shared drift, and confirmation bias are curator-inferred risks supported by the dependency graph, not measured failure rates.

Low-Quality Data Filtering is deliberately permissive. It discards only pairs with `delta_r <= -epsilon_t`; pairs whose updated response scores slightly below the preceding response remain as regularization. Their true label-noise rate is unknown, and Appendix F shows that alternative filtering rules trade AlpacaEval-2 policy performance against RewardBench RM performance. The variance threshold measures dispersion of current-RM scores, not calibrated probability that a pair direction is correct. Length control introduces another shortcut channel because chosen responses are the shortest among above-average-scoring candidates.

The theoretical guarantee has stronger premises than the experiment verifies. Appendix D assumes exact optimization in both steps and unbiased pseudo-label estimation, then argues monotonic expected-reward improvement. No independent evidence establishes either premise for DPO, neural RM fitting, or updated-versus-previous pseudo-labels. RM-based checkpoint selection can guarantee improvement only under the RM criterion being measured; it cannot by itself establish improvement under latent human preference.

Data and release auditability are incomplete. The paper does not disclose immutable UltraFeedback record IDs, exact subset counts, whether the 2,000 selection prompts are disjoint, decontamination, generated-token totals, seeds and variance for the main tables, accepted/discarded pair counts, pseudo-label accuracy, reward calibration, or full run logs. No dynamic pair stream or failed samples are released. The paper-linked GitHub repository declares Apache-2.0 but currently contains Alignment Handbook material rather than Mutual-Taught code, configs, data, or checkpoints; that license cannot be treated as a license for absent method artifacts or upstream data.

Evaluation also has a circular or proxy character. GPT-4-Preview-1106 judges AlpacaEval-2 and the in-distribution RM comparison; RewardBench evaluates agreement with benchmark preferences. These are useful external surfaces, but neither directly audits the training pair labels. The main instruction-following gains coexist with drops on GSM8K and MMLU and with non-monotonic RewardBench subcategories. Benchmark performance therefore supports a conditional training-recipe result, not a claim that the constructed data is high quality, unbiased, uncontaminated, or broadly transferable.
