The strongest policy result is the controlled comparison in Table 1 under the paper's Llama-3-8B-Instruct and UltraFeedback setup. The base model scores 23.1% length-controlled (LC) win rate on AlpacaEval-2 and 20.6% win rate on Arena-Hard. Mutual-Taught Iter1 reports 38.4% and 33.9%; Iter2 reports 54.1% and 38.4%. The strongest displayed iterative DPO baseline after three iterations reports 47.2% LC on AlpacaEval-2 and 34.7% on Arena-Hard. Mutual-Taught uses only two thirds of the prompt portions for policy updates, whereas iterative baselines use all three portions and three iterations. AlpacaEval-2 uses GPT-4-Preview-1106 as baseline and judge; Arena-Hard uses GPT-4-0314 as baseline and GPT-4-Preview-1106 as judge. (Paper §5.1–5.2, Table 1; Appendix A)

Reward-model evidence is mixed rather than uniformly positive. On RewardBench, FsfairX-Llama3-RM-v0.1 scores 84.7 average and Mutual-Taught Iter1/Iter2 score 85.8/87.0; GPT-4o-2024-08-06 is listed at 86.7. The Reasoning component rises from 86.4 to 95.8 and 95.7, but Chat moves from 99.4 to 98.3/98.2, Chat-Hard from 65.1 to 63.9/66.3, and Safety from 87.8 to 85.1/87.8. For in-distribution evaluation, the two-iteration policy generates responses for 2,000 randomly sampled UltraFeedback test prompts and GPT-4-Preview-1106 judges pairwise RM choices; Figure 3 reports progressively better updated-RM comparisons, but this is another model-judged evaluation rather than a human pseudo-label audit. (Paper §5.2, Figure 3, Table 2)

The data-type ablation directly supports the mixed-data construction claim. The paper compares RM training on self-training data, policy-comparison data, and their mixture. Using self-training alone reduces downstream policy performance relative to the mixture by 6.3 points on AlpacaEval and 3.5 points on Arena-Hard; policy-comparison alone also underperforms the mixture, while the paper reports a smaller policy effect and some RM deterioration. Appendix F compares filtering strategies: the adopted Low-Quality Data Filtering is better on AlpacaEval-2, whereas High-Quality Data Selection and Direct Self-Training are slightly better on RewardBench. This is a task-dependent trade-off, not evidence that one filtering rule produces universally correct pairs. (Paper §5.3, Figure 4; Appendix F, Figure 7)

Two additional results define the generalization boundary. When the iterated RM guides one DPO iteration for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 on UltraFeedback, AlpacaEval-2 LC rises from 42.0 with the base RM to 45.5 with Iter1 RM and 46.8 with Iter2 RM. However, the additional Open LLM Leaderboard table shows Mutual-Taught below the base model on GSM8K (70.67 versus 75.21) and MMLU (64.13 versus 65.71), while improving HellaSwag (81.37 versus 78.48) and TruthfulQA (55.21 versus 51.64); the average changes only from 67.76 to 67.85. These results do not establish broad reasoning improvement. (Paper Table 3; Appendix G, Table 4)

The extended-iteration experiment supplies an important negative result. Dividing the original 40,000 policy prompts into four portions produces AlpacaEval-2 LC scores of 34.7, 41.0, 44.9, and then 40.3. At iteration 4, the selected checkpoint's model-selection win rate is 57.7%, below `tau=60%`, so early stopping returns the iteration-3 policy. This shows the stabilization rule reacting to observed degradation; it does not prove that the RM-based validation criterion always detects true preference deterioration. (Appendix H, Table 5)

All numbers above are author-reported and were not independently reproduced for this Card. They support a policy/RM co-training recipe under the reported model, prompt source, judges, and budget. They do not measure human correctness of the generated preference pairs, retained-versus-discarded pair precision, RM calibration, contamination, or release quality. Artifact inspection further weakens reproducibility: the official paper prints a GitHub URL, but its only current branch is an Alignment Handbook snapshot with no Mutual-Taught-specific implementation, configs, data, checkpoints, tags, or releases.
