The authoritative record is the ACL Anthology version published in the Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, Volume 1: Long Papers, July 2025, pages 16285–16298. The paper and its appendices ask how a learned reward model can remain useful after an optimizing policy moves away from the response distribution on which that reward model was trained. Repeated human reannotation would address the shift but adds cost; keeping the RM fixed leaves a reward-hacking and miscalibration channel. (Paper §1; ACL record)

This work belongs in `data_construction_open_release_recipes` because its central object is not a new static corpus but a recipe for constructing changing preference views during training. One E-step record consists of an UltraFeedback prompt, five responses from the current policy, current-RM scores, and a length-controlled chosen/rejected pair for DPO. One M-step record consists of a reward-update prompt, a response from the selected updated policy, a response from the preceding policy, their current-RM scores and margin, the iteration-specific standard-deviation threshold, and an accept/discard decision for a pseudo-preference. The main RM update also mixes these policy-comparison records with self-training preferences from the policy update. (Paper §4.2–4.3, §5.1, §5.3; Appendix B–C)

The paper does not solve general preference acquisition, provide new human ground truth, or establish that a learned RM is a sound verifier. It studies synthetic, pairwise feedback for instruction-following policies initialized from Llama-3-8B-Instruct and FsfairX-Llama3-RM-v0.1 on UltraFeedback. The feedback contract is judgment-based: the current scalar RM determines candidate order, checkpoint selection, pseudo-pair filtering, and subsequent training data. That makes the method relevant to preference learning and reward modeling, but not evidence for programmatically verified outcomes, SFT data, or a generally correct reward oracle.

Atlas inclusion is useful because Mutual-Taught makes reward-model refresh part of data lineage: policy changes create new comparisons, and those comparisons change the model that will label the next policy data. The L4 content boundary is the complete ACL paper, appendices, official bibliographic records, and inspection of the GitHub URL printed in the paper. The mechanism and reported tables are traceable, but training reuse is blocked: the official link currently resolves to an Alignment Handbook snapshot rather than a verified Mutual-Taught implementation, and no dynamic pairs, rejected-pair logs, configs, checkpoints, or split manifest were found.
