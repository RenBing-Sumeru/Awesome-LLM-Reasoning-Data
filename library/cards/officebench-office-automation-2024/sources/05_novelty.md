The prior-work baseline is agent-environment, tool-use, and benchmark-harness papers in the same atlas category. The change introduced by OfficeBench: Benchmarking Language Agents across Multiple Applications for Office Automation is the particular task object and feedback contract: office tasks, app states, document/spreadsheet/email artifacts, actions, and evaluator outputs scored by OfficeBench evaluator and multi-application task success checks. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19056; project: https://github.com/zlwang-cs/OfficeBench; code: https://github.com/zlwang-cs/OfficeBench; data: https://github.com/zlwang-cs/OfficeBench; doi: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.19056), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
