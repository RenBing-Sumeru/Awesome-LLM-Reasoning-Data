The 26K scale supports comparisons across four task families. The main analysis finds that explanation quality depends on both task and perceived difficulty, and that low-quality LLM explanations more often suffer from lack of concision than from cohesion or word choice. Differences between humans and judges also show that no single automatic evaluator is absolute ground truth. These results support the central claim only under the released data, model scales, candidate budgets, and evaluation protocols; cross-domain use requires new validation.
