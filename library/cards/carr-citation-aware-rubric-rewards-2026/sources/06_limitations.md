First, labels in CaRR data and training protocol still depend on humans, environments, executors, or model judges, so missed cases and bias become reward blind spots. Second, although the release contains complex search questions with decomposed single-hop rubrics, citations, and chain-level rewards, coverage of languages, environments, robots, tasks, and model sources remains limited and public release creates contamination risk. Third, offline scores do not automatically become real RL or deployment gains. Reuse should audit deduplication, agreement, false positives, unseen-environment generalization, licensing, and safety boundaries.
