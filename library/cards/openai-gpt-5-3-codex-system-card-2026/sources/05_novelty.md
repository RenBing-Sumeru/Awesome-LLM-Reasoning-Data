The useful novelty for this atlas is disclosure precision, not a claim that the intervention itself is unprecedented. Many system cards mention safety training at a method-family level. This report exposes a more specific causal target inside RL: introduce conflicting user edits during rollouts and positively reinforce the model when it does not revert them. That makes the target behavior and high-level feedback direction legible.

The disclosure stops before a data or verifier contribution. It does not define the unit of an edit, the provenance of the underlying task and repository, the user-model policy, the trajectory schema, the detector for reversion, the reward scale, the aggregation rule, or the relationship to other objectives. Nor does it release the intervention cases. Consequently, the Card should not characterize the system card as an open safety dataset, a new verifier, a complete RLVR recipe, or a reproducible coding-agent environment.

The closest comparison for Track 12 is a frontier system card that lists broad alignment methods without an inspectable behavior-level condition. GPT-5.3-Codex narrows that condition, but its reuse value remains an audit checklist: future reports can be compared on whether they disclose prompts, repository states, edits, trajectories, reward decisions, environment versions, and lineage.
