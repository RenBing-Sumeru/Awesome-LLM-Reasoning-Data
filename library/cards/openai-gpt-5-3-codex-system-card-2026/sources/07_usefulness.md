For the Frontier Reports and Data Disclosure Ledger track, this Card provides a bounded comparison unit: one explicit behavior target, one explicit direction of reinforcement, one separately named evaluation, and a long list of undisclosed construction fields. It helps readers avoid collapsing a model-specific training statement, a CLI prompt, product sandboxing, monitors, red teaming, and benchmark harnesses into a single imagined safety-data pipeline.

The current disclosure can support comparative audits of system cards and the design of future reporting checklists. A reusable package would need task and repository identifiers with revisions and rights; user-model identity and prompts; time-ordered edits, observations, actions, tool calls, and model responses; the edit-preservation detector or judge; reward values and aggregation; accepted and rejected trajectories; environment and tool versions; sampling and optimizer metadata; split and decontamination records; and item-level lineage to checkpoints and evaluations.

It cannot support training a coding agent, reproducing the intervention, building a reward model, validating a verifier, or replaying an environment. It also should not be used to infer that the broader GPT-5.3-Codex post-training mixture is known. The destructive-actions evaluation is useful as evidence that OpenAI measured the targeted behavior, but its result is not evidence that the hidden training records, reward implementation, or overall data mixture are high quality.
