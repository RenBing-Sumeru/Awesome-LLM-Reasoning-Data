The data-facing novelty is the disclosed composition of adaptive reasoning supervision, deliberation-driven data repair, and a broad multi-service reward layer. Correct short attempts are retained, failed attempts are extended by Hunyuan-T1, and the combined histories train a teacher that can choose between short and long reasoning. A multi-model competition then turns judge and expert findings into targeted SFT additions. Finally, two GRPO stages consume domain-specific rewards rather than one universal correctness signal.

The reward disclosure is unusually broad for a frontier report. Reference-conditioned generative judgment, binary answer consistency, tool-using critique, executable-code tests, classifiers, refusal heuristics, hallucination checks, action-level rules, and domain score fusion are described within one system. The report also gives separate counts for GRM human labels, executable code, and two RL stages, which helps prevent them from being collapsed into a single vague post-training corpus.

What is not new should remain explicit. SFT, teacher-generated data, rejection sampling, pairwise reward modeling, ensemble judging, sandbox execution, GRPO, KL regularization, and dynamic sampling all have precedents. The distinct contribution is their reported orchestration around an adaptive long-short model and large proprietary infrastructure, not a newly released dataset, verifier, or optimizer implementation.

For the atlas, this is a direction signal and a disclosure-ledger case, not proof of an open recipe. Its reuse value comes from the stage decomposition and feedback taxonomy. Full reproducibility still requires immutable records, source and rights manifests, generator and judge revisions, reward calibration, rejected outputs, executable environments, rollout histories, code, and weights.
