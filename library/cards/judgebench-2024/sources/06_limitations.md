Correctness holds only relative to the source task and the constructed pair label. A judge can score well by exploiting artifacts in the pair distribution, and a low score may reflect unfamiliar source domains rather than a universal inability to judge. The public release is small enough to be memorized or contaminated. API judges can drift over time, local reward models depend on tokenizer/model revisions and GPU settings, and order-swap decisions must be reported consistently. The benchmark should not be read as a general human preference evaluator or as evidence that a judge is safe to use as a training reward in unrelated open-ended tasks.
