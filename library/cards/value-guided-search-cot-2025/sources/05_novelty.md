The prior-work baseline is step-delimited process supervision: define reasoning steps, obtain human, LLM-judge, or repeated-rollout labels for those steps, train a PRM, and use it to rank candidates. Standard test-time baselines then sample full responses and choose by majority voting, weighted voting, or best-of-N. Both interfaces are costly for long CoT: dense step annotation scales poorly, while full-response sampling spends budget before selection can prune a path.

VGS changes the supervision object. A training example is a randomly cut roll-in plus a completion from one fixed policy, and the final three-class outcome is applied across the rollout tokens. The learned score therefore estimates policy-conditional completion success from arbitrary token prefixes rather than correctness of manually segmented steps. Search consumes that score at coarse 4,096-token boundaries, so the data-generation unit and the inference selector share a prefix-value interface.

The new element is not final-answer verification, regression via classification, beam search, DVTS, or voting individually. Nor is the paper’s main contribution simply collecting more CoT. Its concrete change is the combination of (1) multi-model roll-ins for prefix diversity, (2) fixed-policy rollouts for a defined value target, (3) dense token-prefix supervision from terminal labels, and (4) block-wise selection under an explicit generation budget. The 2.5M-pair release makes that interface inspectable, although its token-ID packaging and missing rejects limit some analyses.

For this track, the direction signal is that rollout data can train a selector for future search rather than only supply accepted SFT traces or a pass@k statistic. The approach also exposes a reusable audit question: a value target is conditional on the rollout policy and filter distribution, so changing generator strength, prompt mixture, or failure retention can change what the same score means.

Before reuse, compare against full-response majority voting and a matched-budget selector, verify `math-verify` edge cases, measure calibration across generator sizes, retain all-failure groups in a separate audit split, and record full branch decisions. Performance improvements alone do not establish that the token-level targets represent semantic reasoning progress.
