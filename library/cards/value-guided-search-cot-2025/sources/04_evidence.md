All numbers below are author-reported or read from the official releases; no independent reproduction was verified. They support the paper’s data-and-search pipeline under the stated math settings, not a general claim that the released traces are intrinsically high quality.

- **Release scale and label conditions.** Paper §2.2 reports 2.8 million roll-in/roll-out pairs before removal of all-failure prompt groups and about 2.5 million afterward. The official OpenR1-VM viewer exposes 44,509 train rows, each with 56 rewards, roll-in token sequences, rollout token sequences, processed answers, and labels. This validates the serialized scale and grouping, but not label correctness beyond the `math-verify` contract.
- **1.5B generator at \(N=256\).** Appendix C.1, Table 2 reports a four-benchmark average accuracy of 45.7% for VGS with DeepSeek-VM-1.5B across AIME-24/25 and HMMT-24/25, versus 39.7% for majority voting. On the two 2025 test sets alone, Table 1 reports 39.8% versus 31.6%. These comparisons share the reported inference budget; they do not measure the precision or calibration of token-level values.
- **Larger generators and distribution shift.** Table 2 reports averages of 56.4% for VGS versus 50.5% for majority voting with the 7B generator at \(N=128\), and 61.0% versus 59.6% with the 14B generator at \(N=64\). The narrowing 14B margin is consistent with the authors’ limitation that a value model trained under the fixed 1.5B rollout policy can become out of distribution for stronger generators.
- **Efficiency.** The paper reports average response length of 11,219 tokens for VGS versus 12,793 for the base DeepSeek-1.5B response, a reduction above 12% (Appendix C.7, Figure 15). Appendix H estimates classifier overhead at 0.024% of the 1.5B generator’s FLOPs. The latter is an analytical approximation and excludes some real systems costs.
- **Ablations and negative evidence.** Reported AIME-24 sweeps favor 4,096-token blocks and beam width 2; DVTS helps at larger budgets, and random block selection underperforms value guidance (Paper §4, Figures 6–8). A qualitative rejected beam contains a critical error yet still receives value 0.337. These results show that the selector affects search, while also documenting that its score is neither a proof nor a calibrated guarantee for each trajectory.

The public repository contains value-model training and block-search code, and the model and both datasets are downloadable. However, benchmark gains cannot establish upstream provenance, completeness of filtered-out records, correctness of every label, or the quality of missing discarded search branches. Those remain separate audit questions.
