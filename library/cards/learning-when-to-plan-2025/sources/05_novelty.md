ReAct and fixed-frequency plan-and-act agents allocate reasoning by a constant rule, while classical planning systems often use a separate planner or explicit search procedure. This work instead teaches one LLM to expose a binary, per-timestep compute decision through optional natural-language plan emission. Its controlled SFT comparison uses identical action sequences with or without plan text, and PPO subsequently couples the decision to environmental consequences. This composition makes planning allocation observable in the trajectory and adjustable through token-cost penalties.

The individual ingredients—natural-language plans, behavior cloning, PPO, sparse environment reward, fixed-frequency ablations, and plan-conditioned action generation—are not new. The direction signal is their use to learn when to regenerate a plan rather than requiring a plan before every action. For this atlas, a reusable object would preserve the observation/history, previous plan, decision to plan, new plan tokens, action, token cost, environment transition, reward, and later replanning event. Benchmark progression is evidence for the learned policy under Crafter/BALROG, not a data-quality label. The absence of released SFT/RL records prevents checking whether the apparent adaptive behavior is broad or concentrated in particular prompt styles, intervals, seeds, or achievements.
