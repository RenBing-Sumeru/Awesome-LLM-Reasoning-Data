The zero-shot study evaluates Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct over 100 seeds in each of POGS and Crafter while varying fixed planning frequency. Performance is non-monotonic in output-token cost: intermediate frequencies outperform both never planning and always planning, and the POGS always-plan condition backtracks most. This establishes a compute/performance trade-off for the evaluated prompts and environments; it does not by itself show that a model can learn state-dependent allocation.

The training study compares dynamic-plan and action-only models across base-plus-PPO and SFT-plus-PPO conditions. The paper reports that explicit-plan SFT improves Crafter progression over the same action sequences without plans, that PPO after plan-aware SFT improves sample efficiency and reaches more advanced achievements, and that base-plus-PPO dynamic planning performs worse than base-plus-PPO action only. Qualitative traces show replanning when health is low and a failure where a valid-sounding iron-pickaxe plan omits checking furnace accessibility. In a separate human-steering Best-of-N analysis, the plan-aware SFT+PPO agent completes Crafter under human plans after about 20 attempts; autonomous agents do not complete it. These are method and policy findings, not proof that the undisclosed SFT trajectories, rewards, or planning decisions are uniformly correct. No public dataset, trained checkpoint, or currently inspectable code snapshot was verified.
