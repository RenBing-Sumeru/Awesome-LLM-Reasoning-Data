The one-sentence contribution is that ShoppingBench builds a real-world intent-grounded shopping benchmark and sandbox so LLM agents can be evaluated on complex shopping goals rather than simple product lookup. The core mechanism is to sample real products, derive grounded intents, simulate user instructions, expose shopping/search tools, and score whether the final trajectory satisfies product relevance and intent constraints.

The data object is a shopping instruction plus environment state, tool calls, retrieved product lists, product-detail observations, optional web-search observations, final answer/product package, and evaluation scores. The feedback contract is mixed but mostly environmental: product relevance, knowledge constraint, same-shop constraint, budget/coupon constraint, cumulative product relevance, and absolute success rate all tie the trajectory to concrete product and constraint checks.

Category rationale: this belongs in `environment_agent_trajectory_data` because it is an interactive shopping harness with trajectories and tool-use feedback. Closest comparisons are WebShop and other e-commerce/web-agent benchmarks; ShoppingBench differentiates itself by targeting richer grounded intents, a large sandbox, and a trajectory-distillation path from GPT-4.1-generated successful tool-use trajectories to a smaller Qwen3-4B agent.
