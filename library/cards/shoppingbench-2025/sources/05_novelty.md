The prior-work baseline is e-commerce benchmarks that mainly ask agents to find or purchase products under simpler intents. ShoppingBench changes the task from lookup to grounded shopping decision-making: agents must satisfy multiple product attributes, use long-tail shopping knowledge, find several products from the same shop, and reason about coupons and budgets.

The direction signal is that shopping-agent evaluation is becoming a constraint-satisfaction and trajectory-quality problem. A model can retrieve relevant products but still fail absolute success because an attribute is missing, the shop constraint is violated, a coupon does not apply, or the budget calculation is wrong. This makes the benchmark more informative for tool-use and failure analysis than a single final-answer match.

What is not new: synthetic user-instruction generation, tool-calling traces, SFT, and RL are established ingredients. The reusable contribution is the particular sandbox, intent schema, scoring contract, trajectory distillation protocol, and released code/data. Reuse checks should inspect product-data rights, contamination risk, live web-search drift, evaluator edge cases, and whether the released split remains stable.
