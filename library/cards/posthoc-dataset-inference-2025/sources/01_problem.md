The official ICML 2025 paper asks how a data owner can test whether an LLM was trained on a suspect text collection when no trustworthy, matched non-member set was reserved. Conventional dataset inference compares target-model membership features on suspect and known non-member data. Even slight topic, date, or document shifts can be mistaken for membership: on a 1,400-post single-author corpus, the original method reports p-values between 0.001 and 0.002 for post sets known not to be members.

This work belongs in `data_construction_open_release_recipes` because its central artifact is a recipe for manufacturing the missing reference set. One audit instance begins with suspect documents, segments and shuffles them, trains a generator on one sequence partition, and turns a shared prefix from another partition into a natural-suffix/synthetic-suffix pair. Target-model membership features and two classifier probabilities are then compared statistically.

The method does not establish record-level lineage, identify an exact training checkpoint or date, or decide ownership and infringement. Its result is a set-level hypothesis test conditional on the generator, target-model access, feature extractor, classifier, split, and significance threshold. The paper, appendix, code, and released Pile-derived archive support a detailed Card, but missing immutable versions, data licensing, checkpoints, and paper-run configuration justify retaining `partial` status and `L3_summary_ready` rather than upgrading curation state.
