The motivating failure is quantitative. On known non-member blog posts, conventional DI reports p-values of 0.002, 0.001, 0.001, 0.001, and 0.001 as the cap rises from 5 to 25 sequences per blog, falsely declaring membership in all five settings (Table 1). With the complete proposed pipeline, the single-author member set gives text-only/combined AUC 53.8/55.6 and p=0.01, while the non-member set gives 53.8/53.9 and p=0.13, yielding the intended member/non-member decisions (Table 3).

Suffix completion is the closest synthetic match among the generation methods tested. In Table 2, BoW/GPT2 AUC is 76.2/99.0 for ICL paraphrasing, 79.2/99.2 for ICL text completion, 50.2/58.9 for preference optimization, and 50.0/52.2 for suffix completion. These AUCs quantify distinguishability between natural and synthetic text; values near 50 do not prove semantic correctness, absence of memorization, or dataset rights.

On 16 Pile subsets with deduplicated Pythia-1B, every displayed member p-value is at most 0.04 and every non-member value is above 0.05, so Table 4 makes the correct binary decision for all displayed rows. The safety margin is uneven: non-member p-values are 0.06 for StackExchange, 0.07 for Github and EuroParl, and 0.09 for FreeLaw. Thus the surrounding prose claim that all non-member values exceed 0.1 is not supported by its own table. Appendix results also show correct decisions across Pythia 410M, 1.4B, 2.8B, and 6.9B, plus OLMo-7B on Wikipedia and Common Crawl, but remain within controlled known-split experiments.

Table 5 supplies both false-negative and false-positive ablations. ICL paraphrasing, ICL completion, preference optimization, no segment-and-shuffle, and no suffix comparison all give p=1.0 for member and non-member sets, missing membership. Without post-hoc calibration, both member and non-member produce p below 0.001, a false positive for the non-member set. The complete method gives p below 0.001 for the member and 1.0 for the non-member in that Pile ablation. Removing the weight constraint gives 0.004 and 0.43, which is correct in this aggregate case but weaker than the authors' constrained setting.

Figure 5 reports that fewer than 1,000 total queries reach significance for most datasets, while 2,000 total paired queries give the strongest consistency across all evaluated member sets. This is a power result under the paper's datasets and feature pipeline, not a guaranteed query budget for arbitrary models. The code and released JSONL archive increase auditability, but no independent reproduction is reported in the checked official artifacts.
