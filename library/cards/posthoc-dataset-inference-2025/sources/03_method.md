Inputs are suspect natural-language documents and a target causal language model. The single-author experiment collects 1,400 posts, removes figures, tables, videos, hyperlinks, and other non-text elements, and uses 450 posts as known members for one-epoch LoRA fine-tuning of deduplicated Pythia-410M. The Pile experiments treat train as member and combined validation/test as non-member. Selected subsets cover web, academic, biomedical, legal, code, email, multilingual, and chat text; four subsets described as potentially copyright-problematic are excluded.

For reference construction, documents are segmented into short sequences, capped per source document, shuffled, and split at sequence level. The paper reserves 2,000 sequences for generator inference and trains on the remainder; the repository additionally assigns indices 2,000–3,999 to generator validation. Meta Llama 3 8B is LoRA-finetuned by next-token prediction. Each inference sequence is divided at a shared prefix: its natural continuation becomes the suspect suffix, and the generator completion becomes the synthetic held-out suffix. Pile configurations use 32 or 64 tokens and per-document caps chosen by subset; the authors recommend selecting token length where the text-classifier AUC is smallest.

The generator uses LoRA rank 32, 100 epochs, 0.03 warm-up ratio, and a linear scheduler. The rendered appendix omits the numeric learning rate. Generation temperature, decoding budget, random seeds, exact target checkpoints, and paper-run outlier fractions remain unknown. The public Pile archive is described as four JSONL files per subset: member original, member generated, non-member original, and non-member generated. `text` stores clean text and additional entries store augmentations.

Calibration begins by splitting paired suspect and synthetic data into classifier-training and test portions. A randomly initialized two-layer GPT2-style text classifier receives only text; a combined classifier receives text plus target-model MIA features such as likelihood-derived scores. Both train for 20 epochs, while a positive-weight linear membership layer trains for 200 epochs. Positivity is enforced through a sigmoid parameterization so an inverted generation artifact cannot acquire a negative weight and mimic membership.

For each natural/generated pair, the pipeline computes a text-only separation and a combined separation. A one-sided difference-comparison t-test asks whether adding target-model membership features improves separation. The test is repeated under different random splits and combined with Sidak family-wise correction. At 0.05, rejection supports aggregate membership; non-rejection is inconclusive. The paper trains each classifier on 1,000 natural plus 1,000 synthetic items and tests on another equally sized paired set; it equates total test sample count with target-model queries.

Reproduction requires pinning the target-model weights, Meta Llama 3 8B revision, source-corpus snapshot, segmentation and caps, sequence shuffle, generator split, LoRA and decoding settings, MIA feature list, outlier policy, classifier architecture, random splits, test direction, and multiplicity correction. The current repository loads target weights locally and has no tagged release, so it does not establish operation through a closed sampled-text-only API.
