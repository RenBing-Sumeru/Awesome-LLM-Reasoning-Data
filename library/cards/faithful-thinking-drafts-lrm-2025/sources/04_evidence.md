Counterfactual experiments across six models show selective faithfulness within thinking drafts. Rewriting some steps substantially redirects later trajectories, while replacing other steps with conflicting content barely affects continuation. More importantly, models frequently fail to follow their own draft conclusions faithfully, and Draft-to-Answer dependence is weaker than surface consistency suggests. The findings show that long CoTs cannot be treated wholesale as monitoring evidence, although intervention strength and sampling randomness affect measurement. Model differences also show that longer drafts are not necessarily more faithful, so token count or self-correction frequency cannot serve as proxies. The experiment supports selective causal influence within drafts and frequent weak dependence of answers on draft conclusions. It does not prove that unchanged steps are useless because intervention naturalness affects outcomes.
