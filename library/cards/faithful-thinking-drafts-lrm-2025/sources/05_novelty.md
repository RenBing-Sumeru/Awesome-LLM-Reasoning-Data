Prior CoT-faithfulness analyses often delete whole explanations or ask after the fact whether information was used, conflating dependencies among draft steps with dependence between the draft and answer. This work separates the two causal questions and performs controlled interventions at specific positions, producing reproducible counterfactual trajectories. Its novelty is dual-level causal measurement and intervention data rather than a new reasoning model or textual correctness classifier. This shifts process monitoring from observing correlated text to asking whether model behavior would change if the step were different. Rewriting CoTs and observing answers is not new. The change is separating within-draft dependence from draft-to-answer dependence and generating repeated counterfactual outcome distributions at each position.
