Earlier scalar RMs provide efficient but fixed-dimensional rewards; critique-augmented or generative judges add text but are not necessarily trained to improve under repeated sampling. SPCT's main change is to make the evaluation principles themselves generated, condition them on the current query and candidate set, and optimize the joint principle–critique–score behavior with rule-based online RL. The Meta RM then treats sampled reward trajectories as objects that can be evaluated before aggregation.

For the atlas, this is a direction signal because verifier output becomes a search trace: multiple judgment perspectives, critiques, scores, and second-order selections contribute to one reward. A reusable release would expose that hierarchy rather than only final scalar scores. Generative critiques, preference labels, GRPO, voting, and learned reward models are not individually novel. The reported benchmark scaling is a quality signal for the end-to-end judge, not proof that the underlying principles, critiques, labels, or source mixture are independently correct.
