The paper states that generative RMs are substantially less efficient than same-scale scalar RMs and that DeepSeek-GRM can lag scalar models on verifiable tasks. Its possible use as a process reward model is suggested rather than established. Parallel sampling reduces wall-clock latency only when sufficient parallel hardware is available; total generated tokens and serving cost still grow. Meta RM guided voting adds another learned model whose distribution shift and failure correlations matter.

The 1,070K general-instruction component is in-house, and the reward mixture combines internal and public data without a complete manifest or per-item license lineage. Re-tagging preferences, rule-based MATH filtering, hinted sampling, and rejection of easy cases all alter the training distribution. Model-generated principles and critiques may rationalize existing bias or take shortcuts; the paper itself observes hinted-trajectory shortcuts and sensitivity to KL regularization. Released checkpoints improve artifact access, but raw examples, sampling failures, rejected trajectories, seeds, and code remain unverified. Benchmark gains therefore cannot substitute for data or verifier audits.
