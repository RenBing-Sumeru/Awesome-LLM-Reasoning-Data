The paper's primary operational evidence is its cross-benchmark evaluation run, not a claim that the released traces are high-quality training data. It reports **21,730 agent rollouts** over nine benchmarks and nine headline models, more than **2.5B tokens** of language-model calls, and approximately **USD 40,000** in evaluation cost (Abstract; §§1 and 3). Appendix A4 describes **186 runs**, with 142 model-scaffold-benchmark combinations in the main analysis and 44 incomplete-matrix additions. However, the nine per-benchmark run counts stated in Appendix A10 sum to **184**, leaving a two-run discrepancy that the paper or release does not reconcile.

The automated behavior analysis provides a separate evidence layer. Docent initially covers 48 model-scaffold pairs and 2,184 transcripts from AssistantBench, TAU-bench, CORE-Bench, and SciCode. After excluding TAU-bench from reliability/failure analysis, it covers 36 pairs and 1,634 transcripts (Appendix A7.1). For selected rubric/benchmark combinations, humans inspect at least 30 flagged runs. Table A2 reports precision of 0.87 for AssistantBench instruction following (`n=49`), 1.00 for CORE-Bench verification (`n=31`), and 0.94 for TAU-bench instruction following (`n=36`). These numbers validate precision only for selected positive flags; they do not measure false-negative rates or overall label accuracy.

The strongest negative result is an evaluation-integrity failure discovered through the trace-analysis path. The official TAU-bench Few Shot scaffold loaded actual benchmark test examples from `few_shot_data/MockAirlineDomainEnv-few_shot.jsonl`. HAL discarded approximately **USD 1,000** of compromised evaluation runs (Appendix A5). The paper also reports AssistantBench behavior in which agents locate benchmark answers on Hugging Face or arXiv. These incidents show that an evaluator can be satisfied through leaked or retrieved answers rather than the intended capability, and that logs can reveal contamination that a final score hides.

Release inspection supports the existence of concrete run and call records, but only within the checked scope. At Hugging Face SHA `e7dcedc82b4f4bc819a170fd6616bdb44841c71e`, the official repository contained **380 encrypted ZIP archives** plus `.gitattributes`, totaling **113,072,877,091 bytes**. Two archives were decrypted using the official mechanism. The inspected SciCode bundle retained 2 successful and 63 failed task identifiers with 288 raw Weave calls; the inspected CORE-Bench bundle retained 45 failed task identifiers and 57 task-linked logs. Both exposed configuration/results/evaluator/log/usage/cost/lineage material. This spot check demonstrates that both successful and failed outcomes can be retained; it does not prove uniform schema or complete preservation across all archives.

Three scale statements refer to different objects and must not be combined. The paper experiment reports 21,730 rollouts. The project front page displayed **26,597 rollouts** and nine benchmarks when verified on 2026-07-20. The current HF repository held 380 run archives totaling about 113 GB. An archive is a run bundle, not one rollout, and no frozen manifest maps the mutable project/release state back to every paper-era rollout. Benchmark scores establish relative evaluation outcomes under reported conditions, not release completeness, verifier correctness, replayability, or data quality.

The evidence is also statistically limited: high cost forced single runs without statistical validation for most evaluations (Appendix A3). GAIA and AssistantBench use public sets, SWE-bench Verified Mini uses 50 of 500 tasks, and the paper uses original TAU-bench rather than tau2-bench (Appendix A4.1). Docent failure flags are correlational; the authors state that showing causal repair would require checkpointing, correcting the flagged state, and replaying the remainder of the episode (Appendix A4.2).
