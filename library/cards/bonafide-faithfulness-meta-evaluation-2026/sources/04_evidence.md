Against BonaFide ground truth, most prominent metrics operate near chance and exhibit strong prediction biases, with performance generally degrading on longer CoTs. The best trace-level metric reaches only 0.70 AUROC and the best step-level metric 0.59; neither transfers reliably across tasks and models, while stronger intervention metrics require extensive additional sampling. The results show that high scores or mutual correlation do not demonstrate measurement of faithfulness and provide a reproducible baseline for new metrics. Even a slightly stronger but expensive metric may be unsuitable for online monitoring, so the paper compares accuracy, bias, transfer, and call budget together. The result supports that most current metrics are near chance on BonaFide and degrade with length. It cannot establish identical failure on every natural task because bottleneck tasks are engineered to recover truth.
