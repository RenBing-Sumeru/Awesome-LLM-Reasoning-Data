Existing CoT-faithfulness metrics estimate faithfulness by deleting steps, rewriting traces, or measuring answer changes, but lack directly verifiable ground truth. Researchers therefore compare correlations among metrics or report absolute values. Step importance, plausible explanation, and faithful computation are different properties, so a seemingly effective metric may consistently measure the wrong construct. The absence of truth creates a circular development loop in which correlation with prior metrics is treated as validation even when all metrics deviate from actual computational dependence. Without independent truth, metric development becomes circular: correlation with old metrics is treated as validation even when all measure importance, plausibility, or length bias rather than actual computational dependence.

This work designs tasks whose outputs reveal the necessary intermediate computation, automatically recovers step- and CoT-level faithfulness labels, and meta-evaluates existing metrics against that ground truth.
