The authors evaluate COCONUT, CODI, CoLaR, Latent-SFT, and Render-of-Thought backbones. At every latent step, MC dropout applies a randomly sampled dropout mask after feed-forward layers; Gaussian sampling adds a zero-mean perturbation with tunable scale. They vary the number of parallel trajectories from one to sixty-four and evaluate solution coverage, the latent equivalent of pass@k. Hyperparameters are tuned by coverage@64, with reported architecture-specific practical ranges for dropout and noise.

To train LatentRM, the authors sample latent trajectories and run multiple stochastic completions from every intermediate thought. The fraction ending in the correct answer becomes a thought-quality label. Rather than independent binary classification, the scorer uses a step-wise contrastive loss across candidates at the same position. At inference, cumulative scores guide best-of-N or beam search; beam width is square root of N so its decoding cost is matched to N independent samples. Experiments use GSM8K Test, GSM8K-Hard, MultiArith, and transfer tests on MATH500.
