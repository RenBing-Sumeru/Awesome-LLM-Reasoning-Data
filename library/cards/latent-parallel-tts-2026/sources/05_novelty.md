The new contribution is to make the two operations behind parallel scaling native to continuous reasoning. Earlier latent models can be efficient because they avoid verbalizing thoughts, but that efficiency normally comes with deterministic or weakly stochastic inference. MC dropout and Gaussian perturbation provide principled sources of alternative latent paths, while LatentRM supplies a scorer where token probabilities and textual process rewards are unavailable.

The contrastive training formulation is important because it evaluates thoughts relative to alternatives at the same reasoning depth. The paper does not claim that latent vectors become interpretable or that a latent reward is a proof checker. Instead, it establishes an operational contract: stochastic latent paths provide coverage, rollout-derived labels train a selector, and a matched aggregation budget tests whether more hidden-space computation improves final answers.
