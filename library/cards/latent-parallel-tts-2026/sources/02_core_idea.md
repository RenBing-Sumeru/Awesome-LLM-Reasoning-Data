The method supplies separate primitives for exploration and selection. Monte Carlo dropout keeps dropout active during inference and samples alternative effective weight configurations, representing epistemic uncertainty. Additive Gaussian Noise perturbs each latent thought with a controlled isotropic vector, representing input or trajectory uncertainty. Both mechanisms create parallel hidden-state paths without turning those paths back into long textual chains.

For selection, LatentRM extends the latent backbone with a scalar head that scores the promise of each intermediate thought. It is trained comparatively at every reasoning step, using the empirical correctness of stochastic completions as a quality signal. Cumulative step logits rank whole trajectories, enabling best-of-N and beam search. The framework therefore treats latent-space sample count as an adjustable compute budget, while retaining a model-native scorer for what cannot be inspected in language.
