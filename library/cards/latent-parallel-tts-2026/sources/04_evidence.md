Both stochastic mechanisms produce increasing coverage as more paths are sampled, with diminishing marginal gains at larger N. The framework also transfers beyond the small initial backbones. On MATH500, Render-of-Thought with a 4B Qwen3-VL backbone improves from 20.3% to 22.0% at N equal to 16. LatentRM produces gains for both best-of-N and beam search under matched budgets, while the analysis shows distinct exploration geometries: dropout tends toward structured directed expansion, whereas Gaussian noise broadens local diversity around the deterministic trajectory. The evidence supports scalable latent exploration, not a claim that any noise level is safe.
