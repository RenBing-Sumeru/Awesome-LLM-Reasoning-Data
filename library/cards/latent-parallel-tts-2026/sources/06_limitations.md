LatentRM training requires many stochastic completions to label intermediate thoughts, which can be costly and can inherit errors from the final-answer metric. Noise and dropout settings are backbone dependent; excessive perturbation can make trajectories incoherent, while too little produces redundant samples. The main evidence is arithmetic reasoning with relatively small latent backbones, and hidden-vector scores are difficult to audit for factual, safety, or tool-use failures. A gain in coverage also does not by itself establish a favorable wall-clock trade-off.
