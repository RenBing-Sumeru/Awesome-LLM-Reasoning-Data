Verifier-guided test-time scaling can improve a small reasoning model by expanding several partial solution paths and repeatedly scoring them. On an edge device, however, this search does not resemble ordinary single-sequence decoding. Paths finish a reasoning step at different times, dynamically share and diverge from prefixes, and require both a generator and a verifier to occupy a small memory budget. A naive serving stack therefore spends part of its inference budget waiting for stragglers, recomputing cached prefixes, or constraining batches because the two models compete for memory.

The paper asks how to preserve the accuracy benefit of verifier-guided search while making its latency and goodput acceptable on a single consumer GPU. Its target is not a new reward model or search objective. Instead, it studies the execution pattern shared by beam search and related tree-style methods, then asks how the available test-time compute can be scheduled so that useful reasoning work, rather than hardware idle time, consumes the budget.
