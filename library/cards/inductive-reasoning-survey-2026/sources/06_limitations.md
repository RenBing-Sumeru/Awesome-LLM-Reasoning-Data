The survey cannot make all cited scores directly comparable. Underlying papers may use different models, examples, tasks, definitions of success, and test-time budgets. Induction also has a built-in ambiguity: multiple general rules can agree with a limited set of observations, so a correct-looking output is not by itself proof that the intended rule was recovered. The survey does not supply one common dataset or evaluation harness that resolves these differences. Use it to frame a question and locate relevant work, then return to each primary paper for its data, protocol, and evaluation details. In particular, check how observations were sampled, which held-out cases were used, and whether alternative rules could explain the same examples. These checks limit overclaiming when transferring a conclusion between benchmarks.
