Use this paper when choosing an intervention for a task that requires extrapolating from examples, or when a model appears persuasive but its generalization is uncertain. Its taxonomy turns a broad design decision into a precise comparison: should you add examples, alter post-training, or allow more exploration at inference time? The answer should follow the failure you have observed, not a generic preference for one lever. Its benchmark discussion is also a starting point for checking whether a claimed gain measures generalization rather than recognition of familiar patterns. Before adopting a method, describe the available observations, the desired extrapolation, and the test cases that would falsify the proposed rule. The survey helps make those design choices explicit and helps prevent a result on one task from being treated as evidence for every form of induction.
