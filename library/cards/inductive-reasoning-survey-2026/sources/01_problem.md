Inductive reasoning asks a model to move from particular observations to a general rule. This is harder than matching a familiar pattern: with only a few examples, several rules can look plausible, and a useful answer must continue to work on cases that were not shown. The authors identify a practical gap. Induction matters for knowledge generalization, yet work on it had been scattered across improvement methods, task settings, and benchmarks rather than summarized in one account. This ACL 2026 long paper therefore surveys the literature; it does not introduce a new training set, a single model, or one universal score. For a reader, the central problem is to distinguish a model that has inferred a rule from one that has merely produced a convincing continuation of the observed examples.
