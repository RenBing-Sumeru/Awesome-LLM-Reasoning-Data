For `data_construction_open_release_recipes`, RSR provides a reproducible pattern for making trajectory selection conditional on the model that will consume the data. It is most useful when multiple teacher responses exist for the same prompt and the builder wants to preserve informativeness without choosing a trajectory that is wholly alien to the student distribution.

An auditable raw record should preserve the upstream prompt ID and revision, source subset and difficulty, teacher checkpoint/endpoint, generation run and seed, exact chat template and decoding settings, response, truncation status, and license lineage. A scoring record should add the student checkpoint and tokenizer revisions, rendered prompt, assistant-token span, per-token or aggregated clipped rank and surprisal, r_max, precision, RSR, correctness/parser output, and compute environment. A decision record should retain all 33 candidate IDs, complete ranking, selected ID, reject-all policy, and rejection reasons.

The public 165,000-candidate pool supports re-scoring for a new compatible student, teacher-style analysis, selector ablations, and construction of alternative 33-to-1 SFT sets. The five selected files can serve as examples of the final training object, but scores should be recomputed whenever the student or formatting pipeline changes. Builders should add an independent correctness gate when incorrect mathematical supervision is unacceptable, even though the reported aggregate ablation did not improve the mean.

For atlas readers, the work demonstrates why "best teacher," "verified answer," and "highest student likelihood" are different contracts. It also demonstrates the audit gap between releasing raw and selected data versus releasing the decision lineage that explains every selection. Downstream benchmark gains motivate the recipe; they do not by themselves certify the transformed dataset.
