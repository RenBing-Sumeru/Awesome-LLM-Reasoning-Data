Table 4 evaluates dataset-level association across only 11 teacher points per student. Absolute Spearman correlations between RSR and post-SFT performance are 0.855, 0.845, 0.918, 0.818, and 0.845 for the five students, averaging 0.856. The strongest listed non-RSR average is 0.65 for rule-based quality; verified accuracy averages 0.33. This supports RSR as a teacher-dataset ranking signal in the studied pool, but correlation does not prove that every low-RSR trajectory causes an improvement.

The component ablation is informative. Average correlation falls from 0.856 to 0.700 without rank clipping and to 0.391 without the surprisal-weighted aggregation; r_max = 50 gives 0.696 and r_max = 500 gives 0.822. Estimating each teacher from 200 trajectories gives 0.864. These results show that the exact aggregation and clipping contract matters, while also motivating the lower-cost teacher-selection setting.

For 33-to-1 trajectory selection, Table 6 reports that minimum-RSR sets achieve the best listed math averages for all five students: 78.6, 28.5, 53.2, 61.4, and 34.8. Random selection yields 70.2, 22.1, 45.7, 53.9, and 27.9; LLM-judged quality yields 73.4, 25.6, 51.8, 59.1, and 32.8. These are post-training benchmark results averaged over three fine-tuning seeds, not direct labels of individual trajectory quality.

The no-selection comparison is not a full 165,000-row control: it merges one run from each teacher into 55,000 rows. RSR's 5,000-row sets score 51.3 on average across students versus 52.5 for that 55,000-row baseline, with substantial student variation; LLaMA-3.1-8B favors the larger set (41.5 versus 28.5). Correctness-filtered RSR averages 50.7 versus 51.3 without the default filter, so the ablation does not show a mean benefit from correctness filtering, but it also does not establish that incorrect selected rows are safe or useful.

Teacher selection based on 200 samples per candidate produces an average 48.3 for the top two RSR-ranked teachers, close to the 48.7 oracle and above the listed alternative selectors. GPQA-Diamond evaluation averages 40.4 for RSR-selected math data, 36.6 for LLM-judged selection, and 35.3 for random selection, but the paper itself notes the out-of-domain results are less stable. The selected data are also highly concentrated: QwQ-32B supplies 45.1%-76.6% of rows depending on the student.

The official release verifies artifact existence and row-level message format, not complete decision lineage. Benchmark improvements do not prove mathematical correctness, contamination safety, license compatibility, teacher-output rights, or general utility beyond the five studied base students.
