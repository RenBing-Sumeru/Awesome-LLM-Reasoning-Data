The prior-work baseline is student likelihood: low surprisal or high log-probability favors familiar trajectories, while stronger teachers, longer responses, verified answers, rule-based scores, LLM judges, gradients, and influence estimates provide alternative selection signals. The paper's specific change is to combine absolute unfamiliarity with relative familiarity through a clipped rank-to-surprisal ratio computed under the intended learner.

The novelty is operational rather than a new supervision source. The same candidate trajectory receives a different score for a different student, turning "data quality" into a student-conditioned matching decision. The work then tests this decision at three resolutions: correlation over teacher datasets, per-prompt 33-to-1 trajectory selection, and low-resource teacher selection from 200 scored examples.

The release is also unusually useful for recipe study because it includes all 165,000 raw teacher trajectories and five selected SFT sets. However, it does not release a complete selection ledger connecting each chosen record to its teacher/run, RSR components, correctness outcome, rank among 33 candidates, and the 32 rejected alternatives. Open files therefore calibrate data volume and content, not full auditability.

What is not new must remain explicit: RSR does not introduce SFT, long-CoT generation, token surprisal, token rank, correctness verification, or the upstream mathematics prompts. It has no established theory and does not prove low RSR is universally better. A sound reuse test must compare exact-student RSR with likelihood, length, correctness, judge, and random controls under matched candidates and training budgets.
