Rank-Surprisal Ratio (RSR) is computed under the exact target student. For each assistant-response token t_k, surprisal is -log p_theta(t_k | context), while rank is one plus the number of vocabulary tokens assigned strictly higher probability. The paper clips rank at r_max = 100 and defines trajectory RSR as the sum of clipped token ranks divided by the sum of token surprisals. This is equivalent to a surprisal-weighted average of token-level rank/surprisal ratios; lower RSR is preferred.

The two terms encode the paper's "informative alignment" hypothesis. High surprisal supplies absolute unfamiliarity, so the trajectory is not merely imitating what the student already predicts. Low rank supplies relative familiarity, so the observed token remains near the top of the student's alternatives even when its absolute probability is low. A low ratio is intended to identify supervision that is novel without being wholly off-distribution for that student.

RSR is a suitability score, not a mathematical verifier, safety judge, universal reward, or model-independent quality label. The default 33-to-1 trajectory selector computes RSR for all candidates under one student and keeps the minimum; it does not require the selected answer to pass Math-Verify. Correctness filtering appears only in an ablation. Changing the student checkpoint, tokenizer, chat template, response span, or rank-clipping rule changes the scoring contract and requires recomputation.
