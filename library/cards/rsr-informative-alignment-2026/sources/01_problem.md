The official ACL Anthology record, ACL 2026 paper, author repository, and Hugging Face dataset are the primary sources for this Card. ACL Anthology records the work as a long paper in the Proceedings of the 64th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, pages 42123-42150, with DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1950.

Long-chain-of-thought distillation often chooses a teacher by model size, teacher benchmark score, likelihood under the student, or final-answer correctness. The paper shows why those shortcuts are insufficient: the same teacher's trajectories lead to different post-SFT outcomes for different pretrained base students, and strong teachers do not consistently produce the best student. The target question is therefore not simply whether a response is correct or globally high quality, but whether it is informative while remaining learnable for one exact student checkpoint.

The construction surface is concrete. A fixed 5,000-problem mathematics prompt set is paired with 33 candidate trajectory datasets generated by 11 teachers over three runs, giving 165,000 raw trajectories. For each of five students, one of the 33 candidates is selected per prompt to create a separate 5,000-row SFT dataset; together the public repository contains 190,000 rows. The work belongs in `data_construction_open_release_recipes` because it releases both a multi-teacher candidate pool and student-conditioned selected sets, while exposing how the selector, correctness, source lineage, and rejected alternatives must be audited separately. The Card remains L3 because important record-level provenance, contamination, and license-chain fields are incomplete.
