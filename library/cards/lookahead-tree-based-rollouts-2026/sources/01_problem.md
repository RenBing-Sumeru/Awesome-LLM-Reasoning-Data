Published as an ICLR 2026 conference paper, Lookahead Tree-Based Rollouts (LATR) addresses a specific bottleneck in online Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR): the several completions sampled for one prompt often differ locally but collapse onto nearly identical reasoning trajectories. In GRPO- or DAPO-style training, such homogeneous groups also have homogeneous terminal rewards, leaving little group-relative signal even when the sampling budget is eight full responses.

The paper treats rollout construction, rather than a new optimizer or reward model, as the intervention. For each Countdown or DAPO-Math prompt, the current policy builds a width-bounded dynamic tree: token alternatives create branches, short simulations reveal whether a child actually diverges from its parent, similarity pruning removes redundant branches and their descendants, and the survivors become a group of full completions. Countdown expressions receive a programmatic format/correctness score; math completions receive a binary exact-answer score. There are no human step labels, learned judges, or process-reward labels.

The online data object is therefore a prompt-conditioned group/tree of up to eight current-policy trajectories with token probabilities, parent and birth information, lookahead segments, prune/keep decisions, terminal answers, scalar rewards, and group-relative advantages. This object belongs in **Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data** because branching, selection, rejected paths, and a fixed rollout budget determine the training signal. It is not test-time tree search: the paper evaluates trained policies with repeated stochastic decoding, and LATR is used while producing on-policy RL groups.

The public Hugging Face artifact has a narrower object. Its four Parquet files expose processed Countdown and math prompt/reward records—fields such as `prompt`, `data_source`, `ability`, `reward_model.ground_truth`, and task metadata—but not generated completions, token probabilities, tree nodes, branch lineage, lookahead windows, pruning decisions, per-completion rewards, rejected paths, or policy-update membership. The Card reaches L4 because the full paper and appendices, official ICLR/OpenReview records, pinned implementation, and processed release support a detailed bilingual account. L4 does not certify missing raw trees, exact release manifests, decontamination, licenses, or agreement between the paper and released math scripts.
