The prior-work baseline in this paper is token-level stochastic sampling for group-based RLVR. Sampling different next tokens does not guarantee different trajectories because branches can reconverge into nearly identical continuations. Other tree-search methods often use search to choose an inference answer or derive step/process values. LATR instead changes the online rollout worker while retaining the terminal programmatic reward and GRPO/DAPO update.

What changes is the selection interface for a fixed response-count group. Candidate tokens must pass probability gates; a branch then has to survive trajectory-level lookahead and similarity pruning before it consumes the remaining completion budget. This makes branch birth, simulated continuation, prune/keep decision, surviving full response, and terminal reward the natural audit object. The public release stops before that interface: it provides processed task and reward inputs, not a serialized tree.

The individual components—token probabilities, branching, edit distance, stochastic completion, terminal verification, and group-relative RL—are not new in isolation. The paper-specific contribution is their integration into a hybrid online rollout recipe whose explicit aim is to prevent local sampling differences from collapsing back into the same reasoning path. Its ablations matter because arbitrary random branching, missing/random pruning, and token-level lookahead do not reproduce the same result.

For reasoning-data research, the direction signal is concrete: group composition can change the variance and usefulness of verifiable rewards even when prompt source, optimizer, terminal checker, nominal number of responses, and base policy are held fixed. This separates “how many rollouts” from “which trajectories occupy the rollout budget.” It also creates a new attribution requirement: gains can come from search topology and rejection policy rather than from a new corpus, verifier, or objective.

Before reuse, compare stochastic and LATR groups at matched generated tokens and wall-clock cost, report unique-path counts rather than only returned-row counts, audit semantic rather than lexical diversity, and retain pruned/rejected branches. The novelty claim should not be expanded into claims of new step supervision, a learned verifier, test-time compute scaling, or a released trajectory dataset.
