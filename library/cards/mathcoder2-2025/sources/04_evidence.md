The paper separates the 16.5B-token basic corpus from the 2.7287B-token translated-code addition. On Llama-3-8B continued pretraining, adding translated code reports 38.4% four-shot MATH and 69.9% GSM8K, improvements of 3.1 and 4.1 points over training on the basic corpus alone. Four different 7B/8B base families improve on the reported average across five mathematical benchmarks.

The authors then perform supervised fine-tuning: two epochs on general mathematical instructions followed by three epochs on NuminaMath CoT or TIR. Results support the claim that continued-pretrained checkpoints can enter later mathematical post-training, but they combine corpus, optimizer, SFT data, decoding, and evaluation choices. They do not prove that every released document is correct or that the public snapshot reproduces the paper corpus.

The execution evidence is weaker than the paper's prose suggests. Running teacher-generated Python can reject syntax and runtime failures, but the expected result and program share one author. The checked code also skips equality comparison for long or nonnumeric outputs. Passing therefore cannot be interpreted as an independent proof that the extracted source mathematics or reasoning steps are valid.

Appendix H reports overlap decreasing from 0.21% at 3-grams to 0.00% at 13-grams. This is aggregate author-reported evidence, not a released per-match ledger. The public decontamination code expects a different field and emits a different object shape from the current text-only release, so the hosted decontaminated files are not transparently reconstructed by the checked script.
