MATH's correctness contract is weaker than formal verification: it grades final answers, not proof validity or semantic faithfulness of intermediate steps. Equivalent mathematical expressions can be hard to normalize, and generated answers may be correct but formatted outside the grader's expectations. The public train and test sets create contamination risk. Difficulty labels and subject tags are useful metadata but not a complete ontology. Reusing worked solutions for training changes the task from held-out evaluation to supervised reasoning-data construction.
