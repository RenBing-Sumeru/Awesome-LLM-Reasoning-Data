The arXiv-v1 evidence is deliberately small and synthetic. In the three-step planted pivotal SCM, the middle refund decision is the causal failure point: resampling it produces rescues, whereas resampling the later step does not. In the two-factor AND-interaction SCM, the paper reports Shapley values of 0.44, 0.45, and approximately 0, with summed efficiency 0.909 against the analytic total 0.91. The corresponding repository test uses bad-action probability 0.3, 80 permutations, 12 samples per evaluation, and antithetic pairing. The paper and test also show why isolated contrastive effects can double-count the interaction; the code assertion requires their total absolute effect to exceed 1.2. These results validate behavior on planted mechanisms, not real-tool causal identification or data quality.

The repository manuscript adds a separate Who&When evaluation after arXiv v1. It uses the Algorithm-Generated 126-instance subset and `llama3.2` 3B as agent surrogate, environment simulator, and extract-then-compare judge, with K no greater than 8 and a five-step horizon. The manuscript reports 121 completed instances, five infrastructure casualties, and factual failure reproduction on 120/121. Its point-estimate `argmax` rule reports 48.8% agent accuracy, 20.7% exact-step accuracy, and 71.9% within plus or minus 3; the `cliff` rule reports 49.6%, 15.7%, and 68.6%. The uncertainty-aware result is negative: the CI-gated locus fires on only 22/121, 82% abstain, and 76% of annotated mistake steps receive no rescue at K no greater than 8.

Those Who&When numbers are author-reported repository-only results, not an arXiv v2 and not independently reproduced. The complete result JSONL is gitignored and absent, so per-instance seeds, realized K, excluded IDs, and error causes cannot be audited. Likewise, the official Python 3.12 tests were inspected but not run locally because `uv` and required dependencies such as `structlog` and adapter packages were unavailable; this is a limit of the present audit environment, not evidence that the project failed its tests. No benchmark score here proves that CAR trajectories are a released or training-ready dataset.
