The contribution is to turn a recorded stochastic agent episode into an explicit structural-causal replay object: preserve a factual prefix, intervene at a selected step, rerun the affected suffix, score complete branches, and attribute changes in bad-outcome probability to decisions rather than to transcript appearance alone.

The trajectory is formalized as a sequence of states, actions, observations, and a terminal outcome. CAR exposes five intervention families: `do_resample` redraws an action from the same policy; `do_action` forces an action; `do_observation` replaces an environment return; `do_context` changes the recorded context; and `do_policy` swaps the policy. Deterministic replay first reconstructs provider messages and compares request digests, then measures repeated action-signature matches while injecting recorded observations. Counterfactual forward replay is different: it asks the supplied live or mocked Environment for fresh observations after the branch.

The feedback contract is mixed. A caller-supplied `OutcomeFunction` maps a complete trajectory to a label and score in `[0,1]`. `RuleOutcome` is deterministic but only observes what its rule encodes; `JudgeOutcome` applies an OpenAI-compatible LLM rubric and adds model noise, bias, and prompt-attack exposure. Contrastive attribution estimates the change in bad-label probability when step `k` and its suffix are resampled, uses a Wilson interval for the intervened proportion and a bootstrap interval for the difference, and reports the latest significantly rescuing step as a point of commitment. Shapley attribution samples coalitions of held factual actions to allocate trajectory-level marginal contributions with uncertainty.

The closest Atlas comparisons are Who&When Pro, AgenTracer, and TraceElephant, which localize failure agents or steps in collected failure logs. CAR differs by defining a reusable intervention and replay interface over caller-produced trajectories rather than releasing a labeled benchmark corpus. The repository later uses Who&When logs through a `llama3.2` 3B surrogate because the original policies and states are unavailable; that surrogate boundary demonstrates, rather than removes, the gap between static logs and causal replay.
