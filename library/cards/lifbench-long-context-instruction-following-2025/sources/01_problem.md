Long-context benchmarks often test whether a model can retrieve a passage, but rarely whether it consistently follows complex instructions embedded in long inputs. In real tasks, instructions may appear early in a document, use varied wording, and contain variables, formatting rules, or conditional constraints. A model may succeed on one instance yet become unstable as context length or expression changes. The paper therefore studies how to construct scalable long-context instruction-following data and how to evaluate constraint satisfaction and cross-input stability through rule-based rubrics without relying on another LLM.
