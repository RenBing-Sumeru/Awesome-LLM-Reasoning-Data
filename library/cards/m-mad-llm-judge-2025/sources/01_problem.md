LLM judges for machine translation still trail learned metrics and a single holistic prompt obscures why a translation is good or bad. M-MAD replaces that single judgement with a structured multi-agent evaluation framework.
Human MQM annotation is expensive, while learned metrics often need references and training data; single-agent LLM judges have weak segment-level correlation, coupled error templates, and unstable attribution. Thus a system-level-looking score may not support sentence-level diagnosis or auditable error reasons.
Existing approaches also mix accuracy, fluency, style, and terminology in one template, allowing salient error types to dominate a total score. The authors therefore separate finding an error from synthesizing quality so a final verdict can point back to evidence.
