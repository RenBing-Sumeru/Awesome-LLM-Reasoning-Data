The authors present this as the first in-depth review of multilingual reasoning in language models. Its new value is the joint treatment of language diversity, reasoning tasks, data resources, and benchmarks, rather than a new optimizer or model architecture. It is a strong orientation document, but it should not be read as evidence that one method dominates every language or task.

Its distinctive contribution is thus an evaluative frame, not a claimed universal solution. It makes visible where work on reasoning has relied on particular linguistic resources and where language-focused evaluation omits the reasoning task itself. That joint perspective is valuable for assessing whether a proposed advance changes the underlying problem or only a narrow experimental slice of it.
