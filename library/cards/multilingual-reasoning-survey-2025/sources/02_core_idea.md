The central contribution is a structured map of multilingual reasoning research. It links motivations and foundational challenges to the data resources used for training, then to benchmark-based evaluation and reported methods. For a reader, the useful comparison is whether a claimed improvement changes the language coverage, the reasoning procedure, or only the evaluation setting.

This sequence makes the comparison concrete: resources determine what languages and examples are available, benchmarks determine what counts as success, and methods operate within both constraints. A result is easier to interpret after locating it on all three dimensions. The survey discourages treating an English-centered method name or an aggregate score as sufficient evidence of multilingual reasoning.
