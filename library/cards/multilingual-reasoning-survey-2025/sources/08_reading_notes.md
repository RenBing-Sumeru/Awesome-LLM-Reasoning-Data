Read the challenges and motivations first, then follow the paper's progression from data resources to benchmarks and method comparisons. Keep four questions visible: which languages are included, which reasoning task is tested, what resource supports training, and how is performance measured? Those questions prevent translation quality from being mistaken for reasoning ability.

Add a fifth question when following references: are results reported separately for the included languages or only in aggregate? Then compare like with like, rather than combining papers with different language sets and tasks. This reading discipline helps identify whether a claimed improvement is genuinely multilingual, a transfer observation, or a measurement artifact of the selected benchmark.
