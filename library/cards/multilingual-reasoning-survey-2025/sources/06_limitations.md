Results across languages can be difficult to compare because models, scripts, resources, and tasks may differ. The paper explicitly foregrounds misalignment, bias, and low-resource challenges, so an apparent aggregate gain may conceal uneven language coverage. Because it is a survey, it also cannot replace direct inspection of data provenance, prompts, and evaluation protocols in primary papers.

Do not infer that translation quality and reasoning quality fail or improve together. The survey's framing implies multiple possible bottlenecks, including the language representation, available resources, task wording, and evaluation construction. When a result is important, inspect whether it reports per-language behavior and whether low-resource languages are actually represented rather than only named in the motivation.
