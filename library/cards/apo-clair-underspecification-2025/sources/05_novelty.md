Previous methods typically accept existing preference pairs and change only the loss, or synthesize two independent answers whose differences are difficult to attribute. CLAIR changes the pair itself through minimal contrastive revision, while APO makes likelihood-direction assumptions explicit. The novelty is the joint diagnosis of data and objective underspecification and the demonstration that they interact. AI feedback, rewriting, and preference losses are not individually new; the contribution is a more identifiable pair plus a controllable objective family.
