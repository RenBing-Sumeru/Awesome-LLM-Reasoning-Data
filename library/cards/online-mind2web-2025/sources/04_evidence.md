Human evaluation reports Operator at 61% success and Claude Computer Use 3.7 at 56.3%, while the other evaluated systems cluster around 30%. These numbers support the paper's main diagnosis: strong systems remain far from reliable on broad live-site tasks, and offline or narrow benchmarks can overstate progress.

Against Online-Mind2Web human labels, the GPT-4o WebJudge variant averages 83.6% agreement. WebJudge with o4-mini reaches 85.7% agreement and a 3.8-point absolute success-rate gap; WebJudge-7B reaches 87.0% agreement and a 3.9-point gap while using 2 model calls per trajectory. Screenshot thresholds 2, 3, and 4 yield 79.3%, 81.8%, and 77.0% agreement in the reported ablation. GPT-4o at temperature 0 is still stochastic: across 3 runs, average success-rate standard deviation is 1.1% and the maximum is 1.8%.

On the 1,302-trajectory AgentRewardBench transfer test, reported precision is 73.7% for GPT-4o, 75.7% for WebJudge-7B, and 82.0% for o4-mini. The paper also warns that o4-mini can obtain high precision with low recall. Agreement and aggregate success-rate gaps therefore do not prove calibrated rewards, exact replay, or complete data retention. They measure evaluator alignment on the released evaluation setting, subject to frame selection, judge bias, site executability, and human-label uncertainty.
