The human verification campaign attempted 1,255 of 1,266 questions; 11 were not attempted. Solvers came from the trainer pool, could not answer their own questions, did not receive the gold answer, and were instructed not to use named AI assistants. After about two hours, 888 gave up and 367 reported a solution; 317 of those 367 solutions matched the reference. These counts support the claim that the set is difficult for the tested humans and that most reported solutions agree with the reference. They do not provide released human search trajectories or prove that every single reference answer is exhaustive.

Table 3 reports accuracy/calibration error in percent under named model versions: GPT-4o (`gpt-4o-2024-08-06`) 0.6/69; GPT-4o with browsing (`gpt-4o-search-preview-2025-03-11`) 1.9/82; GPT-4.5 (`gpt-4.5-preview-2025-02-27`) 0.9/68; o1 (`o1-2024-12-17`, medium) 9.9/65; and Deep Research 51.5/91. The Deep Research revision is not disclosed. The high calibration errors for browsing-capable systems support the authors' warning that they can be confidently wrong, but the paper text inspected does not specify the exact calibration-error formula.

Figure 1 and Section 4.3 report that Deep Research accuracy rises smoothly across runs with different browsing effort, without disclosing the effort unit, token/tool/page budget, latency, cost, or exact checkpoint. Sections 4.4-4.5 generate 64 outputs or trials per question: majority vote, confidence-weighted vote, and best-of-N all improve over one attempt by 15%-25%, with best-of-N highest. The wording does not unambiguously distinguish relative percent from percentage points. Across 64 Deep Research trials per item, 16% of tasks have 100% pass rate and 14% have 0% pass rate. For most zero-pass items, revealing the answer lets Deep Research find supporting web evidence, but “most” is not quantified and those runs are not released.
