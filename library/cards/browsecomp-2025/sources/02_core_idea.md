BrowseComp's contribution is to construct factual questions that are easy to verify once the answer is known but deliberately hard to locate, then score an agent's final answer rather than its browsing process. Human trainers begin from a person, event, or artifact, collect discriminating facts, and invert those facts into a question designed to resist ordinary search and contemporary models. This produces an answer-level evaluation surface for persistent information seeking, calibration, and test-time-compute studies.

The released data object is a question/reference-answer pair plus topic and canary metadata. In the CSV, the question and answer are Base64-encoded XOR ciphertext. The official decryptor derives a repeated key from SHA-256 of the row's public `canary`; because the canary/password and decryptor are released together, this is reversible leakage friction and canary-based filtering support, not cryptographic access control.

The feedback contract is judgment-required and answer-level. The judge receives the question, full model response, and gold answer; it extracts the proposed final answer, explains meaningful differences, permits a small margin for numerical answers, and is instructed to emit `correct: yes` or `correct: no`. It can assess semantic answer agreement under its prompt, but it cannot observe whether browsing occurred, whether retrieved evidence was factual, whether citations support the answer, whether the path was efficient or reproducible, or why the agent failed. Confidence supports calibration and parallel selection analysis, not process supervision.

Relative to fixed-corpus retrieval and browser-environment benchmarks, BrowseComp deliberately leaves the web and agent scaffold model-specific while keeping the terminal label simple. Relative to SimpleQA and Humanity's Last Exam, its distinctive object is a human-inverted question constructed to require persistent browsing. BrowseComp-Plus is a later, separate related record; its fixed corpus and evidence annotations are not properties of original BrowseComp.
