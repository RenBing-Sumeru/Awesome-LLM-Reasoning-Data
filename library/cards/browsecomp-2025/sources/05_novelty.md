Prior browsing benchmarks often use relatively direct questions, fixed retrieval corpora, navigation goals, or environment-specific terminal predicates. SimpleQA supplies short factual QA, and Humanity's Last Exam supplies the query and grader prompt pattern referenced by the released harness. BrowseComp changes the task construction: human trainers begin from an answer-bearing factual seed, collect multiple discriminating attributes, invert them into a question, and then deliberately filter for failure by models, ordinary Google search, and selected humans.

The resulting object couples 1,266 public answer targets with a live-web challenge, calibration prompt, LLM answer judge, and repeated-sampling analysis. This makes persistent search difficulty, answer-level uncertainty, and test-time-compute returns observable without requiring a single standardized browser implementation. Its strongest direction signal for reasoning-data research is the explicit separation between the public task/answer table and the private or model-specific search process.

What is not new is short-answer QA, LLM-as-a-judge scoring, confidence elicitation, repeated sampling, majority voting, best-of-N, or web search individually. The work also does not introduce a replayable environment, evidence/citation score, browser-action schema, or open trajectory dataset. The 1,266-row scale and performance tables are not themselves evidence of data quality.

Before reuse, inspect the single-answer adjudication, paper grader identity, current scorer repair, live-web version drift, canary handling, contamination exposure, dataset-specific rights, and absence of success/failure traces. Read BrowseComp-Plus only as a later separate record: its fixed corpus, qrels, and evidence documents must not be attributed to original BrowseComp.
