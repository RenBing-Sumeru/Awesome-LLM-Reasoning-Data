TTI's central contribution is to make interaction horizon a curriculum and data-selection variable: the current Gemma 3 12B policy rolls out web tasks online, terminal success selects whole episodes, and the action steps inside selected episodes become cross-entropy behavior-cloning targets. Although the paper places this under online RL and describes it as online STaR, the released update is positive-only filtered behavior cloning, not policy-gradient RLVR.

The supervision contract has two levels. At the episode level, reward is 1 for task success and 0 for failure; only non-empty trajectories with `trajectory_reward > 0` survive, and code also drops terminal `ANSWER [N/A]`. At the state-action level, `TrajectoryStepDataset` flattens every retained episode into processed observation messages paired with the current policy's action text. Consequently, terminal feedback selects an episode but does not label which intermediate step was helpful, harmful, or merely tolerated by a successful outcome.

The feedback source is mixed rather than interchangeable. WebArena uses task-specific ground-truth evaluators whose released rows contain URL, string, or program/HTML evaluation specifications; this programmatic/environmental verifier can inspect the final task state but not the causal quality of each action. WebVoyager uses a prompted multimodal success judge over the task, final response, and screenshots. The paper identifies Gemma 3 27B and reports 88.9% accuracy against WebArena ground truth, but does not disclose class balance, calibration, or false-positive and false-negative rates. The score therefore supports the reported detector check, not a general claim that judged episodes are error-free.

The horizon curriculum begins at 10 interactions and increases multiplicatively toward 30. A replay buffer retains successful trajectories and emphasizes newer experience; in the released trainer, subsampling combines linear recency and inverse web-domain-frequency weights at `alpha=0.5`. This couples the learned data distribution to horizon, current-policy competence, evaluator decisions, domain frequency, and replay age.

The closest decision boundary is the paper's own contrast between "thinking" and "doing": increasing per-step reasoning effort or adding a prompted `check-again` pass is not the same intervention as obtaining new environment observations. Relative to a fixed long-horizon online baseline, TTI changes when long trajectories enter collection and training. The new data-facing element is the joint horizon curriculum, terminal-success filter, and replay-weighted state-action imitation; browser actions, chain-of-thought prompting, binary outcome evaluation, and behavior cloning are existing components assembled for this interaction-scaling question.
