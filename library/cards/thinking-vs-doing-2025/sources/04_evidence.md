The reported evidence covers controlled interaction studies and full TTI training, but it does not independently validate the released data as a complete corpus. WebVoyager evaluation contains 427 tasks across 13 domains, and full WebArena contains 812 tasks. The preliminary WebArena interaction study reports a random 62-task analysis/test subset and the remaining 750 tasks for online training; the exact IDs and seed for that split are not published as a manifest.

In the paper's controlled studies, increasing the opportunity to interact can outperform spending the same intervention on repeated per-step reasoning, because new actions reveal new observations. The benefit is not monotonic under every mechanism: repeatedly prompting the agent to check again can turn correct responses into incorrect ones, while a fixed long horizon can produce aimless exploration and noisy credit assignment. These negative results motivate the curriculum rather than proving that longer episodes are intrinsically higher-quality data.

For the full comparison, Table 3 reports 64.8% average WebVoyager success for the TTI Gemma 3 12B agent. The authors describe it as the strongest aggregate result among the compared open-weight agents trained on open-source synthetic data. That is a model/benchmark result under the paper's comparison scope; it has not been independently reproduced here and does not establish trajectory correctness, split cleanliness, judge calibration, licensing, or corpus completeness.

The paper also reports 88.9% success-detector accuracy when the prompted WebVoyager judge is checked against WebArena ground truth. This supports using the detector in the reported experiment, but aggregate accuracy without class balance, confusion counts, calibration, or adversarial tests cannot quantify false acceptance of failed trajectories or false rejection of successful ones. WebArena's programmatic ground-truth evaluators avoid that particular learned-judge dependency, yet they still supply only terminal outcome feedback and can inherit environment or task-specification brittleness.

The official release supplies implementation evidence for the data pipeline: runtime transition fields, positive filtering, state-action flattening, replay selection, scripts/configs, and two trained checkpoints can be inspected. It does not supply the successful and failed rollouts consumed by training. Therefore the strongest supported conclusion is mechanistic: TTI demonstrates a concrete loop in which interaction budget changes the pool of successful on-policy behavior available for BC. Benchmark performance is not evidence that the selected data is broadly high-quality or safely reusable.
