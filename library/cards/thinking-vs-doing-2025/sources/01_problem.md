The durable primary record is arXiv:2506.07976 v2 (2025-06-10). The paper also has SEA @ NeurIPS 2025 and ARLET workshop records, but the SEA page explicitly opts out of archival proceedings publication; this Card therefore records the venue as an arXiv preprint rather than a NeurIPS proceedings paper. The verified primary artifacts are the full paper and appendices, the official project page, the `test-time-interaction/TTI` repository pinned at commit `92ec04f5e4f2e4e40b99ecb1652191030a54a74a`, and two author-linked Hugging Face checkpoints.

The paper asks whether a web agent can improve by increasing the number of environment interactions available at test time, rather than spending more reasoning tokens before each action. Extra interaction can expose new page state, permit backtracking, and support replanning, but a longer fixed horizon also creates aimless exploration and noisier attribution of terminal success. TTI turns that tension into a post-training data problem: which successful behaviors appear when the interaction horizon expands, and how should those episodes be selected and replayed?

One method-level episode begins with a task goal and URL. At each step the policy sees an accessibility tree, a set-of-marks screenshot, the latest three observations, and all previous actions, then emits chain-of-thought-bearing text ending in one of six browser actions: click, type, scroll, go back, Bing/search, or stop/ANSWER. The runtime record additionally carries the next observation, reward, `done`, final and optional reference answers, trajectory reward, Monte-Carlo return, and maximum horizon. An episode terminates when the agent stops or reaches the horizon; success is a binary terminal outcome. The public release is narrower: five task JSONL files, code, prompts/configuration, and two checkpoints are available, but the collected `.pt` rollout episodes, screenshots, replay manifest, and immutable environment snapshot are not.

This makes the work a core `environment_agent_trajectory_data` entry: state, action, observation, terminal predicate, evaluator identity, replay selection, and environment version all change the resulting supervision. It does not provide step-correctness labels, a general robotics result, or a reusable public rollout corpus.
