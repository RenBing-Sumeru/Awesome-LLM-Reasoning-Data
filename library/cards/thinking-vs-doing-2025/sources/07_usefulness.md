For the `environment_agent_trajectory_data` track, TTI is most useful as a construction and audit recipe, not as a downloadable training-trajectory dataset. A builder can reproduce the modular flow—task source, multimodal browser state, policy action, terminal evaluator, positive-episode filter, state-action serializer, replay sampler, and horizon scheduler—while recording evaluator identity and environment version as first-class lineage fields.

The Card suggests four concrete track-level uses. First, use TTI as a filtered-BC baseline when comparing fixed versus expanding interaction horizons; do not label that baseline RLVR. Second, log both episode-level terminal reward and step-level serialization so later audits can distinguish the selection unit from the loss unit. Third, keep WebArena programmatic evaluation and WebVoyager prompted judging as separate feedback adapters with separate version, calibration, and error records. Fourth, design a replay manifest that includes task ID, site/domain, policy checkpoint, evaluator checkpoint/prompt, horizon, seed, collection iteration, terminal outcome, retention decision, and recency/domain sampling weight.

It is also a useful negative-control checklist. Preserve failed and `ANSWER [N/A]` episodes outside the training loss; compare positive-only BC with a policy that can learn from failures; report raw attempts, successful episodes, unique tasks, duplicates, replay occupancy, and per-domain selection rates; and test whether gains persist under a clean task split and a separately pinned judge. These experiments would locate whether the gain comes from interaction opportunity, curriculum ordering, success filtering, replay composition, or evaluator errors.

Reuse class: **reading/audit and code-level reproduction reference only**. The task JSONL files can be inspected, and the two checkpoints can support carefully licensed evaluation, but training-data reuse is blocked pending an explicit code/task license, clean paper-aligned split manifest, pinned Gemma 3 27B judge and prompt, released rollout/screenshot lineage, and reproducible environment snapshot. Benchmark scores alone do not remove those blockers.
