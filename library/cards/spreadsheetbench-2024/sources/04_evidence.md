The main evidence is that the released benchmark is large enough and hard enough to separate spreadsheet agents from humans. The paper reports 912 instructions, 2,729 test cases, 3,917 sheets, 2,019 single-sheet files, and 710 multi-sheet files. It also reports realistic spreadsheet complexity, including multiple tables, non-standard relational layouts, formatting cues, and large files.

Reported model scores remain low. In the official experiments, GPT-4o single-round reaches 18.35% soft and 15.02% hard overall, while GPT-4o multi-round reaches 16.96% soft and 13.27% hard. GPT-3.5 improves under multi-round feedback but remains at 7.09% soft and 5.37% hard. Human performance is reported at 71.33% soft and 62.00% hard, and Copilot in Excel is evaluated only on a 5% subset with one test case per instruction. These numbers support the claim that file-state spreadsheet manipulation is still difficult, but they are bounded by the official evaluator, sampled product subset, execution environment, and public benchmark release.
