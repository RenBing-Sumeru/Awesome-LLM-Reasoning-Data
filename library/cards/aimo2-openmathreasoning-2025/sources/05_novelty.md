The prior-work baseline is a one-pass mathematics distillation corpus: source problems are paired with teacher CoT, wrong final answers are rejected, and the retained text feeds SFT. OpenMathReasoning broadens this into three linked data objects and publishes the combined recipe.

First, CoT generation mixes DeepSeek-R1 and QwQ-32B at large candidate budgets and uses a hardness estimate to allocate more rollouts. Second, TIR is bootstrapped iteratively rather than expected to emerge from prompting a long-reasoning model: a small, strictly filtered code-using seed trains a generator, which creates a larger set for the next model. Third, GenSelect turns groups containing correct and incorrect solutions into traces for generative test-time selection rather than only majority voting.

The paper also exposes a useful engineering distinction between selection rules and training value. Stage-0 TIR uses answer, code-use, code-block-count, novelty, and significance filters; later stages drop novelty/significance because the filter reduces downstream performance. GenSelect summary cleanup improves length but costs roughly 1-2 points. These outcomes show that a stricter or cleaner-looking filter is not automatically a better training objective.

The work does not introduce CoT distillation, Python tool use, LLM answer judging, SFT, or candidate selection in isolation. Its concrete contribution is the integrated scale and the iterative construction pipeline. Verification remains answer-level, majority-induced targets are not proof labels, and the nine-field release does not preserve structured execution events or step labels.

For reasoning-data research, the most important release-level novelty is partly corrective: the official data card distinguishes 306K solution-bearing problems from the paper's earlier 540K processing count and separately identifies 193,170 problem-only rows. That correction makes versioned release accounting a first-class part of the recipe rather than treating headline scale as immutable.
