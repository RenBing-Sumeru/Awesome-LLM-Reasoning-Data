ModeX treats each sampled response as a graph node and sets an edge weight to the sum of unigram, bigram, and trigram Jaccard similarities. It uses the Fiedler vector of the graph Laplacian to propose a binary cut. When conductance is below tau, it retains the larger partition, breaking equal-size ties by total edge weight, and repeats; when conductance reaches tau, recursion stops. The response with maximum weighted degree in the final cluster is returned. ModeX-Lite applies one such partition to partial paths every T tokens, prunes the non-retained group, and delays centroid selection until decoding ends. This is evaluator-free in the narrow operational sense: no reward model or auxiliary judge selects the answer. It is not verifier-backed—lexical consensus and graph connectivity are selection heuristics, not evidence that the chosen content is true or functionally correct.
