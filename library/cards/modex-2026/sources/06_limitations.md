Jaccard similarity is lexical: valid paraphrases can have weak edges, while repetitive or templated errors can form a dense cluster. The method assumes that the dominant mode is desirable; shared hallucinations, systematic bias, or mode collapse can therefore be amplified rather than rejected. The conductance threshold, zero-threshold Fiedler split, tie rule, N, and ModeX-Lite pruning interval all affect survival, and partial-prefix pruning can remove a path that would later become correct or informative. Multi-path generation consumes more aggregate energy and may increase latency and memory. The benchmark subsets use fixed early test items, but seeds, candidate-generation settings, exact item-order provenance, contamination controls, and per-item traces are undisclosed. The code is released, but generated candidates, similarity matrices, partitions, rejected paths, pruning logs, and result manifests were not verified as released. Task-score gains do not demonstrate trace completeness or selection correctness.
