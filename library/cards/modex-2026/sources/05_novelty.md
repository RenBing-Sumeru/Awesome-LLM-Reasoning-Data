Best-of-N sampling, self-consistency, spectral clustering, conductance, and centroid selection are established components. ModeX's specific contribution is their composition into a no-auxiliary-model selector for open-ended text: exact-match voting is relaxed into an n-gram-weighted response graph, recursively filtered by a Fiedler cut, then represented by an existing centroid response. ModeX-Lite moves the graph decision inside decoding so prefixes can be discarded before completion. Unlike a reward model, this selector requires no task-quality labels or selector training; unlike synthesis, it never constructs a new answer. The novelty is therefore a deterministic aggregation and pruning recipe. Its modal-consensus premise remains a hypothesis whose benchmark behavior does not certify the correctness, provenance, or reuse quality of the underlying candidate records.
