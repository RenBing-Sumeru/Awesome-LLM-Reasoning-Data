The primary source is the EMNLP 2025 main-conference paper, pp. 17135–17148 (ACL Anthology 2025.emnlp-main.866; arXiv:2505.17829v1). SRCA asks a specific test-time-search question: when a model has already spent compute producing intermediate reasoning prefixes, can those prefixes expose useful answer hypotheses rather than being discarded as soon as a beam changes or terminates?

The paper identifies two linked problems in PRM-guided search. First, selecting only locally high-scoring continuations can make different branches converge on the same answer or reasoning direction. Second, conventional tree search typically returns only a few completed paths, even though an intermediate prefix may already support a correct answer before later reasoning degrades. The proposed object is therefore not a released training dataset: it is an online, per-prompt search record consisting of a step-delimited prefix, a temporary checkpoint completion, PRM scores, answer-cluster membership, selected beams, and optionally a reconstructed candidate ending at that checkpoint.

The reported evaluation surface comprises GSM8K, MATH500, AIME1, and the English mathematical no-image subset of OlympiadBench. This belongs in `rollout_search_test_time_trace_data` because it specifies how a rollout tree is expanded, scored, retained, and converted into a final answer at inference time. It is not evidence for a new process-supervision corpus or a policy-training recipe: no SRCA code, data, trace archive, or record-level manifest was verified. (Paper §1, §3, §4.1, Appendix A.1.)
