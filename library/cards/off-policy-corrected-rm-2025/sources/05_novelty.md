The main technical novelty is to apply off-policy correction inside reward modeling rather than only inside policy optimization. OCRM interprets the stale reward model as a distribution-shift estimator problem and uses known language-model likelihoods to target the reward-model risk of the current policy.

This differs from collecting fresh on-policy preferences. RLP-SPG, one compared baseline, generates current-policy replies and pseudo-labels them; OCRM keeps the original SFT comparisons and labels fixed. It also differs from ordinary PPO-RLHF, which continues using the initial reward model, and from DPO-style methods that directly optimize the policy on a static comparison set. The paper argues that WPO's current-policy probability weight is not the correct density ratio unless the data-generating policy is uniform, whereas OCRM explicitly includes the behavior-policy denominator.

The paper provides an estimation-error analysis. With bounded loss and bounded importance weights, the target-risk excess loss of the weighted reward-model estimator is controlled by a Rademacher-complexity term scaled by the weight bound. This formalizes why support and weight magnitude are first-class audit requirements. It does not prove that the entire RLHF procedure reaches a globally optimal policy.

For this atlas track, the data novelty is conceptual and operational: the recipe constructs a new effective empirical distribution without constructing new comparison records. Each stage changes which fixed examples dominate optimization. Treating those weights as part of lineage makes reward-model refresh auditable while preventing an inflated claim that OCRM released a new preference dataset.

The practical contribution is broader than the weighting formula. Stage-local KL references, reward-model refresh cadence, value-network and optimizer resets, and released scripts form the executable scaffold. Ablations indicate that some of these choices contribute independently, so the complete result should not be attributed to importance weighting alone.
