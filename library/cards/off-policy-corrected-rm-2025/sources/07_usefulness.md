For the `data_construction_open_release_recipes` track, this paper is useful as a recipe for transforming a fixed preference corpus into stage-specific reward-model training views. It broadens data construction beyond acquisition: provenance includes not only which records exist, but also which policy induces their effective weights at each training stage.

The recipe is directly relevant to reward modeling and preference learning when a team retains the behavior-policy checkpoint that generated its comparisons. It offers a controlled alternative to paying for repeated on-policy labels. It is not evidence that old data always replaces new data, and it should not be categorized as an SFT corpus, an on-policy preference release, or a general-purpose reward dataset.

Useful applications include:

- reproducing OCRM on TL;DR or the filtered Alpaca-Instructions setup using the official scripts;
- comparing fixed-data reweighting against fresh on-policy collection or pseudo-labeling at matched compute and label cost;
- auditing weight tails, effective sample size, support coverage, and label-error amplification across policy stages;
- testing whether alternative ratio clipping, flattening, or relative weighting improves the variance-bias trade-off;
- studying refresh cadence jointly with value-network recovery, KL reference choice, and optimizer resets;
- extending the method to comparison pools generated by multiple known behavior policies.

A well-formed downstream artifact should keep two layers separate. The canonical dataset layer contains the unchanged prompt, chosen response, rejected response, and label. A run-specific layer records behavior/current log probabilities, raw ratios, transformed weights, stage policy identifiers, and reward-model checkpoints. Without the second layer, the effective distribution is not reproducible; without the first, the weighting lineage is uninterpretable.
