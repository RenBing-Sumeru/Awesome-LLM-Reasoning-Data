The primary training source is Bridge V2, reported as more than 40,000 real-world WidowX trajectories from 24 environments. For each instruction–observation–expert-action tuple, a reference policy generates candidate actions; clustering reduces redundancy, and all pairwise comparisons from the same initial condition are grouped for training. The preferred action is the one with lower RMSE to the demonstration action. The paper reports 20 million synthetic comparisons. The 7B verifier replaces LLaVA’s unembedding layer with a reward head and optimizes a Bradley–Terry loss that incorporates the magnitude of the RMSE preference gap.

At every deployment timestep, RoboMonkey draws initial actions from the VLA at positive temperature, majority-votes the discrete gripper command, fits separate Gaussian distributions to translation and rotation, samples augmented candidates, scores them, executes the highest-scored action, and observes the next state. In one real-robot configuration, five initial OpenVLA samples produce 16 candidates; the optimized serving path reports roughly 650 ms for sampling and verification. Candidate counts, temperature, and hardware are configuration-specific rather than fixed properties of the data object.
