Under the paper's LLM-as-judge Pass@1 evaluation on 103 text-only GAIA validation questions and 680 WebWalkerQA test questions, Table 1 reports consistent gains over the corresponding vanilla ReAct policies. Qwen2.5-7B WebDancer scores 31.0 versus 18.4 on GAIA average and 36.0 versus 24.2 on WebWalkerQA average. Qwen2.5-32B scores 40.7 versus 31.0 and 38.4 versus 31.9. QwQ-32B scores 51.5 versus 37.8 and 47.9 versus 24.1. These are author-reported live-agent results, not independent reproductions.

The paper's best-performing model reaches Pass@3 of 64.1% on GAIA and 62.0% on WebWalkerQA. Figure 4 reports that RL improves Pass@1, Pass@3, and Consistency@3 more clearly for the non-reasoning Qwen models; for QwQ, the gains are smaller and the authors associate the weaker signal with sparse rewards on very long trajectories. A cold-start ablation that begins RL without SFT reaches only 5% Pass@3 on GAIA, supporting the paper's claim that trajectory SFT is important for learning the interaction format before on-policy optimization.

Table 3 isolates a transfer tradeoff. For QwQ-32B, Long-CoT SFT reaches 58.25 Pass@3 and 39.66 Consistency@3 versus 44.66 and 28.33 for Short-CoT, but its invalid rate rises from 0.97% to 13.27%. Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-32B also show higher invalid rates under Long-CoT—21.36% and 13.59%, respectively—than under Short-CoT—0.65% and 4.20%. This supports a data-format conclusion, not a general claim that longer reasoning is always better.

Figure 3 compares open-only, CRAWLQA-only, E2HQA-only, their union, and the final filtered subset; the final 6,550 Long-CoT set outperforms larger unfiltered alternatives under the reported low-data setting. Figure 5c finds little sensitivity to decoding temperatures 0.5–0.7 and attributes more variation to live-web non-stationarity. Both interpretations depend on the unreleased complete data and historical environment, so they remain author claims. (Paper Table 1, Tables 2–3, Figures 3–5, Appendix E.)
