WebDancer is most useful as an auditable blueprint for building an information-seeking agent, and only secondarily as a small public-data resource.

For recipe design, it cleanly separates four layers: synthesizing difficult questions, sampling tool-using trajectories, selecting successful trajectories for supervised fine-tuning, and then applying outcome-reward reinforcement learning. Implementers can reuse that separation while recording the query generator, teacher model, tool implementation, sampled candidates, filtering decisions, reward outputs, and model checkpoint for every stage.

For schema inspection, the two 200-record sample files show concrete question/answer and ChatML-style trajectory objects, including tool calls, observations, and assistant reasoning. They are suitable for parser tests, UI demonstrations, format conversion, and small-scale audit exercises. They are not evidence of a license-cleared, complete training release and should not be represented as the 60,000-question CRAWLQA pool, the 40,000-question E2HQA pool, or the 14,228 selected SFT trajectories.

For experimental baselines, the paper provides useful design choices: sample (N=5) candidate trajectories during rejection sampling; mask observation tokens from the SFT loss; sample 16 rollouts per RL question; and combine a 0.1 binary format reward with a 0.9 binary Qwen-72B-Instruct answer reward. A faithful adaptation should retain raw judge outputs and all-zero/all-one groups, report judge calibration, and test reward sensitivity rather than treating the reward as ground truth.

The strongest justified reuse class is therefore **reading/audit plus sample and schema inspection**. Full training reconstruction remains blocked until the project releases the complete corpora, exact train/split/decontamination manifests, failed and rejected trajectories, RL rollout and reward logs, modified training code, environment versions, and a stable web-content snapshot or cache.
