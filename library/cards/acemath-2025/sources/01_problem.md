AceMath asks how to turn a math-specialized base model into a strong instruction follower without sacrificing its mathematical competence, and which data choices make that post-training reliable. The primary source is Findings of ACL 2025, published July 2025, and the decision boundary is a three-file SFT release rather than the reward-model corpus reported elsewhere in the same paper.

The relevant data object is a staged collection of role-content prompts and answer targets: code and math establish the first general stage, code, math, and general-domain instructions rebalance the second, and a selected math corpus performs the final specialization. Its evaluation surface is greedy pass@1 on seven math benchmarks plus controlled removals of synthetic data, cross-checking, and general SFT, making it directly relevant to reusable instruction, demonstration, and rationale data.

L4 facts: official paper Findings of ACL 2025, DOI 10.18653/v1/2025.findings-acl.206; official non-gated dataset `nvidia/AceMath-Instruct-Training-Data`; exact split counts 2,261,687, 1,634,573, and 1,661,094; role-content messages plus answer schema, construction, filtering, decontamination, staged consumers, ablations, non-commercial terms, and reuse risks verified.
