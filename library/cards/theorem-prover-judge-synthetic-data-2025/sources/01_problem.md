Synthetic mathematical reasoning is useful only when the question, solution, and supervision signal agree. Language-model judges can reproduce their own mistakes, while exact-answer filters do not inspect intermediate reasoning. A theorem prover offers executable feedback, but only after natural-language questions and solution steps have been translated faithfully into formal statements.

This paper addresses both sides of that bottleneck: it generates new problems by reverse question answering from GSM8K and MATH solutions, then uses Lean to classify two candidate answers. The resulting object is therefore not just a list of question-answer pairs. It is a routed record containing a synthetic question, two natural-language traces, their formalisation attempts and diagnostics, and a final SFT, DPO, disposal, or unresolved-error decision.
