The principal risk is specification alignment. Lean checks the formal statement it receives, while GPT-4o creates that statement and participates in its round-trip alignment check. The manual audit's five false positives and Appendix J's verified-but-wrong example confirm that execution can succeed when the natural-language semantics are wrong. One annotator and 54 cases provide only limited evidence about this failure rate.

Selection also changes the distribution. Error is not mathematical falsity, but unresolved Error traces are excluded; longer traces require more retries, so retained data can be biased toward easier-to-formalise material. False/False pairs are disposed, losing some failure diversity. The corpus is small and concentrated in a few mathematical domains, and problem difficulty is bounded by the generators' ability to pose, solve, and formalise their own questions.

Several reported details conflict internally: Section 4.3 says about 40% of examples require iterative refinement while Appendix E says 50%; Section 3 describes the GPT-4o-mini evaluator using the final two output sentences while Appendix D says the final three; Algorithm 1 appears to stop after its first verified proof step although the prose requires all steps to verify. The appendix also reports approximately $50 for 300 samples and $0.60 per sample, which cannot both be the same average cost. These discrepancies require implementation artifacts or author clarification.

Reproducibility and reuse remain partial. The paper-cited RLTPF GitHub URL returned 404 on 2026-07-22, and no accessible SIMQA rows, proofs, prompts, retry logs, discarded pairs, checkpoints, or immutable environment were confirmed. Exact Lean/mathlib versions, source-row and split manifests, evaluation-overlap checks, decontamination, and data/model licenses are missing.
