For the programmatically verifiable outcome data track, this paper provides a concrete example of separating Verified, False, and Error rather than collapsing them into one reward. Its pair-routing table can inform SFT and preference-data design, and its false-positive audit is a useful warning that verifier success is conditional on correct formalisation.

For the open construction-recipe track, the paper documents a reusable conceptual pipeline: reverse-generate a problem, round-trip its specification, formalise each response, retry execution errors with diagnostics, and allocate pairs by joint outcomes. A practical implementation should preserve every intermediate object—source ID, formal statement, back-translation, alignment decision, per-step Lean output, retry history, and route—so auditors can distinguish mathematical rejection from tooling failure.

The method is most useful as a design pattern or audit checklist until the release is restored. Reusers should add independent semantic-alignment review, pin the Lean/mathlib environment, retain rather than silently discard negative and error traces, publish lineage and licenses, and test source/evaluation overlap. Benchmark gains should remain downstream evidence, not a substitute for record-level quality controls.
