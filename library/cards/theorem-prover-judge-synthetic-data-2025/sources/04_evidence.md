The paper reports that iterative autoformalisation raises Lean execution success from 60% to 87%. Figure 4 decomposes outcomes more precisely as 23.1% question-formalisation error, 5.4% answer-formalisation error, 30.8% Verified, and 40.8% False; the two error categories sum to 28.5%. Table 11 further divides question-stage failure into 13.1% autoformalisation error and 10.0% alignment-check error. These figures describe pipeline yield, not semantic correctness of all retained records.

A manual audit covers 54 successfully executed cases and one annotator with a mathematics and statistics bachelor's degree. Against the human labels, TP-as-a-Judge has 32 true positives, 3 false negatives, 5 false positives, and 14 true negatives (F1 0.87, recall 0.91). o1-mini has 25, 7, 12, and 10 respectively (F1 0.72, recall 0.78). The theorem prover therefore performs better in this small comparison, yet five of 19 human-negative cases are false positives. Appendix J shows a concrete case in which a semantically wrong answer verifies, demonstrating that successful Lean execution is not sufficient evidence of natural-language alignment.
