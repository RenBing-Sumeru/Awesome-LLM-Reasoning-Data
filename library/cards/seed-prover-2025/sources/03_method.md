The disclosed pipeline has five linked parts:

1. **Prompt sourcing and curriculum.** RL uses cited open formal-math ingredients—Big-Math, NuminaMath, CriticLean, Lean Workbook, and DAPO—plus undisclosed in-house formalizations. Problems with proof rate above `1/4` are excluded as too easy; a proposer creates easier variants for single-pass failures. Exact snapshots, mixtures, counts, and split exclusions are unknown.
2. **Record construction.** Prompts randomly expose natural-language hints/proofs, similar or proved lemmas, failed lemmas/attempts, prior summaries, and Lean compiler feedback. Outputs place named `lemma` blocks before the main `theorem`; lemma pools can carry statement, complete proof, difficulty, and dependencies. Sampling probabilities and retained/failed record counts are not reported.
3. **Training contract.** Multi-stage, multi-task RL based on VAPO uses reward `1` for a Lean-verified proof and `0` otherwise, plus an undisclosed format penalty for lemma-first structure. Difficulty, quality, and maximum output length increase over stages. Optimizer, rollout totals, hardware, reward aggregation, and exact VAPO changes are unknown.
4. **Lean environment.** The unreleased LooKeng REPL exposes `init_state`, `run_tac`, and `verify_proof`, supports whole-proof and stepwise interaction, version switching, memory controls, native Lean checking, and asynchronous concurrency. Most reported evaluations use Lean 4.14.0; MiniCTX-v2 uses Lean 4.16.0.
5. **Scaled inference.** Light samples Pass@8–16 candidates and refines each up to 8–16 rounds with compiler feedback and self-summary—roughly Pass@64–256 and 1–2 hours. Medium selects failed lemmas, spends an 8×8 light budget on each, inserts proved lemmas into the outer prompt, and can produce proofs over 1,000 lines; aggregate outer limits are unknown. Heavy starts with an empty lemma pool and, by default, 5,000 proposer conjectures (10–50 per call), proves/disproves them using light search, expands from proved facts for days, ranks thousands of facts by proof rate, LLM-judged semantic relevance, and proof length, removes unrelated or short-proof items, retains hundreds, and runs a final medium search.

Seed-Geometry is a separate construction pipeline. Statistics from more than 20 years of olympiad geometry drive a programmatic generator that reports more than 230M unique auxiliary-requiring problems and 38B BPE tokens, generated over more than seven days. A policy maps context to auxiliary constructions; an extended DSL and C++/Pybind11 forward-chaining engine provide the symbolic terminal check. Final inference uses stepwise beam search with GPU policy processes and asynchronous CPU pools. The corpus, engine, policy, DSL, deduplication rule, and training code are not released.
