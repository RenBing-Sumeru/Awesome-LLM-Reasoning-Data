Open reasoning models were typically specialized for either mathematics or code, while general alignment corpora did not teach difficult planning, tool execution, correction from feedback, and objective correctness in one inspectable record. This left open models well behind proprietary reasoning generalists and made it hard to tell which post-training actions were actually correct.

The paper constructs UltraInteract as one preference tree per instruction: actor models propose step-marked text or code actions, an environment and critique expose errors, and objectively correct nodes or trajectories become SFT demonstrations while correct/incorrect branches also form preference pairs. The direct release is a reusable reasoning post-training corpus plus Eurus consumers trained from its correct actions.
