The method co-trains a Llama-3.3-70B policy and a Llama-3.3-70B world model. Accepted live-browser trajectories supervise the policy and are converted into transition examples in which the world model predicts the next accessibility-tree observation from the recent observation/action history and a base-model-generated transition rationale. The iteration-2 world model then acts as a virtual web server: alternating its predicted observations with actions from the iteration-1 policy produces up-to-seven-step synthetic trajectories for queries not solved during real self-improvement, and successful synthetic trajectories are added to policy SFT. Separately, WMLA uses the world model to simulate candidate actions at inference time and lets GPT-4o score predicted progress. Training acceptance is therefore a Llama-3.3-70B binary judgment, live execution supplies environmental feedback, and WMLA uses a GPT-4o scalar judge; none is a programmatic proof of correctness.
