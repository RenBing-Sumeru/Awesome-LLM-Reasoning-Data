First, labels in RBM-1M still depend on humans, environments, executors, or model judges, so missed cases and bias become reward blind spots. Second, although the release contains more than one million robot trajectories with frame-level progress and same-task preferences, coverage of languages, environments, robots, tasks, and model sources remains limited and public release creates contamination risk. Third, offline scores do not automatically become real RL or deployment gains. Reuse should audit deduplication, agreement, false positives, unseen-environment generalization, licensing, and safety boundaries.
