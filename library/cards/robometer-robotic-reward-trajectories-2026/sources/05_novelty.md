Closest prior work uses general preference pairs, terminal success, or step accuracy without representing the domain-specific states and failure modes of robotic reward modeling. This paper changes the record structure and feedback contract by centering RBM-1M and combining inputs, candidates, environmental or evidential state, and labels in one auditable example through collecting successful, failed, and suboptimal trajectories across robots and tasks and annotating progress plus pairwise preference. The contribution is task-specific data construction, capability decomposition, and practical evaluation rather than sample count alone.
