The prior-work baseline is code-generation and executable-program benchmarks with unit-test or judge feedback. The change introduced by ScienceAgentBench: Toward Rigorous Assessment of Language Agents for Data-Driven Scientific Discovery is the particular task object and feedback contract: official records expose scientific task inputs, optional expert knowledge, generated self-contained Python programs, execution artifacts, evaluation logs, costs, and rubric/programmatic scores. scored by programmatic execution, task-specific scoring rubrics, gold/evaluation programs, cost metrics, and GPT-4o judging for some visualization outputs determine success. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05080; venue: https://openreview.net/forum?id=6z4YKr0GK6; openreview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=6z4YKr0GK6; project: https://osu-nlp-group.github.io/ScienceAgentBench/; code: https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ScienceAgentBench; data: https://huggingface.co/datasets/osunlp/ScienceAgentBench; huggingface: https://huggingface.co/datasets/osunlp/ScienceAgentBench; doi: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2410.05080), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
