The authors explicitly limit DCQ to verbatim contamination; metadata, paraphrase, and other non-verbatim leakage are outside its estimate. Its core assumption is that exposure lets a model recognize the exact original despite word-level variants, so failures of recall can understate exposure. Perturbation quality and positional bias are model- and dataset-specific, requiring a fresh BDQ rather than reuse of another model's slots. Before reuse, manually inspect variants and report uncertainty ranges, not only the maximum percentage. If an item is short, terminology-dense, or label-determined, few valid synonym substitutions may exist and quiz quality needs additional human audit.
