The central contribution is a model-aware data schedule rather than a new standalone corpus. For pretraining, each candidate source is reduced to a representative subset: a FineWeb-Edu classifier keeps educational-quality scores above 4, Ask-LLM ranks reasoning relevance and retains the top 10%, a domain-specific Ask-LLM pass refines code/math/knowledge assignment, and semantic deduplication reduces each source subset to roughly 10,000 examples. Combined subsets form capability-probing distributions for Code, Math, and Knowledge.

The recipe then extends AutoMixer. Separate domain models and ten checkpoints provide self- and cross-capability influence estimates; later checkpoints receive larger weights, and aggregated influence determines dataset-level sampling ratios. Mid-training makes the feedback model-dependent: it retains samples with estimated influence above zero, recomputes dataset weights, trains the model, and repeats for two phases.

The feedback contract is mixed and heuristic. FineWeb-Edu predicts educational quality; Ask-LLM judges perceived reasoning relevance; probe NLL and AutoMixer approximate transfer to selected capabilities. These signals can rank and filter records, but they do not verify factual truth, intermediate reasoning, or final-answer correctness. Compared with uniform mixing, ordinary Dolmino mid-training, or post-training-only distillation, the distinctive direction is to connect capability probes and evolving model state to source weights across the full training stack.
