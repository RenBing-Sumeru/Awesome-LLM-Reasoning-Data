Prior contamination work commonly identifies leakage through benchmark overlap or anomalous performance. This paper instead connects contaminated reasoning to a chain of internal geometric observations: spectral concentration, lower-dimensional computation, weakened whitened coupling, and mutual-information decay. It also turns that account into a causal intervention evaluated by consistency with the base model. The contribution is therefore a mechanistic diagnostic-and-recovery framing, not a new benchmark or a claim that all contaminated data share one signature. Its value depends on the causal tests: the geometric measurements become more than descriptive only insofar as changing the narrowed computation changes the stated consistency outcome. This makes the proposed object useful for analysing a failure mode, while leaving its cross-setting stability as an empirical question.
