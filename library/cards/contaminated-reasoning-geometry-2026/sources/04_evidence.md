The central claim is that a detectable geometric narrowing, rather than a score change alone, mediates contaminated reasoning. The paper compares contaminated and base models on leaked versus clean inputs, examining representation spectra, whitened representation--gradient structure, and the effect of an intervention that restores the narrowed computation. Under this controlled comparison, the intervention improves average consistency with the base model by up to 11.03 absolute percentage points over the strongest baseline. This result supports recovery of behavioural consistency on the evaluated leaked inputs; it does not establish that the diagnostic is universal across models, datasets, or contamination processes. The comparison is informative because clean items act as a control for the same general task setting, while the intervention tests the proposed mechanism instead of merely correlating a diagnostic with leakage.
