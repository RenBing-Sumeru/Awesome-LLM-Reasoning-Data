Reasoning benchmarks can contain leaked items, so a model may return convincing chains of thought by recalling contaminated examples rather than by carrying out the intended inference. This makes scores and explanations unreliable, while the mechanism that produces the altered behaviour remains difficult to diagnose.

The paper studies this failure as an internal representation-and-gradient phenomenon. It characterizes a geometric signal of contaminated reasoning and uses that signal to intervene on leaked inputs, aiming to distinguish and partially restore the base model's reasoning behaviour rather than treating a high benchmark score as sufficient evidence. The distinction matters whenever a benchmark score is used as evidence of a model capability, because leaked memorization can imitate both the answer and the form of a rationale.
