**Method and verifier limits.** A Python program can execute while encoding an irrelevant, circular, or mathematically invalid transformation; the accompanying natural-language comment is not separately checked. Terminal equivalence can accept a lucky final answer despite defective intermediate reasoning, or reject a valid answer because extraction/equivalence is brittle. A Q-value aggregates outcomes observed under one policy and finite tree, so it can rank an underexplored sound step below a shortcut. PPM training inherits these labels, and repeated policy/PPM refresh can amplify early checker or search biases. These are curator inferences from the documented contracts; the paper does not report calibrated false-positive/false-negative rates.

**Data and release limits.** Both public datasets are train-only flattened tables. The SFT rows omit source ID, round, seed, search budget, visits, Q-values, rejected branches, and terminal-verdict evidence; PPM rows additionally omit Q margins and tree topology. The 747K pool, raw MCTS trees, trained policy/PPM checkpoints, and complete rejected trees were not found. Exact source proportions, transformations, deduplication, split construction, and release-row mappings are unknown. Top-level MIT labels on the code and two dataset cards do not reconcile rights or attribution for MetaMath, NuminaMath, MATH, AMC/AIME, or GPT-4-generated derivatives.

**Experimental and reproduction limits.** No documented decontamination or semantic benchmark-overlap audit was found. The paper's candidate/rollout defaults differ from the checked `12`-candidate/`48`-iteration configs, and the paper's one PPM epoch differs from the README's two-epoch example. Per-record budgets, random seeds, temperature, software/container versions, and exact checkpoints are missing. Consequently, the reported benchmark and scaling curves cannot currently be tied to an immutable public run.

**Evidence limits.** The 19-of-20 label-error observation comes from a small unreleased manual audit. Reported benchmark improvements are evidence about the authors' integrated system, not proof that every released row is correct, uncontaminated, appropriately licensed, or beneficial for a different model and objective.
