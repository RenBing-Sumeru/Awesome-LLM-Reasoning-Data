The evidence supports the following reconstruction, while exposing several settings that must be pinned before replication.

1. **Collect and augment problems.** Assemble 747K answer-keyed word problems, primarily from MetaMath and competition-level NuminaMath. Seed GPT-4 with 7.5K MATH-train and 3.6K AMC-AIME training problems; request ten GPT-4 solutions for each synthetic problem and retain a problem when at least three solutions are consistent. Exact source counts, IDs, canonicalization, and deduplication are not released.
2. **Bootstrap round 1.** Use DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct (236B) as the policy. At each node, propose code-augmented natural-language steps, concatenate the Python program, execute it, and keep runtime-valid children. Round 1 uses eight rollouts and five candidates per step.
3. **Assign terminal and step values.** Extract a terminal answer and compare it with the ground truth. Back-propagate +1 or -1 through visited nodes. A runtime error, depth limit, or absence of valid children can terminate with negative reward. Code execution and answer equivalence are distinct checks.
4. **Create SFT rows.** Rank correct trajectories using average/minimum Q, retain up to two per prompt, and exclude responses containing `error`. Synthetic problems with trajectory accuracy below 50% are removed.
5. **Refresh the policy.** Fine-tune Qwen2.5-Math-7B from the initial base for two epochs, sequence length 4096, batch 128, AdamW, and learning rate 7e-6. The resulting SLM becomes the next round's generator.
6. **Create and train PPM data.** For mixed-outcome problems, take two high-Q candidates whose descendants reach correct terminals and two low-Q candidates whose descendants reach wrong terminals. The repository additionally filters pairs to Q-margin at least 0.5 and edit distance at least 20. Train a scalar-tanh-head PPM with Bradley-Terry pairwise ranking for one paper-reported epoch, batch 512, and learning rate 7e-6.
7. **Run rounds 2-4.** Round 2 uses SLM-r1 with terminal-guided MCTS, normally 16 rollouts and eight candidates per step. Rounds 3-4 initialize node values with the previous PPM before terminal updates. Round 4 uses 16 candidates and two trees/seeds; hard unsolved problems receive 64 and, if needed, 128 extra rollouts.
8. **Package and consume outputs.** Use correct trajectories for policy SFT, preference pairs for PPM training, and the PPM to guide later-round and test-time MCTS. The public release flattens these outputs into SFT and PPM tables rather than releasing the tree substrate.

The paper default also reports maximum depth 16 and UCT constant 2. Generation compute was ten nodes of 8×80GB H100 GPUs for about two weeks in bootstrap and fifteen nodes of 4×40GB A100 GPUs for roughly three days per ordinary later round, with the expanded final round taking about one week (Appendix A.2; Table 7). Paper-exact replay is blocked because per-item seeds and realized budgets, temperature, dependencies/container, trained checkpoints, and raw trees are absent. The checked configs use `n_generate_sample: 12` and `iterations: 48`, whereas the paper describes other candidate/rollout settings; the README's two-epoch reward-model example also differs from the paper's one PPM epoch.
