The central mechanism is gated competition. A deterministic structural gate rejects unparsable, out-of-scope, type-incompatible, or constraint-contradictory moves without a verifier call. Surviving moves receive a hybrid pre-verification score made of a structural distance-to-goal term and a learned residual trained to rank verifier-accepted moves above rejected ones within the same state.

The method then allocates a different top-k verification budget to each state. When survivor scores are close, the decision is locally uncertain and more candidates are verified; when they are separated, fewer calls are spent. The verifier still makes the accept/reject decision, so the learned score is a budget-prioritization device rather than an unverified substitute for correctness.
