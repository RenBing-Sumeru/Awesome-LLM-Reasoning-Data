Official model cards say pretraining draws on publicly available and licensed data plus information from Meta products and services, including publicly shared Instagram and Facebook posts and interactions with Meta AI. The models use early-fusion multimodality; the vision encoder is based on MetaCLIP and is trained separately with a frozen Llama model. Meta also reports MetaP hyperparameter transfer, FP8 training, 200 pretraining languages, and specialized-data mid-training for long context. Source inventories, modality proportions, filters, and split membership are unknown.

Maverick post-training uses a curated multimodal curriculum: remove more than 50% of SFT examples judged easy by Llama models, run lightweight SFT on the harder remainder, apply online RL to harder prompts, alternate training with continuous filtering for medium-to-hard prompts, and finish with lightweight DPO for response-quality corner cases. The report does not name the SFT authors, judge checkpoint, RL algorithm or reward, rollout count, DPO preference source, thresholds, or optimizer settings.

Behemoth is a separate branch. Its post-training prunes 95% of SFT data, uses policy pass@k to build an increasingly hard curriculum, filters zero-advantage prompts, mixes capabilities in batches, varies system instructions, and uses fully asynchronous online RL. Only the Behemoth-to-Maverick codistillation relation is explicit for a released model; these Behemoth post-training settings must not be generalized to Scout or Maverick. Safety tuning separately combines vendor-generated human data and synthetic data selected with LLM-based classifiers, but the records and selection models are not released.
