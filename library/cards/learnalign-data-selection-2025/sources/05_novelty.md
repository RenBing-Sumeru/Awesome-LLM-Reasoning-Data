Prior selectors score instructions by perplexity, length, uncertainty, external models, SFT-oriented gradients, or full-training influence. Prior RLVR work such as LIMR and one-shot RLVR shows that small prompt sets can be effective but requires substantial training during selection. LearnAlign's specific change is to compute alignment under the GRPO objective and reintroduce a magnitude-like learning-potential term after cosine normalization through p(1-p).

The row-average pairwise score favors prompts that are simultaneously near the warmed-up policy's current capability boundary and directionally aligned with the wider pool. This makes consumer policy, verifier, and checkpoint part of the data-construction lineage rather than treating selected IDs as timeless data quality.

Gradient alignment, success-rate difficulty, random projection, top-N selection, and binary RLVR rewards are not individually new. LearnAlign is also not a new verifier, dataset release, process-supervision source, or fully online curriculum. Its useful direction signal for the atlas is the coupled recipe and the evidence that selection compute, scoring state, and rejected candidates are material parts of an RLVR data artifact.
