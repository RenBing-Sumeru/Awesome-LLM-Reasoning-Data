- **Checkpoint-relative score:** rankings depend on the warmed-up policy, tokenizer, prompt template, pool, answer parser, projection, and random seeds. They can become stale after policy updates and are not shown to transfer across model families.
- **Coarse sparse feedback:** eight binary rollouts restrict p to increments of one eighth. No adaptive rollout budget or per-prompt uncertainty interval is reported, and final-answer correctness does not validate intermediate reasoning.
- **Undisclosed verifier:** the GSM8K and boxed-answer prompts are shown, but the exact extractor, normalization, and equivalence logic are not. Parser false accepts or rejects would affect p, gradients, ranking, and downstream results.
- **Gradient approximation:** the efficient DAPO path takes one correct rollout per prompt, omitting failure-gradient information. The paper does not explain what is computed for a prompt with no correct rollout; random-projection dimension and seed are also unknown.
- **Missing release:** no official implementation, selected or rejected IDs, scores, eight-rollout groups, correctness labels, projected gradients, warmup membership, checkpoint hash, or run manifest was confirmed. A statement that code will be released is not a current artifact.
- **Pool and split ambiguity:** main text uses DAPO-MATH-17K, whereas Appendix F/H captions refer to DAPO-MATH-14K without explaining the effective pool or mapping. No decontamination or duplicate audit is reported.
- **Scale boundary:** the explicit matrix is quadratic. The reported matrix step is fast for 10^3–10^4 prompts, but Nyström, cascade, LoRA-space gradients, and learned surrogates are proposed extensions rather than validated ultra-large-scale implementations.
- **Evidence scope:** models are limited to Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B and selection pools are mathematical; CRUX is evaluation-only. Training-seed variance and hardware model are not reported, so timing and accuracy attribution remain bounded.
- **Rights and content audit:** the ACL paper is CC BY 4.0, but upstream prompt, generated-rollout, subset, and future-code terms require separate checks. The Responsible NLP checklist reports no explicit identifying/offensive-content check, relying on the datasets' widespread use and an assertion that they contain no personal information.
