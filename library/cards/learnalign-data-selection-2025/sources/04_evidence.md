On GSM8K, Table 1 reports 77.5% pass@1 with 1,000 selected prompts and 78.3% with 2,000, versus 77.0% after full-data training on 7,473 prompts. The same table reports 74.8% and 76.4% at 100 and 500 prompts. These are single paper-reported outcomes under one model/setup, not intrinsic-quality measurements of selected records.

For 1,000 prompts selected from DAPO-MATH-17K, Table 2 reports a five-benchmark average of 42.4 for Qwen2.5-3B versus 44.9 with full-data training, and 54.6 for Qwen2.5-7B versus 58.9 full. Thus the compact subset initially approaches but does not universally match full data. With longer training, Table 5 reports that the 3B subset reaches 83.8/67.8/36.9 on GSM8K/MATH500/AMC2023 at 2,000 steps, while its full baseline at 2,174 steps is 83.6/65.8/31.0; the analogous 7B comparison is mixed across metrics.

Component evidence is directionally supportive but limited. Removing warmup, learnability, or gradient similarity reduces several reported metrics; learnability-only and pass@8 filtering remain strong baselines, while LearnAlign is higher on the three Table 13 benchmarks. Adding K-means feature diversity does not consistently improve results. Three warmup subsets yield GSM8K 79.3–81.2, MATH500 60.2–61.8, and AMC2023 28.3–29.5, showing some checkpoint-path sensitivity rather than invariance.

The Qwen2.5-3B time audit reports 8h55m for selection—2h2m warmup, 2h41m rollouts, 4h12m gradient estimation, and 12.7 seconds matrix selection—plus 2.4 hours selected-subset training, compared with 42.3 hours full training. The hardware is described only as a single GPU. No official code, selected subsets, run logs, or gradients are available for independent reproduction or record-level inspection.
