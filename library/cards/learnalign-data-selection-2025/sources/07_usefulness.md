For the Data Construction and Open Release track, LearnAlign is useful as a recipe for policy-aware RLVR prompt selection when answers are programmatically checkable and the up-front scoring cost can be amortized. It clarifies that an auditable subset needs more than selected text: preserve upstream ID/revision, warmup membership, policy and tokenizer hashes, prompt template, all eight outputs, parsed answers, raw rewards, p, V, gradient/projection configuration, row score, rank, keep/reject flag, and downstream run assignment.

Reuse class: **reference and reconstruction recipe; blocked pending artifacts for faithful reproduction**. A researcher can implement a controlled approximation from the equations and settings, but cannot reproduce the published subset or validate selection decisions without code, checker, projection, seeds, and row-level records. Existing GSM8K or DAPO releases should not be described as LearnAlign-selected data.

Useful controlled comparisons include random selection, learnability-only, pass@8 filtering, cheaper embedding or feature similarity, full-rollout versus one-correct-rollout gradients, and static versus periodic rescoring. Report selection and training compute separately, including rollout tokens and gradient cost; subset benchmark accuracy alone cannot attribute gains to prompt quality.

A dynamic or staged policy should recompute scores and retain every selection snapshot. Publishing both accepted and rejected candidates would expose how the curriculum changes, allow parser and score audits, and connect subset construction to the exact policy that consumed it.
