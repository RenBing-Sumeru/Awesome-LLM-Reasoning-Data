Frontier reasoning reports often reveal model capability while leaving the training data opaque. That makes it difficult to distinguish four separate claims: model weights are downloadable; a training dataset is downloadable; the process that generated and filtered those records is documented; and benchmark scores show the resulting model's behavior. Llama-Nemotron is unusually useful because NVIDIA provides evidence in all four categories, but the evidence is not equally complete.

The report describes three heterogeneous Llama-derived models: LN-Nano (8B), LN-Super (49B), and LN-Ultra (253B). Their path includes neural architecture search, recovery distillation or continued pretraining, supervised fine-tuning on reasoning-on and reasoning-off examples, reasoning reinforcement learning, instruction-following optimization, and preference alignment. For this Card, the central data object is the official Llama-Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset rather than the architecture search itself.

The audit problem is to connect a released row to its upstream prompt, synthetic generator, filtering decision, training stage, and final model. The official artifact exposes useful row fields and NVIDIA documents detailed domain pipelines, yet complete record-level source lineage, rejected generations, verifier outputs, exact run membership, and a reconciled count ledger remain unavailable. Model benchmark results cannot close those gaps because performance does not establish provenance or data quality.
