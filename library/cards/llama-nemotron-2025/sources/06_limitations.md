Release accounting is not internally complete. The paper reports 33,011,757 synthetic examples, the dataset card lists generator-specific counts that do not directly sum to that domain table, and the current viewer estimates about 3.91M SFT rows. These quantities may count candidates, responses, released records, or different versions, but the official sources do not provide a crosswalk. The RL viewer also reports a schema-cast failure. Reusers should pin a commit and count raw rows rather than quoting one number as the undisputed corpus size.

Lineage is only partially record-level. Rows carry category, generator, license, version, reasoning mode, and intended model membership, which is valuable. They generally do not provide the exact upstream URL or source record, extraction date, prompt-generation seed, every transformation, all candidates, rejection reason, verifier output, or final run/step membership. A license field does not by itself supply required attribution or resolve generator-model terms.

Verification is heterogeneous and imperfect. Math answer-equivalence judging and majority voting can preserve consensus mistakes or invalid reasoning with a correct endpoint. Code post-processing confirms structure and syntax in the report, but universal execution against tests is not established. Reward-model selection depends on calibration and can be exploited; thresholds, score distributions, false accepts, and rejected responses are not released. Format rewards enforce tags, not reasoning quality.

Decontamination is bounded by domain and named benchmarks. Math, code, and science describe checks, but exact benchmark revisions, thresholds, matched examples, and removal ledgers are missing. General, chat, safety, function-calling, and instruction-following data lack a comparable corpus-wide report. A manual finding of less than 0.3 percent code overlap is informative but does not substitute for a released match ledger.

The RL data release does not reproduce the RL runs. It lacks full policy rollout groups, sampled outputs, per-component rewards, log probabilities, reference-policy values, curriculum batch assignment, discarded easy prompts, optimizer states, and exact model checkpoints at each step. The paper identifies algorithms and many hyperparameters, but the exact NeMo-Aligner development branch and configurations are not pinned.

Finally, benchmark scores and throughput are model evidence, not data evidence. They cannot establish prompt rights, absence of contamination, trace correctness, or safety coverage. The official opt-out statement says NVIDIA performed legal review for confidential, PII, and copyright material, but it does not publish audit methodology, counts, adjudication, or residual-risk measurements.
