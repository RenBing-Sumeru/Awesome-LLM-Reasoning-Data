The official [addendum PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/8df7697b-c1b2-4222-be00-1fd3298f351d/codex_system_card.pdf) and [publication page](https://openai.com/index/o3-o4-mini-codex-system-card-addendum/) establish codex-1's o3 lineage, real-world coding-task RL, deployment container, and the three Codex-specific safety-training families. The original [o3 and o4-mini System Card](https://openai.com/index/o3-o4-mini-system-card/) provides only broad parent-family source classes—public internet, third-party-partnered, and user/human-trainer/researcher-provided or generated information—and filtering statements; it is not a codex-1 task manifest.

The addendum reports safety evaluation results: malware refusal rates of 0.97 on a synthetic benchmark and 0.98 on an internal-expert golden set; correct acknowledgement of impossible or constrained tasks rising from 0.15 before training to 0.85 after training on synthetic unexpected-state samples; and 0.98 success at ignoring prompt injection in the Codex coding environment. It also reports standard refusal and StrongReject comparisons with o3. These measurements describe the stated evaluation protocols, not the size, provenance, independence, legality, or quality of the training records.

Preparedness is a separate evidence layer. OpenAI's Safety Advisory Group judged codex-1 below the High capability threshold in the three tracked categories, and the addendum says additional coding data did not substantially change its frontier-capability profile. This is a deployment risk classification based on capability evaluations; it neither validates the hidden training data nor specifies the coding-agent reward.
