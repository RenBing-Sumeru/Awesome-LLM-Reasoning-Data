The survey draws on recent research across different tasks, so its evidence is a synthesis of prior studies rather than a single cross-task experiment. Its key practical point is that confidence and calibration must be assessed against observed correctness in the relevant setting. A score or probability is meaningful only relative to the task, the definition of a correct answer, and the procedure used to compare predictions with outcomes. Consequently, a signal that appears useful in one setting should not automatically be treated as a reliability guarantee in another. When reading evidence, check whether the evaluation concerns the same type of answer and decision that the eventual application requires.
