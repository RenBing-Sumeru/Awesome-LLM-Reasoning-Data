The survey separates two questions that are often conflated. Confidence estimation asks how to obtain a signal expressing how reliable an answer is expected to be; calibration asks whether that signal agrees with observed correctness under a specified task and evaluation protocol. A number that ranks answers by apparent certainty is therefore not automatically a calibrated probability. The paper organizes technical advances, challenges, applications, and future directions around this distinction. For readers, its core lesson is to judge a confidence signal in context: identify what it estimates, how correctness is defined, and whether the claimed alignment was measured on the intended use setting.
