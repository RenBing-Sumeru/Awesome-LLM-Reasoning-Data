LLMs can be impressive across tasks yet remain unreliable when their outputs contain factual errors or other mistakes. A response that sounds certain is not necessarily correct, so wording and apparent fluency should not be read as a reliability signal. This NAACL 2024 survey asks how a system's confidence can be estimated, how that confidence can be calibrated against observed correctness, which technical advances have been proposed, and where such estimates may be applied. The problem is inherently task-dependent: confidence only has practical meaning when it can be compared with the relevant correctness outcome. The paper surveys prior work and releases no new model, benchmark, or universal confidence scale.
