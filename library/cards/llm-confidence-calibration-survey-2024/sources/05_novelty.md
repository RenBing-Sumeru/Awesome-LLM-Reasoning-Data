The paper addresses the stated lack of a comprehensive overview of confidence estimation and calibration in LLMs. Its contribution is an organizing account of lessons, technical advances, applications, and open challenges, rather than a claim that one estimator is best for every model and task. The useful novelty for a reader is the explicit separation between a confidence output and demonstrated calibration. That separation changes how claims should be read: a method may produce an informative signal without showing that the signal is numerically aligned with observed correctness in every setting. The survey makes these comparison boundaries visible rather than collapsing varied methods into one reliability claim.
