Process reward models are intended to judge the correctness of intermediate reasoning steps, not merely whether a solution eventually reaches the right answer. Building such a model requires a data and feedback contract for step boundaries, labels, error location, and the relationship between a local step judgment and a later outcome. These objects are often harder to audit than released reward-model weights.

This report examines the gap directly. It argues that Monte Carlo outcome estimation can confuse future answer reachability with current-step correctness, and that Best-of-N evaluation can reward a process model for selecting a correct answer with flawed reasoning. The Card treats the work as a Track 12 disclosure record: it records the reported construction interface and what remains unavailable for independent inspection.
