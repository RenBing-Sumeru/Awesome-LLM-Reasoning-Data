The ACL Anthology record provides the citation and abstract. Use this overview first to decide which direction a question belongs to: causal methods used to evaluate or improve language models, or language models used to support causal work. The distinction matters because the desired output, evidence, and assumptions differ. For every cited result, retain the causal target, available data, and assumptions required for interpretation. A response that sounds causally plausible does not identify a causal effect, and an LLM-assisted workflow does not remove the need to state what the causal task requires. The survey connects this literature to fairness, safety, and explainability, but those motivations should not replace a stated causal question. Its role is to map two-way collaboration and point readers toward primary studies. Claims about a particular estimator, model behavior, dataset, or empirical result should be checked in those primary sources, where assumptions and measurement design are reported.

When using the card, write down what intervention or relationship is being considered and what observation could support the conclusion. This prevents a system's fluent explanation from being confused with evidence. It also makes clear why two studies with similar language may be answering different causal questions.
