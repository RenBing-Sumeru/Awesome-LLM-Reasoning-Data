STORM decomposes document-to-derivation-data construction into six roles instead of asking one model to read a full paper, choose an advanced formula, invent a self-contained question, and reproduce its proof in one prompt. The Math Expression Extractor identifies formulas, theorems, lemmas, and corollaries and converts them to LaTeX. Query Draft turns an expression and its dependencies into a derivation/proof question. Answer Retriever is instructed to extract the answer from the source rather than solve it. Context Collector finds omitted definitions and dependencies. Question Refiner folds that evidence into a self-contained question. Answer Filter distinguishes derivations/proofs from definitions or quotations.

The intended grounding contract is document extraction, not free-form teacher reasoning. The appendix instructs Answer Retriever to search the first occurrence, appendix, and relevant sections; skip unsupported questions; include the complete derivation; preserve source meaning; expand referenced formulas; and append evidence location. Yet the final three-field rows discard that evidence field and all intermediate formula/context records. A consumer can inspect the coarse `paper` identifier but cannot directly verify page-level grounding without redoing retrieval.

Human feedback is the terminal verifier. Experts familiar with the source papers review generated question-answer pairs, retain acceptable examples, reject low-value examples, and manually improve borderline ones. The stated criteria are reasoning type, problem clarity, derivation correctness, and reasoning density. The paper calls these “five” principles but enumerates Q1–Q4 only. No annotator count, topic assignment, numeric threshold, independent double review, inter-annotator agreement, adjudication, or item-level before/after revision is released.

Benchmark feedback has two further forms that must remain separate from curation. For open-ended STORM-BORN answers, each evaluated model produces three responses per question and human experts score a fully correct derivation as 1, with partial credit proportional to completed key steps. For a separate NuminaMath-1.5 formula-derivation test, DeepSeek-R1 compares a candidate with a ground-truth proof and scores correctness, completeness, and similarity from 0 to 2. Multiple-choice variants permit exact option-letter scoring, but the distractors are not proven equivalent to evaluating a generated derivation.

The contribution is thus a human-judged, source-retrieval recipe rather than a formal verifier or reward model. Its released `question`/`whole_label` pairs support SFT, and the 27-row test plus options support evaluation. No process labels, preferences, scalar reward, executable environment, or formal proof object justify RL or process-supervision claims.
