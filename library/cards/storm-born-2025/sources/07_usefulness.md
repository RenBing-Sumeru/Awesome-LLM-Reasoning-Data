For `data_construction_open_release_recipes`, STORM-BORN offers a compact case study in converting full scientific documents into reasoning supervision. Builders can reuse the six-role decomposition—expression extraction, dependency-aware question drafting, source-answer retrieval, context recovery, self-contained rewriting, and answer-type filtering—while replacing opaque web interactions with versioned calls and preserving every intermediate record.

For SFT, the 100 `question`/`whole_label` pairs provide long, LaTeX-heavy targets that emphasize derivation and proof rather than short numerical answers. The 73-row train split is immediately loadable, but its size makes results seed-sensitive, and source overlap with test requires either a document-disjoint resplit or explicit disclosure. Training should retain `paper` and add page/equation evidence rather than stripping provenance.

For evaluation, the 27-row open-ended test can support expert-scored derivation studies, while the identical 27-row choice variants provide a cheaper exact-letter proxy. The full 100-row choice file can support controlled diagnostics but must not be mixed with the released train split without leakage. Researchers should audit whether distractors are logically wrong, stylistically detectable, or copied across examples before reporting option accuracy.

The dataset is especially useful for studying verifier limits. One can compare source-span checking, independent mathematicians, proof-oriented LLM judges, symbolic spot checks, and multiple-choice conversion; measure errors such as algebra slips, unproven assumptions, notation misuse, and logical leaps; and quantify how much source context is needed for a self-contained prompt. The paper's own warning that LLM judges overrate flawed derivations makes independent calibration essential.

Reuse class: conditionally usable for `sft` and `evaluation` after source/license review, evidence reconstruction, secret-safe code handling, and source-disjoint splitting. It is useful as an audit seed for document-grounded reasoning data. It is blocked as a complete reproduction of the 2,000-to-100 selection process, a preference/reward dataset, or a formally verified proof corpus. Benchmark gains and low solve rates do not remove those boundaries.
