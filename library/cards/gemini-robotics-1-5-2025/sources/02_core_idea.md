The central recipe is Motion Transfer: a VLA architecture and co-training procedure intended to align knowledge across ALOHA, bi-arm Franka, and Apollo humanoid data, while also mixing public Internet text, image, and video. A single general-evaluation checkpoint controls all three embodiments without embodiment-specific post-training, allowing skill transfer to be tested across robots.

The second idea is explicit embodied thinking. Given an instruction and perception, the VLA generates a natural-language thought, appends it to context, and then produces continuous actions. Thoughts may express a hierarchy from high-level plans to few-second segments and primitive-motion inner monologue. This is an inference interface; the report does not disclose who authored training-time thinking targets or how they were generated and filtered.

The full agent uses ER 1.5 as an orchestrator. It processes user input and environmental feedback, decomposes a task, optionally calls Google Search or user-defined tools, sends natural-language step instructions to the VLA, and uses success detection to decide when to switch subtasks. This separates semantic planning from low-level control while keeping progress and failure visible.
