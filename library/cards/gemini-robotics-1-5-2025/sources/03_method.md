The training mixture includes robot-camera images, text instructions, robot sensor records, and continuous action records from thousands of tasks across ALOHA, bi-arm Franka, and Apollo humanoid platforms. Public Internet text, image, and video data are also included. Each image is paired with original and synthetic captions generated using Gemini and FlexCap. Per-platform episode counts, hours, action dimensions, sensor schemas, sampling weights, and source manifests are not disclosed.

Motion Transfer co-trains across robot embodiments and data sources so that skills available in one embodiment's records can transfer to another. The report compares single-embodiment/no-MT, multi-embodiment/no-MT, and full-MT variants, and evaluates tasks whose target-robot data were collected only on another embodiment. The architecture, alignment object, loss, optimizer, batching, curriculum, and negative-transfer safeguards remain unspecified.

With thinking enabled, Gemini Robotics 1.5 generates a natural-language trace from the instruction and perception, appends it before the action, and then emits continuous numerical robot actions. The report demonstrates planning, implicit subtask-completion awareness, and recovery behavior, but does not identify a teacher, annotator, generation prompt, coverage rule, or acceptance filter for training-time thinking labels.

ER 1.5 produces plans, coordinates, tool instructions, progress estimates, and binary success/failure judgments. Real-time success detection consumes images plus a task instruction at 5 Hz under simulated inference latency; an offline variant has unlimited inference time. The long-horizon evaluator assigns task-specific progress points and labels failures as planning, success-detection, or action failures. These are evaluation and audit contracts, not claimed RL rewards.

Safety auto-red-teaming transforms ordinary training or evaluation tasks into malicious instructions, corrupted scenes, or rollout-time environment disturbances. An AutoRater judges correctness and safety and may return a reasoning trace. The report says generated training data helps mitigate vulnerabilities, supporting a safety-training use, but gives no corpus count, judge calibration, acceptance rate, or learning objective.
