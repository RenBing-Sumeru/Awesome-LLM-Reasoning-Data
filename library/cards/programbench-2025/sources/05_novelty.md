The prior-work baseline is code-generation and executable-program benchmarks with unit-test or judge feedback. The change introduced by ProgramBench: Can Language Models Rebuild Programs From Scratch? is the particular task object and feedback contract: programming prompt, generated answer/code, checker output, and score. scored by programmatic tests or exact answer checks. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03546), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
