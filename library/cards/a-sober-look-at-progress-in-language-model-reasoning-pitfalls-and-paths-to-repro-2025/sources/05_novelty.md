Earlier reasoning papers usually introduce an RL/SFT recipe and report its own benchmark run, leaving the comparison protocol partly inherited from each project. This paper's change is to treat the protocol itself—seeds, decoding, templates, answer extraction, hardware, and software—as the object of controlled study, then re-run heterogeneous methods on it. Its novelty is not a new reasoning trainer or benchmark: it makes variance and comparability first-class evidence for judging claimed progress.
Docker packaging and multi-seed reporting are individually familiar practices; the distinctive contribution is applying their joint audit to current reasoning-method claims and releasing the resulting run-level record.
It therefore changes the decision rule for progress: a headline score must survive a disclosed, matched protocol before it is treated as comparative evidence.
