1. Position: it audits mathematical-reasoning progress by standardizing the evaluation conditions that can otherwise create apparent gains.

2. Method handle: vary seeds, decoding, templates, answer matching, and stack first; then compare methods under one protocol. Multi-seed averaging is the key quality gate.

3. Artifact handle: Sober Reasoning releases Docker evaluation code, prompts, outputs, a leaderboard, and an MIT CSV dataset with 65.6k log rows; it is an evaluation-log dataset, not training traces.

4. Evidence anchor: Table 3 uses six benchmarks, 10 seeds for AIME/AMC and 3 elsewhere; DeepScaleR is 37.0±6.6 versus R1-Distill 28.7±4.8 on AIME'24, while several claimed RL gains are not significant.

5. Reuse decision: use it to audit a math leaderboard before accepting a training claim; first match model revision, prompt, decoding budget, answer extractor, seeds, and benchmark version.

The 30/40-example AIME'24/AMC'23 sets are specifically too small for a one-run ranking; report the variance even if a model's mean is higher.
