The central claim is that many reported reasoning gains are not robust under a common protocol. In Table 3, the authors evaluate RL and SFT variants on six math benchmarks with LightEval/vLLM, tuned model-specific decoding, suitable templates, 10 seeds for AIME/AMC and 3 for the other tasks. For the R1-Distill-1.5B family, DeepScaleR reaches 37.0±6.6 on AIME'24 versus 28.7±4.8 for R1-Distill, while the paper reports no statistically significant improvement for several other reported RL gains. The same table shows SFT OpenThinker at 30.5±6.2 versus Qwen-Instruct at 12.3±3.2 on AIME'24. This supports a protocol-bound comparison, not a universal verdict on RL or SFT; model families, benchmarks, and training data differ.
The sensitivity study explains why: across 20 seeds, Figure 2 reports variation up to 15 percentage points on AIME'24 and 13 on AMC'23, both very small test sets.
Because one solved item moves Pass@1 by several points on these sets, this is direct evidence for repeated-run reporting, rather than evidence that any individual score is false.
