Earlier judge papers typically validate a newly fine-tuned evaluator on its own meta-evaluation benchmark, which can make it appear comparable to GPT-4. This work changes the unit of evaluation: it evaluates the evaluator under format transfer, adversarial superficial-quality bias, fine-grained factuality/toxicity/safety tasks, and prompt interventions. Its central contribution is therefore an empirical reliability diagnosis, not a new reward model or benchmark. The auxiliary generation-versus-classification comparison supports the claim that fitting the same supervision, rather than retaining a generative head, drives the observed behavior.

It does not show that all fine-tuning is harmful; it specifically identifies the interaction of single-task supervision and fixed templates as the audited risk.
