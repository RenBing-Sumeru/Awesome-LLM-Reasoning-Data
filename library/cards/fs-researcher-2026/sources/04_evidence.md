The paper reports report-quality and citation metrics on DeepResearch Bench, comparative judgments on DeepConsult, and answer accuracy on the verifiable BrowseComp subset. It also compares matched backbones, ablates the persistent workspace, dual-agent separation, and section-wise writing, and varies Context Builder rounds. More builder computation grows the knowledge base and is positively associated with several report-quality measures, while the case study also shows readability decreasing as evidence density increases. File I/O is reported as a negligible share of wall-clock time.

This evidence supports the system claim that persistent workspaces can change long-horizon search and writing behavior under the tested harnesses. It does not certify each archived page, note, citation, or report claim. Most open-ended scores use LLM-as-a-judge, the web is mutable, and a correlation between builder compute and report score is not record-level data-quality proof.
