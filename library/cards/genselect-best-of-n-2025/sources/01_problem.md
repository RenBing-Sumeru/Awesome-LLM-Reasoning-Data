GenSelect addresses a concrete Best-of-N bottleneck: after a model samples many independent mathematical solutions, how should a reasoning model choose one without scoring every candidate in isolation or paying for exhaustive pairwise comparison? The work was presented as a poster at the 2nd AI for Math Workshop at ICML 2025; the arXiv record was submitted on 23 July 2025. It belongs in Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data because the operative object is a pool of sampled attempts plus selector traces, tournament decisions, and a final outcome under a stated inference budget.

For the target experiments, one record starts with a Comp-Math-24-25 problem, 64 solutions sampled by QwQ-32B or DeepSeek-R1-0528, final answers extracted from those solutions, summary representations of their reasoning, one or more GenSelect comparison traces ending in a zero-based candidate index, tournament-round metadata, and answer correctness or an HMMT LLM-judge result. The paper evaluates prompt-only selection; it neither trains a selector nor establishes an RL or SFT recipe. Its evidence supports an L4 Card because the paper, appendix, prompts, models, budgets, and results are documented, but the exact Comp-Math candidate pools, selector generations, chosen-index manifest, judge outputs, permutations, and seeds were not released.

A related official artifact needs a separate boundary. OpenMathReasoning exposes 565,620 `genselect` rows constructed for training from AoPS-centered problems. Those rows pack indexed candidate summaries into `problem` and selector reasoning plus `Judgment: IDX` into `generated_solution`. They are attributable GenSelect data from the same NVIDIA line, but they are not the target paper's QwQ/DeepSeek Comp-Math-24-25 evaluation traces.
