The selector sees summaries rather than the original long reasoning traces. A summary can omit a local algebraic error, an invalid recovery after backtracking, or uncertainty that would change a comparison. The paper's preliminary "no significant benefit" statement does not include a released ablation corpus or enough detail to audit what information the summarizer preserved. QwQ summaries are regenerated by Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, adding a second model's compression behavior to the lineage.

Self-GenSelect uses the same reasoning model as candidate generator and selector. Shared model priors, answer-format habits, or correlated mathematical errors can therefore survive both stages. The generated `Judgment: IDX` is also a brittle interface: malformed text, an out-of-range index, or a rationale inconsistent with its last line needs an explicit parsing and failure policy, which the paper does not report in detail.

Tournament output can depend on candidate order, bracket composition, and early elimination. Repeating random permutations and majority-voting 32 selected answers reduces observed variance but does not guarantee invariance, and multiplies selector compute. The reported budget comparison equates numbers of generation calls rather than measured tokens, accelerator time, energy, or end-to-end latency. Because selection is sequentially downstream of generation, the call-count framing can hide a substantial deployment cost.

The evaluation covers only 256 recent competition-math problems. HMMT results depend on an LLM judge, whose outputs and error analysis are not released; judge false positives and false negatives can therefore affect the main gains. The paper does not disclose decontamination against the 2024–2025 questions or possible generator pretraining overlap. Exact candidate pools, full traces, selector outputs, correctness manifests, prompt/version hashes, seeds, and permutation logs are unavailable, blocking end-to-end replication and training reuse of the target object.

The related OpenMathReasoning release improves artifact access but has different limits. Its rows contain summaries rather than original candidate traces; accepted rows were filtered to selectors that chose a correct candidate, so failed decision traces are absent; `Judgment: IDX` and candidate groups are packed into text rather than normalized fields; and public rows omit per-candidate labels. The CC BY 4.0 dataset label and Apache-2.0 code license are explicit, but row-level rights for AoPS source posts are not documented in the inspected release material.
