For the Rollout, Search, and Test-Time Trace Data track, GenSelect supplies a concrete schema for separating generation from selection. A reproducible record should include problem and source; generator and decoding settings; every candidate's stable ID, full trace, summary, extracted answer, and order; group and bracket membership; selector model, prompt version, generated comparison, parsed index, and parse status; repetition and permutation IDs; selected answer; answer checker or judge version and output; token, call, and latency budgets; and retention flags for selected, non-selected, and failed candidates. With these fields, researchers can audit whether gains come from a better candidate pool, better comparative reasoning, repeated voting, or additional compute.

The method can be used as an evaluation baseline for pointwise versus comparative verifiers, an ablation surface for branching factor and candidate order, and a test-time compute policy for studying how generation and selection budgets interact. The unreleased target traces are suitable only as a reading and audit reference: the paper supports reproducing the high-level evaluation design, but not direct training reuse or exact result replication.

The related OpenMathReasoning `genselect` split can support format inspection and, subject to its CC BY 4.0 terms and source-rights review, experiments on selector-trace training. Its 565,620 rows expose complete packed candidate-summary groups, non-selected summaries, selector reasoning, and a parseable judgment. Reusers should first normalize the packed index into a standalone field, reconstruct per-candidate labels where justified, document parse failures, and recognize that incorrect selector runs were filtered out. Any SFT use belongs to that release's lineage; it must not be reported as a training use demonstrated by the target GenSelect paper.

Useful comparisons within the Atlas include `aimo2-openmathreasoning-2025` for the construction and release lineage, and `learning-generative-selection-2026` for later work that trains a selector rather than relying only on prompt-time behavior.
