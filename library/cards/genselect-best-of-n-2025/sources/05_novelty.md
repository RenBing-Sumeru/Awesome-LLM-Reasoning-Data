Pointwise outcome reward models and GenRMs assign each candidate an independent score or correctness decision; pairwise schemes exploit comparison but either pay \(O(N^2)\) for exhaustive ranking or use a deep binary bracket. GenSelect's concrete change is the feedback interface: one prompted reasoning model jointly reads several indexed solution summaries, produces a comparative rationale, and names one index. An N-ary bracket then applies that interface beyond the context limit. The paper's direction signal is therefore comparative selector traces and tournament structure, not a new benchmark, generator, learned reward model, or training algorithm.

Several components are inherited. Candidate sampling and majority voting are standard test-time scaling mechanisms; the N-ary knockout design follows PairJudge-RM; solution summaries are an input compression choice; and QwQ-32B and DeepSeek-R1-0528 are used without selector-specific fine-tuning. The paper's novelty is narrower than "training a better verifier": it shows that existing reasoning models can be prompted to perform N-way selection and studies branching factor and generation-versus-selection allocation on competition math.

For reasoning-data research, the useful change is the record boundary. A GenSelect trajectory can retain all candidate summaries, their order and group assignment, the generated comparative analysis, parsed selected index, tournament edges, repetitions, permutations, generation and selector budgets, and separately computed correctness. That structure exposes non-selected candidates and selection decisions that majority-vote-only records discard. Before reuse, however, one must verify whether the stored candidates are full traces or summaries, whether failures are retained, how indices are parsed, and whether compute is normalized by calls, tokens, or latency.

The 565,620-row OpenMathReasoning `genselect` split is relevant implementation lineage, not evidence that the paper released its experimental corpus. Its packed fields make the candidate group and selector output inspectable, but the public schema does not provide standalone chosen-index or per-candidate correctness columns, and correctness-filtered construction drops failed selectors.
