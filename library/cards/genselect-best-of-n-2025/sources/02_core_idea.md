The contribution is to turn comparative verification into text generation over an entire candidate set. Given a problem and indexed solution summaries, GenSelect asks a reasoning model to screen contradictory answers, analyze the surviving methods, compare mathematical precision, completeness, clarity, efficiency, and robustness, and finish with `Judgment: [IDX]`. The native selector signal is therefore a generated rationale plus a discrete index, not a calibrated scalar reward. It can observe the problem, the supplied summaries, candidate order, and final answers; it cannot inspect reasoning details lost during summarization, ground-truth correctness during selection, hidden model state, or whether the candidate pool covers a correct approach.

When all candidates do not fit in context, the method organizes the same comparison into an N-ary knockout tournament. For 64 candidates, the paper contrasts a binary bracket requiring 63 comparisons over six sequential rounds with a 16-way bracket requiring four first-round calls and one four-way final over two rounds. The final reported setup uses branching factor \(N=8\), two rounds, 32 repetitions under random candidate permutations, and majority voting over the selected answers. Pool size, branching factor, and selector repetitions are separate fields and should not be collapsed into a single "N".

The nearest baselines are majority voting, Qwen2.5-Math-RM-72B as a discriminative outcome reward model, a pointwise QwQ GenRM, and pairwise tournament schemes such as PairJudge-RM. GenSelect changes the comparison interface from one-candidate or two-candidate scoring to joint N-way generated reasoning; it reuses prompting, solution summaries, knockout tournaments, and majority aggregation rather than introducing a new base model or verifier-training objective. The related OpenMathReasoning split demonstrates a training-data serialization of this interface, but its construction predates and differs from the paper's unreleased evaluation pool.
