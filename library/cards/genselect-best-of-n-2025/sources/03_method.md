The target-paper pipeline is:

1. **Inputs.** Build Comp-Math-24-25 from 30 AIME 2024, 30 AIME 2025, 62 HMMT November 2024, 68 HMMT February 2024, and 66 HMMT February 2025 problems. Proof questions and questions with estimated partial credit are excluded, leaving 256 evaluation problems.
2. **Candidate generation and representation.** QwQ-32B or DeepSeek-R1-0528 samples independent solutions at temperature 0.6. The main comparisons use 64 candidates per problem. `Ans(Y)` extracts each final answer; `Summary(Y)` represents the long solution. Reasoning-model solutions naturally expose a post-thinking solution, while QwQ summaries are regenerated with Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct using the Appendix A.1 prompt. The paper says preliminary experiments found no significant benefit from full traces over summaries.
3. **Selection.** QwQ-32B or DeepSeek-R1-0528 acts as its own prompt-only GenSelect verifier. A direct call jointly compares up to the context-limited group size and emits comparative reasoning ending in `Judgment: [IDX]`. Larger pools enter an N-ary knockout tournament; each winner becomes a candidate in the next round.
4. **Aggregation and outcome.** Table 3 evaluates one or eight QwQ selections, with majority voting when repeated. The final setup partitions 64 candidates into \(N=8\) groups, runs two tournament rounds, repeats the tournament 32 times under random permutations, and majority-votes the answers selected by those runs.
5. **Evaluation.** AIME-style questions use extracted-answer correctness. HMMT uses the LLM-judge setup cited by the paper, so the overall verification contract is mixed. Pass@64 is an oracle upper bound, not a deployable selector.

Final reported decoding uses a 32K maximum output length, temperature 0.6, and top-p 0.95. The budget ablation compares \(2N\) solution generations plus majority voting with \(N\) solution generations plus \(N\) GenSelect generations; this matches generation-call counts approximately, not tokens or latency, and selection cannot begin until candidate generation finishes. The paper does not report target-pool decontamination, exact prompt-version hashes, random seeds, judge outputs, or a released execution manifest.

The related OpenMathReasoning construction is a different pipeline: answer-labeled solution pools are summarized; each problem yields eight distinct groups of 2–16 summaries containing at least one correct and one incorrect candidate; QwQ-32B selects among them; selections choosing an incorrect candidate are removed; and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct cleans the accepted comparison trace. Roughly one million generations were filtered to 565,620 public rows. The public nine-field schema retains the complete candidate-summary group and non-selected summaries inside `problem`, and retains the rationale plus parseable index inside `generated_solution`; it does not expose a standalone chosen-index column or a per-candidate correctness-label vector.
