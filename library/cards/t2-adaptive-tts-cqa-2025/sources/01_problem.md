Contextual question answering contains both direct questions that need a short grounded lookup and multi-hop questions that require substantial decomposition and synthesis. A single inference policy wastes resources at one end of this spectrum: always producing elaborate chains adds irrelevant steps and can introduce spurious evidence for simple questions, while always answering quickly leaves hard questions under-reasoned. Fixed token limits and hand-designed early-stop rules reduce some waste, but they decide in advance what the model should consider difficult.

The paper asks how a model can choose its own appropriate reasoning depth for a new document-question pair. The decision target is not merely whether to stop generating tokens. It is which reasoning strategy, including the required subquestions and evidence focus, should guide the answer; the selected strategy is meant to allocate concise processing to straightforward questions and more detailed computation to structurally complex ones.
