T2 differs from a simple early-exit controller because it does not only predict a scalar budget or a stop point. It generates and selects an explicit reasoning plan from structurally analogous questions. The plan carries the intended subproblem structure and reasoning skills, and it determines which evidence should enter the final prompt. This turns test-time adaptation into a choice over inspectable strategy records rather than a hidden rule that merely shortens every chain.

The paper also makes contextual QA, rather than only mathematical answer search, the setting for adaptive scaling. Its mechanism couples structural parsing, synthetic analogues, multi-criteria selection, and focused context extraction. The distinctive contribution is the claim that the same model family can use its performance on generated analogues to choose the depth of processing for a new question, thereby treating strategy selection itself as an inference-time allocation decision.
