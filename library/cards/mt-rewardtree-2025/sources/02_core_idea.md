The paper’s central contribution is a search-to-preference interface for translation: compare two next tokens under the same prefix using the expected quality of their sampled completions, then turn the selected sibling contrast into pairwise data for an implicit PRM. In the approximate MCTS loop, the current prefix is the state and a next token is the action. The model expands only its top-2 logits, produces three full translations per child, assigns each child the mean COMETKiwi score of those rollouts, keeps the higher-valued branch, and repeats until EOS.

The construction feedback contract is `judgment_required`, not programmatic correctness. COMETKiwi observes a completed translation and emits a scalar quality estimate; it does not observe human intent, reference-grounded adequacy, the causal quality of an individual token, or whether a lower-probability branch outside the top-2 would ultimately be better. A prefixed pair operationalizes step-level supervision because `chosen` and `rejected` share the generated prefix and diverge at a sibling token, but the released schema does not expose that prefix or token in separate fields. DPO or KTO then derives an implicit token reward from policy/reference log-ratios rather than from human token labels.

The closest within-paper comparison is the arbitrary or “vanilla” pair variant, where two translations need not preserve the identical-prefix sibling contrast. MT-RewardTree changes the selection unit from sequence-only preference to a token-anchored branch decision backed by rollout values. Its research direction is therefore metric-guided search-to-process supervision for machine translation. The critical boundary is that the paper describes a tree-valued construction process while the release exposes selected pairs, not an auditable tree corpus.
