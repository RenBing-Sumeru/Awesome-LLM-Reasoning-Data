Formal program synthesis is fragmented across Dafny, Verus or Rust, and Lean, whose benchmarks differ in sources, specification styles, and automation, making model results hard to aggregate. Single-language small sets cannot reveal whether models depend on SMT automation, systems constraints, or explicit theorem proving. Natural-language coding benchmarks only approximate correctness through tests.

This work aggregates and expands tasks across three verification ecosystems into a 12,504-specification benchmark. Models receive files containing `sorry`, unimplemented functions, or verification holes, and generated code or proofs are checked by the Dafny verifier, Verus, or the Lean kernel. The release also stores compilable tasks, translation-induced non-compiling issues, natural-language descriptions, source lineage, and results from 55,397 model experiments for cross-tool vericoding analysis.
