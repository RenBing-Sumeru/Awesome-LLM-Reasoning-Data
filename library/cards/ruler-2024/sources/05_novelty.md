The prior-work baseline is nearby benchmark and evaluation-surface datasets that share the same feedback contract. The change introduced by RULER: What's the Real Context Size of Your Long-Context Language Models? is the particular task object and feedback contract: official records expose configurable long-context retrieval, tracing, and aggregation tasks, prompt/task metadata, a model output surface, and a score or success target. scored by the official evaluator, answer key, judge, hidden test, metric, or environment predicate determines success. That combination is the direction signal, not a generic claim that models are better or worse.

The quality signal is the presence of official artifacts (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06654; venue: https://openreview.net/forum?id=kIoBbc76Sy; openreview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=kIoBbc76Sy; code: https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER; data: https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER; doi: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2404.06654), source metadata, and explicit audit fields for split, license, lineage, and failure modes. What is not new is the general idea of benchmarking or judging model outputs. Before reuse, inspect artifact version, license, split, evaluator/judge implementation, data lineage, prompt/scaffold settings, and contamination risk.
