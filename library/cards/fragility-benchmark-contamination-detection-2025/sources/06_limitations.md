The authors state that they do not propose a new detector and that their results concern the tested detection families, training setups, and benchmarks. A near-chance AUROC therefore means a particular audit protocol fails, not that the benchmark is certainly contaminated or that no alternative evidence exists.

Their analysis assumes a known member/non-member split and mostly white-box likelihood access. Real frontier-model audits may lack both; before applying the conclusion, construct independent controls and test sensitivity to split, response format, and model access.
