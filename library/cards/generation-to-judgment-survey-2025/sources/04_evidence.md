The survey does not claim a new trained-model score. Its evidence is coverage: it gives a formal I/O definition, six attribute classes, ten methodology families, and four benchmark categories, then connects them to applications in alignment, retrieval, reasoning, self-evolution, and agent decision-making. This supports the claim that LLM judging is a broad development component rather than only an NLG metric. It cannot prove that any named judge is reliable; that requires the underlying benchmark, human agreement, and adversarial tests.
